# batch-14 — what was decided and why

## SB 1164 keeps its label; the plaintiff limits are ported, not new

The bill widens voting-rights liability considerably: two separate bans instead of one, state
agencies brought in, intentional discrimination expressly not required, and the Attorney General
added as a plaintiff. Against that it adds a 45- or 60-day notice period before suing. A plaintiff
who wins in court recovers reasonable costs and fees (§14029(h)(1)); only a plaintiff who prevails
from the notice letter alone, without suing, is capped near $30,000 (§14029(h)(3)).

Those limits look like counterweights, but they mirror a safe harbor that already exists in the
same code for these claims. The bill moves an existing constraint onto the new causes of action
rather than inventing one, and the remedy rules stay plaintiff-friendly. The label stands and the
description states the waiting period and the cap so a reader can weigh them. Review correction
(2026-09-03): the first draft said cost recovery "would be capped near $30,000" without the
notice-letter-only condition; the enrolled text limits the cap to that case, and the descriptions
now say so.

## SB 1173 is not simply pro-defendant, and the description avoids that framing

Letting a defendant request an instruction on a closely related lesser offense usually helps the
defence. But a lesser-offense instruction can also produce a conviction where a jury might
otherwise have acquitted outright. The description says what the bill does and what the three
conditions are, without characterizing who benefits.

## SB 1037's deleted criterion is described, not hidden

The bill hands the judgment on unreasonable rate increases from a federal agency to state
regulators and adds a catch-all. It also deletes the current instruction that regulators weigh the
state cost targets when judging an individual rate, replacing it with an annual affordability
report. That moves the cost-target link from binding on decisions to published in a report, and
the description says so.
