# batch-24 — what was decided and why

## Two bills that act on the state, not on anyone else

**SB 998** adds a disability discrimination coordinator, four deputy coordinators, and written
duties for all of them. **SB 98** requires schools and colleges to notify their communities when
immigration enforcement is confirmed on site.

Both were kept. Both descriptions end by saying what the bill does not do: SB 998 creates no new
duty for any school district, and SB 98 adds notification duties only, leaving officers' access
rules untouched. Those closing sentences matter more than usual here, because both titles invite a
reader to assume a broader change than the text makes.

## SB 909 keeps its label with the safety valve named

The bill raises every prevailing-wage penalty, most of them unchanged since 2012. It also leaves
in place the power to reduce a penalty for a promptly corrected good faith mistake, or where the
penalty would be disproportionate to the violation. That is existing law carried forward rather
than something the bill grants, and the description says so.

## SB 827 reaches less far than its own subject suggests

It adds a fiscal training requirement for local officials, and that requirement does not reach
school districts, even though the ethics training it sits beside does. The bill also sets no
penalty for failing to comply. Both facts close the description.
