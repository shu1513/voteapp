# batch-19 — what was decided and why

## AB 1296's official title is wrong for its contents

The title refers to school district reserve funds. The operative text is entirely about housing:
the state housing department must build a form letting school districts register interest in using
their own land for housing, publish what comes in, and provide advisory technical assistance. This
is what a gut-and-amend leaves behind, and a record written from the title alone would be simply
false. The description opens by saying the title refers to reserve funds and the text is about
housing.

## AB 1539 is the first measure in the campaign to earn `election_integrity`

Earlier batches moved voter-access bills to `civil_rights` because that area's description is
about elections being secure, accurate, auditable and trusted, not about access. AB 1539 fits the
description directly: it requires a sworn certification that a presidential ticket is eligible
under the Twenty-Second Amendment before the names go on the ballot.

The description also states the limit that matters. The Secretary of State is given no power to
reject a certification, so the whole check rests on the signer's exposure to a perjury charge.

## AB 1439 was dropped for having no operative duty at all

Every verb in it is "requests" — it requests a university write a study and requests two pension
boards hand over data, and it adds a clause letting the boards decline on fiduciary grounds. A
yes vote commits nobody to anything. This is the same test that dropped SB 57 in batch-16, applied
to a bill that looks more substantial.

## AB 1577 dropped on the disclosure dimension

The data center bill compels operators to report energy use to the state and to local permitting
agencies, and in the same act creates a new public records exemption and limits publication to
aggregated, anonymized figures. Information flows to government while public access narrows. That
is two directions on one dimension, so no label.
