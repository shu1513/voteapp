# batch-17 — what was decided and why

## SB 177 is named like a tax and is actually a report

The bill is titled the Fair Share from Big Corporations Act, which sounds like a charge on large
employers. It is not. It appropriates $10,000 and requires the Department of Finance to hand the
Legislature options by March 2027, one of which must involve a premium on employers with 250 or
more employees. It bills no one, and it switches off entirely if Congress repeals the federal
Medicaid provisions first.

The description says all of that and ends by stating plainly that the bill requires a report, not
a payment. It is kept rather than dropped because the duty is mandatory, which is the line drawn
in batch-16.

## SB 36 keeps its label, with the caveat stated

The bill is overwhelmingly a tightening: price gouging becomes something a consumer can sue over
rather than only a crime, penalties rise for people displaced by an emergency, listing platforms
gain takedown duties, and rent protection reaches leases of any length. It leaves the 10 percent
cap and the 30- and 180-day windows untouched, which the description says.

One provision runs the other way. The bill removes the Legislature from the bodies that may extend
price-gouging protections, while letting it end an extension the Governor made. That is a real
change to who controls the protections, so it is in the description rather than left out. It does
not reverse the bill's direction, so the label stands.

## Three measures dropped in this pass

SB 281 requires a verbatim immigration warning before a guilty plea, then bars anyone whose plea
predates 2026 from using a missing warning to reopen their case. Stronger warnings ahead, no
remedy behind.

SB 354 rewrites insurance privacy law with opt-in consent, a ban on selling data and deletion
rights, then caps what a consumer can recover, grants immunity for disclosures made under the
article, preempts inconsistent state privacy law, and exempts title insurance, commercial
insurance and most agents. Too much of it runs against the rest.

SB 333 is another single-county tax-ballot authorization.
