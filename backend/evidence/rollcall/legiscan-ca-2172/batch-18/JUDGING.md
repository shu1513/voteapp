# batch-18 — what was decided and why

## SB 747 keeps its label because its limits are scope, not direction

The No Kings Act creates a state-court route to sue over federal constitutional violations,
reaching federal officials, with legal fees available. It is hedged more than almost any bill in
the campaign: immunity tracks federal law exactly and the bill says so twice, sovereign immunity
is untouched, official-capacity money claims are excluded, judges get two shields, the retroactive
window allows only nominal and compensatory damages, and the right to move a case to federal court
is expressly preserved.

Every one of those narrows what the new claim reaches. None of them takes away something that
exists today. That is the test from AB 1078, so the label stands and the description states the
immunity parity plainly, because a reader would otherwise assume a state civil rights bill weakens
qualified immunity. This one deliberately does not.

## Two gun-adjacent bills, opposite outcomes

**SB 937 was kept.** It adds flash-bang grenades to the existing crowd-control limits and bars
them, along with explosive door-breaching charges, in immigration enforcement. Everything in it
restricts. The carve-outs preserve existing law rather than loosening it.

**SB 948 was dropped.** It requires live-fire training before anyone can get a firearm safety
certificate, and in the same act triples the window for bringing a gun into the state from 60 to
180 days, sets the penalty at an infraction rather than a misdemeanor, and grants an amnesty to
late filers who come forward on their own. That is the AB 1078 pattern exactly: tightening and
loosening on one dimension, so no label.

## SB 274 was dropped for the same reason, and it is worth naming

The license plate reader bill adds a 60-day deletion rule, one permitted police purpose, per-query
logging and mandatory training. It also removes transit agencies, local transportation and public
works departments, and airports from coverage entirely. Those bodies run plate cameras too, and
for them the bill is a deregulation. Tightening for one set of agencies while exempting another
set is mixed direction on the same dimension.

## Committee-authored budget bills, and why they keep getting dropped

Eight measures in this stretch came from the Committee on Budget and Fiscal Review. A vote on a
trailer bill usually reflects a position on the budget package as a whole rather than on any
provision inside it, and the bills bundle unrelated policy that points in several directions at
once. SB 172 alone runs from boxing fees to 911 procurement and both adds and removes oversight.
None of them can carry an honest stance label.
