# batch-13 — what was decided and why

## Fourteen measures read and dropped alongside these sixteen

The drops are recorded with reasons in `../worklist/ledger.json`. Three patterns account for
almost all of them.

**Budget trailer bills.** AB 137, AB 143, AB 144, SB 122 and SB 125 all carry a one-word or
generic title and bundle unrelated policy. AB 144's digest runs to 33 items across Olympic
licensure, vaccine authority, lab fees and immigrant Medi-Cal. No single label is honest.

**Bills that cut both ways.** SB 1004 adds state officers to the police mask ban but cuts the
civil damages rule from six torts to two. SB 1135 is called the Wildlife Coexistence Act, yet its
only immediately operative provision lets dogs pursue wolves, a listed species, while the
protective half waits on an appropriation. SB 1105 strips federal arrest authority while granting
it to tribal officers and preserving it for park rangers.

**Measures where no available area fits.** AJR 29 is a joint resolution that changes no law.
ACA 8 sets aside the independent redistricting commission for congressional maps while declaring
support for independent commissions nationwide; that is too contested to label in either
direction, so it is recorded as read and dropped rather than judged.

## Two vetoed civil-rights bills kept

AB 7 and AB 742 both concern descendants of American slavery, and both are permissive rather than
mandatory: AB 7 says colleges "may consider" a preference, and AB 742 depends on a separate bill
creating a certifying bureau. Weak effect is not the same as unclear direction. Both were vetoed,
and the descriptions say so and describe what the bill would have done.

## AB 2393 keeps its label despite a police carve-out

The bill lets someone falsely arrested by a private actor claim fixed damages, and expressly does
not reach peace officers or their employers. A reader seeing "false arrest damages" would assume
police, so the description states the exclusion plainly. The carve-out narrows who can be sued; it
does not push the other way on the plaintiff's remedy.

## A spelling error reached the database and was corrected

Batch-13 was judged, then a hand check found British forms the screen did not know about, including
"instalments" in AB 2428 and "licence" in two bills. The judgments file was corrected, but by then
the import had written 77 records carrying the old spelling. Re-judging and re-importing rewrote
them in place, which is what the importer does with a corrected approved judgment. The screen now
covers these forms, and two false positives it introduced — "analysis" and "enrolling", both
correct American English — were removed from the pattern.
