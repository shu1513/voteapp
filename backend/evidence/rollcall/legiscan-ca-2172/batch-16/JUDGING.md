# batch-16 — what was decided and why

## Bills kept, with their weakness stated in the description

Three of the six are weaker than their titles imply, and each description says so rather than
letting the title carry the impression.

- **SB 437** funds reparations research and orders a way to verify descent from an enslaved
  person. It pays no claim and decides no one's eligibility. The description ends by saying that.
- **SB 551** rewrites the stated purpose of the prison system around normalization and dynamic
  security. Most of it is findings and a mission statement; the training clause says the
  department "should" act, not "shall". The description says it creates no prisoner right and no
  way to enforce one.
- **SB 630** loosens oversight of parks land deals, letting the department appraise land it is
  buying. It is deregulatory, but gated by a price cap, a public meeting, three reports to the
  Legislature and a 2033 sunset. All of that is in the description.

## SB 418 is two bills sharing a topic

One half requires a year's supply of hormone therapy. The other bans insurer discrimination on
race, national origin, disability, sex, gender identity and more. A legislator could support one
and not the other, so the description covers both and notes that only the hormone half expires,
at the start of 2035. It also states that Medi-Cal managed care plans, which cover most enrollees,
were excluded — the carve-out that most limits the bill's reach.

## Four measures read and dropped

SB 477 forces an individual to wait for a related group case to end before getting a right-to-sue
notice, which cuts against the deadline relief that is the rest of the bill. SB 63 pairs a
14-year Bay Area transit sales tax with conditions letting a committee withhold money from the
same operators. SB 512 is another local tax-ballot bill. SB 57 is the permissive study described
in PLAN.md. Reasons are in the ledger.
