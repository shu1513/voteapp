# batch-23 — what was decided and why

## A bill that strengthens the law and then repeals its own strengthening

SB 1323 makes several immigration-enforcement protections at hospitals and clinics mandatory
rather than optional, and requires a physical judicial warrant rather than any warrant. Every one
of those changes expires on January 1, 2030, when the earlier and weaker text returns.

It was kept, because for three years the law is genuinely tighter and a legislator voting for it
voted for that. The description ends by saying the strengthened provisions expire and the earlier
versions come back, so no reader mistakes it for a permanent change.

## Kept measures whose titles promise more than the text delivers

- **SB 1255** would create a state designation for colleges serving Latino students. It carries an
  insignia and no money, and nothing in it operates until the Legislature appropriates funds.
- **SB 1388** is called a Risk Reduction Program. It provides advice and guidance about insurance.
  It provides no insurance, no backstop and no money.
- **SB 787** would appoint a counselor, convene working groups and publish reports on clean energy
  manufacturing. It buys nothing, spends nothing, and the fund it creates is left empty.

Each description says so in its closing sentence. These were kept rather than dropped because each
carries at least one mandatory duty on a named body, which is the line drawn in batch-16.

## SB 24 keeps its label with the carve-out named

The bill bars utilities from charging ratepayers for fighting a public takeover of their service.
Two things survive that cut against it: payments under federal labor-management agreements may
still be charged to ratepayers, and advocacy before regulators about the utility's own operations
stays excluded. Both are in the description.

## Eight measures dropped

Four cut both ways. SB 610 adds disaster protections for tenants while removing the in-place market
value payment owed to displaced mobilehome residents. SB 809 pairs driver-friendly classification
rules with an amnesty relieving employers of misclassification penalties. SB 450 shifts the cost of
a deficient out-of-state home study onto adopting families. SB 1166 runs its duties equally against
a transit district and its union.

The other four fail different tests already settled: SB 1154 is permissive authority no one must
use, SB 1014's headline estimate is expressly not binding, SB 762 is thirteen separate local tax
authorizations, and SB 578 fits no research area.
