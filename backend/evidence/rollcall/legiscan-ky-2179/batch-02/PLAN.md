## The two measures batch-01 dropped

**HB 424 is now imported with no stance.** Batch-01 dropped it because the Act
broadens the grounds for removing a tenured faculty member while also
lengthening the notice period before removal. That is the same two-strand shape
as HB 90 and HB 695, which batch-01 imported as `general`, so dropping it was
more conservative than the campaign's own precedent. It carries `general` here
and the description names both strands.

Re-reading it also corrected a claim in the batch-01 notes: those notes said the
Act turns a closed list of removal grounds into an open-ended one at the
comprehensive universities. KRS 164.360 already read "except for cause, which
shall include", both before and after, so that claim is wrong. Nothing was
imported on it, so nothing in the database was affected.

**HB 684 stays dropped.** It runs at least four ways at once: a 60-day
surveillance recording requirement that also puts a 60-day clock on asking for
the video, certified write-in lists taken off public view, candidate residence
addresses removed from three of the four public registers it touches, and
certification of results gated on a hand-to-eye audit. Unlike HB 424's two
strands, no honest three-sentence description can carry them all.

## What was dropped in this batch

Eleven more measures were read and dropped under filter 5, with a reason
recorded on each roll in the worklist. Four kinds:

- **Budget measures** carry no single subject: HB 546, HB 775, HJR 30, HJR 46.
- **Omnibus Acts** with no single direction: SB 25 (revenue bonds, a Medicaid
  oversight board, fire marshal appeals and a rebuild of the Auditor's office),
  HB 566 (the Horse Racing and Gaming Corporation), HB 501 (a pharmacy rule
  bundled with an amendment to HB 495).
- **Measures that relax and tighten in the same sentence**: HB 196, which cuts
  mine emergency technicians on shifts of ten or fewer miners from two to one
  while adding one for every extra fifty miners underground; SB 207, the School
  Innovation Act, which grants local waivers from statute while mandating
  state-approved instructional materials; SB 162, which requires suspected
  unemployment fraud to be referred to prosecutors within 30 days.
- **Too thin to name a subject**: SB 245, whose only change is that a Fish and
  Wildlife commissioner whose reappointment the Senate declines vacates the seat
  at adjournment.
