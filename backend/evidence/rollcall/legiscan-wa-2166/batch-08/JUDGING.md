# Washington batch-08 — how each measure was judged

Source for every measure: the **Final Bill Report** on leg.wa.gov, "Synopsis as Enacted".
The Background was read for Senate Bills 5040, 5068 and 5175, where the Summary alone could
not show what changed.

### Senate Bill 5040 — interest arbitration, `labor_rights` yes = for, no = null

The Background shows police officers counted as uniformed personnel only in cities or towns
of 2,500 or more and counties of 10,000 or more. The act removes both thresholds and adds
municipal airport officers. Interest arbitration is glossed because nothing else in the
description makes sense without it. Collective bargaining is the area's own mechanism.

### Senate Bill 5041 — strikers and unemployment, `labor_rights` yes = for, no = null

Striking workers were disqualified for the whole strike. From 1 January 2026 the
disqualification ends on the second Sunday after the strike starts, benefits last at most
six weeks, the waiting week applies, benefits are repaid if a final judgment finds the
strike unlawful or back pay covers the weeks, multi-employer lockouts are no longer
disqualifying, and charges go to the struck employer. It all expires at the end of 2035.
The area's description names the right to organize, and the act's whole operative content
supports a strike. The labor backfill's rule that general unemployment policy stays out of
`labor_rights` does not reach this act, which is about strikes only.

### Senate Bill 5068 — noncitizen public employment, `immigration` yes = for, no = null

The Background shows certification required citizenship, permanent residency or DACA
status. The act lets agencies hire anyone with federal work authorization as custodial
corrections staff and as deputy prosecutors, gives a liability shield for rejecting an
applicant whose work authorization is too limited, and ends mandatory decertification of an
officer barred from firearms only by immigration status, where federal law would allow
carrying on duty. The area's description welcomes lawful immigration.

### Senate Bill 5083 — hospital payment caps, `healthcare_affordability` yes = for, no = null

From 2027, public and school employee plans pay most Medicaid hospitals no more than 200
percent of Medicare in network and 185 percent out of network, with separate limits for two
counties' children's hospitals, a 150 percent floor for in-network primary and behavioral
care, a ban on balance billing, and exemptions for critical access and sole community
hospitals not owned by larger systems. Lower prices paid for care are the area's goal.

### Senate Bill 5093 — pregnancy loss, `womens_reproductive_rights` yes = for, no = null

Ends coroner and medical examiner jurisdiction over deaths from known or suspected
abortions, premature births and stillbirths, repeals the crime of concealing a birth, and
from 1 July 2027 requires detention facilities to report pregnancy losses anonymously. Each
change removes a route to investigating a person after a pregnancy ends.

### Senate Bill 5101 — hate crime victims at work, `labor_rights` yes = for, no = null

Reasonable leave and safety accommodations for an employee who is, or whose family member
is, a hate crime victim, with verification allowed and discrimination barred. The act does
not say the leave is paid, so the description does not either. House-only (Senate 40-8).

### Senate Bill 5104 — coercion by immigration threat, `labor_rights` yes = for, no = null

Civil penalties for an employer that threatens a worker's or family member's immigration
status to deter the exercise of wage and labor rights, with deadlines, per-act counting and
confidentiality. The operative content protects the exercise of labor rights, so the area
is `labor_rights` rather than `immigration`. House-only (Senate 40-9).

### Senate Bill 5122 — premerger notification, `corporate_accountability` yes = for, no = null

The Uniform Antitrust Pre-Merger Notification Act. A company filing a federal
Hart-Scott-Rodino form must file it with the Attorney General too if based in Washington,
if its in-state sales in the business involved reach 20 percent of the federal threshold,
or if it is a health care provider here. Confidentiality and a $10,000 a day penalty are
carried.

### Senate Bill 5175 — solar panel takeback delay, `environment_and_public_health` yes = **against**, no = null

The Background gives the old dates: plans due 1 July 2024 and a sales ban from 1 July 2025.
The act moves them to 31 January 2030 and 31 January 2031 and adds an advisory committee.
A yes vote postpones an existing environmental standard by more than five years, so the
label is yes = against. Nay is null because a no vote does not enact anything; it only
keeps a deadline that had already passed. House-only (Senate 49-0).
