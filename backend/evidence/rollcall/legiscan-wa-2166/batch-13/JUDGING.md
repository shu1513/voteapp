# Washington batch-13 — how each measure was judged

Source for every measure: the nonpartisan House Bill Report **as passed House** (`HBR APH`)
on leg.wa.gov, which describes exactly the version the House voted on, checked against that
version's text. No Senate version exists for any of them.

- **House Bill 1002.** Exempts PTSD claims by county coroner and medical examiner staff
  from the rule that stress-caused mental conditions are not occupational diseases, with a
  pre-hire exam condition for staff hired after 2026-01-01. Workers' compensation is a
  worker protection.
- **House Bill 1080.** Mandatory fees must sit inside the advertised lodging rate;
  government taxes and fees in the total before booking; $10,000 civil penalty per
  violation. Consumer protection.
- **House Bill 1291.** Labor and delivery exempt from the deductible, including state and
  school employee plans; HSA plans set at the federal minimum. Lower out-of-pocket cost.
- **House Bill 1423.** A pilot for noise enforcement cameras inside street-racing camera
  zones, until 2028-07-01, with audio limits and a demographic placement report.
  Enforcement against illegal noise and racing.
- **House Bill 1622.** Public employers must bargain over AI that affects wages or
  performance reviews. The exemption is narrow: a third-party update to technology already
  in use that does not meaningfully affect wages or reviews (read from the engrossed text,
  which is tighter than the report's paraphrase). The House version carried a null-and-void
  clause tied to 2025 budget funding, and the description says so.
- **House Bill 2041.** Cuts continuous postpartum Apple Health coverage from 12 to 6 months
  for people approved from 2026-07-01. The report's background confirms current law is 12
  months. A coverage cut, so yes = **against**.
- **House Bill 2095.** A rebuttable presumption of negligence for drivers who injure or
  kill a vulnerable road user in a protected area, with listed exceptions. Road-safety
  accountability.
- **House Bill 2244.** Removes about ten public records exemptions and adds one
  (archaeological site locations). The bill moves toward disclosure as a whole, which is the
  transparency strand of anti_corruption.
- **House Bill 2464.** Next-business-day incident reporting by private detention
  facilities, Attorney General enforcement, and annual police reports. The report frames it
  as protecting detained people's rights and never says the facility holds immigrants, so
  it is civil_rights, not immigration.
- **House Bill 2515.** Data centers of 20 megawatts or more: cost-protective tariffs,
  water and energy reports, and 80 percent new clean power by 2030 and 100 percent by 2045
  for new or expanded centers. The clean-power requirement sets the area.
- **House Bill 2637.** Adds personal details (age, birth date, address, location data, ID
  numbers, utility data) to the Public Records Act exemptions, with voter and property
  record carve-outs. Personal data protection.
