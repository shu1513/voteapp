# Washington batch-03 — how each measure was judged

Source for every measure: the **Final Bill Report** on leg.wa.gov, "Synopsis as Enacted",
written by non-partisan legislative staff with no sponsor statement of intent. The
report's Summary is the delta index; it is not the source for limits.

## Per measure

### House Bill 1232 — private detention facilities, `corporate_accountability` yes = for, no = null

The act widens the definition of a private detention facility to reach non-profit
operators, not only for-profit ones. The Department of Health must write rules on
disability access, lighting, hot and cold running water, housekeeping and waste. Covered
facilities take on new duties for food service and staffing, staff training, private
visits, a free telephone on each floor, infection control, and written policies against
abuse and neglect. The department may inspect at any time, must publish what it finds,
and for a repeat or uncorrected violation may impose conditions or fine the operator up
to $10,000 a violation, capped at $1 million.

`corporate_accountability` follows the batch-02 medical debt reasoning: every operative
duty binds a private operator, and the enforcement runs against that operator.

Nay is null. The realistic objection is the cost and reach of state regulation, which is
a different axis from holding a contractor to account.

### House Bill 1710 — Voting Rights Act preclearance, `civil_rights` yes = for, no = null

A local government goes on the Attorney General's list if in the past 25 years it lost or
settled a voting discrimination case, or made a covered change without the approval this
act requires. A listed government must obtain a certificate of no objection before it
changes its method of election, its district lines, its plan of government, or its rules
on interpreters and non-English voting materials. The certificate must be issued if the
change will not diminish the affected class's ability to elect its preferred candidates
and breaks no other law. Refusals are appealable, and the Attorney General or an
aggrieved person may sue.

`civil_rights` is the anti-discrimination area and this act is anti-discrimination
enforcement for a protected class. **It is not a voter-access measure**, which matters —
see the two drops below.

### House Bill 1750 — abridgment standard, `civil_rights` yes = for, no = null

The act writes a separate test for abridgment claims: a violation occurs when an election
practice results in, is likely to result in, or is intended to result in a material
disparate burden on a protected class's ability to vote or take part. The government's
only defense is to show by clear and convincing evidence that the practice is narrowly
tailored, necessary to a compelling particularized interest, and that no less burdensome
alternative exists. Proof of intent is not required, and the court may not weigh how long
the practice has been used, whether other jurisdictions use it, or how many class members
were not burdened.

Both dilution and abridgment are glossed in the description, because the whole act turns
on the difference between them.

### House Bill 2225 — companion chatbots, `ai_regulation` yes = for, no = null

An operator must disclose that the chatbot is artificial, at the start and every three
hours, and every hour where it knows the user is a minor or the chatbot is directed to
minors. It must take reasonable measures against sexually explicit or suggestive content
with minors and against manipulative techniques that prolong an emotional relationship.
Every operator must run a protocol for detecting suicidal ideation or self-harm,
referring users to crisis resources and blocking content that encourages self-harm, and
must publish that protocol and last year's referral count. Violations are unfair acts
under the Consumer Protection Act.

**The act takes effect on 1 January 2027**, so the description says "will set".

**House-only**: the Senate passed it 43-5, which fails the divided gate.

### Senate Bill 6002 — automated license plate readers, `data_privacy` yes = for, no = null

Washington's first regulation of the technology. Agency use is unlawful except for named
police purposes, parking enforcement, and traffic or commercial vehicle work. Use is
banned for immigration enforcement, for tracking protected speech, and near schools,
places of worship, courts, food banks, health care facilities and immigration facilities.
Agencies must register with the Attorney General and certify policies and training. Data
must be deleted within 21 days, with listed exceptions; agencies may not sell data or
grant outside access except in judicial proceedings; a match alone is not reasonable
suspicion; willful violations are gross misdemeanors and injured parties may sue.

The description explains what the reader is, because the number of limits is meaningless
without it. Nay is null: the realistic objection is that the limits hamper policing,
which runs on the public safety axis rather than the privacy one.

**Senate-only**: the House passed it 84-10, which fails the divided gate.

### Senate Bill 6026 — homes on commercial land, `housing_affordability` yes = for, no = null

A city or county of 30,000 or more that plans fully under the Growth Management Act may
not exclude residential use from commercial or mixed-use zones. The exclusions are
carried but summarized: industrial zones, land within 3,200 feet of a working refinery,
landmark demolition, land outside the urban growth area, military and airport buffers,
older tax increment areas, certain shorelines and critical areas. It may not require
ground floor commercial on more than 40 percent of its commercial and mixed-use acreage,
and never for publicly subsidized affordable housing. Each city must offer a waiver
process, and the state rule overrides conflicting local rules after 18 months.

Nay is null: the argument against upzoning sits inside `housing_affordability` itself.

### Senate Bill 6081 — sex designation records, `data_privacy` yes = for, no = null

The Public Records Act exemption for licensing records is widened to cover sex
designation. The Department of Licensing may not display or transmit a record of a change
without the person's consent for a specific purpose, and a credential issued after a
change must show only the current designation with no indicator that a change occurred —
including in the data sent on for voter registration. Vital records held by the
Department of Health, and supporting documentation transferred to the State Archives, get
the same protection, and are also placed beyond discovery, subpoena and other compulsory
process. The department may refuse a data request where the information could reasonably
be used to cause harm.

`data_privacy` rather than `civil_rights`: every operative provision is a limit on
disclosure of a record, which is the privacy area's own mechanism. The civil rights
strand is real but it is the motive, not the machinery.

## Dropped after reading

**House Bill 1916 — voter registration challenges.** Two strands that run opposite ways
inside `election_integrity`. It raises the standard for challenging a registration from
clear and convincing evidence to beyond a reasonable doubt, makes dismissal mandatory for
a defective challenge, and creates offenses for knowingly false or baseless challenges.
It also compels county auditors to cancel a Washington registration once a more recent
out-of-state registration is verified, and to confirm a death before cancelling. One
strand makes removals harder, the other makes them automatic. No research area carries a
single defensible direction over the act as a whole. Filter-5 drop.

**Senate Bill 5077 — automatic voter registration expansion.** Voter-access expansion.
`election_integrity` in this taxonomy is a secure, accurate and auditable count, not the
size of the electorate, and the campaign dropped exactly this class in California
(AB 930 and AB 1249). Filter-5 drop, and the reason is recorded in the worklist so the
same measure is not re-triaged later.

**Senate Bill 6035 — voting services for military, overseas, Native American and disabled
voters.** Its operative content is a schedule of meetings between county auditors and
tribes, a biennial forum, and a feasibility study of an electronic ballot portal that the
Secretary of State *may* conduct. A meeting duty and an optional study are not an
operative policy change. Filter-5 drop.

## Deferred, not dropped

**House Bill 1296 — public education.** A large omnibus with two genuine strands: student
nondiscrimination and a new state complaint process on one side, and a rewrite of the
parental notification rights list on the other. It deserves a two-label judgment and a
full read of a 634-line report, so it is left as a candidate in the worklist rather than
rushed into this batch.
