# Washington batch-04 — how each measure was judged

Source for every measure: the **Final Bill Report** on leg.wa.gov, "Synopsis as Enacted",
written by non-partisan legislative staff with no sponsor statement of intent.

## Per measure

### House Bill 1183 — building code reform, `housing_affordability` yes = for, no = null

Cities and counties planning under the Growth Management Act must allow an insulated
exterior wall to project up to 8 inches into a setback, a roof to exceed its height limit
by 8 inches for insulation and by at least 48 inches for solar panels, and floor area to
be measured from the interior face of the exterior wall. They may not require facade
modulation, upper-level setbacks, or off-street parking for affordable housing, passive
house construction, conversions, modular construction or mass timber construction. The
parking limit yields only to a study by credentialed planners showing a significant
safety loss. Minimum unit sizes for low-income and very low-income housing are capped.

"Setback" is glossed in place, because every other limit in the description depends on
it. The parking exception is carried rather than dropped: without it the description
overstates the reach.

### House Bill 1795 — restraint and isolation, `public_education_quality` yes = for, no = null

Mechanical restraint, chemical restraint, and any physical restraint or escort that is
life-threatening or restricts breathing or blood flow are prohibited outright. Restraint
and isolation may no longer be used for an imminent likelihood of serious harm to
property, only to a person. Isolation may not be written into the plan of a student with
an individualized education program or a Section 504 plan, and restraint may be written
into one only on a parent's request, a licensed provider's written statement of medical
necessity, and the parent's informed written consent. No new room may be established
primarily for isolation. The duties now reach every provider of public educational
services, not school districts alone.

`public_education_quality` follows the Maryland precedent (HB 197, restorative
practices), which put school discipline practice in this area rather than in
`civil_rights`.

### House Bill 1875 — sick leave for immigration hearings, `immigration` yes = for, no = null

Paid sick leave may be used to prepare for or attend a judicial or administrative
immigration proceeding involving the worker or a family member. Where the absence exceeds
three days and the employer asks for verification, it must accept a letter from an
advocate, attorney, member of the clergy or other assisting professional, and must also
accept the worker's own written statement. Neither may disclose anything about the
immigration status of the worker or the family member, and providing one does not waive
privilege. Transportation network company drivers get the same right on the same terms.

Nay is null: the realistic objection is the burden of a new leave category on employers,
which runs on a different axis from immigration policy — the same reasoning batch-02
applied to the immigrant worker notice act.

### House Bill 1971 — hormone therapy refills, `healthcare_affordability` yes = for, no = null

A health plan issued or renewed from 1 January 2026 that covers prescription hormone
therapy must reimburse a 12-month supply dispensed at one time, but only where the
medication stores safely at room temperature. The exceptions are all carried: the
enrollee asks for less, the prescriber directs less, the drug is a controlled substance,
the last quarter of a plan year after a year's supply has been dispensed, and a temporary
90-day limit during a dispensing shortage.

The description explains what hormone therapy is and lists the conditions it treats,
because the act's reach is otherwise easy to misread as covering one use only.

**House-only**: the Senate passed it 40-9, which fails the divided gate.

### House Bill 2266 — shelter and supportive housing, `housing_affordability` yes = for, no = null

Transitional and permanent supportive housing must be allowed in any zone inside an urban
growth area where dwelling units or hotels are allowed; indoor emergency shelters and
emergency housing must be allowed wherever hotels are, and in further zones where the
comprehensive plan shows a projected need. None may be sited on industrial land. A local
government may not impose standards more restrictive than those for other lodging or
residential development in the same zone, though objective regulations such as height,
setback, lot coverage and stormwater still apply. Design review must be administrative
and objective, with no public predecision hearing. The operator certification a city may
require is a closed list, and the city may ask for nothing beyond it.

The closed list matters and is stated as a limit, because it is what stops the
certification becoming an open-ended veto.

### House Bill 2320 — firearm manufacturing, `gun_control` yes = for, **no = against**

The existing manufacturing bans are extended to three-dimensional printers, computer
numerical control mills and digital firearm manufacturing code. Making a machine gun,
bump-fire stock, undetectable firearm, short-barreled shotgun or short-barreled rifle by
those means is a class C felony. Making or assembling an untraceable firearm, and making
a frame or receiver or unfinished frame or receiver, run a civil infraction, then a
misdemeanor, then a gross misdemeanor, with an exception for a federal firearms
manufacturer or a dealer repairing existing firearms. Selling, transferring or
distributing manufacturing code to anyone not federally licensed is barred, as is
possessing it with intent to distribute or to manufacture.

All three machines and the code are glossed, because a reader cannot judge the act
without knowing what it reaches.

**Authored nay.** Single subject, the whole operative content is the research area's own
mechanism, and the mainstream objection is to that mechanism — the same test that carried
the firearms act in batch-01 and the abortion medication act in batch-02.
