# Washington batch-11 — how each measure was judged

Source for every measure: the **Final Bill Report** on leg.wa.gov, "Synopsis as Enacted",
including each Partial Veto Summary.

### House Bill 1345 — rural detached accessory dwelling units, `housing_affordability` yes = for, no = null

Lets fully planning counties allow one detached accessory unit per rural lot outside urban
growth areas, under limits on size (1,296 square feet), siting (150 feet, shared driveway),
metered water within groundwater limits, septic capacity and lot size, with enforcement and
reporting. More homes is the area's supply goal. Senate-only.

### House Bill 1409 — clean fuels program, `environment_and_public_health` yes = for, no = null

The survey flagged this as needing a full read, because it tightens the schedule but
replaces the Clean Air Act penalties. It raises the 2038 target to 45 percent (or 55 on
findings), adds yearly cuts, removes the pause beyond 10 percent, and caps the standard at
20 percent after 2030 until a new biofuel plant is permitted. The penalty change swaps
general penalties for program-specific ones of comparable weight ($10,000 a day for
missing reports, four times the credit price for unretired deficits). On balance the act
tightens the standard, so yes = for. The biofuel cap is carried so the tightening is not
overstated.

### House Bill 1494 — multifamily tax exemption, `housing_affordability` yes = for, no = null

Widens where cities and large counties may offer the exemption, lets tenants keep income
status up to 150 percent of the limit, requires deed restrictions on owner-occupied
projects, and adds graduated penalties instead of all-or-nothing cancellation. It is a tax
preference, but its only purpose is affordable housing supply. So it is judged as housing,
not dropped as fiscal machinery.

### House Bill 1903 — statewide low-income energy assistance, `social_programs_and_welfare` yes = for, no = null

Creates a program, subject to appropriation, delivering bill credits through
participating utilities, tiered by need, supplemental to existing programs. Deferred since
batch-03 until the veto message was read. The veto removed only the advisory group.

### House Bill 2156 — Attorney General investigators, `public_safety_and_crime_control` yes = for, no = null

Designates limited-authority peace officers in the Attorney General's office for economic
and financial crimes, with training and background requirements, electronic service of
judge-approved warrants on businesses, and no power to arrest, detain, carry weapons or
physically serve warrants. The limits are carried in full, because they are what the
debate was about.

### House Bill 2215 — fuel suppliers in cap-and-invest, `environment_and_public_health` yes = for, no = null

From the second compliance period, lowers the coverage threshold from 25,000 to 500 metric
tons for newer gasoline, diesel, biodiesel and propane suppliers, lets Ecology adjust it by
rule, and conditions state and local contracts on program participation. The lubricant
exemption runs the other way but is minor next to the threshold change; it is stated.
House-only.

### Senate Bill 5232 — Housing and Essential Needs, `social_programs_and_welfare` yes = for, no = null

Continued aid for elderly or disabled adults on Social Security leaving the program with
urgent housing need, flexible forms of aid, and administrative rate alignment. The veto
restored current eligibility law, and the description says so.

### Senate Bill 5520 — wrongly convicted persons, `civil_rights` yes = for, no = null

The Background shows the old claim rules. The act moves filing to a preponderance
standard, admits new exculpatory information regardless of admissibility, stops treating
guilty pleas and false or coerced confessions as fabrication, and requires the Attorney
General to concede qualifying claims, while keeping clear and convincing evidence to win.
Fair treatment under law is in the area's description.

### Senate Bill 6309 — transit permitting, `public_infrastructure` yes = for, no = null

Lets regional transit authorities apply for permits before owning the land, exceed height
and setback limits where needed, and skip subdivision rules for acquired land. Building
transportation systems is the area's goal. Sound Transit is named as the example because
it is the state's regional transit authority.

### Senate Bill 6346 — income tax on income above $1 million, `personal_income_tax_reduction` yes = **against**, no = null

A 9.9 percent tax on individual Washington taxable income above a $1 million standard
deduction, starting in 2028, with a charitable deduction capped at $100,000, long-term
capital gains left to the existing capital gains tax, and 5 percent of revenue dedicated to
early learning. The standing rule drops fiscal machinery, but this act is exactly what the
`personal_income_tax_reduction` area measures, so it is judged. A yes vote creates an income
tax, so yes = against. The pending lawsuits are mentioned in one sentence.

### Senate Bill 6355 — Washington Electric Transmission Authority, `public_infrastructure` yes = for, no = null

Creates a public authority to expand and upgrade transmission, with priorities, a
negotiation-before-eminent-domain rule and no state debt. Eminent domain is glossed.
