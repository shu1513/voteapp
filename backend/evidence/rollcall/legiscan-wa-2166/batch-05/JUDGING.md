# Washington batch-05 — how each measure was judged

Source for every measure: the **Final Bill Report** on leg.wa.gov, "Synopsis as Enacted",
written by non-partisan legislative staff with no sponsor statement of intent. Where the
Summary alone could not show what changed, the report's Background was read as well.

## Per measure

### House Bill 1081 — solicited home sales, `corporate_accountability` yes = for, no = null

For a deal signed from 1 January 2026 in which a buyer solicits an owner whose property
is not on the market, the owner may have an appraisal by a licensed appraiser of the
owner's choosing at the buyer's expense. An owner who takes the appraisal may cancel
within four business days of receiving it; one who does not may cancel within 10 business
days of signing. Both rights must appear in at least 10-point bold type and be
acknowledged. Deals where either party has a licensed agent are exempt, and the Consumer
Protection Act enforces the rest.

The agent exemption is carried, because it decides who the law reaches. Every duty binds
the soliciting buyer, which is why the area is `corporate_accountability`.

### House Bill 1154 — solid waste enforcement, `environment_and_public_health` yes = for, no = null

Local health departments keep primary permitting, but the Department of Ecology must
review and approve every landfill permit and renewal, and from 1 August 2027 a landfill
permit is not valid without that approval. Ecology may enforce when a local department
defers to it or its enforcement is inadequate, which the act defines by deadlines: no
inspection of a credible complaint within 45 days, no notice within 60 days of seeing a
violation, no action within 90 days of a notice, or no substantial progress for 180 days.
Ecology must give 30 days' notice unless there is an immediate and substantial danger.
Both agencies may suspend permits and impose penalties of up to $5,000 a day, rising to
$10,000 a day after 14 days.

⚠ **The House voted twice, 58-39 and then 57-39 on concurrence.** A hand-written record of
the 58-39 vote describes the first passage, a different vote, and was left alone.

### House Bill 1687 — social housing, `housing_affordability` yes = for, no = null

The Summary reads like a definition, so the Background was read to find the change. The
Housing Cooperation Law already let the state and local governments give public housing
authorities land, funding, loans, infrastructure and zoning exceptions. **This act extends
that same assistance to social housing public development authorities**, which it defines
as public corporations that build and run rental housing for all incomes, kept affordable
by public subsidy and by cross-subsidy from higher-income tenants. The income bands (up to
80 percent of median, 80 to 120, and above 120) are carried because they define who the
housing is for.

It is an authorization, not a mandate, and the description says so: "lets them give the
same help". Nay is null: the argument against public housing sits inside
`housing_affordability` itself.

### House Bill 2294 — grocery and pharmacy covenants, `corporate_accountability` yes = for, no = null

A new private agreement that bars or restricts a grocery store or pharmacy, or caps its
size, where local land use would allow it, is void and unlawful. Three limits decide the
reach and are all carried: agreements already in force are untouched; a store that closes
to relocate may keep a restriction for up to five years if it reopens a similar or larger
store within 2 miles and two years; and a retail center may still cap the number of such
stores, but may not enforce that cap once a store there has been closed for over a year.
Local governments may extend those limits for good cause. New agreements must be reported
to the Attorney General within 10 days, and the Attorney General and local governments
may sue.

`corporate_accountability` rather than `cost_of_living_reduction`: the operative content
voids private anti-competitive agreements, and nothing in it touches a price.

### House Bill 2367 — coal and cap-and-invest, `environment_and_public_health` yes = for, no = null

Emissions from coal-fired power plants operating by 22 July 2011 were exempt from the
cap-and-invest program; the exemption now covers only emissions before 1 January 2026.
The act also removes the limit on imposing further greenhouse gas requirements on such a
plant or on coal power purchased from it, and repeals the sales and use tax exemptions for
coal burned to generate electricity.

The report does not say how many plants this reaches, so the description does not either.
Cap-and-invest and allowances are glossed, because the exemption means nothing without
them. Nay is null: the realistic objection is electricity cost and the plant's
negotiated transition, which runs on a cost axis rather than an environmental one.

### House Bill 2384 — life care contracts, `corporate_accountability` yes = for, no = null

From 1 July 2027 a continuing care retirement community that offers life care contracts
must file an actuarial analysis at its second registration renewal and every other renewal
after that, showing it can meet its obligations under moderately adverse conditions. The
Office of the Insurance Commissioner reviews it, registration waits on that review, and the
result is linked from the state's public list. Only a registered community may call itself
a life care community.

Actuary and life care contract are both glossed. **Senate-only**: the House votes were
78-14 and 78-17, neither divided. The Senate's roll is on its own amended text, which the
House then accepted, so it is a vote on the text that became law.

## Dropped after reading

**House Bill 1321.** A military force from another state may not enter Washington for
duty without the Governor's permission or presidential authority. No research area carries
a direction on that question. Filter-5 drop.

**House Bill 2475.** The Office of Equity must write language access guidelines by 2027,
agencies must report their implementation plans by 2028, and a proposal on interpreter
shortages is due. Guidelines, reports and a proposal are not an operative policy change —
the Connecticut rule that a plan is not a program. Filter-5 drop.

## What was checked

- Every roll's tally matches Washington's bill history and the Final Bill Report.
- Every roll is its chamber's vote on the text that became law.
- No measure in this batch sits on a partially vetoed act.
- One duplicate retired before importing. The office-scoped sweep and the tally sweep
  agreed on it independently. Every other hit was a sponsorship record, a different vote
  on the same bill, or an older Washington act that reuses the number.
