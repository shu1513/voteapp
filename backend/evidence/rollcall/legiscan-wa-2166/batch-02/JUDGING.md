# Washington batch-02 — how each measure was judged

Source for every measure: the **Final Bill Report** on leg.wa.gov, "Synopsis as Enacted",
written by non-partisan legislative staff with no sponsor statement of intent, plus the
session law text where a qualification had to be confirmed. The report's Summary is the
delta index; it is not the source for limits.

## Per measure

### House Bill 1170 — labelling AI content, `ai_regulation` yes = for, no = null

A covered provider is one whose generative system has over one million monthly users and
is publicly accessible in Washington for personal use. Where commercially and technically
reasonable it must attach provenance data to video, image or audio its system creates or
materially alters, and must use reasonable methods to make that data hard to remove or
tamper with. Products offering only video games or interactive experiences are excluded,
and so are state, local and tribal governments.

Every one of those limits is carried in the description, because they decide who the law
reaches. **The act takes effect on 1 February 2027**, so the description says "will make".

**House-only**: the Senate passed it 46-3, which fails the divided gate.

### House Bill 1462 — hydrofluorocarbons, `environment_and_public_health` yes = for, no = null

Global warming potential limits for newly produced bulk HFCs: 1,500 CO2e from 2030 and
750 CO2e from 2033. Reclaimed refrigerants, aircraft use and certain federally authorised
applications are exempt. Ecology may grant a renewable exemption where compliance is
technically or economically infeasible, but only if the holder carries out a plan to meet
the limits, and a 30-day emergency exemption. Violations carry Clean Air Act penalties.

The description explains what a hydrofluorocarbon is and what the multiple means, because
the number is meaningless without it. The refrigerant transition task force and the later
rulemaking are real parts of the act but are not described: they direct a study and future
rules, and the Connecticut SB 1 lesson is that a plan is not a programme.

### House Bill 1491 — transit-oriented housing, `housing_affordability` yes = for, no = null

⚠ The first read of this report covered only the station-area DEFINITIONS, and a
description written from that alone would have asserted a density requirement without
having seen one. Reading on found it: fully planning cities must allow at least 3.5 floor
area ratio on average in rail station areas and 2.5 in bus station areas, with a further
1.5 for buildings that are entirely affordable, workforce or permanent supportive housing
for 50 years. This is the California batch-05 rule — a measure is not judged until the
whole thing is read.

Floor area ratio is glossed in place. The exclusions cities may apply (shorelines,
critical areas, landmarks, historic districts and others) are carried but summarised.

Review correction: the half-mile rail radius is not universal. A commuter rail stop in a
city of 15,000 or fewer people gets a quarter mile (section 51 of the act), which changes
which lots the density rule reaches. Both chamber descriptions now say so.

Nay is null: the argument against upzoning sits inside `housing_affordability` itself.

### House Bill 1747 — Fair Chance Act, `civil_rights` yes = for, no = null

An employer may not categorically exclude anyone with a criminal record, and must make a
conditional offer before obtaining record information. It may never take a tangible
adverse action over an arrest record or juvenile conviction, EXCEPT where an adult is out
on bail or released on personal recognizance pending trial. For an adult conviction it may
act only with a legitimate business reason.

The exception and the adult-conviction standard are both carried; without them the rule
reads as absolute. "Recognizance" is glossed as "a promise to appear".

Review correction: the act phases in (15 or more employees from July 1, 2026, fewer from
January 1, 2027) and does not cover employers whose workers have unsupervised access to
children or vulnerable adults, law enforcement and criminal justice agencies, employers a
federal or state law requires to check records, or federal-contract work that bars people
with records. The descriptions had presented the rules as live from July 2025 with a
single exception; they now carry the dates and the exempt employers.

### House Bill 2105 — immigrant worker notice, `immigration` yes = for, no = null

From 1 October 2026 an employer receiving a federal notice of inspection of employment
eligibility records must post and distribute a worker notice within five days, naming the
agency, the date, the records sought and a statewide immigrant and refugee rights contact,
in English and the five most common other languages. The Attorney General must publish a
model notice by 1 September 2026, and using it satisfies the duty.

Nay is null rather than "against": the realistic objection is the administrative burden on
employers, which runs on a different axis from immigration policy.

### Senate Bill 5184 — parking minimums, `housing_affordability` yes = for, no = null

Caps on what a city or county may require: half a space per multifamily unit, one per
single-family home, two per 1,000 square feet of commercial space, and no minimum at all
for a listed set including affordable housing, senior housing, child care, small homes and
small commercial spaces, ground-floor non-residential space in mixed-use buildings, and
conversions. Accessible parking under the Americans with Disabilities Act is unaffected —
carried, because without it the description overstates the reach.

Review correction: cities of 30,000 or fewer people are EXEMPT, not merely given longer.
Places of 50,000 or more had 18 months and places between 30,000 and 50,000 three years.
The first draft said "more than 50,000" and gave every smaller place three years.

### Senate Bill 5480 — medical debt, `corporate_accountability` yes = for, no = null

Providers, facilities and collection agencies may not report medical debt to credit
bureaus, and bureaus may not report it; a debt that is reported becomes UNENFORCEABLE. The
definition covers debt owed to a business whose primary work is medical services, products
or devices, and it counts even where the bill is not past due or has been paid. Cosmetic
surgery is excluded unless it is reconstructive after injury or disease.

`corporate_accountability` follows Maryland's Fair Medical Debt precedent (HB 1020), not
`healthcare_affordability`: every operative duty binds a company.

### Senate Bill 5494 — lead-based paint, `environment_and_public_health` yes = for, no = null

Commerce runs a state renovation, repair and painting programme for pre-1978 residential
and child-occupied buildings. Work must be done by certified firms using certified
renovators or people they trained, meeting Commerce's standards. Commerce runs training
and certification and may set cost-recovery fees. State rules must be at least as
protective as federal ones, and Commerce may not penalise a violation the federal
Environmental Protection Agency has already acted on — carried, because it limits the
state's enforcement.

### Senate Bill 5917 — abortion medications, `womens_reproductive_rights` yes = for, **no = against**

The Department of Corrections may sell, distribute or deliver abortion medications to
health care providers and entities using them for reproductive health care, which the act
defines to include abortion and medical management of early pregnancy loss. It must
coordinate with the Department of Health and prioritise bulk distribution, including to
Indian health care providers. The former cap of list price plus a $5 fee is removed and no
payment need be taken.

**Authored nay.** Single subject, the whole operative content is the research area's own
mechanism, and the mainstream objection is to that mechanism rather than to cost or
administration — the same test that carried the firearms act in batch-01.

## Dropped after reading

**House Bill 2521.** Its listed subject is firearms background checks; the act removes the
$18 fee cap so the State Patrol may recover its administration costs, and does nothing
else. A fee level carries no defensible direction in any research area. Filter-5 drop.

## What was checked

- Every roll's tally matches Washington's bill history and the Final Bill Report.
- Every roll is its chamber's vote on the text that became law.
- No measure in this batch is partially vetoed.
- 10 duplicates retired before importing; the 113 `related` flags were investigated rather
  than assumed to be noise, and the five substantive near-misses were each read and
  correctly left alone.
