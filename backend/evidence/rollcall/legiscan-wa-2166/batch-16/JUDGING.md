# Washington batch-16 — how each measure was judged

Source for every measure: the nonpartisan Senate Bill Report **as passed Senate**
(`SBR APS`), checked against the passed version's text where a detail mattered.

- **Senate Bill 5906.** No immigration status collection by schools and child care
  providers, and no entry by immigration agents to nonpublic areas of schools, child care
  sites, health care facilities, adult family homes, colleges and auditors' offices
  without a judicial warrant or court order. Limits enforcement in sensitive places.
- **Senate Bill 5924.** Pharmacist prescribing from 2027-12-01 for listed categories and
  for minor, self-limiting or test-guided conditions. Wider access to routine care.
- **Senate Bill 5947.** A 19-member board to design a universal state health plan that may
  start only after a federal waiver, federal funding and enabling legislation. The
  contingency is stated in the description.
- **Senate Bill 5956.** No sole reliance on automated systems or surveillance data for
  discipline, no facial recognition tracking, no watchlists built only by automated
  systems, no inferring sensitive traits from biometrics, and limits on sharing with police.
  Rules for AI systems.
- **Senate Bill 5972.** Removes the 70,000-population floor on interest arbitration for jail
  staff. Bargaining is the area's own mechanism.
- **Senate Bill 5984.** AI disclosure at the start and every three hours for companion
  chatbots, a self-harm protocol, and Consumer Protection Act enforcement. The rules on
  sexual content and manipulative engagement apply **only** when the operator knows the
  user is a minor or the chatbot is aimed at minors. This was read from section 4 of the
  engrossed text, and the description says so.
- **Senate Bill 5993.** Medical debt interest capped at 1 percent simple from 2027, none
  during a pending charity care screening, and refunds on debt later reduced or found
  invalid. Lower cost of care.
- **Senate Bill 6049.** Public records exemptions for hate crime survivors' identifying
  details, individual answers to voluntary state staff surveys, and Healthy Youth Survey
  answers; de-identified data stays releasable. Personal data protection.
- **Senate Bill 6184.** Removes the six-county limit and expiry from a youth housing
  program, lets stipends go to youth directly, ends a 12 percent overhead cap, and lets
  crisis residential centers delay parental notice for a compelling reason. Wider support
  for homeless youth.
