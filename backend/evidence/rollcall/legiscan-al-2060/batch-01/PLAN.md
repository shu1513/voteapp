# Alabama 2023 second special session batch-01 — selection plan

The whole session is one fight: the congressional map the Legislature drew after the Supreme Court
decided *Allen v. Milligan*. Two competing maps were filed, SB 5 and HB 5.

## How the batch was selected

1. **Divided** — 4 of 10 kept floor votes.
2. **Consequential** — the measure became law. 2 rolls, both on SB 5.
3. **A nameable subject** — congressional reapportionment.
4. **One roll per measure per chamber, preferring the vote on the enacted text.** SB 5's Senate
   passage of 2023-07-19 (24-8) is superseded by its concurrence in the conference committee report
   on 2023-07-21 (24-6), which is the vote on the text that became Act 2023-563. Only the
   concurrence is imported.
5. **A defensible direction, or a deliberate no-stance import.** Imported with no stance.

## What is in the batch

1 roll on 1 measure, 24 records across 24 candidates.

| Measure | Roll | Vote | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| SB 5 congressional reapportionment | Senate 1350905, 2023-07-21 | 24-6 | general, no stance |

## Why only the Senate

The bill history records a House vote on SB 5 (`Read Again a Third Time and Pass as Amended`,
2023-07-21) and a House concurrence the same day, but **no roll call for either exists in the
dataset**. What the dataset holds for the House on SB 5 is the amendment adoption, 76-26. The 2026
special session's SB 1 has the same shape. Nothing here can recover the missing votes, so SB 5 is
represented by its Senate concurrence alone.

## Remaining

HB 5, the House's competing map, is divided in both chambers and died. It is `batch-02`.
