# Idaho 2025 batch-03 — how each measure was judged

Same method as the earlier batches. The act is the only source, read with
`tools/id_text.py`. One body per measure, with the yes and no descriptions
generated from it. Where a measure was amended, the engrossed print was read.

## House Bill 32 — chapter 11, mask mandates

Bars the state, counties, cities, school districts, public health districts and
officials from requiring a mask, and bars a mask as a condition of entry,
education, employment or any service. A recommendation must carry a notice that
it is not a requirement. Hospitals and health care facilities are excluded, as
are workplaces where protective gear is part of the job. Environment and public
health, a yes vote is against.

## House Bill 40 — chapter 13, income tax

Cuts the individual, trust, estate and corporate rate from 5.695 percent to 5.3
percent, removes precious metal bullion gains from Idaho taxable income, and
lets military retirees deduct retirement pay at any age subject to a work income
test under sixty-two. Personal income tax reduction, a yes vote is for.

## House Bill 86 — chapter 33, electric vehicle charging

Bars the state and every local government from requiring charging stations,
charging parking spaces or the wiring for them in a building plan, and
overrides local ordinances that already do. Environment and public health, a yes
vote is against.

## House Bill 96 — chapter 274, flags

Limits a government body to a listed set of flags, so a political or cause flag
may not be flown. Schools, colleges and universities fall outside the definition
of a government body, so the rule does not reach them. That exclusion was added
by the Senate amendment and is in the text that became law. Civil rights, a yes
vote is against.

## House Bill 134 — chapter 163, breast cancer screening

Requires health plans to cover one supplemental breast screening a year, at no
cost to the patient, for a person at higher risk. The statute lists what counts
as higher risk and what imaging qualifies. Healthcare affordability, a yes vote
is for.

## House Bill 149 — chapter 87, mortgage trigger leads

Makes it a prohibited practice to solicit a mortgage applicant from a bought
trigger lead without saying up front that the caller is unconnected to the
original lender and bought the person's data from a credit bureau, or without
making a firm offer of credit. Contacting people who opted out of prescreened
offers or who are on a do-not-call list is barred. Data privacy, a yes vote is
for.

## House Bill 240 — chapter 194, campus speech

Makes the outdoor areas of a public college open to speech, so free speech zones
end, while leaving reasonable time, place and manner rules. Narrows harassment
to speech severe and pervasive enough to deny equal access to education, bars
viewpoint-based security fees, and gives an affected person a right to sue.
Civil rights, a yes vote is for.

## House Bill 339 — chapter 276, voter roll accuracy

Builds a monthly data feed to the Secretary of State from health and welfare,
transportation and correction records, plus an annual tax commission request.
The feeds carry names, dates of birth, social security numbers, addresses,
photographs, digital signatures and citizenship information. Election integrity,
a yes vote is for. The description says what data moves, because the breadth of
the feed is the part people argue about.

## Senate Bill 1007 — chapter 89, school board comment

Removes the rule confining public comment at a school board meeting to agenda
items, so a district resident may speak on school matters generally. Boards keep
time limits and conduct standards, and closed-session subjects stay off limits.
Anti-corruption, a yes vote is for, because the act widens public access to a
governing body.

## Senate Bill 1046 — chapter 179, fetal development lesson

Requires every district and charter school serving grades five through twelve to
show a three-minute high-definition ultrasound video and a computer-generated
animation of every week of development in the womb, and to repeat that material
in any class covering human biology, contraception or sexually transmitted
infections.

Public education quality, a yes vote is against. The direction was argued. The
act does not add a subject, it fixes specific required media in statute and
makes them compulsory inside unrelated lessons, which takes curriculum choice
away from teachers and boards. The description states plainly what the lesson
must contain so a reader can weigh it.

## Senate Bill 1141 — chapter 248, public camping

Bans camping or sleeping in public across Idaho and forbids cities and
countywide highway districts from allowing it, with the attorney general able to
sue a local government that knowingly does. Recreational camping, permitted
events and authorized overnight parking are carved out. Housing affordability, a
yes vote is against, because the act penalizes sleeping outdoors without adding
any place to sleep.

## Senate Bill 1210 — chapter 318, Idaho Medical Freedom Act

Renames and rewrites the Coronavirus Stop Act. The ban no longer covers only
Covid vaccines; it covers any medical intervention, defined as any procedure,
treatment, device, drug, injection, medication or medical action to diagnose,
prevent or cure disease or to change a person's biological function. Businesses,
ticket sellers, schools from preschool through university including private ones,
and every level of government are barred from requiring one. Federal law, some
foreign travel for work, and the existing school immunization statutes are the
exceptions. Environment and public health, a yes vote is against.
