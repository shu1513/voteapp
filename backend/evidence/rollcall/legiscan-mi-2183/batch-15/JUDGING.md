## The measures

### SB 111 — elder and vulnerable adult protection orders
New section 2950p of the Revised Judicature Act. Petitioners: people 60 or
older, vulnerable adults as defined in section 174a of the Penal Code, and people
with developmental disabilities. Subsection (2) lists the conduct an order may
restrain, including purchasing or possessing a firearm and harm to a pet;
subsection (3) adds financial exploitation relief, including barring access to
the petitioner's assets, returning property after a hearing, damages up to the
small claims limit and attorney fees. The court must issue an order on
reasonable cause.

### SB 257 and SB 260 — civil claims for criminal sexual conduct
SB 257 rewrites section 5851b: claims by any victim (the "while a minor" limit
is struck) may be brought by the later of 10 years after accrual, age 42, or 7
years after discovery of the injury and its cause; with no limit after a
conviction; and older barred claims are revived for 1 year, with recovery from a
single defendant capped at $1,500,000 per incident. SB 260 adds section 7d of the
governmental immunity act for public universities, colleges and school
districts, as described. SB 258, SB 259 and SB 261 are dropped as procedural
companions.

### SB 78 — juvenile felonies
New section 1j of 1965 PA 213 with the conditions in the description: felony
committed before 18, discharged from corrections, 15 years since release, no
conviction after 18 and no other set-aside, no pending charge, no positive drug
test since the conviction, a record showing the applicant is not a danger, and
not a listed sex offense. The application must include extensive supporting
material.

### SB 154 — access to reproductive health care
New act. A first offense is a misdemeanor of up to 1 year and $10,000; a repeat
offense a felony of up to 3 years and $25,000; nonviolent physical obstruction
carries lower penalties; bodily injury and death carry longer felonies. Parents
acting toward their own minor child are excluded. The attorney general, a
prosecutor or a local government attorney may sue for an injunction and
damages.
