## Dropped from this block

- **HB 5821** — it caps the state's approved elementary reading curriculum list at
  fifteen, and at the same time lets a district use its own locally developed
  literacy curriculum instead of anything on the list, with the deadline pushed to
  2027-2028. It tightens and loosens the same requirement, so it has no honest
  single direction. This is the more striking because HB 5819, in the same
  package, moves a deadline the other way and is kept.
- **HB 5810**, requiring the presidential fitness test; **HB 4150**, removing
  application evaluation fees; **HB 4157** and **HB 4158**, assessment pilot
  programs and M-STEP naming; **HB 6130**, a trust fund change. All are narrow or
  administrative.
- **HB 5364**, requiring legislative approval before the state academic standards
  are revised. Legislative oversight of standards reads as accountability or as
  politicizing the standards, and the text supports both.
- **HB 4153**, district-level teacher certification, and **HB 4156**, changes to
  the merit curriculum. Both read as flexibility or as lowered requirements.
- **HB 4238**, programs and agreements between public schools and foreign
  countries. Needs a full read of the engrossed text before a direction can be
  defended.

## Deferred, not dropped

**HB 5520**, grade 3 reading requirements. Its engrossed print is a 59,000
character rewrite of section 1280f and it has no Fiscal Agency analysis. It
deserves a careful read of its own rather than a rushed one at the end of a
batch. It is Michigan's read-by-grade-3 law, so it matters.
