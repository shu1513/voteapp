# Michigan batch-01 — selection

LegiScan session 2183, the 103rd Legislature, 2025-2026.

**8 roll calls over 7 measures. 516 candidate records across 113 candidates.**
Five of the eight are House votes, which is where the reach is: a Michigan
House roll reaches 93 candidates and a Senate roll 16.

Closely divided means the smaller side is at least a quarter of the larger.

Every one of the 75 divided rolls in the enacted pool passed the roll-attribution
check: the roll's own printed roll-call number appears in its own bill's history
on the same date. That is the test that caught Alabama filing one printed roll
number under two different bills. Michigan had no such case.

## Filter 4 did the heaviest work, and it cost the House

**Michigan's House passes its own version early on a near party-line vote, then
concurs in the Senate substitute months later almost unanimously.** The early
divided vote is a vote on text that did not become law, so it cannot be
attributed to anyone as a position on the act.

## What this batch dropped, and why

### Michigan's two biggest 2025 laws both fail filter 5

**HB 4002, earned sick time, and SB 8, the minimum wage.** Both are compromises
written after the Michigan Supreme Court revived two 2018 ballot initiatives,
and each one moves in two directions at the same time.

HB 4002 raised the accrual rate to one hour of paid sick time for every 30 hours
worked, up from one in 35, and raised the yearly cap from 40 hours to 72. It
also created a lighter set of rules for small businesses and let an employer
make a new hire wait 120 days before using accrued time. Which way the act
points depends entirely on which version of the law you measure it against, and
the House Fiscal Agency's own analysis says so on its first page.

SB 8 set the minimum wage at $12.48, then $13.73 in 2026 and $15.00 in 2027,
which is faster than the law it replaced. It also kept a permanently reduced
minimum wage for tipped workers, rising only to 50 percent of the full rate by
2031, where the revived initiative would have removed the tipped rate
altogether.

Neither has an honest single direction on its own text, so both are dropped
under filter 5. Together they cost four slots, two of them House slots. This is
worth flagging: Michigan's most consequential 2025 votes are unreachable, and
they would also have run into the fact that **there is no labor research area**.
Michigan is the eighth state where that gap has cost measures.

### Dropped as appropriations
HB 4572 (Selfridge Air National Guard Base), HB 5630 (school aid) and SB 878
(supplemental). A vote to fund the government carries no honest direction, which
is standing practice across this campaign.

### Dropped as administrative machinery, failing filter 3
HB 4098 and HB 4099 (electronic hearings of the tax tribunal), HB 4808 (remote
meetings of one state board), HB 4694, HB 4695 and HB 4798 (three near-identical
rewrites of the recreational authorities act), HB 4824 (the definition of
"brand" in liquor law), HB 4471 (re-employing a retired county worker), SB 52
(authority grant funds and bonds), SB 581 (the definition of a downtown
district), SB 721 and SB 722 (commercial redevelopment acts), SB 565 (a
reimbursement fund), SB 574 (a trust fund disbursement), HB 4961 (income tax
conformity) and HB 4968 (insurance reporting).

### Left for a later batch, not dropped
HB 4183 (the motor fuel tax increase), HB 4556 and HB 4557 (the Michigan merit
examination), HB 5806, HB 5807 and SB 966 (housing opportunity tax credits),
HB 6043 (Tri-Share child care), SB 903 (weighted school funding), SB 723
(transformational brownfield plans), SB 106 (a fund-raising registration plate),
SB 577, SB 578 and SB 579 (fee bills in the road funding package). These need
their enacted text read before a direction can be defended, and this batch ran
out of room rather than out of candidates.
