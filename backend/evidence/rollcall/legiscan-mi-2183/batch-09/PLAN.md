## The public safety rule used in this batch

`public_safety_and_crime_control` names both accountability and justice-system
performance, so a bill that only raises a penalty or sets a mandatory minimum
for conduct that is already a crime has no single honest direction (the South
Dakota SB 179 rule). This batch keeps only bills that **create a new offense for
a clear harm** that current law does not name: fertility fraud (HB 5036 and its
charging window HB 5037), approaching a first responder after a warning
(HB 5574), and shooting at an occupied vehicle (HB 5744; current law names
shooting at a building, not a vehicle).

Dropped under that rule: HB 4255 and HB 4256 (fentanyl mandatory minimums),
HB 4690 and HB 4691 (fleeing mandatory minimums), HB 5079 (a higher penalty for
disarming an officer by force), HB 5157 (life for a drug delivery that causes
serious injury), and HB 5831 (a 25-year minimum for terrorism). Sentencing
guideline companions (HB 5038, HB 5080, HB 5158, HB 5576, HB 5745) and HB 5575's
definitions follow their substantive bills and are dropped.

## The corporate accountability shields

HB 5652, HB 4949 and HB 4582 each take away a way to hold a business or property
owner answerable in court: a nuisance suit by neighbors, a noise suit against a
range that expands, and a premises injury claim over an open and obvious hazard.
HB 5298 moves the cost of a passenger's injury benefits from the limousine or
bus operator's insurer to the passenger's own. HB 4933 removes licensing of
agencies that charge job seekers a fee. On the area's own terms each yes vote
is against.

## Dropped from this block

- **HB 5497**, mortgage minimums: it raises the minimum loan sizes lenders may
  set, but also brings out-of-state lenders under the anti-redlining law.
- **HB 5281**, litigation funding: consumer funder rules and a disclosure duty
  that helps defendants pull opposite ways.
- **HB 4115 and HB 4116**, a tie-barred pawnbroker trade of higher penalties for
  a higher interest cap.
- **HB 4390 and HB 4391**, roadside saliva drug tests; **HB 5537**, kratom ban.
