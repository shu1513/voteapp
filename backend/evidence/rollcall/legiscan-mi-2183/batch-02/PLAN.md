## Dropped in this batch, with reasons

### HB 4556 and HB 4557 — no defensible direction (filter 5)
Both change the Michigan Merit Examination. HB 4556 removes the requirement that
the college entrance exam include a writing component, removes the requirement
to print each subject score on a pupil's transcript, and moves parallel language
out of the School Aid Act into the School Code. HB 4557 repeals the School Aid
Act section that language came from.

Removing a writing test can be read as lowering what the state measures, or as
removing a test the exam vendor no longer offers. The acts do not say which, and
the Fiscal Agency summary for these bills carries no rationale section. A
direction here would be an assertion about motive rather than a reading of the
text, so both are dropped. Two Senate slots at 24-9.

### SB 106 — outside the research areas, and low public salience (filter 3)
It creates a fundraising registration plate for companion animal welfare and a
fund that pays the Michigan Pet Alliance. Divided in both chambers, 86-22 in the
House and 24-12 in the Senate, but no research area describes animal welfare
funding, and the no-stance route is closed. This is the same call as Alabama's
dog-tethering bill. Two slots dropped, one of them a House slot.

## Two notes on the earlier drops, now that the pool is closed

The pool's biggest losses were not to filter 3 or 5 but to **filter 4**: the
Michigan House votes its own version early on a near party-line vote and then
concurs in the Senate substitute almost unanimously, so 12 of its 22 slots were
votes on text that never became law. batch-01/PLAN.md lists all twelve.

The second loss was **Michigan's two biggest 2025 laws**, HB 4002 on earned sick
time and SB 8 on the minimum wage, both dropped under filter 5 because each moves
in two directions at once. Both are labor measures and **there is no labor
research area**, so even a clean direction would have had nowhere to go.
