# Michigan batch-06: energy and state rules

LegiScan session 2183, the 103rd Legislature, 2025-2026. Not-enacted scope.

**7 roll calls, 7 measures, 648 candidate records.** All seven are House votes,
which is where the reach is: a Michigan House roll fans out to about 93
candidates.

## The 7 roll calls

| measure | date | tally | research area | a yes vote is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HB 4283, Upper Peninsula coal replacement engines excused from clean energy filings until 2050 | 2025-05-07 | 83-26 | environment_and_public_health | against |

| HB 4160, no state rule stricter than the federal standard without a clear and convincing need | 2025-09-24 | 59-41 | environment_and_public_health | against |

No label carries a direction for a no vote.

`environment_and_public_health` is defined as protecting air, water, climate and
community health through standards, enforcement and prevention. Each of these
bills removes or blocks a standard aimed at that end, so a yes vote reads as
against the area. HB 4160 reaches every state rule, not only environmental
ones; it is labeled here because Michigan's best-known stricter-than-federal
rules are its environmental and drinking water standards, and the description
says plainly that it covers all state agencies.

## Dropped from this block

- **HB 4007.** Its engrossed text only defines "coal replacement engine" and it
  takes effect only if HB 4283 does. HB 4283 carries the substance.
- **HB 5557**, the longest averaging period for the air emissions inventory. The
  text does not show whether a longer period weakens or strengthens pollution
  control, so no direction is defensible.
- **HB 4039, HB 5815, HB 5816, HB 5817, HB 5818**, the rule-making process
  package (two-for-one rule cuts, no court deference, full re-review of agency
  decisions, legislative approval of major rules, a new oversight committee).
  Whether each makes government work better is the dispute itself.
