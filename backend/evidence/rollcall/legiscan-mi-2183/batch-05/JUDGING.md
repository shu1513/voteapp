# Michigan batch-05: judging

Not-enacted measures, so there is no Public Act. Each description comes from the
**engrossed print**, the text the chamber actually passed. The nonpartisan House
and Senate Fiscal Agency analyses were used as an index, and every claim was
checked against the print.

## The measures

### HB 4492, tip sharing — labor_rights, a yes vote is against
The House Fiscal Agency analysis describes the current law as 2025 PA 1 (SB 8):
tipped workers keep all their tips except what they choose to share, the employer
must explain the tipped wage in writing, and the worker must consent in writing.

The engrossed print deletes the keep-all-tips condition, the "in writing" and
"written consent" words, and the sentence saying tips and service charges do not
count toward wages due. It adds a new subsection (5) letting an employer require a
tipped worker to share tips with other employees, as long as the worker receives
enough tips to cover the gap between the tipped wage and the full minimum wage.

**Markup note.** The deletion of the keep-all-tips paragraph does not show as
struck text in the flattened print; the House analysis and the struck cross
reference in subsection (4) ("[[(1)(d),]] <<(5),>>") confirm it. This is the
Michigan unmarked-change trap again.

### SB 145, pay history and credit questions — labor_rights, a yes vote is for
New subdivision (d) of section 13a prints unmarked. It bars asking a job
applicant about past wages, fringe benefits, credit score or credit history, or
seeking that information otherwise. Three exemptions, all quoted in the
description: employers required by law or a securities self-regulatory
organization to use the information, federally insured deposit-taking financial
institutions and their affiliates, and registered broker-dealers and investment
advisers. The wage-disclosure rule in (a) now also reaches written policies and
practices that prohibit or discourage disclosure.

### SB 319, minimum staffing — labor_rights, a yes vote is for
Both new subsections (11(2) and 15(10)) print unmarked. For employees under the
compulsory arbitration act (1969 PA 312: police, fire and related public safety
workers), minimum staffing becomes part of "other conditions of employment". The
duty lifts to "may, but is not required to" when constitutional revenue-sharing
payments, or property tax collections because of a fall in total taxable value,
drop below the preceding fiscal year.

### SB 49, workplace safety fines — labor_rights, a yes vote is for
Section 35 replaces the $7,000 and $70,000 caps (and the $5,000 floor for
willful violations) with "the equivalent federal penalty". New subsection (11)
requires the director to start a rule changing the state penalty "not later than
10 working days after the date that a federal penalty is changed". Section 61(2)(c)
adds reports and information about an employer's request for consultation to the
public-records exemptions. The bill also moves the act's duties to the Department
of Labor and Economic Opportunity, following Executive Order 2019-13.
