## Dropped from the labor block

- **HB 4001**, the House's minimum wage bill. It would repeal the 2018 wage law
  that a court ruling revived, set a slower schedule to $15, and cut the youth
  wage from 85% to 75% of the minimum. But it would also raise the training wage
  from $4.25 to 75% of the minimum. Measured against the law in force on the vote
  date, it raises the wage; measured against the revived law due weeks later, it
  lowers it. This is the same baseline problem that dropped HB 4002 and SB 8, and
  the mixed-bill rule (a bill that both widens and narrows worker protections gets
  no labor label) applies on its own.
- **HB 5727**, youth employment. It would end school work permits and the state
  registration system, and remove the ban on deviations that allow minors to work
  from midnight to 5 a.m. or in hazardous jobs. It would also add new limits: 8
  hours a day in a week without school, 6 days a week, and a weekly average of 8
  hours a day. It widens and narrows protections at once, so the mixed-bill rule
  drops it.
- **HB 4017**, a lower fine for a family farm that fails to report a death or
  injury. The cut reaches only a death or injury of the farm's owner or the
  owner's close family, so it says little about protecting hired workers.

SB 50, SB 49's companion, is dropped separately as technical: it deletes
sentencing guidelines for a section that never took effect.
