# Michigan batch-11: judging

Not-enacted measures. Each description comes from the **engrossed print**, read
with the fixed markup reader (batch-06/JUDGING.md), and the House Fiscal Agency
analysis used as an index.

## The measures

### HB 4170 — income tax rate
Section 51 now lists dated rates: 4.25% from October 2012 to the end of 2022,
4.05% for 2023, 4.25% for 2024, and 4.05% on and after January 1, 2025. The
existing revenue-growth trigger in the new subdivision (f) gains the sentence
"A rate reduction calculated under this subdivision is effective for all
subsequent tax years and is the current rate going forward", reversing the
Court of Appeals reading that a triggered cut lasts one year.

### HB 4747 — out-of-state college and ABLE plans
The floor substitute adds, from January 1, 2026, contributions to another
state's section 529 qualified tuition program and section 529A ABLE program to
the existing deductions, within the existing $5,000 single and $10,000 joint
caps for each, and adds penalized withdrawals from those plans to income.

### HB 4467 and HB 4466 — minors' gender transition treatment
HB 4467 is a new act. Section 5 lists the barred practices (puberty blockers and
related drugs, testosterone, estrogen or progesterone above normal levels for
the minor's age and sex, sterilizing surgery, genital construction surgery,
removal of healthy tissue) when done to alter appearance or affirm a perception
of sex inconsistent with the minor's sex. Exceptions include medically
verifiable disorders of sex development and treatment of harm from an earlier
procedure. Section 7 creates a private action (2 years, or until age 38 for a
person treated as a minor) with attorney fees; section 8 lets the attorney
general enforce. HB 4466 adds "a final order or judgment under the protecting
minors from chemical and surgical mutilation act" to the Public Health Code's
grounds for discipline, with a certified court record as conclusive evidence.
The descriptions use neutral wording rather than the act's short title.

### HB 4915 — implicit bias training
New section 16149: no rule requiring implicit bias training for licensure or
renewal, and R 338.7004 rescinded.

### HB 4242 — patient record location
New subsection 16213(9): off-site storage, including through a medical records
company, must be physically maintained in a state or a Canadian province.

### HB 5974 — minors' medical records
The definition of "patient" loses the clause giving a minor who lawfully
obtained care without parental consent or notice the exclusive right to those
records. New section 5(5): except as federal law provides, a provider may not
restrict a parent, guardian or person in loco parentis from examining or
obtaining a minor's record. New section 10 applies licensing sanctions.

### HB 4356 — inactive voters
New 509bb(2) to (4). The engrossed print says 8 years, not the 10 in the
introduced-bill summary. A signature mismatch on a returned card marks the
record challenged; an undeliverable notice marks it challenged and leads to
cancellation if the voter does not vote or act by the first business day after
the second November election. Subsection (1) keeps the rule that a clerk may not
cancel a registration solely for failure to vote.

### HB 4602 — test decks
Section 794a: each board of election commissioners controls its own test deck
process under the Secretary of State's rules; the Secretary may not restrict the
sources used, give one vendor exclusive control, or centralize all ballot data
with one vendor; the procedures must be public and reproducible without
proprietary systems. The struck subsections are the 2002 transition rules for
the uniform voting system, which the House analysis describes as obsolete.
