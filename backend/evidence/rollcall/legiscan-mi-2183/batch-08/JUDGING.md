## The measures

### HB 5933, HB 5934, HB 5935 — state building and trade permits
These apply only where the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs is the
enforcing agency ("the department"), so each description says "when the state
is the permit office". HB 5933 keeps the existing 10 and 15 business day
deadlines and adds new subsection (2): the department must ask for changes
within those periods and may not require new changes afterward, except under
section 11a. It also adds "additional permit" (electrical, plumbing, mechanical,
demolition, foundation-only, temporary structure and shell building permits) so
the rules reach trade permits. HB 5934 adds section 11c, a fee refund when the
department misses the deadline. HB 5935 adds section 11b: a denial must cite the
provision relied on, and an application not flagged as incomplete within 7 days
is considered complete.

### HB 5936, HB 5937, HB 5938, HB 6038 — environmental permits
HB 5936 changes section 1307(7) from "15% of the greater of" the application fee
or first periodic charge to the total amount, and adds a duty to cite the
statutory basis for every approval or denial. HB 5937 cuts the administrative
completeness period in section 1305 from 30 days to 7 and adds a processing
period for wetland permits under section 30304 of 30 days, or 90 days if a
hearing is held. HB 5938 moves the optional wetland hearing from within 60 days
to within 30 days and adds subsections (9) and (10) on permit-holder change
requests: the department may add only related changes, may not readdress parts
already approved, must name them within 7 days, and a request does not restart
the process. HB 6038 adds 30306(8): site visits and change requests only before
the application is administratively complete.

### HB 5082 — wetland assessments
Section 30321(3) "within a reasonable time" becomes "60 days", and (10) changes
"may charge a fee ... based upon the cost" to "shall not charge a fee".

### HB 5504 and HB 5505 — digital offices
HB 5504 creates the State Digital Service Office within the Department of
Technology, Management, and Budget, exercising its duties independently of the
director. It reviews software projects estimated at $1,000,000 or more and
oversees information technology activities costing more than $500,000. The
House analysis of the introduced bill described a five-year life; the engrossed
substitute has no such limit, so the description does not mention one.

HB 5505 creates an office of digital oversight in the Legislature, with an
officer appointed by a majority of each house for 3 years, removable for cause
by two-thirds. It may investigate state agency information technology projects,
services and purchasing on a complaint or on its own, and may overtake, join or
collaborate on projects.

### HB 4052 — legislator nondisclosure agreements
A new act: a member of the Legislature or the member's staff may not enter into
a nondisclosure agreement about work done in the member's legislative capacity,
and one entered into, amended, extended or renewed after the effective date is
void. The builder refused the first draft because it used the word "signed",
which the guard reserves for enacted laws; the description now says "entering
into" and "made".
