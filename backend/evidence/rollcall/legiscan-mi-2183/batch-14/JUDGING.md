## The measures

### SB 246 and SB 247 — hazardous waste
SB 246 is a 104,000-character rewrite of Part 111 and related parts. The
description rests on three verified pieces. New section 11122 bars new
multisource commercial hazardous waste operating licenses and expansions until
5 years after the effective date or until the first updated state plan is
adopted and implemented, whichever is later. New 11125(9) bars a new or expanded
facility, including a class I well, in a city, village, township or county that
has one operating or had one within 25 years, with listed exceptions. Section
11109 raises the landfill fee on TENORM from $5.00 to $12.50 a ton from January
1, 2026, adjusted every fifth year. The capacity cap in 11125(8) is not quoted,
because its "one-fifth of the plan limit" wording could not be explained in a
sentence without reading the plan. SB 247 adds section 62506a: a 0.417-cent per
gallon fee on class I multisource commercial disposal wells, adjusted yearly,
cleanup waste exempt, deposited in a disposal well host community fund.

### SB 46 — mobile home park water
Section 3 lets the director enter a waterworks system at a mobile home park or
seasonal mobile home park that consists solely of customer site piping, to
investigate a complaint that water arriving at a home is not adequate or
healthful or fails state drinking water standards. The Senate analysis also
describes broader authority over customer site piping; the description keeps to
the verified entry power.

### SB 576 — energy efficiency revolving fund
New section 475 of the Management and Budget Act. The department prioritizes
state projects that reduce the state's carbon footprint and sets terms with
agencies covering energy savings.

### SB 462 — tobacco retail licenses
Retail and third-party facilitator licenses, photo identification for every
sale, and escalating administrative fines for sales to people under 21 of $250,
$500 and $2,500 with a 7-day suspension at the third violation, and $10,000 with
revocation at the fourth violation in 3 years (section 1(3)(d) of the
Senate-passed S-1). Review fix: the first description gave the range as $250 to
$2,500 and left out the fourth tier. Re-judged and the 15 records rewritten in
place (import-rerun-report.json).

### SB 947 — dam safety
Strikes the exemption for projects licensed under the Federal Power Act, adds a
staggered registration process in 5 rounds of up to 2 years, requires current
inspection, evaluation, monitoring, maintenance and safety plans, and requires
engineers who have completed or consulted on 5 similar projects.

### SB 373, SB 515, SB 967 — housing
SB 373 adds 3(1)(o) to the Truth in Renting Act. Review fix: the House passed
SB 373 99-5 on 2026-08-25 (House Roll Call 345, no substitute) and the Senate
ordered it enrolled on 2026-09-09; the first description said the House had not
voted. Re-judged as awaiting the governor and the 17 records rewritten in place
(import-rerun-report.json). SB 515 adds section 1e to the
landlord-tenant act, with a civil infraction of up to $500. SB 967 adds section
279 to the Income Tax Act, for tax years from January 1, 2027, with
proportionate recapture when the federal credit is recaptured.

### SB 962, SB 232, SB 807, SB 234, SB 310, SB 311 — others
SB 962: the database and institute by November 5, 2027, through an agreement with
one or more public research universities. SB 232: "If sufficient funds are
appropriated to expand the reconnect program, this minimum-age criterion may be
reduced to as low as 21", not after fiscal 2032. SB 807: automatic enrollment
unless the employee opts out, contributions changeable or stoppable at any
time, no state liability for benefits, and employers free to offer their own
plan. SB 234: new section 1304 of the Revised School Code. SB 310: new section 6b
of the child care licensing act. SB 311: a new act adjusting rates by the
Detroit-Warren-Dearborn price index from October 1, 2027.
