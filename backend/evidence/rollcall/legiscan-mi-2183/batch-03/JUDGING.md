# Michigan batch-03 — judging

These are not-enacted measures, so there is no Public Act. **Every description
was written from the engrossed print**, which is the text the chamber actually
passed, with the nonpartisan Fiscal Agency analysis of that same version used for
orientation. Michigan publishes an "as Passed by the House" or "ANALYSIS AS
PASSED BY THE SENATE" for most of these, which is exactly the right document.

## The version check

Every measure here has an engrossed print. Four adopted a substitute or a floor
amendment before the roll (HB 4024 substitute h-2, HB 4765 substitute h-1,
HB 4720 substitute h-1, SB 224 substitute s-1, plus floor amendments on SB 961,
SB 964 and SB 533), so the committee summary alone would have been stale. The
engrossed print carries all of them.

**⚠ A Michigan trap confirmed here: a wholly new section prints with no markup.**
The engrossed print marks changes to existing law with strike-through and
underline, but a brand-new section is simply printed. SB 226 shows one marked
character in the whole bill, and its substance is a new subsection (8) adding
three buildings. HB 4765's new section 496c is likewise unmarked. So a low
marked-change count is not evidence that a bill does little; find the new
sections and read them.

## The measures

### The Senate's firearms package — SB 224, SB 225, SB 226, SB 331
All four `gun_control` with a yes vote for, all passed 2025-06-25.

SB 224 adds bump stocks and multiburst trigger activators to the existing list in
section 224 of devices that may not be made, sold, offered for sale or possessed,
which is a felony. The description explains what a bump stock does, because the
term is not self-explanatory.

SB 225 and SB 226 are a pair and are described separately so a reader is not
given two records that read the same. SB 225 adds the Michigan State Capitol
Building, the Anderson House Office Building and the Binsfeld Senate Office
Building to the places where a **concealed pistol** may not be carried. SB 226
adds the same three buildings to the places where **any firearm** may not be
possessed, hidden or not. Both were read in the engrossed text, and the
three-building list was found in the new subsections rather than in the marked
changes.

SB 331 is the unserialized-firearm bill: no making, buying, selling or passing on
a gun, frame or receiver without a valid serial number, and 18 months later no
knowingly possessing one; no unlicensed manufacture by 3D printer or milling
machine; an unlicensed person capped at five firearms a year and required to
notify the State Police within ten days.

### HB 4765 — civil_rights, a yes vote is against
The Secretary of State would have to verify every registration applicant's
citizenship against state and federal databases. An applicant whose citizenship
cannot be verified is designated a **federal-only voter** in the qualified voter
file and may not vote in state or local elections until they supply documentary
proof. New section 496c lists what counts, from a birth certificate to tribal
enrollment records.

This goes to `civil_rights` rather than `election_integrity` because it governs
who may take part and on what terms, which is the standing split.

**⚠ The bill's title says more than its text does.** The title reads "proof of
United States citizenship when registering to vote **and identification when
voting**", and several hand-written records took that to mean a photo ID
requirement at the polls. New section 496c was read in full and the whole of the
engrossed print searched: the identification provisions are the documents used to
prove citizenship, and there is no separate poll ID requirement. **The
description does not claim one.** This is the caption-versus-text trap again, and
here the caption was in the official title rather than in a summary.

### HB 4720 and HB 5845 — election_integrity, a yes vote is for
HB 4720 bars any electronic voting system bought from January 2026 from
containing parts made or supplied by a company on the Federal Communications
Commission's list of equipment presenting a security threat, and bars the Board
of State Canvassers from approving one. HB 5845 requires electronic poll book
flash drive data to be kept 22 months before the drive may be erased and reused.

Both are about a secure and auditable count, which is what this area covers.

### SB 961, SB 963 and SB 964 — the Senate's voting rights package
SB 961, the State Voting Rights Act, bars a local government or state agency from
any rule or method of election that leaves a protected class participating less
than other voters or that impairs their access to the political process, and sets
up a pre-suit notification and remedy process. SB 963 requires language
assistance where enough of a local population shares a language and has limited
English, with the Secretary of State publishing the list each odd-numbered year
and reimbursing some counting costs. Both are `civil_rights` with a yes for.

SB 964 is the reporting arm: from 2028 a local government must tell the Secretary
of State when it reorganizes or changes its method of election, when a voter asks
to view voting equipment, and which organizations may appoint election
challengers, with each notice posted within five days. Its own text is a
disclosure duty about how elections are run, so it goes to `election_integrity`
with a yes for rather than to civil rights.

### SB 533 — election_integrity, a yes vote is for
A civil fine up to $1,000 for knowingly making a false statement about the time,
place or manner of an election, voter eligibility, criminal penalties for voting,
or a person's own registration, made with intent to stop them voting; up to
$10,000 for an entity that employed the person for election work, with a
rebuttable presumption protecting a local government that did not know.

### HB 4024 and HB 4469 — civil_rights, a yes vote is against
HB 4024, the student restroom privacy act, requires every multiple-occupancy
restroom or changing area at an educational institution to be used by sex, with
sex defined as what appears on a person's original birth certificate. HB 4469
provides that Michigan's civil rights law does not stop a K-12 school subject to
Title IX from setting athletics eligibility by biological sex as listed on the
original birth certificate.

### HB 4664 — civil_rights, a yes vote is against
Blocking traffic on a public street is already unlawful and is normally a civil
infraction. The engrossed text adds a new subsection (5) making it a misdemeanor
punishable by up to 93 days and $5,000 **when the person is taking part in an
assembly on a highway**. Singling out participation in an assembly for a criminal
penalty is the direction. The rest of the bill's marked changes are style only,
replacing "person" with "individual"; the substance is that one subsection, which
is why it had to be read to the end.

### SB 34 and SB 30 — civil_rights, a yes vote is for
SB 34 is a single phrase: it adds "or lactating status" to the definition of sex
in the civil rights act, which already covers pregnancy and childbirth. SB 30,
the biased and unjust care reporting act, requires the health department to
collect reports from pregnant and postpartum patients whose care was not
unbiased, dignified, safe or properly consented to, to use them to identify
obstetric racism and obstetric violence as the act defines those terms, and to
report de-identified totals to the Governor and to legislative committees.

## ⭐ The duplicate sweep, and the trap it caught

**41 hand-written duplicates retired.** These are the newsworthy votes, so manual
research had already covered many of them: 19 on HB 4765, 8 on HB 4024, 7 on
HB 4469, 3 on HB 4720, 2 on HB 4664, and one each on SB 331 and SB 961.

**Matching on subject and date alone would have retired three valid records.**
Michigan passed **HB 4066**, "single-sex sports teams; require", 58-46 on
2025-05-22 — the same day the House passed HB 4469 by 59-45. Two companion bills,
one subject, one day, tallies one vote apart. Three hand-written records describe
HB 4066 and cite "58 to 46"; a subject-and-date sweep pulled them in as HB 4469
duplicates.

**The rule that fixed it, and that later batches should use: a record is a
duplicate only if it cites the same tally as the roll, or names the bill number.**
Applied here it confirmed 41 and rejected exactly those 3. The reason written on
each retirement says it was confirmed by the tally rather than by subject.

HB 4066 is still in the pool and has not been worked. When it is, its description
must make clear which of the two bills it is.
