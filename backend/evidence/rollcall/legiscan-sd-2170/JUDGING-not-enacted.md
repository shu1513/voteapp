## Things the text said that the title did not

- **HB 1085 is not a flat moratorium.** The title says moratorium; the text bars
  a carbon dioxide pipeline permit only until the federal Pipeline and Hazardous
  Materials Safety Administration finalizes its proposed safety rules, and
  repeals itself once the attorney general certifies that has happened. The
  description says so.
- **SB 157 is a hoghouse that undid itself into something plainer.** Amendment
  157C replaced the whole bill, and its own title line shows the change: "An Act
  to address labor trafficking in South Dakota" struck, "require proof of
  workers' compensation insurance for contractors of public improvement"
  written in. The workers' compensation version is what the Senate voted, and
  that is what the description describes.
- **HB 1228 does two things.** It makes an environmental impact statement
  compulsory before a carbon dioxide pipeline permit, and it doubles the cap on
  what an applicant can be charged for the review. Both are named.
- **SB 196 was narrowed before the vote.** Amendment 196C turned a curriculum
  mandate into a duty on the Board of Education Standards to fold the Oceti
  Sakowin Essential Understandings into content standards the next time it
  revises them. The description describes the amended version, which is what
  the Senate rejected 7-28.

## Direction calls worth stating

- **Ten Commandments displays are civil_rights, yea against.** The same call as
  Arkansas SB 433, North Dakota HB 1145 and Kentucky HJR 15.
- **School chaplains are civil_rights, yea against.** The same call as North
  Dakota HB 1456, which is the same bill.
- **Keeping medical debt off credit reports is corporate_accountability, yea
  for.** The same call as Washington SB 5480 and Maryland HB 1020.
- **A sales tax refund for data centers is corporate_accountability, yea
  against**, on the Delaware HB 310 precedent, where taking a tax break away
  from very large power users was scored for.
- **SB 67, a state apprenticeship office, went to reduce_wealth_gap.** There is
  still no labor or workplace research area, and this is the ninth state where
  that gap has forced a second-best label.

## Ballot-access fights were dropped, not scored

Four measures were screened in on a first pass and then dropped on a second:
HB 1169 (signature thresholds for an initiated constitutional amendment),
HB 1220 (affidavit-driven voter roll removal), SB 103 (vote centers) and, in
2026, HB 1087 (paid petition circulators). Missouri HJR 3, the same measure as
HB 1169 in another state, carried the no-stance `general` label, which is
barred on a candidate record. The disagreement in all four is about method, not
direction.

**SB 188 was kept**, because it is different in kind: South Dakota lets any
voter vote absentee without a reason, and the bill would have allowed it only
on a listed ground. That is a plain narrowing of who may vote absentee, the
same class as Arizona HB 2440.

## Version check, per roll

Every roll was judged against the version printed on or before its own date,
plus any floor amendment adopted after that print and before the vote. The
18 late amendments are listed by `sd_lateamend.py`. HB 1223 and SB 51 changed
meaning; the rest restated the bill.
