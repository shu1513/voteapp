# Indiana 2025 batch-09 — the education trio

Three measures were read in full: SB 358, SB 366 and SB 373. One was kept, giving two rolls.

No AI provider was called at any point.

## Kept

| Measure | Area | Yes means | Rolls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SB 358, various education matters | `public_education_quality` | for | House 58-28, Senate 37-12 |

**SB 358** has two strands and both run the same way. On reading, any school where fewer than
70% of pupils passed the state reading test must join the state literacy training programme for
teachers, subject to funding; the approved curricular materials list is extended to English and
language arts; the department must consult teachers when evaluating those materials; and
age-appropriateness becomes an approval criterion. On the education scholarship account
programme, a parent who signs up as a provider may not claim tuition for teaching only their own
child, that violation is grounds for revoking provider status, the treasurer may close the
account and claw back money, and the attorney general may seek a civil penalty up to $5,000.
Raising a standard and blocking self-dealing in a school funding programme both sit inside the
area's own mechanism.

**The counter-provision is named in the description.** The act widens the definition of a
voucher-eligible school to include one holding only provisional accreditation, which lowers a
bar in the same act that raises others. The three-year delay of the curricular materials list
deadline is a second, smaller one.

## The nay side is null

A no vote may object to the mandate on low-scoring schools, or to the scholarship account
enforcement, rather than to either strand as such.

## Dropped

**SB 366, education matters,** is a genuine omnibus. It changes a superintendent's master's
degree from required to preferred, which cuts against the area. It repeals the caps on how many
committee seats a union may appoint while deleting the option of discussing the evaluation plan
with the teachers' representative, so it widens one form of teacher voice and narrows another.
It removes an open door law exemption, which adds transparency. It extends a charter school's
building clock and adds two report mandates. No single direction holds.

**SB 373, various education matters,** is six unrelated administrative items: moving special
education authority from the state board to the department, dropping an association seat from a
bus advisory committee, letting any approved licensing exam substitute for a named one, replacing
summer school cost reimbursement with per-student funding, and decertifying dormant scholarship
granting organisations. A `government_efficiency` reading is available but weak, and the reader
said so plainly: these are low-salience housekeeping changes and a voter could reasonably see no
direction at all. That hedge is the reason for the drop. The summer school rewrite also removes
the guarantee that priority science-of-reading courses have all eligible costs covered outside
the formula, which is a possible service cut rather than an efficiency gain.
