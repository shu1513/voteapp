# Indiana 2025 batch-06 — selection

Ten measures were read in full. Two were kept, giving two rolls. Eight were dropped, and two of
those are worth reading closely, because they were dropped for reasons that had nothing to do
with their subject.

No AI provider was called at any point.

## Kept

| Measure | Area | Yes means | Roll |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SB 365, education matters | `public_education_quality` | for | House 66-24 |
| HB 1079, property matters | `corporate_accountability` | against | House 64-21 |

**SB 365** is titled like an omnibus and is not one. Every operative section is career and
technical education and its link to work: tracking whether a student who finished a career
course is in a matching job at one, three, five and ten years, publishing course information on
a public website across four agencies, and creating a career coaching chapter to replace an
older one. Better outcome data and career guidance are the area's own mechanism.

**HB 1079** is the smallest measure in the campaign so far. It changes one number in one
sentence: a warehouse or storage business may sell a tenant's unclaimed goods 45 days after
notice instead of 90. Small does not mean undirected. Halving the window in which a person can
reclaim their belongings before a business may sell them reduces consumer protection, which is
named in `corporate_accountability`'s own description. There is no counter-strand, because there
is nothing else in the act.

## Both nay sides are null

A no vote on SB 365 may object to the reporting burden on schools rather than to career
guidance. A no vote on HB 1079 may object to the storage industry's costs of holding goods
rather than to the tenant's window.

## Two measures dropped for procedural reasons, not subject

**SB 331, amended registration, was dropped by the judge's own guard, and the guard was right.**
The act requires a vehicle owner to tell the Bureau of Motor Vehicles within 30 days of changing
the car's colour, and makes it a Class C misdemeanor to change the colour after using the car in
a crime and not report it. That is single-subject and would have scored
`public_safety_and_crime_control` `for`. Its only divided roll is the House third reading of
2025-04-14, 72-22. But the House voted again on 2025-04-22, adopting the conference committee
report 73-17, and that is the chamber's decisive vote. `rollcall:judge` refused the judgment and
named the later roll. The later roll is not divided, so it cannot be used either, and the
measure is lost. Five members changed sides between the two votes, so this is not a formality:
attributing the act to the earlier vote could misstate a member's final position.

**HB 1144, courts, was dropped as a version trap.** The act abolishes the Blackford superior
court, creates two Hamilton superior courts and adjusts magistrate counts, which would have
scored `government_efficiency` `for`. Its only divided roll is the Senate third reading of
2025-04-15. The bill then went to a conference committee, and word-level comparison shows the
enrolled act drops two things the Senate voted for: the abolition of the Carroll superior court
on 2026-12-31, and a Gibson County magistrate appointment change. With no other divided roll,
the measure is lost.

## Dropped under filter 5

**HB 1390, bureau of motor vehicles.** Towing rotation policies for police agencies, a one-year
deadline to invoice a toll, electronic registration certificates, alternative licence plate
designs and their fees, and a change to the special group plate threshold. Several unrelated
subjects.

**SB 448, higher education and workforce development matters.** Workforce data coordination and a
stackable credentials framework, alongside a provision moving the collection of graduates'
income data linked to their school identification number. Education alignment and data privacy
pull different ways in the same act.

**SB 255, education matters.** Two unrelated halves: new alternative pathways to a teaching
licence, and an extension of released-time religious instruction for secondary students with
background check duties on the providers. The licensure half is itself contested, since easier
entry reads as more teachers or as lower standards depending on the reader.

**HB 1221, pension matters.** One-time payments to retired teachers, public employees, excise
police and state police, funded through a new treasurer-set surcharge, plus date extensions. A
payment to retirees is not a household cost reduction and not a safety-net programme, and the
surcharge mechanism cuts against spending restraint. No area fits.

**HB 1275, alcohol and tobacco commission matters** and **SB 43, study of location of gambling
operations.** Alcohol and gambling regulation map to no research area, and SB 43 only
commissions a study.

## Where the 2025 session now stands

19 measures remain open. Every one of them is the state budget, an education omnibus, an
elections omnibus, or an agency bill. On the evidence of batches 04, 05 and 06 the expected
yield is close to zero, and the remaining reading cost per record is the highest in the
campaign.
