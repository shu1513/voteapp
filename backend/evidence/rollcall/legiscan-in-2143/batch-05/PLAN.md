# Indiana 2025 batch-05 — selection

The first batch worked on the 2025 session since batch-04, which kept nothing. Twelve measures
were read in full from their enrolled acts. Four were kept, giving five rolls. Eight were
dropped.

Batch-04 warned that what remained of this session was omnibus work and that the keep rate
should be expected to be low. That held: the four keeps are the four narrowest measures in the
set, and every measure with "Various" or an agency name in its title was dropped.

No AI provider was called at any point.

## Kept

| Measure | Area | Yes means | Rolls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SB 140, pharmacy benefits | `healthcare_affordability` | for | Senate 39-10 |
| HB 1634, math education | `public_education_quality` | for | Senate 38-11 |
| SB 409, employee absence for certain meetings | `public_education_quality` | for | Senate 30-19 |
| HB 1037, storm water management | `environment_and_public_health` | against | Senate 31-18, House 68-20 |

**SB 140** writes a whole new chapter governing the firms that administer drug benefits. The
provisions that decide the direction are patient-facing: a walk-in pharmacy within 30 miles,
no forcing a member to a pharmacy the firm owns and no cheaper cost-sharing for using one, and
an express right for a pharmacist to tell a patient about a lower cost alternative. The
counter-strand is real and is worth naming here even though it does not reverse the direction:
the reimbursement floor, which bars paying a pharmacy less than the firm pays itself and sets a
minimum tied to acquisition cost plus a dispensing fee, is a cost that a plan may pass on.

**HB 1634** is the cleanest keep in the batch. Screening every child in kindergarten through
grade 2, tiered intervention through grade 8, automatic placement in advanced middle school
maths with a parental opt-out, and accreditation consequences for teacher preparation courses
that do not teach evidence-based methods are all the area's own mechanism.

**SB 409** is narrow and is kept as narrow. It protects a parent's job for one school
attendance meeting and one special education case conference a year. The limits are stated in
the description rather than hidden: five days' notice, no longer than needed, proof of
attendance, unpaid, a duty to try to attend online, and the chapter ends in 2029.

**HB 1037** bars any local rule on construction site run-off that goes beyond the state's own
stormwater permit and voids the ones that exist. Its one counter-strand, the retained power to
warn and then stop work on sites under an acre, is stated.

## Every nay side is null

A no vote on SB 140 may object to setting a minimum a plan must pay a pharmacy rather than to
the patient protections. A no vote on HB 1634 or SB 409 may object to a new duty on schools or
employers. A no vote on HB 1037 may object to the state overriding local government rather than
to the stormwater standard itself.

## Dropped under filter 5

**HB 1461, road funding.** The largest measure in the set and a clear omnibus. It is for public
infrastructure through the matching grant fund, bridge responsibility rules and low water
crossing work. It is against cost of living twice over: it triples the ceiling on the Marion
County wheel tax and vehicle excise surtax, and it removes the requirement that the General
Assembly approve new interstate tolling, deleting along the way the study of discounts for
in-state and commuter drivers. It also carries a railroad income tax credit paid for out of
local road money and a bar on Indianapolis spending its share on sidewalks, bike lanes or
greenways. No single direction is honest.

**SB 287, school board matters.** Puts party affiliation on the school board ballot and makes a
major-party claim depend on having voted in the last two primaries or on a county chairman's
certification. More information on the ballot and party officials gatekeeping how a candidate is
labelled are both defensible readings inside `election_integrity`.

**HB 1633, study of election issues.** Directs the secretary of state to study election timing
and vote centers and report by November 2025. A vote to commission a study is not a vote on any
policy.

**HB 1196, membership of aviation boards.** Exempts entities under 38,000 population from the
party-balance cap on aviation boards. Easier to staff a small board and a weaker structural
safeguard are both readings, and the measure is tiny.

**SB 525, annexation** extends one city's annexation power to certain towns by population
bracket. **SB 108, charity gaming** and **SB 209, electronic pull tabs in charity gaming**
regulate gambling. None of the three maps to a research area.

**HB 1460, drainage systems** is dropped for a different reason. Its subject is workable: it
would have scored `housing_affordability` `for`, since it puts deadlines on plat review and
deems a plat approved if the commission misses them. But its only divided roll is a version
trap. The text the House passed said a unit "may use data from another state ... as
appropriate"; the enrolled act says a unit "must use data regarding a location ... in the
closest proximity" and rewrites the whole proximity rule. With no other divided roll, the
measure is lost.

## Where the 2025 session now stands

This batch leaves 22 measures open, and the honest expectation is that almost none of them
survive. What is left is the state budget, four bills titled "Various education matters" or
"Education matters", two titled "Various elections matters", and a run of agency bills. The
2026 session, worked in parallel, is the better use of the next batch.
