# Indiana 2025 batch-10 — the last two, and the session closes

Two measures were read in full: HB 1001, the state budget, and SB 1, local government finance.
**Neither was kept.** Nothing was judged and nothing was imported, so the database is unchanged
by this batch. This directory holds the selection reasoning only.

With this batch **every divided-and-enacted measure in both Indiana sessions has been read in
full and dispositioned.**

No AI provider was called at any point.

## HB 1001, state budget

A yes vote cannot honestly be scored for or against any research area, and the reason is not
that a budget is hard to read. It is that this one carries at least five unrelated policy
programmes, and a legislator could have voted yes for any of them.

- **A large tax increase.** The cigarette tax rises from $0.04975 to $0.14975 per cigarette, two
  dollars on a pack of twenty. Other tobacco goes from 24% to 30% of wholesale, closed-system
  vape cartridges and retail e-cigarettes both double from 15% to 30%, and the cigar cap goes
  from $1 to $3. The public utility fee rises from 0.15% to 0.175% of gross revenue, and a new
  $26 small claims service fee is created.
- **A Medicaid benefit restriction.** The statutory guarantee of unrestricted access to mental
  health drugs is repealed, along with the ban on prior authorisation for antidepressants,
  antipsychotics and antianxiety drugs and the procedural protections around restricting them.
- **A voucher expansion.** The 400% income cap on choice scholarship eligibility is deleted from
  June 29 2026, making the programme universal.
- **A higher education governance overhaul.** Degree programmes below graduate-count thresholds
  must be eliminated unless the commission approves, tenure becomes subject to post-tenure
  productivity review with probation and possible dismissal, faculty governance bodies become
  advisory only, and all nine Indiana University trustees become governor-appointed.
- **A spending control.** The budget director must withhold at least 5% of every agency salary
  and operating appropriation in both years, releasable only with the governor's approval.

Inside a single area the act contradicts itself. On public education quality it raises the base
per-student amount while cutting the poverty multiplier and the level-one special education
grant, repealing the curricular materials fund, and reducing required state board meetings from
monthly to annually. On healthcare affordability it moves the Medicaid share of tobacco revenue
from 2.46% to 59.38% while repealing guaranteed access to mental health drugs. On cost of living
every provision points one way, upward, but that is a side effect of how the health funding was
raised rather than the act's subject.

**A process note worth keeping.** The first read of HB 1001 worked from the Senate committee
version, because the enrolled file was still downloading and showed as zero bytes. The reader
said so plainly rather than presenting a committee-version answer as an enrolled-act answer, and
the measure was re-read against the enrolled text before it was dispositioned. That mattered:
a citation-level check found the cigarette and e-cigarette tax increase, the voucher income cap
removal, the mental health drug repeal, the post-tenure review and forced degree elimination,
the governor-appointed trustees, the abolition of the Governor's Workforce Cabinet and the
pre-K income cut are **all absent from the committee version**. A reader of the committee print
would have seen a budget bill with fiscal management provisions and missed every one of them.
The drop stands either way, but the direction a keep would have carried was not the same.

## SB 1, local government finance

This was the closer call of the two, and it was weighed rather than assumed. Most of the
property tax package genuinely reduces what a homeowner pays: the flat $48,000 homestead
deduction is replaced by a percentage deduction growing to 66.7% by 2031, a new supplemental
credit gives 10% of the bill up to $300, and a county-option deferral programme is created. On
that alone it would score `cost_of_living_reduction` `for`.

Three things in the same act stop that being honest.

- **It authorises higher income taxes on the same households.** The county maximum expenditure
  rate rises from 2.5% to 2.9%, and a new municipal local income tax of up to 1.2% is created for
  cities and towns of 3,500 or more. A city resident could face both.
- **Its largest single cut is not for households.** The business personal property exemption goes
  from $80,000 to $2,000,000 and the 30% valuation floor is dropped for new equipment. Because
  Indiana rates are a levy divided by the base, shrinking the business base shifts weight onto
  the taxpayers who remain rather than lowering anyone's cost.
- **It carries an unrelated school strand.** Referenda are confined to general elections,
  referendum revenue must be shared with charter schools above a threshold, and one school
  corporation is dissolved outright. A voter who supports the tax relief and opposes the school
  changes has no way to split the vote.
