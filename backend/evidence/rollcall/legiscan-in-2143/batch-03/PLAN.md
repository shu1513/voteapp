# Indiana batch-03 — selection

**4 measures, 8 rolls, 386 records across 102 candidates.** Imported on the local `voteapp`
database 2026-09-02. Production untouched.

Twelve measures were shortlisted from the 123 open rolls, chosen for having a House roll
(worth about 83 records against about 12 for a Senate roll) and a title that suggested a
single subject. Four were kept and eight were dropped. Every enacted text was read in full
before the call was made.

## Kept

**SB 450, Article V convention** — `anti_corruption`, a yes vote scores `for`. Rules for the
people Indiana would send to a convention on amending the United States Constitution: five
years' residence, a five-year bar on anyone who has been a registered lobbyist, bars for a
crime of moral turpitude or a felony sentence served in the past ten years, a ban on gifts
worth $200 or more, and a standing instruction never to vote for an amendment touching the
Bill of Rights or other named parts of the Constitution. Senate 1484850 (33-16) and House
1524703 (69-27). The House passed the bill without amendment, so both chambers voted the
text that became law.

**SB 423, small modular nuclear reactor pilot program** — `cost_of_living_reduction`, a yes
vote scores `against`. House 1548365 (67-29) and Senate 1551274 (34-12).

**SB 424, small modular nuclear reactor development costs** — `cost_of_living_reduction`, a
yes vote scores `against`. House 1522403 (59-30) and Senate 1526255 (32-15). SB 423 and
SB 424 are separate acts with nearly identical titles; SB 423 is a partnership programme
that ends in 2035, SB 424 is a permanent change open to any utility acting alone.

**SB 2, Medicaid matters** — `healthcare_affordability` and `social_programs_and_welfare`,
a yes vote scores `against` on both. This is the measure batch-02 fetched and held because
its enacted text runs to 990 lines. House 1539781 (66-28) and Senate 1551292 (37-10).

## Dropped, with reasons

**SB 10, voter registration — two independent faults.** The Senate's divided roll of
2025-02-04 was taken on a text that contained two whole regimes the act does not: a
free-standing IC 3-7-38.2-2.5 mailing programme reaching only voters "whose registration
record is in active status" whose address the county had separately found wrong, and an
interstate data-sharing push built on Indiana's own IDEA membership programme. The act
replaced the first with a broader subsection reaching *every* voter who missed two general
elections, and the second with a new IC 3-7-38.2-19 asking states for bilateral agreements.
That is the California SB 707 version trap. The House roll was on the enacted text and was
the better roll, but LegiScan records Representative Dvorak as not voting where the official
journal records a nay. A missing member does not merely cost one record: the tally the
description has to cite becomes 66-25 where Indiana's own journal says 66-26. The judge
refuses a description that does not cite its roll's tally, and it is right to. With both
rolls unusable the measure drops.

**HB 1006, prosecutors — same defect plus a version trap.** LegiScan records Representative
Baird as a nay on the final House roll where the journal records him excused, which would
have written a false record. The earlier House roll cannot stand in for it: the version the
House passed in February carried five regimes the act does not, including a direction to the
state comptroller to withhold funding from a county with a non-compliant prosecutor, a new
public prosecution fund, a special prosecutor unit and an increase in indigent defence
reimbursement from 40% to 50%. The act keeps only the review board, whose sole power is to
write a report. The measure also carries two subjects.

**HB 1007, energy generation resources — filter 5.** Three subjects: a 20% state tax credit
for manufacturing small modular reactors, a new expedited approval track at the utility
commission, and new restrictions on retiring existing power plants. The direction is
genuinely contested inside each candidate area. Large customers must reimburse at least 80%
of the project costs allocable to them, which protects other ratepayers, but the remainder
is recovered from general ratepayers with a profit, and four separate clauses approve a
filing automatically if the commission runs out of time. The retirement provisions restrain
utilities in a bill that otherwise frees them.

**HB 1008, Indiana-Illinois boundary adjustment commission — filter 3.** An eleven-member
advisory commission that may only "discuss and recommend", whose recommendation binds nobody
without the Indiana General Assembly, the Illinois legislature and the United States
Congress all agreeing. No research area fits.

**HB 1115, emergency possessory orders — filter 5.** The act adds two fast-track grounds for
a landlord to recover a rental unit, one of them a crime by the tenant or the tenant's guest
with no conviction required, the other a tenant lying to obtain the lease. In the same
breath it raises the standard of proof for every case in the chapter from probable cause to
a preponderance of the evidence, which makes a landlord's waste case harder, and preserves
the rights of tenants who are crime victims under IC 32-31-9. A safety ground and a
lease-fraud ground do not point the same way.

**SB 425, energy production zones — filter 5.** Six subjects. It exempts large generating
plants on former power-plant and former-mine sites from all local zoning, expressly
excluding wind and solar from the definition; rewrites the general local permit statute with
new deadlines, a three-year rule freeze and written-findings duties; sets rules for
development agreements; creates a shorter zoning-adoption path for advisory plan commission
jurisdictions; renames terms in the Lake Michigan seawall permit chapter; and caps local
moratoriums on non-utility electricity projects at one year, which is a grant of local power
in an act otherwise removing it.

**SB 457, carbon dioxide sequestration — filter 5, and the House roll is unusable.** The act
makes underground carbon storage easier to permit: it drops the agricultural-impact
agreement from the pipeline certificate, exempts on-site pipelines from the certificate and
so from its library filing, published notice and public meeting, and lets the state force
unwilling pore space owners into a project before the federal storage permit has issued. It
also abolishes the carbon dioxide storage facility trust fund, whose money could be used
only for long-term monitoring, and sends the balance to the general fund — while leaving in
place the rule that the state assumes all liability once a project is complete. Against
that, it raises fees sharply and creates civil penalties. Storing carbon dioxide and
weakening landowner protection point opposite ways inside
`environment_and_public_health`. Separately, LegiScan records Representative Pryor as a nay
on the House roll where the journal records a yea.

**SJR 21, terms of members of Congress — filter 3.** Indiana's application to Congress for a
convention to propose congressional term limits. It is single-subject and cleanly drafted,
but no research area fits: `anti_corruption` is about transparency, ethics rules and
enforcement, and term limits are none of those. The House roll is also unusable, which would
have left the Senate's twelve records on their own.
