# Indiana 2025 batch-07 — the health pair

Three measures were read in full: HB 1002, HB 1003 and HB 1004. Two were kept, giving three
rolls. This batch is where the 2025 tail finally paid, and it paid because two of its three
measures are single-subject despite carrying omnibus-looking titles.

No AI provider was called at any point.

## Kept

| Measure | Area | Yes means | Rolls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HB 1003, health matters | `healthcare_affordability` | for | House 67-25, Senate 30-20 |
| HB 1004, health care matters | `healthcare_affordability` | for | Senate 37-13 |

**HB 1003** is titled like an omnibus and is not one. About sixty of its sixty-nine sections push
the same way: price posting for laboratories, imaging centers and hospitals; the written price
estimate deadline cut from five working days to two; free copies of X-rays and of an electronic
health record; a ban on billing an office visit with hospital facility codes, backed by a $1,000
a day penalty; the removal of trade secret shields from pharmacy benefit manager audits. Nothing
in it raises a patient's costs. Two counter-provisions are worth recording even though they do
not change the direction: IC 16-51-1-10 narrows who the facility-fee rules cover, from providers
owned in part by a nonprofit hospital system to those employed by one, and IC 16-42-26.5(6)(d)
puts a manufacturer liability shield inside an otherwise accountability-increasing act.

**HB 1004** caps what large nonprofit hospital systems may charge employers at 260% of Medicare,
audits compliance yearly at $10,000 a day, and from 2029 strips the state tax exemption from a
large system whose average prices exceed the state average. It also rebuilds Medicaid hospital
financing, taking approval power from a committee drawn from a hospital industry list and giving
it to the state Medicaid office. The strongest counter-provision is an exemption from the
facility-fee restrictions for urgent care sites in municipalities under 20,000 people, which
preserves the extra charge in exactly the small towns the rest of the act is aimed at. Two
smaller ones: the new managed care assessment fee is a levy on insurers with nothing barring
them from passing it into premiums, and 260% of Medicare is a ceiling well above what Medicare
pays.

## Both nay sides are null

A no vote on either may object to the compliance burden or to the state setting prices, rather
than to the patient-facing effect.

## HB 1004 keeps only one of its four rolls

Its House and Senate third readings both predate the conference committee, which rewrote the
bill at 0.89 similarity across 355 differing runs. That leaves the two conference report rolls,
and **the House one fails the member-list check**: LegiScan reports 67-23 where the official
journal reports 68-23, with Representative Behning in the journal's yeas and absent from
LegiScan's. Only the Senate conference roll survives. **The worklist flag has now been a perfect
predictor across both sessions: six flagged rolls checked, six failed, and no unflagged roll has
failed.**

## HB 1002 dropped

218 sections, about 95 of them repeals across the education title: the elementary counsellors and
school psychologists programme, arts education, dropout prevention, remediation, summer school,
cultural competency, school improvement plans and student enrichment grants among them. It also
strips the teacher evaluation procedure of its rating categories, its written plan, its public
meeting and its right to a different evaluator. The opposite pulls are real: it repeals the human
trafficking in-service training mandate while adding a new training gate on an initial teaching
licence and a trauma-informed mandate on teacher preparation; and it cuts state spending through
mass repeal while letting schools charge families a new supplies and materials fee. No single
direction is honest.

## A reading-integrity note

**One of this batch's read reports contained three claims that were not in the text.** A report on
HB 1002 asserted that the school board minimum age dropped from 21 to 18, that the act dropped a
charter economic-interest filing and a three-year minimum term, and that it added a wind-down
scheme paying employee payroll first. None of the first two exists in the act, and the third is a
relocation of existing language rather than a new protection. The reader caught this itself,
retracted the claims, re-read the marked passages directly and reissued the report.

Nothing turned on it: HB 1002 was dropped as an omnibus on grounds that survive the correction,
and the erroneous claims never reached a judgment file. It is recorded here because it is the
first time in this campaign that a read report has invented detail, and the lesson is the one the
correction itself drew: **a summary that is not traceable to marked text in the file is not
evidence.** Every claim that reaches a description is checked against the act.
