## Kept

| Measure | Area | Yes means | Rolls |

| HB 1680, various elections matters | `election_integrity` | for | House 65-25, Senate 36-9 |

**HB 1680** is the rarer thing in this campaign: a bill titled "Various" that has one direction.
The weight of the act is security, accuracy and auditability. Anyone registering with an
identity number from a temporary credential must prove citizenship or the registration is
refused after 30 days. A post office box or commercial mailbox may no longer be a residence
address. Third parties may not pre-print information on registration forms they mail. Every
county must report its computer support provider to the state. A person holding a nomination
petition they believe materially false must deliver it to the county board within ten days.
Contributions routed through a payment platform must be reported at gross value before fees.
And the secretary of state gains a standing power to audit how any primary or general election
was run, with named deadlines and a duty to consult statistical experts, where before an audit
required an investigation or a recount.

**The counter-provision is real and is named in the description.** The act strips the Indiana
Election Commission of its power to issue advisory opinions and voids every opinion it issued
before July 2025, with nothing put in their place. That removes accumulated written guidance
county boards relied on, which cuts against uniform administration. Two smaller ones sit
alongside it: the auxiliary party organisation disclosure threshold doubles, and an absentee
affidavit missing an accurate signature date is now insufficient, so a ballot can be rejected
over a clerical omission.

## The nay side is null

A no vote may object to the citizenship proof requirement as a barrier, or to voiding the
advisory opinions, rather than to the audit programme. More than one honest reading is
available, so no voters carry no tag.

## HB 1679 dropped

The sister bill genuinely pulls several ways, and the contrast with HB 1680 is what makes the
distinction defensible rather than arbitrary. HB 1679 hardens the voter list, requiring
maintenance within 48 hours and adding two new triggers, and it also repeals the ban on
photographing and sharing your own ballot outright. It widens access by letting voters email a
replacement-ballot request, and narrows it by deleting fax as an option for voters with print
disabilities and deleting the postal fallback when email cannot deliver in time. It adds
disclosure by making school board candidates file county campaign finance reports, and subtracts
it by stripping candidate street addresses from published lists. Most directly against the area,
it makes a recount petitioner who loses by ten percent or more liable for the other side's
expenses and bars filing a petition earlier than ten days after election day, both of which make
a result harder to check. It also carries a fee schedule with a new dedicated fund and an
impeachment provision belonging to no election theme at all.
