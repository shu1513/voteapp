## Both measures are recorded with NO stance

Neither carries a research-area label. This is the Ohio H.B. 116 precedent — a consequential divided
vote worth recording, with no honest direction — and it is the right call for a different reason in
each case.

**HB 1** redraws the eight congressional districts mid-decade. No research area describes
redistricting: `election_integrity` is about elections being "secure, accurate, auditable, and
trusted", which a map change is not, and `impartiality` is a candidate-evaluation area that is not
even attached to state-legislative offices. There is nothing to label, so the record states what the
Act does and stops.

**HJR 3** is the harder call, and it turns on a distinction worth naming. Several of its strands are
discrete and one-directional — barring foreign money from ballot-measure committees, criminalising
petition signature fraud, requiring a public hearing before a petition reaches the ballot, putting
the full text in every voter's hands. In a statute, those would earn `election_integrity`/for on the
per-strand pattern; batch-01 of the regular session labelled SB 152 exactly that way for the
foreign-money rule alone.

But **HJR 3 is a single ballot question, not a statute.** Its marquee provision — an initiative
constitutional amendment would pass only with a majority in *every* congressional district, on top
of the statewide majority — is why the vote divided, and it maps to no area in either direction.
A voter cannot accept one strand and reject another; they get one yes-or-no on the package. So a
strand label would not describe a separable part of the Act, it would mischaracterise the choice
that was actually put. **When the strands are inseparable in the question presented, per-strand
labelling stops being honest.** That is the line between this measure and HB 495 in the regular
session's batch-02, whose labelled strands are independent statutory changes.

## Facts the descriptions had to get right

- **HJR 3 is a joint resolution, and it has already been voted on.** It needed no governor's
  signature and never became law. Its opening clause offered the November 2026 general election "or
  at a special election to be called by the governor", and the governor took that option: a
  proclamation announced 2026-05-22 put it on the **August 4, 2026 primary ballot as Amendment 4**,
  and his official statement of 2026-08-05 records its defeat. The descriptions say so, and describe
  its provisions as things it "would have" done. It proposed a new Article III, Section 54.
- **The district rule is a majority in EVERY congressional district**, not a majority of districts —
  "approved only if affirmative votes are cast by a majority of voters in each congressional district
  in effect at the time of the vote" (§ 54.6), notwithstanding Article III §§ 51 and 52(b).
- **The foreign-money ban is asymmetric.** A committee may not "knowingly or willfully" receive,
  solicit or accept foreign contributions (§ 54.3(1), first sentence), but the ban on a foreign
  national or foreign adversary *making* a contribution or expenditure is unqualified (second
  sentence). A description must carry the intent element on the committee side and not import it
  onto the donor side.
- **HJR 3 contains no voter-citizenship provision.** The word "citizen" appears only in the
  definitions of "foreign adversary" and "foreign national". A description asserting a
  proof-of-citizenship or noncitizen-voting rule would be inventing one.
- **HB 1's map starts with the 120th Congress** — § 128.346 leaves the post-2020 map effective through
  the 119th, so the new districts first apply at the November 2026 election.
- **HB 1 became law**: approved by the governor 2025-09-29. The descriptions say the governor signed
  it, and make no claim about litigation or referendum petitions, which these documents do not show.
