# Alabama 2023 batch-01 — selection plan

## How the batch was selected

The five filters used throughout this campaign.

1. **Divided** — the losing side is at least a quarter of the winning side. 28 of 1,003 kept floor
   votes.
2. **Consequential** — the measure became law. 21 rolls on 18 measures.
3. **A nameable subject.** Nothing dropped here: unlike 2025, none of this session's divided rolls
   sits on a local act.
4. **One roll per measure per chamber, preferring the vote on the enacted text.** Six of the chosen
   rolls are superseded by a later concurrence in the same chamber and carry
   `acknowledge_later_rolls`; each description says which version that chamber voted.
5. **A defensible direction, or a deliberate no-stance import.** Five measures dropped, four imported
   with no stance.

The roll-attribution check that the 2026 session made necessary was run and is vacuous here: this
session prints no roll call numbers in its descriptions at all.

## Dropped under filter 5, after reading each enacted Act

- **HB 62 (veterans' building code exemption).** A nonprofit veterans' organization that takes over a
  state armory is exempt from building codes for at least 10 years. No research area covers it, and
  the subject is narrow.
- **HB 48 (municipal annexation).** Validates past annexations despite procedural defects. No area
  fits and the subject is narrow.
- **HB 304 (food truck alcohol licences).** Extends an entertainment-district licence to all classes
  of municipality. No area covers alcohol licensing — the same call as HB 593 in the 2026 batch.
- **SB 184 (Department of Corrections spending).** Authorises the department to spend appropriated
  money on recruitment, public education and rehabilitation programmes. Housekeeping.
- **SB 198 (sentencing).** The Act runs both ways inside public safety: it raises the penalties for
  attempt, solicitation and conspiracy while giving judges more discretion on Class C and D
  felonies. Same shape as HB 43 in the 2025 batch and SB 254 in the 2026 batch.
