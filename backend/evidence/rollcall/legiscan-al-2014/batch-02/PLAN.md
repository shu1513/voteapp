# Alabama 2023 batch-02 — selection plan

The measures that were divided but did **not** become law: they passed one chamber and died, or
failed on the floor. 7 of the session's 28 divided rolls.

Filters 1, 3, 4 and 5 apply unchanged; filter 2 is inverted by definition. Each measure was judged
from the version that chamber actually voted — the engrossed print where one exists, the introduced
print where the bill was never engrossed.

## What is in the batch

4 rolls on 4 measures, 276 records across 110 candidates.

| Measure | Roll | Outcome | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| HB 209 handling another voter's absentee paperwork | House 76-28 | died in the Senate | election_integrity, yes = for |
| HB 392 federal gun bans made state law | House 64-36 | died in the Senate | gun_control, yes = for |
| SB 181 statewide due process for school discipline | Senate 11-20 | **failed on the floor** | civil_rights, yes = for |
| HB 229 resentencing under the habitual offender law | House 64-37 | died in the Senate | general, no stance |

SB 181 is the first failed passage vote in the Alabama campaign. It is a real recorded position and
is imported as one; the description says the vote fell short and the bill failed, and never says it
became law.

## Dropped

- **HB 208 (Pregnancy Resource Act).** Dropped on evidence, not on subject. The dataset holds **two
  House rolls with the identical caption on the identical day** — 99-3 and 76-26, both
  `Read a Third Time and Pass as Amended` on 2023-05-17 — while the bill history records only one
  such action. One of them is almost certainly the amendment adoption printed under the passage
  caption, but this session prints no roll call numbers, so there is no way to pin which. Given that
  the 2024 and 2026 sessions have proven roll-attribution defects, the honest answer is not to guess.
  The same measure returned in 2024 as HB 356 with a single clean roll and is in that session's
  batch-02, so the subject is still represented.
- **SB 118 (municipal annexation).** No research area fits, and the subject is narrow. Its enacted
  twin HB 48 was dropped from batch-01 for the same reason.
- **HB 436 (youthful offender age).** The text runs both ways: it widens who may be treated as a
  youthful offender while adding victim notice and an evidentiary hearing where serious injury or
  death is alleged. Narrow subject, contested direction.
