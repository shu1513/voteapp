# Judging notes, Connecticut batch-03

Seven divided House roll calls. **None of the seven became law.** Every
description says so and says how it ended — vetoed, or the Senate never voted.

## Finding the passage roll

Connecticut names no question on any roll, in either chamber: a House roll reads
`House Roll Call Vote 356 AS AMENDED`. The question has to come from the bill's
action trail.

The House descs do carry the chamber's own sequential vote number, and that
number is chronological, so House rolls on one bill and day can be ordered and
aligned to the day's vote actions in the trail. Two rules were applied, and only
rolls satisfying one of them were kept:

- exactly one stored roll that day and the trail's last vote action is a
  `House Passed …` line; or
- the count of stored rolls equals the count of vote actions, and the aligned
  action is a `House Passed …` line.

Alignment fails whenever an amendment was adopted on a voice vote, because the
trail carries an action with no roll behind it. Seventeen of the twenty-three
divided House rolls on non-enacted measures resolved cleanly; the rest were left
open rather than guessed at.

## What each chamber actually voted

Every one of these bills was amended on the floor before passage, so the
committee substitute is not by itself what the House voted. The adopted schedule
was fetched by matching the LCO number printed in the trail line
(`House Adopted House Amendment Schedule A 8356` → amendment titled
`House Schedule A LCO# 8356`). Every schedule carried the same act title as the
base bill, so no subject changed. Two changed the substance enough to matter,
and the descriptions follow the amended text, not the base:

- **HB 6477** — the base put the translation plan on the Commissioner of
  Administrative Services; Schedule A moves it to the office of the Governor.
- **HB 5269** — the base said to "place a monetary limit on" credit card use;
  Schedule A prohibits credit cards outright. The description says prohibited.
- **HB 7210** — Schedule A strikes the Department of Mental Health and Addiction
  Services throughout, leaving the Department of Correction. The description
  names only Correction.

Two needed no schedule. **SB 8** was passed by the House on the Senate's own
Schedule A and its chaptered text is the final text both chambers passed, which
the Governor then vetoed. **HB 7065** is the one bill here whose House rejected
every floor amendment — Schedules A through E all failed — and passed the
committee substitute unchanged.

## Label reasoning

Every label uses `nay: null`.

- **SB 8**, `reduce_wealth_gap`, for. It removes the labor-dispute
  disqualification from unemployment benefits once a strike has run fourteen
  days, which shifts bargaining weight toward workers.
- **SB 1427** and **HB 7210**, `social_programs_and_welfare`, for. Paid family
  and medical leave extended to school employees; and halfway house contracts
  required to honor a resident's discharge plan and barred from discharging
  someone because the facility closed.
- **HB 6477**, `civil_rights`, for. A plan to review and translate the state
  forms people need to reach services, for people with limited English.
- **HB 7087**, `environment_and_public_health`, for. A community solar program
  under the utility regulator.
- **HB 7065**, `public_safety_and_crime_control`, **against**. It removes
  possession of small amounts of psilocybin from the criminal penalties for
  hallucinogens. The direction follows the campaign's usage: this area is
  labeled `for` when a measure tightens criminal penalties, so loosening them
  is `against`. That is a statement about which way the measure moves, not about
  whether it is good policy.
- **HB 5269**, `corporate_accountability`, for. One online gambling account per
  platform and an outright ban on paying by credit card, both limits on what the
  operators may offer.

these is law, and ends by saying how the bill died. Plain-language lint: 14
