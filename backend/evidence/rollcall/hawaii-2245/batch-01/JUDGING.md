## Measure notes

**HB 1961.** A new chapter. The offense is intentionally, knowingly or recklessly blocking passage
into or out of a health care facility, making repeated calls to impede a facility's or a person's
phone lines, or threatening the facility's owners, agents, patients, employees or property.
Penalties: first offense at least $250 and at least 24 consecutive hours in jail; second at least
$750 and at least seven days; third at least $1,000 and up to 30 days. Civil suit by any aggrieved
party without proof of actual damages, with discretionary added damages up to $500 a day ($5,000 for
a facility); attorney general injunctions. "Person" means someone 18 or older (§ -3(c)). The
purpose section says the act "is not intended to affect any rights under federal or state law on
peaceful expressive conduct, including labor demonstrations or exercising the right to strike."
The act covers every health care facility; supporters' focus on clinics is not in the text, so the
label is the crime-and-remedy area, not reproductive rights (the "label the subject, not the
headline" rule).

**HB 2023.** The DOT program and the manufacturer liability shield take effect on approval; the
rest on January 1, 2028. For excessive speeding the court "shall" issue a device permit once the
defendant proves installation and insurance (§291C-105(e)); for racing the court "may"
(§291C-103(c)). Tampering is a misdemeanor; driving without a required device carries three to 30
days on a first offense, 30 days on a second, six months to a year on a third, with license
suspensions of one, two and four years. The description says "at least three days."

**SB 2239.** Opt-out registration at license and ID transactions from January 1, 2027; automatic
name and address updates for registered voters unless declined. Transmission only with proof of
citizenship shown or already on file; a non-citizen is not offered registration; if the documents
cannot verify status the office gives registration information and eligibility instructions
instead (§11-15.7(a)).

**SB 2400.** Adds "persons operating wing-in-ground craft engaged in the transportation of
passengers" to the Water Carrier Act exemptions. The findings section states the Coast Guard
oversees safety and consumer protection; the conference report confirms cargo craft stay regulated.
Direction: it removes the Public Utilities Commission's oversight of these companies
(corporate_accountability, against); the decarbonization goals in the findings are not operative
provisions.

**SB 2694.** The mechanism is mandatory July 2026 through June 2029 and optional July 2029 through
June 2033, tied to the harbors division's wharfage rate increase, capped at 5 percent a year, with
a full rate case in the third year of each cycle. Agricultural-rate customers are exempt. §271G-17(g)
lets the commission waive any requirement of the chapter for a carrier on application. The act is
repealed July 1, 2033. Direction: automatic increases between rate cases and a general waiver
power loosen the constraints on a regulated company.

**SB 2697.** The shoulder ban carries $250, then $1,000 within five years. §249-10 previously
allowed seizure for any delinquent registration tax; the act limits it to taxes delinquent "for
one or more consecutive years," lengthens the hold and notice periods from ten to thirty days,
frees the owner from costs of an improper seizure, and raises the no-auction threshold to $500.
The out-of-state registration fine is $500 to $1,000; the HOV-lane penalty becomes $250 to
$1,000 per conviction, replacing $75/$150/$200. The description states the seizure narrowing
plainly rather than folding it into "tougher enforcement."
