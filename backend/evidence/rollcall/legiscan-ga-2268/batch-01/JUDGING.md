# Judging notes, Georgia 2026 special session, batch 01

Two roll calls on one measure, SB 3, which the Governor signed on 25 June 2026
as Act 1EX.

## What each chamber actually voted

The Senate passed its own version 33-19 on 20 June. The House rewrote it in
committee and passed the substitute 94-79 on 23 June. The Senate then took two
votes the same day: roll 1711340, `Agree To House Substitute As Amended`,
which failed 19-32, and roll 1711341, `Agree To House Substitute`, which
carried 36-16. So both imported rolls voted the House substitute, and that
substitute is the enrolled Act.

Described from the Enrolled print (doc 3450266, 90,670 bytes, byte length and
MD5 verified against the dataset manifest), read in full — it is six sections
and about five pages.

No vehicle-bill trap: SB 3 was introduced as an election bill and the House
substitute stayed on the same subject.

## Supersession

The gate flagged roll 1711340 as a same-day peer of the imported roll 1711341.
1711340 is Senate Vote #27 and 1711341 is Senate Vote #28, so the imported roll
is the later and decisive one. 1711340 is listed under
`acknowledge_later_rolls` with that reason.

Note that the earlier Senate passage vote on 20 June (roll 1710469, 33-19) is
divided and would otherwise qualify. The ladder keeps the last divided roll per
chamber, which is the concurrence vote, so the Senate record reflects the
member's final position on the bill as enacted.

## Labels

Both carry `election_integrity`, `yea: "for"`, `nay: null`.

The Act's substance: a nine-member committee to design a new statewide voting
system built on hand-marked paper ballots, reporting by January 2027 for use in
2028; a bar on the sitting Secretary of State taking any part in choosing,
certifying or buying that system, with any such action he has already taken
declared void; an automatic statewide hand recount whenever a close statewide
race is decided by half a percentage point or less, with the state repaying
counties; a wider list of contests subject to risk-limiting audits; and a delay
of one section of the 2024 election law SB 189 to January 2028.

Moving to hand-marked paper ballots, adding mandatory recounts and widening
audits are for-election-integrity actions on the face of the record, so a yes
vote is tagged `for`.

`nay` is null rather than `"against"`. The provision stripping the sitting
Secretary of State of authority is a dispute about who controls procurement,
not about election integrity, and it is a stated reason members voted no.
Tagging no voters as against election integrity would misstate that. Under the
explicit-nay contract, no voters get no tag on this slug.

## Descriptions

Both cite their own roll call's tally. The description names the committee, the
hand-marked paper ballots, the bar on the Secretary of State and the recount
threshold, which are the four provisions a voter would recognize the Act by.
The audit list and the SB 189 delay are left out to keep the paragraph inside
the length limit. Plain-language lint: 4 descriptions, 0 warnings, Flesch-
Kincaid grade 9.1. The grade sits above the 7th-grade target because
"statewide", "committee" and "Secretary" are unavoidable in naming what the Act
does; shortening further would have cost the recount threshold.
