# Wyoming batch 03: how each measure was judged

Eleven measures, one roll call each. This batch finishes Wyoming: after it,
every one of the 126 closely divided votes on measures that became law carries
a disposition, and none is left as "not yet worked".

Sources, reading method and writing rules are as in batch 01. Every enrolled
act here was read top to bottom with `../wy_text.py`, including both omnibus
water acts in full.

## A source added in this batch

Wyoming's Legislative Service Office publishes a "Summary as Passed" for most
acts, and LegiScan carries it as a supplement. It is nonpartisan, it opens with
no sponsor statement, and it prints its own warning that it is not a
comprehensive abstract. It was used as an index, never as evidence, and every
claim in these descriptions comes from the enrolled act.

It earned its place twice. It confirmed what HB 25 repeals, which the act
itself does not reprint. And it is the reason HB 145 was dropped: the title
reads as a tax cut and the summary shows the act is a tax restructuring.

It was also wrong once, in the direction the campaign expects. The summary for
SF 8 describes the new crime as reaching "any criminal or drug-related
offense". The enacted text reaches only offenses under Wyoming's criminal code
or its controlled substances law. The description follows the act.

## Notes on the harder calls

**The two omnibus water acts were read in full**, schedule and all, not
summarised from their totals. That matters: the reading turned up things a
total would hide. HB 117 adds $80 million to two projects already under way,
the Goshen Irrigation District tunnels and the LaPrele dam rebuild, drawn from
the legislative stabilization reserve rather than the water accounts. SF 70
sets a hard stop on the Alkali Creek Reservoir: no further time extensions are
intended, and from January 1, 2027 its money may not be spent or used to take
land by eminent domain. SF 70 also holds back Newcastle's grant until the state
agrees the town has allowed water sales outside city limits. All three are in
the descriptions.

**SF 16 under civil rights.** The act does not give tribal governments a veto
or a new right in the outcome. It gives them the notice local governments
already get. Fair treatment under law is the area's own language and it fits.
Environment and public health was considered and rejected: the act changes who
is told, not what may be built.

**SF 5 under healthcare affordability.** A bankruptcy option is a financial
tool, but the alternative it replaces in state law is dissolution, which means
the hospital closes. Keeping a rural hospital open is access to care. The
objection to the act is about creditors, which is not an access objection.

**SF 121 under healthcare affordability.** Letting pharmacists assess patients
and prescribe under board rules widens where care can be got, which matters
most in a state with few doctors per county. The mainstream objection is a
scope-of-practice dispute between professions, not an argument that access
should be narrower.

**SF 95 under government efficiency.** The act is a service-delivery change:
outside firms may give driving tests and handle vehicle services. The
privatisation objection is answered inside the act itself, which requires
background checks, bonding, monitoring and audits, so the direction under this
area is not seriously contested.

## What was dropped, and why

Two measures were dropped after a full read because their direction reads two
ways of comparable weight.

**HB 145, electric vehicle taxes.** Its title, "Removing triple taxation for
resident EV drivers", reads as a tax cut. The act cuts the annual all-electric
decal fee from $200 to $100, but it also imposes a new tax of 3.5 cents per
kilowatt hour on electricity sold at direct current fast charging stations and
a new $50 annual decal on plug-in hybrids, while exempting alternative fuel
sales from sales tax. Under cost of living reduction some drivers pay less and
some pay more, and the two halves are of comparable weight.

**HB 318, voter list maintenance.** The act widens the Secretary of State's
data matching to look for evidence of non-citizenship in driver's license
records, to catch a Wyoming voter who later got a license in another state, and
to use the federal SAVE program to check citizenship. It requires the
transportation department to send a monthly list of everyone who indicated
noncitizen status, and it lets officials investigate by any lawful means when
there is evidence an elector has moved away. The existing notice and twenty-day
process is untouched. Under election integrity this is accurate rolls; under
civil rights it is the risk that a list match cancels an eligible voter. This
campaign's standing rule sends questions about who may vote to civil rights,
and there a citizenship check does not have a clear direction either.
**Raised for the operator**, alongside SF 44 from batch 01. Later settled and imported in batch 04.

**Thirty-four other measures were dropped without a full read**, and the
worklist gives a reason for each. Nineteen fail filter 3: the subject is
something like a rodeo license plate, resort hotel liquor licenses, trademark
cancellation procedure or special purpose depository institution rules, which a
voter would not recognise as a policy choice. Fifteen fail filter 5: a
manufacturing tax exemption, the coal severance tax rate, an alternative
teaching certificate, legislative review of agency rules, school district cash
reserve limits, a wind and solar hearing deadline, and several repeals of
budget accounts, all of which read two ways or fit no research area.

For those thirty-four, the reason to drop does not turn on the act's details,
so they were judged from the act's own title and the Legislative Service
Office's catch title and subject line. Where the direction question was
genuinely close, the act or its summary was read: that is how HB 145, HB 318,
HB 199, HB 94 and HB 45 were decided.
