# Wyoming batch 02: how each measure was judged

Eight measures and twelve roll calls. Five of the twelve are veto overrides,
which are the votes that actually made those acts law.

Sources, reading method and the writing rules are the same as batch 01. See
`../JUDGING.md`. Every enrolled act was read top to bottom with
`../wy_text.py`, so Wyoming's struck and underlined text is labelled rather
than silently flattened.

## Why five of these are override votes

HB 36, HB 64 and SF 132 were vetoed by the governor and became law when both
chambers voted to override. For those bills the override is the vote on the
text that became law, so it is the one filter 4 selects. The descriptions say
so plainly: they name the override, give the tally, and note that it cleared
the two thirds the constitution requires.

The House's override caption was being thrown away until this batch. The feed
renders it as `HB0064VT001 Amendment Reconsideration Motion Passed by Roll
Call`, which is not what it is. That is fixed separately, and this batch is
the first to use those rolls.

## The Senate's recede vote on SF 34

The Senate passed SF 34, the House amended it, and the Senate first refused to
concur. It then voted 20-7 to recede, which means to accept the House version
after all. That is the Senate's decision on the text that became law, so it is
the roll this batch uses. Reading the earlier failed concurrence as the
Senate's last word would have been wrong.

## Notes on individual measures

**HB 136 is the clearest case for reading Wyoming's struck text properly.**
The enrolled act strikes `shall pay their group insurance premiums in their
entirety and shall not benefit from contributions` and adds language letting a
department contribute. An extract that dropped the strikethrough would state
the exact opposite of what the act does.

**HB 259 and SF 34 both fund the same Campbell County high school.** SF 34 sets
aside $20 million for it and reduces that dollar for dollar by whatever HB 259
gives the same project. HB 259 gives it $150.9 million, so SF 34's own $20
million for that project is offset. Both descriptions say what their own act
does and SF 34's names the offset.

**SF 160 renames "substance abuse" to "substance use disorder" throughout the
statutes.** A rename is not a change, and the description does not present it
as one. What the act actually does is open the programs to mental illness,
allow statewide referrals, and let the public defender take part.

**SF 4 is the narrowest subject in the batch.** It reaches only state park
staff. It is included because the change is real, a voter can follow it, and
public safety and crime control fits without stretching: certified officers may
now act on a felony they witnessed while working, where before they could act
outside a park only at another agency's request.

## What was dropped from this pass

**HB 199, the Steamboat Legacy Scholarship Act.** Both conference report votes
are closely divided and on the enacted text, so it clears filters 1 through 4.
It fails filter 5. The act renames and expands Wyoming's education savings
account program: it raises the award to $7,000, widens income eligibility to
250% of the federal poverty level, removes the split that reserved most awards
for one eligibility group, and adds a sibling preference. School-choice
financing has been settled in this campaign as having no honest direction, and
that settlement applies here.

**HB 94, charter school authorizations.** The Senate override, 21-10, is
closely divided and is the vote that made the act law. Under public education
quality the direction reads two ways of comparable weight: wider choice against
diverted funding and weaker accountability. Contested direction is
disqualifying.

**HB 45, removing otters from the protected animal list.** The Senate vote,
22-9, is closely divided and on the enacted text. No research area fits.
Environment and public health is about air, water, climate and community
health, and a species' protected status is none of those. The area has to fit,
not merely be reachable.

**HB 122, senior citizen service district levy renewals.** The act moves the
first renewal vote from two years after a district forms to four. That is
election timing, not a subject a voter would recognise as a policy choice.
