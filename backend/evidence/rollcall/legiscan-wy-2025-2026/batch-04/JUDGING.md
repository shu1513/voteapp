# Batch 04: the two operator questions

Batches 01 and 03 each raised one measure for the operator: SF 44 and HB 318,
both from the 2025 session. Both clear filters 1 through 4 and were dropped only
on direction. The operator asked for each to be looked at again and settled on
the best reading. Both are now imported. Each call follows how this campaign has
already judged the same kind of measure in other states, so Wyoming does not
disagree with them.

## SF 44, college sports and biological sex: civil_rights, a yes vote is against

Chapter 144. The Senate's 23-8 concurrence on the House amendments is the only
closely divided roll, and it is on the enacted text.

The act makes the University of Wyoming and each community college run college
sports by biological sex, defined by reproductive system. A birth certificate
counts only if the sex was entered at or near birth or corrected for a clerical
error. It bars a male student from a women's team or a women's slot on a mixed
team, and bars a women's team from playing a team with a known male student. In
individual sports it bars the match only when the school finds it unsafe or
unfair. A female student may play on a men's team when no women's team exists.
There is a private right to sue, with damages capped at $50,000, plus
injunctions and legal fees.

Batch 01 found two equal-treatment arguments pointing opposite ways. That is
true, but the campaign has since settled this class of measure four times, the
same way every time:

- Michigan HB 4469, K-12 athletics by birth-certificate sex, civil_rights, against.
- Arkansas, Arizona and Idaho school-sports measures, civil_rights, against, 159
  records in the local database.

The reason it holds: the act sets a rule that turns on a personal trait and
takes a group of students out of a public program they could otherwise join.
The area is about "equal rights, anti-discrimination enforcement, and fair
treatment under law", and a new exclusion by statute is movement away from
that, whatever its purpose. The fairness claim for female athletes is a real
argument for the act's worth. It is not a second direction under this area.

Wisconsin AB 100 and AB 102 were dropped "on SF0044's reasoning, pending the
same decision". This batch is that decision. They are left for a Wisconsin
follow-up and are not touched here.

## HB 318, voter list maintenance: election_integrity, a yes vote is for

Chapter 152. The House's 47-13 third reading is the only closely divided roll.
The Senate's 29-2 is not divided.

Batch 03 dropped it because two areas seemed to point opposite ways: accurate
rolls under election integrity, and the risk of a wrongful removal under civil
rights. Reading the enacted text settles it.

The act adds data sources and nothing else. The existing driver's license match
must now look for evidence of non-citizenship and for voters who later got a
license in another state, including through the national state-to-state
service. The secretary of state may use the federal SAVE program. The
transportation department sends a monthly confidential list of people who
indicated noncitizen status. When there is evidence a voter moved away,
officials may look into it by lawful means.

Every removal still runs through the unchanged 22-3-116 process: a mailed
notice of intent to cancel stating the reason, and 20 days for the voter to ask
to stay on the list. The act does not change who may vote. It checks voters
against qualifications that already exist.

That is exactly the line this campaign already drew:

- Alabama HB 233 widened the data used to flag a move and kept the wait and the
  rescue before removal. Imported as election_integrity, for.
- Montana HB 423 cut the notice that comes before removal. Dropped as two-sided,
  because it weakened the "trusted" half of the area.

HB 318 is the Alabama case. The civil rights worry is about removal without
notice, and this act keeps the notice.
