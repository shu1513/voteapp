# Montana batch-16 — how each measure was judged

Every judgment rests on the enrolled bill, plus the chapter number from
`api.legmt.gov`. All seventeen rolls agree with Montana's own vote record member
for member. Bill and title lists were generated from the worklist file.

## HB 128 — volunteer responders and dismissal

Chapter 135. New law. "An agency may not terminate the employment of an employee
who has completed the employee's probationary period because the employee has
elected to serve as a volunteer emergency services provider or joined a volunteer
emergency unit or organization, including but not limited to a municipal, rural,
or subscription fire department."

The notice duty is a real condition and is in the record: written notification to
the employer within 30 days of joining, of hire, or of the act taking effect,
depending on the case. A wrongful dismissal claim is provided. Direction **for**
`corporate_accountability`, because it places an enforceable duty on employers.

## HB 129 — volunteer responder tax subtraction

Chapter 638. Amends 15-30-2120. The marked text shows a new subsection (3)(o)
inserted: "subject to subsection (10), for each taxpayer that is a qualified
volunteer firefighter or volunteer emergency care provider, an additional
subtraction of $3,000".

The qualifying conditions are inserted alongside and are in the record: active
unpaid membership of the same unit for the entire calendar year, and completion
of the required training, "a minimum of 30 hours of training as specified in
19-17-108(3)" for firefighters or the hours the medical examiners board sets for
care providers. The enacted title provides an annual inflation increase.

Direction **for** `personal_income_tax_reduction`. See PLAN.md for why this does
not go where the session's business tax breaks went.

## HB 499 — grow your own grants

Chapter 774. Postsecondary institutions become eligible; the requirement to earn
postsecondary credits while in high school is revised; the commissioner of higher
education no longer has to convert a grant to a loan in certain circumstances;
and the programme's termination date is extended. The record says the end date is
pushed back rather than removed, because the act extends rather than repeals it —
the distinction that SB 333 in batch-15 turned on.

Review fix (2026-09-05): the first description said colleges and universities may
"now receive grants under it, not just school districts", which kept districts as
independent recipients. The enrolled text strikes "eligible school districts" from
20-4-601(3)(a) and replaces it with "eligible postsecondary institutions, in
partnership with at least one eligible school district"; (2)(d) is rewritten the
same way. Both chamber descriptions now say programme grants go to colleges and
universities that partner with at least one eligible school district, instead of
to the districts themselves. 85 records rewritten in place.

## HB 573 — transformational learning

Chapter 462. Moves the existing programme's termination up by one year and
establishes a phase two grant programme, with administration duties for the
superintendent of public instruction and the Board of Public Education. Both
halves are in the record, because a reader told only about the new programme
would not know the old one ends sooner.

## HB 586 — child restraints

Chapter 692. Amends 61-9-419, 61-9-420 and 61-9-421. The definitions section is
retitled from "'Properly restrained' defined" to "Definitions", and definitions
of a child safety restraint system and a booster seat are added. The requirement
is restated around age thresholds, and exemptions for certain vehicles and
circumstances are provided.

Direction **for** `public_safety_and_crime_control`. This is a vehicle safety
standard, not a sentencing provision, so the caution recorded in batch-11 — that
the area is not an axis where harsher is automatically "for" — does not bite.

## HB 625 — child abuse reporting and county money

Chapter 327. Amends 15-1-121 and 41-3-210. Two inserted passages carry the act.
A new subsection provides that if a county attorney fails to report the
information required by 41-3-210(3), the attorney general must notify the county
and then report the failure. And in the entitlement share statute: "If the
department of revenue receives notice from the attorney general that a county has
failed to comply with the reporting requirements of 41-3-210(3), a payment
required pursuant to this section must be reduced by 2%."

The record states the 2 percent and explains what an entitlement share is.
Direction **for** `social_programs_and_welfare`.

Review fix (2026-09-05): the first description made the 2 percent cut sound
automatic after any missed report. New 41-3-210(5) requires the attorney general
to notify the county attorney and county commissioners within 10 days of the
missed deadline, and the cut is reported only if the county attorney does not
provide the information within 30 days of that notice; 41-3-210(6)(b) is the
yearly August 15 report to the revenue department naming counties that had not
complied by July 1. Both chamber descriptions now carry the notice and 30-day
cure step. 86 records rewritten in place.

## HB 655 — religious belief in adoption and fostering

Chapter 336. New law. Section 2(1) bars the state from taking discriminatory
action against a provider on the basis that it "has provided or declines to
provide an adoption or foster care service or related service based on or in a
manner consistent with the person's sincerely held religious belief", or
maintains policies on that basis. Section 2(2)(a) extends the same protection to
someone granted or seeking custody of a foster or adoptive child, over how their
religious belief shapes their parenting.

Section 2(2)(b) is a genuine carve-out and is in the record: the state may still
consider whether a person shares a child's religion in order to prioritise
placement with someone of the same faith. Section 3 provides remedies and a
limitation period.

Direction **against** `civil_rights`, following HB 690 in batch-09.

## HB 669 — schooling in inpatient treatment

Chapter 706. Amends 20-7-419 and 20-7-435. Clarifies the tuition calculation
under 20-7-420 for eligible children receiving in-state inpatient treatment of
serious emotional disturbances, and clarifies the superintendent's rulemaking
authority. The superintendent contracts with the qualifying facility to provide
the educational programme. Direction **for** `public_education_quality`.

## HB 694 — placement preferences

Chapter 340. Amends 41-3-101 and 41-3-450. The placement preference order is
retained: extended family including fictive kin, then the child's community, then
a family of similar heritage. A new subsection (4) provides: "Except as provided
in subsection (4)(b), placement outside of the United States is disfavored under
this section", with children under the federal or Montana Indian Child Welfare
Act excepted.

The record states the exception, because without it the rule would read as
absolute. Direction **against** `civil_rights`.

## HB 808 — brownfields

Chapter 475. Creates the Montana Brownfields Revitalization Act with definitions,
a Montana brownfields fund, viability and eligibility rules, rulemaking
authority, a transfer of general funds and a statutory appropriation. It
eliminates the Montana Petroleum Brownfields Revitalization Act, so the programme
reaches contamination beyond petroleum. Direction **for**
`environment_and_public_health`.

## HB 855 — wildlife crossings

Chapter 540. Creates a big game and wildlife highway crossings and
accommodations account, authorises the department of fish, wildlife and parks to
establish a generic specialty licence plate, and provides a statutory
appropriation passing money to the department of transportation for qualifying
projects.

The donation figures are quoted from the act: "a donation of $20 to the
department upon initial issuance of the license plates and a donation of $20 for
each annual renewal". Direction **for** `environment_and_public_health`.

## HB 869 — cardiac arrest in youth sport

Chapter 742. Amends 20-9-236 and 20-9-502 and adds new sections. The
superintendent of public instruction must develop resources; coaches must be
trained and must hold informational meetings; a coach, trainer or official may
remove a youth athlete showing warning signs, and that athlete must be cleared by
a medical professional before returning to play. Buying an automatic external
defibrillator is confirmed as an allowable school major maintenance expenditure,
and a reimbursement grant programme with an appropriation is created.

Direction **for** `environment_and_public_health`.

## The drop

**HB 352** creates a guidelines review committee for the child support
guidelines. It changes who reviews the guidelines, not the guidelines. Reasoning
in `../survey/filter-5-drops.md`.
