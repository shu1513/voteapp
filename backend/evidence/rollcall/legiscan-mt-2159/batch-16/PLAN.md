## Two bills carried over from batch-11

**HB 808** and **HB 855** were deliberately left unbatched in batch-11 because
they had only been skimmed. They are read in full here. Both are real policy
changes, and both are `environment_and_public_health` "for".

## Two bills judged "against" civil rights, and why

**HB 655** bars the state from acting against an adoption or foster care provider
because it gave, or refused to give, a service consistent with a sincerely held
religious belief, and from acting against a foster or adoptive parent over how
religious belief shapes their parenting. A person may sue the state or raise the
act as a defence.

**HB 694** adds that placing a child outside the United States is disfavoured,
with an exception for children covered by the federal or Montana Indian Child
Welfare Act.

Both follow HB 690 in batch-09, which was judged `civil_rights` "against" on the
same footing: an act that narrows the protection available to one group inside a
child welfare system.

## HB 129 sits in a different area from the session's other tax breaks

Batches 10 and 15 judged four business tax breaks — HB 908, SB 326, SB 333 and
SB 534 — as `government_spending_reduction` "against". HB 129 is an individual
income tax subtraction, so it goes to `personal_income_tax_reduction` "for". The
distinction is who receives the break, and it is the reason the areas exist
separately.

## Every roll was checked against Montana's own vote record

All seventeen imported rolls, and every other floor roll on the thirteen measures
read, were compared member by member against
`api.legmt.gov/bills/v1/votes/findByBillId`. **All agree exactly.**

## One drop

**HB 352** establishes a guidelines review committee for the Montana child
support guidelines and sets out its duties. It changes who reviews the
guidelines, not what they say.
