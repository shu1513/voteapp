# Montana batch-12 — how each measure was judged

Montana publishes no neutral prose summary of a bill, so the enrolled text is
both the ground truth and the only source. Every judgment rests on the enrolled
bill, plus the session law chapter number read from `api.legmt.gov`. Every roll
was compared member by member against Montana's own vote record; all seventeen
agree exactly.

Bill numbers and titles for this batch were generated from
`../survey/divided-enacted-worklist.tsv`, not retyped — the rule adopted after
two hand-written briefs went wrong in batch-11.

## HB 245 — the HELP Act

Chapter 35. The title says "revising the workforce development provisions and the
termination date". The termination date half is the substance.

Section 2 repeals section 28 of Chapter 368, Laws of 2015; sections 38 and 48 of
Chapter 415, Laws of 2019; section 17 of Chapter 456, Laws of 2019; and sections
3 and 4 of Chapter 318, Laws of 2021. Those are the end dates carried by the HELP
Act and its later extensions. The act writes no new end date, so the programme
continues with no expiry.

The description states the effect in those terms — "it was written to end on a
set date, and the act repeals those end dates, so the programme has no expiry" —
rather than asserting a figure for how many people are covered, which the act
does not contain.

The workforce half runs the other way. In 39-71-103 the duty to "contract with
one or more private nonprofit or for-profit entities to provide workforce
development services" emphasising "high-demand occupations, particularly in the
health care field and in short-term certification programs for entry-level
cybersecurity analysts" is struck, and replaced with a duty to contact each
participant subject to the community engagement requirements and help them
complete an assessment. Both halves are in the record.

Direction **for** `healthcare_affordability`.

## HB 78 — pet insurance

Chapter 40. Adopts the pet insurance model act. The consumer content was read
rather than assumed: mandatory disclosure of pre-existing condition exclusions,
waiting periods, any provision limiting coverage, the basis or formula for
payment, and the benefit schedule; a warning before purchase where a veterinary
examination is required, including that the examination record may result in a
pre-existing condition exclusion; and a 15-day right to return the policy with
the full premium refunded within 30 days. Direction **for**
`corporate_accountability`.

## HB 207 — candidate filing

Chapter 643. Three changes, all confirmed in the enrolled text.

Filing deadlines for local government office move: 13-1-403(1) strikes "145" for
"105" days and "85" for "90" days, and the write-in declaration deadline moves
from the 65th to the 90th day. A candidate for local or state office must file
before the primary.

A sore-loser rule is added in four places: "An unsuccessful candidate for office
at a primary election may not seek nomination by write-in" at the general.

The signature requirement **falls**. The enacted title itself shows "INCREASING"
struck and "DECREASING" inserted. In 13-10-502(2), "5%" is struck and "4%"
inserted for statewide and federal offices, and a new subsection (2)(b) sets 4%
for legislative and other races.

Direction **for** `election_integrity`: an orderly single filing window, and a
lower petition bar for independent and minor party candidates.

## HB 393 — town meeting recordings

Chapter 271. Amends 2-3-214. The marked text shows the operative addition in
subsection (7)(b): "the governing board of a third-class city" gains "or a town
with a population greater than 300". A new subsection (2)(c) requires those
boards to publish the audio recording within 14 business days with a link on the
board's website, and (2)(d) extends the social-media fallback to them.

Note the asymmetry, which the record states: these boards owe an **audio**
recording, where the larger boards listed in (1) owe audio and video. Direction
**for** `anti_corruption`.

## HB 399 — prior authorization

Chapter 121. Amends 33-32-221 and adds subsections (1)(e), (1)(f) and (1)(g): no
prior authorization for a formulary oral or inhaled non-biologic generic that is
not a Medicare Part D specialty tier drug or a controlled substance; none for a
formulary non-specialty inhaled drug for asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary
disease or chronic lung disease; none for formulary insulin for a patient
diagnosed with diabetes. An adverse determination must be made by a physician
whose specialty covers the condition, and the insurer must supply a list of
reasonable therapeutic alternatives.

The description says "most generic pills and inhalers" rather than "all generic
drugs", because the exclusions for specialty-tier and controlled substances are
real. Direction **for** `healthcare_affordability`.

## HB 476 — newborn safety devices

Chapter 304. New law: the health department awards competitive grants to fire
departments, hospitals and law enforcement agencies for installing and
maintaining newborn safety devices, capped at $20,000 per applicant, funded by a
$160,000 general fund appropriation for the biennium beginning 1 July 2025, with
a report due by 30 September 2027.

**The act terminates on 30 June 2027**, and the record says so, because a reader
should not be told a programme is permanent when it is not. Direction **for**
`social_programs_and_welfare`.

## HB 531 — judicial review of legislative rules

Chapter 318. New law, codified into Title 5, chapter 5. The legislature
"possesses the sole power and discretion to make, enforce, interpret, suspend,
repeal, or waive rules for its proceedings", and "A judicial officer may not
review and interpret a legislative rule unless the rule conflicts with the
Montana constitution or the United States constitution."

Direction **against** `anti_corruption`. The reasoning is the mirror of SB 48 in
batch-11: SB 48 opened the legislature's scrutiny of judges to the public and is
"for"; this act closes the courts' scrutiny of the legislature and is "against".
The constitutional exception is stated in the record so the limit is visible.

## HB 543 — violence against health care employees

Chapter 487. A one-line repealer: section 4, Chapter 516, Laws of 2023 is
repealed. That section was the sunset on the 2023 requirement that health care
employers record and disclose violence against staff. Removing it makes the duty
permanent and changes nothing else. Direction **for**
`corporate_accountability`.

## HB 601 — Medicaid community assister portal

Chapter 465. New law. By 1 May 2026 the department must run an online portal
letting a community assister — "a person or organization who helps individuals
apply for health coverage" — check application status, view notices sent to the
applicant, report an eligibility change, see outstanding actions, and update
contact details. Submitting applications and uploading verification documents are
listed as things the portal **may** allow, not must, and the record does not
promise them. Direction **for** `healthcare_affordability`.

## HB 770 — campaign finance reporting

Chapter 722. Amends 13-37-226. Subsection (1) gains "even if the candidate has no
contributions or expenditures to report". A new subsection (10) provides that
failure to comply "will result in a candidate's name not appearing on the
official ballot for an election as provided in 13-37-126".

The deadlines themselves are unchanged, and the record says so, so a reader does
not infer a new filing burden beyond the nil report. Direction **for**
`anti_corruption`.

## HB 804 — lobbying threshold

Chapter 535. Amends 5-7-112 and 5-7-208. Three changes, and they do not all point
the same way.

The payment threshold is struck at $2,150 and set at $3,000, with the year struck
at 2004 and set at 2025. The inflation adjustment after each general election is
kept. In 5-7-208 a principal must file "electronic reports" rather than "a
report". And in both (2)(b) and (3)(b) the fixed "$5,000" monthly trigger is
struck and replaced by "the amount specified under 5-7-112", which is $3,000 — a
**lower** trigger, so more months must be reported.

Judged **for** `anti_corruption`: electronic filing and the lower monthly trigger
are concrete disclosure gains, and the threshold rise is the inflation catch-up
the statute already contemplated, the previous figure having been set for 2004.
The record states all three changes so a reader can weigh them.

## HB 825 — trusted health decisionmaker

Chapter 539. Amends 50-5-110, 50-5-1301 and 50-5-1303 and adds a new section. A
hospital patient may, at any time after admission and before a provider finds
they lack decisional capacity, name a trusted decisionmaker by telling a provider
"orally or otherwise".

**The ranking is the part a description could easily get wrong.** The statutory
order is: a guardian appointed by the patient with medical decisionmaking
authority; an agent under a medical durable power of attorney or an advance
directive; spouse; adult child; parent; sibling; **then** the trusted
decisionmaker designated under the new section; then a close friend; then any
other proxy. So the new option ranks below the patient's relatives, not above
them, and the record says exactly that. Direction **for** `civil_rights`.

## The three drops

Set out in `../survey/filter-5-drops.md`. HB 167, HB 348 and HB 562 each pull in
two directions at once — a removed public posting that is also a privacy gain, a
higher bar for convening the legislature that is also fewer costly polls, and an
omnibus repeal of agency reports that is both less paperwork and less public
reporting. None has a defensible single direction.
