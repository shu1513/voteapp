# Montana batch-14 — housing, zoning and property

Eight measures, twelve roll calls, 579 candidate records. All eight became law.

This is the most single-minded batch of the campaign: seven of the eight land in
`housing_affordability`, all in the same direction, because the 2025 session
worked through a connected set of building and zoning rules.

## Conditions matter more than headlines here

Two of these are easy to describe wrongly by leaving out the limits.

**SB 213** does not simply permit single-staircase blocks. It requires four
conditions together: no more than six storeys above ground, no more than four
flats on a floor, an automatic sprinkler system, and a window or other emergency
exit in every flat. The record lists all four, because "one staircase instead of
two" without them would read as a straightforward safety reduction.

**SB 532** does not merely allow accessory dwelling units. It bars counties from
five specific requirements: matching exterior design, roof pitch or materials;
owner occupancy of either home; a family, marital or employment relationship
between the occupants; impact fees on the unit; and street improvements beyond
repairing damage the building work caused. The bans are the substance.

**SB 532 also carries a sunset**: subsection 1(7) and section 2 terminate on
30 September 2029.

## The one that is not about housing

**HB 416** requires a homeowners' association to get permission before its agent
enters a member's land, to propose a convenient time, to say which part of the
land it needs, and to accept the owner's demand to be present. It is judged
**for** `civil_rights` as a property owner's control over entry, not as a housing
cost measure, and the record notes that the ordinary public right to use a front
path or door is untouched.

## Two measures dropped after a full read

**SB 101** creates the offences of unlawful squatting and fraudulent sale or
lease, with a removal process and indemnity for police acting in good faith. It
is dropped under filter 5 for the same reason the batch-11 crime bills were: it
protects a property owner and restricts an occupant's process at once, and the
new-offence direction is not one `public_safety_and_crime_control` can settle.

**HB 233** provides for transferring title to open space in a recorded final plat
to a homeowners' association, with a public hearing and retroactive
applicability. It is land-title mechanics with no position a voter would hold.
