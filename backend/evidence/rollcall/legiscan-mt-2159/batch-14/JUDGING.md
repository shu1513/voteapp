# Montana batch-14 — how each measure was judged

Every judgment rests on the enrolled bill, plus the session law chapter number
read from `api.legmt.gov`. All twelve rolls agree with Montana's own vote record
member for member. Bill and title lists were generated from the worklist file.

## Why seven bills share one area and one direction

`housing_affordability` already carries three Montana records in the "for"
direction: HB 492 capping parking requirements, HB 311 on rental application
fees, and HB 810 on rent payment fees, all from batches 09 and 10. The rule those
established is that an act which removes a barrier to building or occupying
housing is "for" the area. Seven of the eight measures here do exactly that, from
different directions — fees, zoning interpretation, building code, tax timing and
minimum unit rules.

That they cluster is a fact about the 2025 session, not a judgment shortcut. Each
was read separately and each could have failed filter 5 on its own.

## SB 133 — impact fees

Chapter 556. Amends 7-6-1601 and 7-6-1602. Two changes. The allowance letting a
local government add "a fee for the administration of the impact fee not to
exceed 5% of the total impact fee collected" is removed. And an impact fee may
not increase by "more than the rate of inflation as determined by the producer
price index by commodity, all commodities".

The record says separately that charges for plan review and inspection are not
affected, because those sit in a different subsection and a reader could
otherwise think all development charges were capped.

## SB 172 — resort tax

Chapter 71. Amends 7-6-1503, 7-6-1504, 7-6-1541 and 7-6-1542. A resort community
may levy an additional resort tax of up to 1%. The revenue could be used only for
infrastructure; "or workforce housing" is added. The requirement that specific
uses be identified in the petition or resolution put to voters survives, and the
record says so.

## SB 213 — single stairway

Chapter 368. Adds subsection (10) to 50-60-203. The department "shall permit
buildings of group R-2 occupancy to have a single stairway serving as an exit for
all units if the building meets the following conditions", and all four
conditions are quoted in the record: six storeys above grade plane at most, four
dwelling units per floor at most, an automatic sprinkler system, and "each unit
has at least one window or other emergency exit provision".

Group R-2 is the residential occupancy class covering apartment buildings. The
description says "flats" rather than repeating the code class.

## SB 214 — nonconforming use

Chapter 369. Amends seven zoning sections. The operative sentence appears four
times across the city and county zoning parts: "Any ambiguity or uncertainty in
the zoning regulations as to whether a nonconforming use is allowed or whether
the use was allowed when it was commenced must be interpreted in favor of the
nonconforming use."

The enacted title also removes "the exception that historic preservation boards
may review applications for zoning permits and variances", and the record states
that too, because it is a loss of review that a reader weighing the act should
see.

## SB 275 — agricultural covenant

Chapter 213. Amends 76-3-211 by adding subsection (1)(c): a governing body may
revoke the covenant and let the division proceed without subdivision review where
"the land is annexed into a municipality".

One detail was checked and is worth recording: the public hearing and written
findings required by subsection (2) apply to revocations under **(1)(b)** only.
The new annexation route is not covered by that requirement. The record does not
claim a hearing applies. It does say sanitary restrictions under Title 76,
chapter 4 survive, which subsection (3) preserves.

## SB 337 — subdivision tax exemption

Chapter 601. New law plus an amendment to 15-10-305. Property undergoing
residential subdivision development is exempt from tax from the start of the next
property tax year once two conditions are met: the revenue department approves an
application, and within 30 days the developer pays "a property tax prepayment
equal to five times the amount of property taxes and assessments due on the
property in the most recent property tax year", payable to the county treasurer
and distributed to the same funds in the same ratio as ordinary property tax.
Subsection (3) adds "a fee of 5%" on top of the prepayment.

The exemption is capped: subsection (4)(a) grants it "for a period not to exceed
5 years". It ends sooner on each lot at the start of the tax year after "a
habitable residential structure has been constructed on the lot" (4)(b), and for
the whole subdivision on 31 December of the year at least 95% of the lots have a
habitable home (5). The record carries the 5% fee, the five-year cap and both
early-end events. (An earlier draft of this note wrongly read the "five" as only
the prepayment multiplier and the "5 year" in the short title as untrustworthy;
review against the enrolled text corrected that.)

## SB 532 — accessory dwelling units

Chapter 619. New law plus an amendment to 76-4-130. Where a county has adopted
zoning, "the regulations must allow a minimum of one accessory dwelling unit by
right on a lot or parcel that contains a single-family dwelling", attached,
detached or internal.

The five prohibitions in subsection (2) are the substance and all five are in the
record: no matching exterior design, roof pitch or finishing materials; no owner
occupancy requirement for either dwelling; no required familial, marital or
employment relationship between occupants; no impact fees on construction; and no
required public street improvements except to repair a street disturbed by the
work.

**Section 4 terminates section 1(7) and section 2 on 30 September 2029.**

## HB 416 — homeowners' association entry

Chapter 452. New law. An association "must receive permission from an owner of
real property before an agent of the homeowners' association may enter". When
seeking it, the association must try to set a convenient date and time and must
say which part of the property it wants to reach. The owner may require their own
presence or their agent's.

Subsection (3) preserves any implied licence the agent has "as a member of the
public to access the areas of real property generally open to the public, such as
sidewalks, front pathways, or the front door of a house". Two further carve-outs
narrow the bill: subsection (5) excludes "condominiums where there are common
elements that must be accessed by entering the unit and for which the declaration
of covenants or bylaws set forth the terms of accessing the unit", and subsection
(6) excludes "easements of record that specifically benefit the homeowners'
association". The record carries all three limits so a condominium owner or an
owner whose lot carries an association easement is not told they can refuse entry
the act does not let them refuse. Direction **for** `civil_rights`.

## The two drops

**SB 101** creates unlawful squatting and fraudulent sale or lease as offences,
provides a removal process, and indemnifies law enforcement acting in good faith.
It fails filter 5 under the rule set in batch-11: it strengthens a property
owner's hand and narrows an occupant's process at the same time, and the area
`public_safety_and_crime_control` cannot settle which way that cuts.

**HB 233** allows title to open space in a recorded final plat to pass to a
homeowners' association, requires a public hearing, and applies retroactively. It
is title mechanics between a developer, a local government and an association,
with no subject a reader could hold a position on.
