# Montana batch-01 — what was selected and why

Nine measures, eighteen roll calls, all from the 2025 Regular Session
(LegiScan 2159). Every one of the nine became law.

## How the nine were chosen

The campaign's five filters, applied in order to the 902 divided floor votes
the fetch stored:

1. **Divided.** The smaller side is at least a quarter of the larger side. That
   leaves 902 rolls.
2. **Became law.** 633 rolls on 373 measures.
3. **A nameable subject that maps to a research area.** Joint resolutions were
   set aside here: Montana joint resolutions do not go to the governor and are
   not law, so a description of one could not say the measure became law, and
   mixing them into a first batch would invite that mistake. That leaves 573
   rolls on 335 bills.
4. **One roll per measure per chamber, preferring the decisive vote.** In
   Montana that is third reading. Eight of the nine measures have exactly one
   third-reading roll in each chamber. House Bill 818 went to a conference
   committee, so its decisive votes are the two conference-report votes of
   April 30, and those are the ones selected.
5. **A defensible for-or-against direction.** Each of the nine carries one
   research area with a direction that follows the area's own description.

Within what survived, the nine were picked to spread across nine different
research areas, and to favour measures a Montana voter would recognise.

## Deliberately left for a later batch

The pool is large and this is a first batch, so most of it is simply unbatched
rather than rejected. `../survey/divided-enacted-worklist.tsv` carries all 633
divided-and-enacted rolls with their disposition. Two items are worth naming
now:

- **House Bill 231, the property tax rewrite.** The session's biggest measure,
  divided across five rolls. Its enrolled text runs to 136 KB and deserves its
  own batch rather than a corner of this one.
- **Measures about vaccines and about medical treatments given during the
  COVID period** — House Bill 807 and Senate Bill 218 among them. Past campaign
  practice is to put a vaccine-adjacent direction to the user rather than
  assume one, so they are held.
