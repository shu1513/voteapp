# Montana batch-17 — how each measure was judged

Every judgment rests on the enrolled bill. Chapter numbers were fetched from
`api.legmt.gov` in this session. All thirty-five rolls agree with Montana's own
vote record member for member.

## Division of labour

The 116 remaining bills were read in parallel by six agents. Their reports are
**reference**: every one was checked against the enrolled text or the API before
it changed a judgment. The agents did not choose research areas, directions or
wording, and did not touch the database or the repository.

That division caught things in both directions. The agents found problems this
session would have missed at this scale — HB 807's two-word change, SB 147's
sunset, HB 162's coordination instructions. And this session rejected agent
framings that did not survive the campaign's own rules, dropping bills the
reports called real policy changes because no area carried a defensible
direction for them.

## HB 162 — resort tax, and the bill that voided SB 172

Chapter 425. Amends 7-6-1501 by adding "workforce and community housing
projects" to the definition of infrastructure, which is what the extra 1% resort
levy must fund.

**This is the act that voids SB 172**, withdrawn from batch-14 in a separate
commit. HB 162 section 2 voids SB 172's section amending 7-6-1503 and prints a
replacement; section 3 voids SB 172's sections amending 7-6-1504, 7-6-1541 and
7-6-1542. Those are all four of SB 172's operative sections.

The record describes the merged position: the housing use, which comes from
HB 162's own definition change, and the population limit in HB 162's replacement
7-6-1503, under which a resort community over the limit may not levy the extra
tax unless it was established before 1 January 2025.

## HB 184 — deaf and hard of hearing

Chapter 237. Most of the act is terminology, and terminology alone would have
been a drop. It survives because two definitions widen. In 49-4-502 a "deaf
person" was someone whose hearing was "totally impaired or so seriously impaired
as to prohibit the person from understanding oral communications"; that is
struck for a person with "a diversity in the hearing process". That definition
governs who may have a qualified interpreter in court. And 20-8-102 changes
eligibility for the state school for the deaf and blind from children who
"cannot be successfully taught" in public schools to those whose hearing or sight
"requires specialized support". Direction **for** `civil_rights`.

## HB 215 — appointments to state boards

Chapter 114. The act works entirely by deletion. Struck in full: "As vacancies
occur and appointments are made, all appointing authorities ... shall take
positive action to attain gender balance and proportional representation of
minorities resident in Montana to the greatest extent possible." Also struck:
the governor's duty to report progress. What survives is the secretary of
state's monthly publication of appointments and vacancies. Direction **against**
`civil_rights`.

## HB 365 and SB 38 — two ways to cut off legal costs

Chapters 450 and 352. Both amend 25-10-711 and both are judged **against**
`anti_corruption`, because each removes a route by which someone who beat the
state recovers their costs.

HB 365 bars a court from weighing "the conduct of the legislative branch in
passing legislation" in a bad faith analysis, and makes that evidence
inadmissible. SB 38 provides that a person who wins an order forcing a veto
override poll, but then loses the poll, "is not considered to have prevailed".

**Both carry the same striking sentence**, and both records quote its effect:
the bar applies "even if a court finds that the defense was frivolous or pursued
in bad faith". HB 365 also applies retroactively to all legislation of the 69th
session, which the record states.

## HB 411 — open space levies

Chapter 653. One insertion carries it. 76-6-109(3) exempted certain property
from "property taxes levied to pay the principal and interest on general
obligation bonds"; the act inserts "Open space levies and property taxes pledged
to the payment of". So the exemption widens from bond repayment to all open
space levies.

The applicability clause is the part a reader needs: it "applies to all open
space levies and bonds, regardless of the approval date". It reaches levies
voters have already passed. Judged **against**
`government_spending_reduction`, on the same footing as SB 534 in batch-15.

## HB 442 — reporting doctors

Chapter 286. Two real deletions. 37-3-402, "Insurer to report to board", is
repealed, ending the duty on an insurer to tell the Board of Medical Examiners
about an allegation of professional negligence. And a sentence is struck from
27-6-605 requiring the medical legal panel to send a copy of its decision to the
provider's licensing board.

**The act's title claims more than it does.** It says the bill eliminates the
chiropractic legal panel's notification duty as well. 27-12-605's body never
carried one; the act strikes the words ", and licensing board" from a section
heading. The record describes only the two real deletions and says the
chiropractic change is to a heading. Direction **against**
`corporate_accountability`.

## HB 443 — doorstep sellers

Chapter 287. Repeals eight sections and creates one. The repealed sections held
the licence and fee, the application, the bond, the display duty, the processing
rules and the consequences of non-compliance. **One of them, 7-21-2408, gave an
aggrieved purchaser a right against the seller's bond**, and the record says so,
because that is a consumer protection lost rather than merely relocated. The new
section gives counties open-ended power to regulate and license instead.
Direction **against** `corporate_accountability`.

## HB 446 — indecent exposure

Chapter 657. The offence and all its conditions are in the record: the exposure
must be knowing or purposeful, the person must know it is likely to cause affront
or alarm, it must be in a "public place" as defined, and it must be to people
"of the opposite biological sex or opposite the person's sex observed at birth".

The definition of public place is quoted in substance because it is the reach of
the act: it includes "restrooms, locker rooms, dressing rooms, and showers that
are designated for multiperson, single-sex use", schools, parks and places of
business. Both exceptions are stated — a minor with a parent or guardian, and a
person with developmental disabilities with a carer.

The registration consequence is the quiet part and is in the record: 46-23-502 is
amended so a conviction under the new subsection (4) is a registrable sexual
offence. Direction **against** `civil_rights`.

## HB 521 — suing a victim

Chapter 315. New law. The record carries the conditions, because without them the
rule reads far wider than it is: the person must have been **convicted**; the
injury must have happened during the commission of that crime; it must have been
caused by a **victim**; and that victim must have used no force or force that was
justifiable under Title 45, chapter 3, part 1. Direction **for**
`public_safety_and_crime_control`. This is a civil liability rule protecting
victims, not a sentencing provision, so the batch-11 caution does not bite.

## HB 710 — subpoenas for child exploitation cases

Chapter 341. New law. A justice or district judge may issue a subpoena on the
application of an Internet Crimes Against Children task force officer. The
conditions are in the record: the investigation must concern the sexual
exploitation of a minor, the officer must swear a written statement, and the
judge must find "the administration of justice requires it". The record also says
what may **not** be demanded — the ten listed items are account and billing
details, and the contents of communications are not among them. Direction **for**
`public_safety_and_crime_control`.

## HB 791 — public nuisance

Chapter 531. The most consequential act in the batch. It repeals nine sections of
the nuisance chapter, rewrites the definition, and abrogates the common law of
nuisance where inconsistent.

The record carries three things. The definition narrows to conditions "arising
out of the use of real property" plus obstruction of public ways and navigable
water. A closed list of things that can never be a public nuisance includes "the
design, manufacturing, distributing, selling, labeling, or marketing of a
product", and names firearms, firearm accessories and ammunition. And the
remedies change: a government entity may recover abatement resources proved by
clear and convincing evidence but **no damages of any kind**, while a private
person needs a "special injury" different in kind from the public's, with class
actions barred.

The four-year carve-out for the Department of Justice's opioid litigation is in
the record too, because it is the one thing the act does not reach. Direction
**against** `corporate_accountability`.

## HB 819 — flags

Chapter 731. New law, no existing statute touched. The record lists the permitted
categories rather than summarising them, because the act works as a closed list.
It also states the separate bar on flags representing "a political viewpoint,
including but not limited to flags or banners regarding a political party, race,
sexual orientation, gender, or political ideology", and the exception for staff
clothing and jewellery outside a dress code. Direction **against**
`civil_rights`.

## HB 888 — local health boards

Chapter 746. Despite its title, **nothing about isolation or quarantine
changes** — those provisions are untouched. The entire operative change is one
new sentence in 50-2-116: "A local board of health may not require a person to
receive any type of vaccination, medication, supplement, or other type of drug
treatment."

The record says no exception is written in for an outbreak, a school, a health
worker or a declared emergency, because the absence is the substance. Direction
**against** `environment_and_public_health`.

## HB 891 and SB 30 — two additions to `anti_corruption` "for"

Chapter 747 and chapter 351. HB 891 gives the health department's inspector
general power to subpoena records relevant to an investigation of waste, fraud or
abuse, signed by both the department director and the inspector general. The
record says plainly that no judge reviews it, which is the difference between
this power and HB 710's.

SB 30 bars a judge from using the rule of necessity to stay on a conflicted case
"because of a conflict of interest in which other judicial officers with less
immediate or less significant conflicts are available".

## SB 97 — venue

Chapter 357. Someone challenging a law passed in the current two-year period must
now bring the case in a county inside the home district of the bill's primary
sponsor, and a court must move a case filed elsewhere. A sponsor who intervenes
can also trigger a move. Direction **against** `anti_corruption`: it moves
challenges to state law out of a neutral forum and into one chosen by the
identity of the sponsor.

## SB 147 — the deferral resolved

Chapter 588. Its whole operative content is "Section 55, Chapter 716, Laws of
2023, is repealed", and the enrolled text never says what that section did. That
is why it was deferred in batch-13 rather than guessed at.

It is now settled from Montana's own statute book rather than from inference.
SB 248 and SB 249, both 2025 acts, reproduce statutes carrying the note
"(Terminates June 30, 2025--sec. 55, Ch. 716, L. 2023.)". Section 55 is the
sunset on the Montana Indian Child Welfare Act. Repealing it means that Act does
not expire. Direction **for** `civil_rights`.

## HB 807 — the held bill, resolved as housekeeping

HB 807 was held as a question for the user. It needs no decision. The act strikes
subsection (4) of 49-2-312 and reinserts it with two words changed: "any vaccine"
becomes "a vaccine", twice. The prohibition on requiring an emergency-use
authorisation vaccine was enacted in 2021 and its scope is unchanged. This is
exactly the struck-block-nearly-identical-to-its-replacement shape the campaign
has been watching for. Dropped as housekeeping.
