## Why these seven belong together

The 2025 session ran a sustained argument about who runs elections and who
watches the courts. Grouping the measures makes the pattern legible instead of
scattering it across unrelated batches.

HB 39 is the one scored against. It repeals Montana's outright ban on political
parties giving money to candidates for judge. Judges still appear on a
nonpartisan ballot, so the effect is party money in races that carry no party
label. The Senate passed it 25-23, the narrowest margin in the Montana campaign
so far.

HB 711 and SB 41 pull the other way on the same theme. HB 711 bars the Supreme
Court from picking anyone who gave to a major party candidate in the past ten
years as the tiebreaking chair of the redistricting commission. SB 41 requires a
replacement district judge to be drawn at random, which closes off judge
shopping. HB 711 scores for `election_integrity` and SB 41 for
`anti_corruption`. Both were first tagged `impartiality`; review caught that
`impartiality` is a judicial-candidate area the product hides from every
legislative view, and the tags were rewritten. See JUDGING.md.

## Three measures read and dropped

- **SB 242** is administrative housekeeping about ballot printing. No direction
  to defend.
- **SB 490** changes late voter registration, but only for federal elections. Its
  meaning depends on whether the baseline is the statute or the deadline
  litigation has actually produced, and the enrolled text does not settle that.
- **SB 45** creates a judicial performance evaluation commission. Eight of its
  eleven seats belong to elected politicians, which is a real pull on judicial
  independence, but the commission gets no power over judges at all and issues no
  retention verdict. Both readings are honest.

Dropping three of ten is the tail behaving as expected. Batches 1 through 3
dropped two of twenty because the clearest measures were worked first.

## Reach

The House rolls carry 73 to 76 records each and the Senate rolls carry 11 each.
All 100 House seats are on the 2026 ballot; only about half the Senate is,
because Senate terms are staggered over four years.
