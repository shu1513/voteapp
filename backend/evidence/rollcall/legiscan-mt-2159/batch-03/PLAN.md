# Montana batch-03 — the greenhouse gas and water quality pair

Two measures, four roll calls. Both became law. Deliberately small: both needed
the enrolled PDF rendered page by page, and one of them turned out to point the
opposite way from its own title.

| Measure | Chapter | Area | Yes vote means | House | Senate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SB 221 greenhouse gas assessment under MEPA | 348 | environment_and_public_health | **for** | 63-35 | 37-13 |
| HB 664 repeal of numeric nutrient standards | 338 | environment_and_public_health | against | 69-30 | 34-16 |

Each measure has exactly one third-reading roll per chamber, and each went
straight to enrolling with no return for amendments, so both chambers voted the
text that became law.

## Why SB 221 scores *for*

Its title says it "provides for a greenhouse gas assessment", and its preamble
says the legislature "strongly disagrees" with the *Held* decision — which reads
as a bill against environmental review. The operative text is the opposite. The
statute had **banned** agencies from evaluating greenhouse gases; SB 221 repeals
that ban, permits assessments, and requires one for any fossil fuel activity.
See JUDGING.md for how that was established, because the extracted text could
not settle it.

Two environment measures pointing opposite ways in one batch is deliberate, and
it is the same pattern Maine set with LD 556 against and LD 1868 for.
