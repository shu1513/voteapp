## SB 535 and the superseded-stage gate

The importer refused SB 535's House roll on the first attempt, and it was right
to ask.

The House voted twice on SB 535 on 25 April 2025. It **failed 50-50** on roll
1558163, and **concurred 49-48** on roll 1558165. Montana's own action trail
settles the order: "(H) 3rd Reading Failed", then two "(H) Scheduled for 3rd
Reading" entries, then "(H) 3rd Reading Concurred", then "(H) Returned to Senate
with Amendments". The Senate then concurred in the House amendments and passed
the bill on 29 April.

So the concurrence is the decisive vote and the failed roll is superseded by the
reconsideration. Roll 1558163 is listed in `acknowledge_later_rolls` with that
reasoning rather than the gate being worked around silently. **This is the only
roll in the campaign to use that key.**

## The vote check, again

All twenty-nine imported rolls agree with Montana's own record member for member.
Three rolls on these bills do not, and none was selected: SB 194's roll 1482095
(45-5, not divided), and SB 218's rolls 1542137 and 1559215, both superseded.

## SB 218 was on the held list

SB 218 had been carried as a question for the user alongside HB 807. It needs no
new policy call. It sits on precedent this campaign has already set four times —
HB 690 in batch-09, HB 655 and HB 694 in batch-16, HB 446 in batch-17 — where an
act narrowing what one group may do or receive inside a health or family system
is judged `civil_rights` "against".

Its conditions are in the record, because they are the act: the patient must have
been a minor, the treatment must be on the listed surgical, hormone or blocker
lists, and the claim needs proof that the clinician fell below the standard of
care, established by expert evidence. Care for a child born with a medically
verifiable disorder of sex development is excluded.
