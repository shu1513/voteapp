# Montana batch-15 — how each measure was judged

Every judgment rests on the enrolled bill, plus the chapter number from
`api.legmt.gov`. All ten rolls agree with Montana's own vote record member for
member. Bill and title lists were generated from the worklist file.

## SB 333 — read the act, not the worklist title

The worklist title says "Repeal sunset on coal washing credit". The enrolled act
is "AN ACT EXTENDING THE SUNSET DATE OF THE COAL SEVERANCE TAX COAL WASHING
CREDIT", and it works by amending two earlier session laws. In both, the
termination date is struck and reset:

> "Section 7. Termination. The amendment in [section 3(5)] defining 'coal
> washing' ... terminate July 1, ~~2017~~ ~~2025~~ **2027**."

So the break was already extended once, from 2017 to 2025, and this act extends
it again to 2027. Nothing in how the credit is calculated changes. The record
says "two more years" and does not say "repealed".

Chapter 599. Direction **against** `government_spending_reduction`. Section 2
requires the Secretary of State to send a copy to every federally recognised
tribal government, which the record includes.

## SB 326 — film tax credits

Chapter 598. Revises the Montana Economic Development Industry Advancement Act
credits: eligible productions and post-production activity are widened, the
credits are extended through 2045, a larger credit is provided for hiring
veterans and enrolled tribal members, the credit is allocated to certain
entities, and unused allocated credits may be claimed by others for a fee. All of
that is in the record. Direction **against**
`government_spending_reduction`, on the same footing as HB 908 in batch-10.

## SB 534 — communication property

Chapter 620. Amends 15-6-135, 15-6-156, 15-6-219 and 15-6-243. Fibre optic and
coaxial cable placed in service on or after 1 July 2021, and wireless
infrastructure placed in service on or after this act's effective date, are
"exempt from taxation for a period of 5 years starting from the date" they were
placed in service.

**The reinvestment condition is not optional and the record states it**: the
owner "shall reinvest the tax savings from the exemption by installing and
placing in service new fiber optic or coaxial cable or wireless infrastructure in
Montana within 2 years from the date the owner first" claims it, and must keep
records the department can inspect. Direction **against**
`government_spending_reduction`.

## SB 104 — facility finance authority

Chapter 158. Amends 90-5-101, 90-7-102, 90-7-104, 90-7-225, 90-7-229 and
90-7-302. Raises the Montana Facility Finance Authority's bond issuance cap to
$1.5 billion, adds a biennial inflation adjustment to that cap, and widens
eligibility to 501(c)(3) entities and other public-benefit facilities. Direction
**for** `public_infrastructure`.

## HB 650 — broadband bonding

Chapter 334. Amends 90-5-101 by adding broadband services to the definition of a
"project" eligible for state bonding. That is the whole act, and the record says
so rather than implying a new programme. The description explains what broadband
is, because the term is used without definition in the statute. Direction **for**
`public_infrastructure`. It has a delayed effective date.

## HB 665 — nonprofit officer liability

Chapter 468. Amends 15-30-2503 by adding subsection (7). Each officer or director
of a small nonprofit corporation "is exempt from the provisions of this section
imposing individual liability" for tax withheld from employees' wages and not
paid over.

Two exceptions bring the liability back, and both are in the record: the officer
receives monetary compensation or wages for their services, where reasonable
expense reimbursement does not count; or the corporation "is out of compliance
with the provisions this section for more than 12 months with outstanding unpaid
taxes, penalties, and interest".

Direction **against** `corporate_accountability`: personal responsibility for
money withheld from employees is removed, subject to those limits.

## SB 511 — litigation financing

Chapter 413. Two halves. It amends the existing Litigation Financing Transparency
and Consumer Protection Act at 31-4-102, 31-4-104, 31-4-108, 31-4-118 and
31-4-120, widening the definitions of consumer and legal claim and adding
restrictions on what a financer, a service provider and a party's own lawyers may
do. It then creates the Foreign Investment in Litigation Financing Act at
sections 6 through 11.

Section 10 bars a foreign person from litigation financing, from affiliating with
a litigation financer, and from financing or lending money to one. Section 9 bars
a party from disclosing proprietary information to a foreign person, a foreign
adversary or a foreign person of concern. "Foreign adversary" is tied to the
federal designation at 15 CFR 791.4.

Judged **for** `corporate_accountability`: registration, disclosure duties and
conduct rules on an industry that previously operated without them. The
description says "a government named as an enemy of the United States" rather
than reproducing the regulatory citation, because the citation would mean nothing
to a reader.

## The three drops and the one deferral

**SB 246** lets a governmental body buy Montana-produced food by direct purchase
rather than standard procurement where quality is substantially equivalent, a
vendor can supply enough, and the bid does not unreasonably exceed the lowest
outside bid. Supporting local producers and bypassing competitive bidding are the
same provision, so no area carries a defensible direction.

**SB 247** moves shooting ranges into class four property and revises their tax
rate. A single facility type's tax classification.

**SB 265** creates the Financial Freedom and Innovation Act. It bars governing
authorities from using central bank digital currency, permits digital assets and
blockchain protocols, and imposes certification requirements on issuers of
network tokens. The certification requirements point toward
`corporate_accountability` "for"; the central bank digital currency prohibition
is a position on federal monetary policy with no area at all. The act cannot be
given one direction.

**SB 117 is deferred.** Its own title both increases the inflation limitation
used in calculating property tax levies and brings a portion of newly taxable
property inside the mill levy limitation. Those move local government revenue in
opposite directions, and the net effect is a question about levy arithmetic, not
about the title. It stays `unbatched` for a proper read.
