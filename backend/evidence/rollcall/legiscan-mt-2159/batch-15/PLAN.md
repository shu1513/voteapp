## The worklist title on SB 333 was wrong, and the enrolled text said so

The survey worklist carries SB 333 as **"Repeal sunset on coal washing credit"**.
The enrolled act is titled **"AN ACT EXTENDING THE SUNSET DATE"**, and its two
sections move the termination date from 1 July 2025 to 1 July 2027. The break is
not made permanent; it gets two more years.

The description says two more years. This is the second time in three batches
that a short title would have produced a wrong record — SB 337 in batch-14 was
the other — and it is why the enrolled text is read every time rather than the
title.

## Three tax breaks, judged the same way

SB 326, SB 333 and SB 534 all continue or create a tax break, and all three are
judged **against** `government_spending_reduction`. That follows HB 908 in
batch-10, which widened the job growth credit and deleted its sunset and was
judged the same way. Whatever one thinks of the policy, a vote to extend a credit
is a vote against reducing what the state forgoes.

**SB 534 carries a condition worth stating**: the owner must reinvest the tax
saving in new cable or wireless equipment in Montana within two years of first
claiming the exemption, and must keep records proving it. The record says so, so
the exemption does not read as unconditional.

## Every roll was checked against Montana's own vote record

All ten imported rolls, and every other floor roll on the eleven measures read,
were compared member by member against
`api.legmt.gov/bills/v1/votes/findByBillId`. **All agree exactly.**

## What was dropped, and what was deferred

Dropped after a full read, reasoning in `../survey/filter-5-drops.md`: **SB 246**
(direct purchase of Montana-produced food), **SB 247** (shooting ranges moved to
class four property), **SB 265** (the Financial Freedom and Innovation Act).

**SB 117 was deferred, not dropped.** It revises the property tax levy limits on
local government, and its own title both "increases the rate of inflation
limitation" and brings a portion of newly taxable property inside the mill levy
cap. Those pull in opposite directions on how much a local government may
collect, and working out the net effect needs a careful read of the levy
arithmetic rather than a judgment from the title.
