# Montana batch-04 — the environment rewrites, plus six others

Eight measures, sixteen roll calls, 682 candidate records. All eight became
law. Two of them, HB 285 and HB 685, are the big environment bills that
batch-03 deliberately held back because each needed the enrolled PDF rendered
page by page.

Each measure contributes one roll per chamber, and in every case the roll
chosen is that chamber's last kept floor vote on the text that became law.
Three measures needed care about which roll that was:

- **HB 285** — the House passed its own version 57-42 in February, then backed
  the Senate's version 63-34 in April. The April roll is the one used.
- **HB 682** — both chambers voted twice. The free conference committee report
  adopted on April 30 is the text that became law, so both April 30 rolls are
  used.
- **HB 759** — the House passed its own version 56-43 in March, then backed the
  Senate's version 59-39 in April. The April roll is the one used.

## Why HB 759 scores *for* while the other seven score *against*

HB 759 closes a way of giving more than the personal limit. A person could set
up several companies and give through each one. The law now counts such a gift
against the individual member's own limit, and bars gifts from companies taxed
as corporations. That is a tightening of the money rules, so a yes vote counts
for `anti_corruption`. The direction follows the text, not the party pattern:
the Senate passed it 40-10, the widest margin in this batch.

## HB 231 is held, and the reason is new

The property tax rewrite, HB 231, was planned for this batch and has been
pulled out. Section 27, section 29 and section 31 of HB 231 each say that if
Senate Bill 542 and HB 231 are both passed and approved, then sections 1
through 23, 25, 30, 33 and 34 of HB 231 are void, and SB 542 is amended to
carry that text instead. Montana's own bill records show the governor signed
both bills on May 13, 2025, so those instructions fired. Almost nothing
substantive survives in HB 231 under its own number.

The policy did become law — it just became law as SB 542. SB 542 is itself an
unbatched divided-and-enacted bill, so HB 231 and SB 542 will be judged
together in a later batch, once SB 542's own text and the transplanted sections
have been read side by side. Writing "House Bill 231 … and it became law" today
would have described provisions that are void.

This is the first coordination-instruction trap in the Montana campaign. It is
recorded in `../CODE-FINDINGS.md` as a judging hazard, not a code defect.

## Reach

The House rolls carry 73 to 76 records each and the Senate rolls carry 11 each.
That gap is structural: all 100 Montana House seats are on the 2026 ballot,
while only about half the Senate is, because Senate terms are staggered over
four years.
