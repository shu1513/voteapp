# Missouri batch-02 — the two parked direction calls

**3 roll calls / 2 measures / 212 records across 117 candidates.**

Batch-01 dropped HB 495 and SB 4 under filter 5 and flagged both as direction calls, on the Ohio
batch-04 / Florida HB 1205 precedent. Both were re-examined against the enacted text and resolved
here — one takes strand labels, one is recorded with no stance at all.

## HB 495 — labelled per strand, with the marquee provision left unlabelled

The reason batch-01 hesitated still stands: the Act's biggest change — a governor-appointed board
taking control of the St. Louis police department, with a city funding floor rising to 25 % of
general revenue — maps to **no research area**, and `public_safety_and_crime_control` runs both ways
inside it (a street-takeover offence, organized-retail-theft felonies and a supervised-drug-use ban
sit beside the repeal of the rioting offence and of the Missouri Incarceration Reimbursement Act).

What changed is the reading of the *other* strands. Two of them are discrete and one-directional, and
the per-strand pattern (Florida SB 700) is exactly the tool for that:

- **`civil_rights`/for** — wrongful-conviction compensation freed from the DNA-only limit and raised
  to $179 a day up to $65,000 a year; a 12-cent-per-minute cap on jail and prison phone calls; limits
  on restraining pregnant and postpartum inmates; racial-profiling, implicit-bias and de-escalation
  training for new officers; the Missing and Murdered African American Women and Girls Task Force.
- **`immigration`/against** — § 43.505 requires every law-enforcement agency in the state to report
  an arrested person's citizenship or immigration status. Under the area description ("welcome
  immigration through a lawful, orderly, and humane system") enforcement-tightening scores against.

The description leads with the St. Louis takeover and the funding floor, states them factually, and
attaches no stance to them. `public_safety_and_crime_control` and `environment_and_public_health`
(the supervised-drug-use ban) are deliberately **not** labelled: each runs both ways here, and the
overdose-prevention question is one where the evidence bases are contested.

## SB 4 — recorded with no stance

`cost_of_living_reduction` is the area this measure is actually about, and it runs both ways: costs
move onto bills earlier (construction work in progress for new gas plants in rate base, future-test-
year ratemaking, PISA sunsets extended) while other provisions push the other way (the revenue-
requirement impact cap cut from 2.5 % to 2.25 %, CWIP recoveries refundable **with interest** if
imprudent, a one-time authority to pass a federal corporate-tax cut through to rates — with the
savings deferred and credited in the next rate case either way — and large-load tariffs so
data-centre customers carry their own share).

Clean side strands do exist — the tightened Fair Competition Law, which keeps utilities themselves
out of HVAC sales while their affiliates and contractors continue under separation and open-
qualification rules (`corporate_accountability`/for), and the hot- and cold-weather shutoff bans plus a utility-burden
discount (`social_programs_and_welfare`/for) — but labelling only those would mislead by omission on
the ratemaking fight that is the whole point of the bill.

So SB 4 follows the **Ohio H.B. 116 precedent**: a consequential divided vote worth recording, with
no honest direction, is imported under `general` with **no stance**. One direction flattens it; no
record at all wastes it. This is a deliberate, documented exception to batch-01's filter 5, not a
change to the rule.

Note for anyone re-reading the popular coverage: the "CWIP for gas and water" framing is half wrong.
Construction work in progress is authorised for **electric** corporations building natural-gas
generating units; gas, water and sewer utilities get a **future test year** instead.
