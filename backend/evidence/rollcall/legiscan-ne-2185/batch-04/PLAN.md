# Nebraska batch-04: the last two measures, read in full

Batches 01 to 03 marked two measures `candidate:later`. Both are long acts with
several subjects, and each needed the whole text read before a direction could
be defended. This batch is that read. **One measure was kept and one was
dropped**, and the pool is now closed.

12 records across 12 candidates.

## The one that was dropped

**LB 530**, motor vehicle homicide and road safety. Eight separate bills were
folded into it, and they pull in opposite directions inside one policy area:

- Penalties go **up**. Tampering with an electronic monitoring device becomes a
  new Class I misdemeanor offense. Motor vehicle homicide of an unborn child
  rises from a Class IIIA to a Class IIA felony, and a second grade rises from
  IIA to II. A drug penalty enhancement is extended to violations that cause
  death or serious bodily injury. A sealed juvenile record now counts toward the
  firearm prohibition. Driving bans for motor vehicle homicide also go up: a
  drunk- or drugged-driving homicide moves from a mandatory one-to-fifteen-year
  ban to a flat fifteen years (§ 3, new 28-306(4)(b)), and the misdemeanor and
  reckless-driving grades, which carried no driving ban before, now let the
  court impose one of up to two years (new 28-306(4)(a)).
- Penalties go **down**. Probation fees become waivable for an offender already
  found indigent or facing undue hardship. A court may extend probation on the
  offender's own joint application rather than revoke it.
- Juvenile detention moves both ways at once. New grounds to detain are added,
  while the age below which a child may never be detained falls from twelve to
  ten and a new five-day review is required for children of twelve and under.

Filter 5 asks for a defensible for-or-against direction. There is none here, so
the measure is dropped. This is the same rule that dropped LB 921 in batch-02,
and it is the second time a Nebraska omnibus act failed it.

## Why LB 415 survives the same test

LB 415 also carries three subjects, but only two of them have a direction and
both point the same way:

- It narrows the paid sick leave law Nebraska voters adopted by initiative in
  2024 — the headline change, and the reason the vote was close.
- It lets the state take unemployment benefit overpayments out of a person's
  gambling winnings, alongside the tax debts it already collects that way. That
  also runs against the person receiving benefits.
- It rewrites the licensing rules for elevator mechanics and contractors, which
  is administrative and carries no direction at all.

Nothing in the act cuts the other way, so one label holds for the whole measure.

## The folded bills

Nebraska records what was merged into an act only after the Governor signs, so
these lines are dated 2025-06-06 even though the amendments were adopted weeks
earlier. Reading them is what settled both measures.

- Into LB 415: LB 402 (the gambling winnings setoff), LB 435 (the Conveyance
  Safety Act), LB 698 (the paid sick leave changes).
- Into LB 530: LB 6, LB 24, LB 124, LB 395, LB 404, LB 556, LB 600 and LB 684.
