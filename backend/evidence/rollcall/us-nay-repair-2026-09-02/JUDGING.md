# Federal nay-stance repair — the reasoning per measure

80 labels on 78 approved federal rolls had no `nay` key. Each needed one decision: does a
no vote on this measure evidence the opposite stance on this research area?

## The rule

Connecticut's test (#960), stated explicitly:

> Author a nay stance only where the act is **single-subject**, its **whole operative
> content is the area's own mechanism**, and the **mainstream objection is to that
> mechanism** rather than to cost, constitutional structure, or a rider.

Direction always follows the **area description**, never the bill's framing. Result:
30 `against`, 2 `for`, 48 `null`.

## Where a nay stance was authored

**gun_control — all 5 `against`.** The area description literally names the mechanisms
these bills use: "background checks, licensing, and safe-storage requirements". H.R. 8 and
H.R. 1446 are background checks; H.R. 7910 raises the purchase age and tightens storage;
H.R. 1808 bans a weapon class; H.R. 2377 is federal red-flag orders. The due-process
objection to red-flag orders is an objection to that mechanism, not a separate axis.

**womens_reproductive_rights — all 5 `against`.** Each is a single-subject access measure
(H.R. 3755 and H.R. 8296 the Women's Health Protection Act, H.R. 8297 interstate travel,
H.R. 8373 contraception, H.R. 239 veterans' contraception copays) and the area is "protect
legal access to reproductive healthcare and individual bodily autonomy".

**immigration — 5 `against`.** H.R. 6, H.R. 1603, H.R. 1573, H.R. 1333 and H.R. 7946 are
all wholly immigration measures pointing at "a lawful, orderly, and humane system". The
"amnesty" objection is the opposing position on the area, not a different axis.

**civil_rights — 6 of 16 `against`.** Kept: H.R. 5 (protected categories), H.R. 8404
(marriage), H.R. 4 (Voting Rights Act preclearance), H.R. 7 (pay discrimination),
H.R. 2062 (age discrimination standard), H.R. 2116 (CROWN Act) — each single-subject
anti-discrimination law.

**corporate_accountability — 6 of 8 `against`.** H.R. 2547, H.R. 2668, H.R. 7688, H.R. 963,
H.R. 1187 and H.R. 3843 are all consumer-protection or enforcement measures binding
companies, which is the area in its own words.

**environment_and_public_health — 1 `against`, 2 `for`.** S.J.Res. 14 restores methane
limits, single subject. H.J.Res. 104 and H.J.Res. 131 are the mirror image: their yes vote
already scores `against` because each reopens federal land to fossil leasing, so a no vote
is `for`. There is no plausible non-environmental objection to either.

**One each:** H.R. 2988 (whistleblower protection, `anti_corruption`) and H.R. 256
(repealing the 2002 Iraq force authorisation, `peaceful_foreign_policy` — the area is
"prioritize diplomacy and de-escalation instead of war", and repeal is exactly that).

## Where the nay side was left null, and why

**Anything whose main content is money.** All 7 `healthcare_affordability` measures, all 6
`public_safety_and_crime_control` measures, both NDAAs, the infrastructure act, and 3 of 4
`social_programs_and_welfare` measures are funding or authorisation bills. A no vote there
reads as fiscal, which is the Maryland HB 1424 precedent.

**Anything where the objection sits on a different axis.** H.J.Res. 17 (whether Congress
may revive an expired ratification deadline — a legal question), H.R. 2662 (limits on the
President's removal power — a constitutional question), H.R. 4445 and S. 4524 (limits on
arbitration and non-disclosure agreements — contract law), H.R. 1065 and H.R. 3110
(workplace accommodation mandates — employer burden), H.R. 1443 and H.R. 4176 (data
collection — reporting burden and privacy), H.R. 803 (wilderness designation — land use),
H.R. 2467 (PFAS liability for passive receivers).

**Anything multi-subject.** H.R. 5376 (the Inflation Reduction Act, on both its labels),
H.R. 1280 (policing omnibus, where qualified immunity was the actual fight), H.R. 1620
(VAWA, with its firearms and tribal-jurisdiction strands), H.R. 5314, H.R. 2543,
H.R. 1319, H.R. 7606.

**Where the area contains both directions.** H.R. 7688's price-gouging ban is labelled
`cost_of_living_reduction` as well, and "price controls raise costs" is an argument inside
that area, so that label is null while its `corporate_accountability` label is `against`.
H.R. 1629's orphan-drug limits trade lower prices against fewer rare-disease drugs, both
inside `healthcare_affordability`.

**Resolutions cancelling a rule, except the two land ones.** S.J.Res. 13, S.J.Res. 15 and
H.J.Res. 88 (the SAVE student-loan plan) all carry a serious "the agency exceeded its
statutory authority" argument, which was the stated rationale and the litigated question.
The two land resolutions carry no such alternative, which is why they are the exception.

**Consistency check that changed an answer.** S.J.Res. 49 ends the tariff emergency, and
`cost_of_living_reduction` literally says "reduce or eliminate tariffs", which argued for
`against`. But its three sibling resolutions — S.J.Res. 37, 81 and 88 — were already
decided as null in the August repair. Splitting the family would have made two members'
records disagree about the same question, so S.J.Res. 49 follows its siblings.
