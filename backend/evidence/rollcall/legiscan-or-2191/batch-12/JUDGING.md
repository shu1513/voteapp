## Why HB 2010 was kept when 47 budget bills were excluded

The standing rule excludes appropriations, because there is no honest
direction on a vote to fund the government. HB 2010 is not an appropriation.
It extends two **standing assessments** — two percent on health insurance
premiums and a matching charge on managed care plans — for six more years, and
the money funds the Oregon Reinsurance Program, which lowers individual-market
premiums by covering part of the most expensive claims. That is a policy
choice about health costs with a direction, not a spending line, so it is
judged rather than excluded.

## HB 3025 repeals a formula

The old law split the cost of education three ways, between the student's
family, the federal government and the state. That whole clause is bracketed
out, and the commission now sets each award on enrolment level and an analysis
of need. Reading only the new language would have missed that a formula was
removed, which is the change members actually voted on.

## SB 141 is a target-setting law, not a funding law

It runs to 423 lines of new text, most of it definitions. The operative core
is a chain: the State Board of Education sets statewide targets, the
department sets targets for groups of similar districts and growth targets for
each district, and districts taking state school money measure against them,
overall and by student group. Two metrics are added — eighth-grade math
proficiency and regular attendance in the early grades.

## Two drops made at the end

- **HB 2453** strips the school equity committee of its duty to advise the
  board, but also lets the superintendent act on its recommendations without
  board approval. The two pull opposite ways.
- **SB 163** adopts the Uniform Parentage Act and rewrites parentage
  presumptions, assisted reproduction, surrogacy, child support, adoption and
  intestate succession together — a technical omnibus with no single
  direction.
