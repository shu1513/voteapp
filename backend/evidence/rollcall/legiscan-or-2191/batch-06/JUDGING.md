## The campaign's first Oregon measures scoring `against`

Every Oregon measure judged before this batch scored `for`, which is the
trifecta shape: the divided-and-enacted set is the majority's agenda. These
two run the other way, and both narrow a protection rather than widening one.

- **HB 3550** removes minor league baseball players from Oregon's minimum
  wage, overtime and meal break laws where a union contract covers their pay
  and conditions. Taking a group out of wage law is `corporate_accountability`
  scored **against**.
- **SB 1173** stops a hospital, clinic or medical practice counting as the
  maker, seller or distributor of a product it hands a patient, so a product
  liability claim cannot reach it — unless it had a hand in designing or
  making the item, or sells it to the public in a retail setting. A shield
  from liability is scored **against**.

**HB 3550 is the only measure in the campaign so far with an authored nay of
`for`.** A no vote is a vote to leave those wage protections in place, which
is the area's own mechanism with no other plausible reading. SB 1173 takes
null instead: a no vote there can rest on health care costs and insurance
rather than on corporate accountability.

## Scope kept

- **SB 1191** carves the act of informing a person of their rights out of
  **three** separate crimes — obstructing government or court business,
  refusing to help a peace officer, and interfering with an officer — not one.
  All three are named.
- **HB 2081** is a duty to analyse, report and aim, not to divest. It binds
  the investment council and treasurer **subject to their existing duties to
  the fund and its contracts**, and the description says so rather than
  implying the fund must sell its fossil fuel holdings.
- **SB 867's** remedy is conditional: the board may order a school to provide
  what a student missed, or repay the family, only where the discrimination
  actually cost the student a class, activity or service.

## A drop made in this batch

**HB 3194** (landowner liability for farmworker camps, and minimum damages
raised to $2,000) was read in full and dropped. It is farm labour housing
enforcement, and no research area covers labour or worker-protection
questions — the same gap that dropped HB 2688 and HB 2944, and that the
California campaign recorded as a tooling gap. Filing it under
`environment_and_public_health` would put it under a slug that misdescribes
why members voted.
