**`public_infrastructure` is new to Oregon in this batch**, and three measures
land there.

## SB 476 carries an authored nay

The area description for `immigration` names "priority for skilled workers,
researchers, and other high-impact contributors". SB 476 is squarely that: it
makes every state licensing board train the staff who handle internationally
educated applicants, and creates a starter licence route for doctors trained
abroad. A no vote is a position on whether foreign credentials should open
Oregon practice, which is the area's own axis, so the nay is authored
`against` rather than left null.

## HB 3410 is a companion, and its condition is stated

HB 3410 amends SB 951 (batch-04) before that Act had taken effect. It widens
the ban on owning a medical practice to reach **contractors** of a management
company, not just its shareholders, directors, officers and staff, and makes
it bite even where a person would otherwise qualify for an exemption. The Act
is written to operate **only if SB 951 becomes law**, which it did, and the
description says so rather than implying a free-standing rule.

## Scope kept

- **HB 2931** does not decide the bridge. It updates the project's legal
  description, allows agreements with Washington, restricts Oregon's toll
  share to constitutional road purposes, and requires tolls to cover the bonds.
- **HB 3336** binds only companies selling more than two million megawatt
  hours a year, and requires an analysis and a plan, not construction.
- **HB 3069** consolidates existing grants — Justice Reinvestment from 2013
  plus a new treatment court program — rather than creating new money.
