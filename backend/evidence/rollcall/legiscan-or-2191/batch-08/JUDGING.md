HB 3646's 31-27 House vote is the closest margin in the Oregon campaign so far.

## Limits carried

- **HB 3646.** The preference is not open-ended. It applies only where the
  employee-owned firm's price is **no more than five percent above** other
  bidders, though an agency may raise that margin by order, and the bidder
  must file proof of the ownership with its bid. "Employee-owned" is defined:
  workers hold at least half the entity, directly or through a plan the IRS
  recognises as qualified.
- **HB 3912.** The size rule is specific — the field of the doctorate must
  appear at **at least a quarter** the size and legibility of the largest print
  used — and "material" is a defined list, not a general word. Both are stated.
- **HB 3148** gives a **one-time** benefit of **up to $100**. Saying the plan
  now provides computers would overstate a capped, single payment.
- **SB 982** does not reach another state's school for deaf or blind students,
  and the description says so.
- **HB 3179** does not cap or roll back rates. It changes what the regulator
  must weigh, and adds a filing duty that bites only when the company seeks a
  residential increase **and** its profit rate is up for review. Describing it
  as limiting bills would be wrong.

## Labels

All six score `for` with `nay: null`. The objections run on different axes:
procurement neutrality (HB 3646), professional speech (HB 3912), local school
control (HB 2684), program cost (HB 3148), regulatory burden and investor
return (HB 3179), and district discretion (SB 982).

**HB 3646 is the campaign's first Oregon `reduce_wealth_gap` label.** The area
is about expanding asset building and economic mobility, which is what
employee ownership does; filing it under `corporate_accountability` would have
misdescribed a procurement preference as an accountability rule.
