# Oregon batch-01 — selection

Six measures, twelve roll calls, both chambers on every measure.

## How these six were chosen

The five campaign filters, in order:

1. **Divided.** The losing side is at least a quarter of the winning side.
2. **Enacted.** The measure became law. Oregon's Governor vetoed seven bills
   this session and every veto was sustained, so nothing here rode an
   override.
3. **A nameable subject.** Each measure has one subject a voter can recognise
   from its own text.
4. **One roll per measure per chamber**, and it must be that chamber's final
   action on the text that became law. In Oregon that means preferring a
   `Third Reading in Concurrence` roll over the earlier `Third Reading` when
   the other chamber amended the bill: SB 243, SB 605 and SB 430 each have an
   earlier divided Senate roll on a superseded draft, and in all three the
   later concurrence vote was taken instead.
5. **A defensible for-or-against direction.** Every measure here carries one
   research area whose direction follows from the area's own description.

All six score `for`. Oregon was a Democratic trifecta in 2025, so the
divided-and-enacted set is the majority's agenda, and the directions are the
mirror image of the Republican-trifecta states — SB 599 is `immigration`/for
by exactly the rule that made Texas SB 8 `immigration`/against.

## Version check

Every one of the twelve rolls was checked against the bill's own history and
its version stack, and **all twelve are on the text that became law**. No
measure needed a per-chamber version split.

- SB 243: Senate passed B-Engrossed, the House amended to C-Engrossed and
  passed it, the Senate concurred. The enrolled Act is SB 243-C, so the
  chosen House and concurrence rolls are both on it.
- SB 605 and SB 430: same shape, enrolled as SB 605-B and SB 430-B.
- SB 599: enrolled as SB 599-A; the House passed the A-Engrossed bill with no
  amendments of its own.
- SB 1098: enrolled as **SB 1098-INTRO**. The only amendments filed were a
  Senate minority report, and the motion to substitute it failed, so both
  chambers voted the introduced text.
- HB 3187: enrolled as HB 3187-A; the Senate's recommendation was "Do pass
  the A-Eng. bill", so both chambers voted the same text.

## Records

12 rolls, **322 records across 61 candidates**, 262 area tags. 61 is every
candidate the crosswalk maps — Oregon's Speaker votes, so there is no
equivalent of the Texas Burrows or Georgia Burns gap.

House rolls carry about five times the reach of Senate rolls here (51 matched
candidates against 10), because all 60 House seats are on the Nov-2026 ballot
while Oregon staggers its Senate and only 15 of 30 districts are up.
