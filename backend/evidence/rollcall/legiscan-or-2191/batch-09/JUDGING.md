## Why SB 426 was kept where three other labour measures were dropped

HB 2688, HB 2944 and HB 3194 were all dropped because no research area covers
labour or union questions. SB 426 is about unpaid construction wages, which
looks like the same family, and the difference is worth stating.

`corporate_accountability` needs a **company-specific target** — the rule the
California campaign set when it moved a blight bill off that slug because the
bill bound all property owners rather than companies. SB 426 binds direct
contractors, a defined class of firms, and makes them answer for a
subcontractor's failure to pay. That is a duty on companies for their
compliance, so the slug fits. HB 3194's landowner liability did not have that
shape, and HB 2688's prevailing wage question and HB 2944's collective
bargaining penalties are not accountability rules at all.

## Scope kept

- **HB 3825** is narrower than "clears marijuana convictions". It reaches only
  the **money** owed on a conviction entered in a **municipal or justice
  court**, for possessing **less than an ounce** of marijuana, **before
  July 1, 2015**, and only where the debt had not already lapsed. What it does
  beyond ending the debt is treat the sentence as served for that money, which
  is what unblocks a later set-aside — the description says so, because
  "wipes out debts" alone would miss the point of the bill.
- **HB 2677** shifts who files, not what qualifies: the county juvenile
  department applies instead of the young person. The conditions are named
  (18 years old, out of juvenile court supervision, no disqualifying adult
  conviction), as is the victim-notice duty that attaches to records from
  before 2024.
- **SB 537** lets employers flag a patient's electronic health record as a
  possible safety risk, which is a real and contestable part of the Act. It is
  described rather than left out.

## Labels

All four score `for` with `nay: null`. Objections run on different axes:
compliance cost and patient privacy (SB 537), finality of criminal judgments
(HB 3825), contractor exposure for another firm's failure (SB 426), and victim
interests in keeping records available (HB 2677).
