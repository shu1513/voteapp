# Utah 2026 batch-07 — how each measure was judged

Sources: the Office of Legislative Research and General Counsel's summary as an
index, and the enrolled text, read through `ut_text.py` with added and deleted
text marked, as ground truth. Each act was also checked against the other 2026
acts that amend the same sections; the four overlaps found are recorded under
the measure they touch, and none changes what is described.

Nine of the ten rolls are Senate rolls: on these measures the House vote was
lopsided and only the Senate divided.

## SB 60 Income Tax Rate Amendments

Cuts the corporate franchise and income tax rate and the individual income tax
rate from 4.5 to 4.45 percent, with retrospective operation to tax years
beginning January 1, 2026. `personal_income_tax_reduction` / for.

## HB 222 Limitation of Actions Amendments

Before this act, a person residing or doing business in Utah could not be held
liable for climate damage from greenhouse gas emissions unless it was proved by
clear and convincing evidence that the person broke an enforceable in-state
emissions limit or the express terms of a permit. The act drops the residency
condition, extends the bar to criminal liability and to "any judicial remedy
under any principle of law or equity", applies it "regardless of the cause of
action or type of relief sought", and replaces the list of gases (removing
water vapor, ozone and chlorofluorocarbons, adding sulfur hexafluoride and the
fluorinated gases). `environment_and_public_health` / against.

## HB 84 Dangerous Weapon Amendments

Removes institutions of higher education from the places where an adult 21 or
older without a concealed carry permit may not carry, so such an adult may now
carry concealed on a campus. Open carry on a campus is barred for everyone, and
permit holders may not carry openly at a K-12 school or a campus, in each case
unless lawfully responding to an active threat. The campus offense in
76-11-205.5 becomes "openly carrying a dangerous weapon at an institution of
higher education", a class C misdemeanor, where carrying a firearm there had
been a class A misdemeanor. The dominant change is the expansion of permitless
concealed carry to campuses. `gun_control` / against.

## SB 234 Rulemaking Amendments

For a rule on drinking water, water pollution, hazardous substances,
contaminated sites, air quality, or solid or hazardous waste, an agency may not
begin rulemaking on a numeric limit for a regulated material that is more
stringent than a federal standard on the same topic. Where no federal standard
exists, the limit must rest on the "best available science" and the "weight of
scientific evidence", and a health rule must show a direct causal link between
exposure at the limit and "manifest bodily harm" in people: a present,
diagnosable disease or injury, not the mere presence of a substance in the body
and not an increased risk alone. Federally required, site-specific,
less-stringent and emergency rules are exempt. SB 148 also amends 63G-3-102 in
2026 but leaves this act's definitions untouched.
`environment_and_public_health` / against.

## HB 214 Firearms Liability Amendments

Replaces the old standard (no qualified civil liability action unless the
injury came from gross negligence, recklessness or intentional misconduct) with
a bar on any claim against a manufacturer, seller or trade association unless
it fits a listed exception and pleads with particularity the defendant's mental
state and proximate cause. The knowing-violation exception now covers only the
named federal firearms and export statutes and their state equivalents, and only
where the violation is the "sole proximate cause". A defendant who wins a motion
to dismiss recovers fees and may appeal a denial immediately; political
subdivisions may not impose their own liability; "qualified product" now
includes firearm accessories. SB 18's only change to 53-5d-102 is a
cross-reference. `gun_control` / against.

## HB 471 Social Services Amendments

For adults in the Medicaid expansion group (19 to 64, not pregnant, not on
Medicare), the department must implement the federal work requirements of
42 U.S.C. 1396a(xx), may not enroll an applicant who cannot show compliance for
the month before applying, may not rely on self-attestation alone, and must
verify compliance again before each redetermination, which for this group comes
every six months. From October 1, 2026, and unless federal law requires
otherwise, medical assistance goes only to a "qualified citizen" (a citizen or
national, a lawful permanent resident, a Cuban or Haitian entrant, a Compact of
Free Association resident, or a lawfully present child), and the department
must cross-check against SAVE, the death master file, address changes,
out-of-state benefit use and, from 2029, the national multi-state enrollment
database. `healthcare_affordability` / against: the area is access to
affordable care, and every strand narrows who gets it or keeps it.

## HB 357 Amendments to Motor Vehicle Data Privacy

Applies the Utah Consumer Privacy Act to motor vehicle manufacturers. For model
year 2030 and later, a manufacturer must provide clearly labeled in-vehicle
privacy controls, unless it shows a model is not technologically capable, and
may offer but not require an app or website. Data needed for vehicle safety and
operation is exempt from the consent requirement. The Motor Vehicle Division
must post owners' rights. The description says "model year 2030 and later"
because an earlier draft said "a carmaker must" without the date, which would
have overstated the act. SB 38's 2026 amendment to 13-61-101 changes nothing in
the lines this act relies on. `data_privacy` / for.

## HB 76 Data Center Water Transparency Amendments

A "large data center" withdraws 75 acre-feet or more a year and occupies at
least 10,000 square feet. Before approving a new one, a city or county land use
authority must notify the water suppliers; from July 1, 2026 the operator of a
new large data center reports to the Division of Water Rights before
construction and annually after operation begins (withdrawals, conservation
efforts, pollution protection), with some information protected as proprietary;
the state engineer enforces. `environment_and_public_health` / for.

**The same-day guard.** The Senate first voted on HB 76 at 10:13 AM on March 5
and it failed 13-15 (roll 1654375). The Senate reconsidered at 11:19 and passed
it 19-5 at 11:22 (roll 1654376), the text the House then concurred in. Utah's
action list carries these times and LegiScan does not, so the judgment names
roll 1654375 in `acknowledge_later_rolls`.

## HB 60 Water Rights Amendments

The state engineer, judging whether a plan is detrimental to the public
welfare, may consider only its effect on the beneficial use of water or on the
quantity, quality or availability of water, and other factors a statute
directs; may not reject on public-welfare grounds a detriment better handled by
another agency, not directly tied to those interests, or negligible; considers a
protest only to the extent it addresses a lawful basis for approval or
rejection; and no longer weighs interference "with the more beneficial use of
the water". Only a person with a particularized injury is "aggrieved" for
judicial review. HB 125 also amends 73-3-8 in 2026, but only its royalty and
mineral lease clause and a cross-reference. `environment_and_public_health` /
against.

## Dropped from the batch-07 shortlist

- **HB 314 Firearm Purchase** — the strands point different ways: it drops the
  state consent form a buyer signed, but lengthens the time the state keeps an
  approved background check from 20 to 30 days.
- **HB 437 Environmental Permitting** — an expedited review option by certified
  outside reviewers, with the agency still making the technical review and the
  decision and public comment preserved; a process change with no direction.
- **HB 329 State Employee Maternity and Leave** — raises paid postpartum leave
  for state employees from three to six weeks. No area fits: the social
  programs area is the safety net, and there is no area for public employment.

## Why every no side is untagged

The no side on each mixes objections that are not the opposite of the area:
revenue on SB 60, drafting and federal preemption on HB 222 and SB 234, venue
and campus policy on HB 84, and administrative cost on the rest.
