# Utah 2026 batch-10 — the direction calls, judged

Same brief, sources and precedents as `legiscan-ut-2137/batch-10/JUDGING.md`.

## Imported

- **HB 174 Sex Characteristic Change Treatment**, House 53-15 (concurrence) and
  Senate 20-6: `civil_rights` / against. From 1/28/2027 providers may not give
  hormonal transgender treatment to a minor who was under 16 on the effective
  date or not already on cross-sex hormones. Treatment to stop is allowed.
- **HB 258 Insurance Coverage Amendments**, House 53-16 and Senate 17-9:
  `healthcare_affordability` / for. A plan that covers transition hormones or
  surgery must cover their reversal, for plans from 2027. It is a coverage
  mandate with no counter-strand in the area.
- **HB 404 Sex-Designated Housing Amendments**, House 50-20 and Senate 19-7
  (third reading; the 2/19 roll is second reading): `civil_rights` / against.
  It creates a fair-housing exemption for single-sex shared housing by sex at
  birth. Government housing is excluded.
- **HB 209 Voting Amendments**, House 51-16 (concurrence) and Senate 17-8. It
  has two labels, because its strands sit in different areas:
  - `election_integrity` / for: election officers review the rolls for
    non-citizens, with notice and 30 days to dispute.
  - `civil_rights` / against: a voter without documentary proof of citizenship
    may vote only the federal ballot. This is the standing "who may vote goes
    to civil_rights" rule.
  - The split ballot is confirmed in the current 20A-3a-201.5, effective
    5/6/2026.
- **SB 295 Intellectual Diversity in Education and Government**, House 48-16:
  `civil_rights` / against.
  - It extends the DEI-ban framework to school clubs. A curricular club may not
    center its purpose on a personal identity characteristic, and no club may
    limit membership by one.
  - Its college debate and event duties and its guest-lecturer exception do not
    offset that in the same area. They are named in the description.
- **HB 259 Parental Access to Children's Medical Records**, House 56-14
  (concurrence) and Senate 22-6: `data_privacy` / against. It forces facilities
  to open a minor's records to parents, with daily and per-record fines. The
  parental-rights argument sits on a different axis.
- **HB 204 Higher Education Student Belief Accommodation**, Senate 17-5:
  `civil_rights` / for. It requires religious and conscience accommodation in
  required courses, with a neutral arbiter and a bar on compelled speech.
- **SB 174 Exercise of Religious Beliefs and Conscience**, Senate 22-7:
  `womens_reproductive_rights` / against.
  - Providers may refuse any service against conscience, and the definition
    names contraception, abortion and gender transition.
  - It keeps the EMTALA carve-out and adds a posted notice and a state
    directory. Access to reproductive care is the clear strand.
  - The provider-freedom and patient-access strands of civil_rights cancel out.
- **HB 136 Unlicensed Driver Amendments**, Senate 20-6 (conference final
  passage): `public_safety_and_crime_control` / for. It requires impound for an
  unverified driver, adds a quick fingerprint, makes a repeat offense a class B
  misdemeanor, and raises the impound fee from $425 to $600.
- **SB 153 Election Amendments**, Senate 22-7 (concurrence):
  `election_integrity` / for. The strand is removal of dead voters within five
  business days of SSA or vital-records data. The privacy strands run both ways
  (most private records become public; list use is restricted with new crimes),
  so `data_privacy` gets no label.

## Dropped

- **HB 118**, no area: CDL applicants sign an English-ability acknowledgment
  and test failures are tracked. It is narrow licensing.
- **SB 268**, mixed. A shield for students' religious expression in assignments
  sits beside a required curriculum standard on "the primacy of religious
  liberty" (the TX SB 11 pattern).
- **SB 170 Vitamin K**, mixed. It adds a duty to explain the risks of skipping
  vitamin K and confirms a parent's right to decline.
- **HB 293**, no area. The enacted change covers sports moratoriums and penalty
  enforcement. The birth-certificate eligibility rules were already law.
