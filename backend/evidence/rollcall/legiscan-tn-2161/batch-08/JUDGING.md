## Version check

**SB 2028** was the only roll to trip the gate. The Senate passed it 24-7 on 12
March; the House amended and passed 69-17, which is not divided (17 is just
under a quarter of 69); the Senate then concurred 26-5 on 23 March, also not
divided. So the Senate's passage roll predates the House amendment. Comparing
the introduced draft (1,533 characters) with the chaptered act (4,071), the
operative rule the Senate voted is carried into the act word for word: no city,
county, town, municipality or metropolitan government may regulate the manner
and means of taking wildlife, including discharging a firearm while lawfully
hunting. The description covers only that rule, so it is true of both texts. The
roll carries `acknowledge_later_rolls: [1667628]`.

No vehicle-bill trap in the nine.

## Label reasoning

Every label uses `nay: null`.

- **HB 1971**, `anti_corruption`, against. It deletes § 1-3-121 and replaces it
  with a cause of action that reaches only political subdivisions — cities,
  counties, school districts, utility districts and the like — and expressly
  cannot be used "to challenge the validity or constitutionality of any state
  statute". A statutory route to hold the state to the law is removed. The act
  also says it does not limit other causes of action or waivers of sovereign
  immunity for constitutional claims, and the description says so, because that
  clause bounds how far the change goes.
- **HB 773**, `anti_corruption`, for. A website carrying official legal notices
  must be free to view, with no fee or subscription, and must be separate from
  and not owned by the newspaper of general circulation.
- **SB 2364**, `civil_rights`, against. It repeals the rules that transferred to
  the attorney general when the Human Rights Commission was abolished, and makes
  discrimination complaints and the material gathered with them confidential
  unless a court orders otherwise. It is the sequel to HB 910 in batch-03.
- **SB 1034**, `civil_rights`, against. It brings a city or county government
  seal bearing imagery of a historic conflict, entity, event, figure or
  organization under the Tennessee heritage protection law, which bars removing
  or altering a protected memorial without state permission.
- **HB 601**, `civil_rights`, **for**. The writ of error coram nobis, the route
  for raising newly discovered evidence after conviction, becomes available to
  defendants who pleaded guilty, no contest or best interest, rather than only
  those convicted at trial.
- **SB 2028**, `gun_control`, against. Local governments lose the power to
  regulate firearm discharge while lawfully hunting.
- **HB 1668** and **SB 1667**, `public_safety_and_crime_control`, for. A new
  offense aimed at following someone from a bank or cash machine while carrying
  break-in tools and intending to steal their cash, and a requirement of proof
  of financial responsibility before a vehicle can first be registered.
- **HB 2175**, `corporate_accountability`, for. Delivery network companies and
  their drivers must carry specified automobile liability coverage while the
  driver is logged on and available and while a delivery is under way, which
  puts the cost of harm on the company and its insurer rather than the public.
