## HB 1707, dropped, and why it matters

HB 1707 is the measure a reader would most expect to find here: it requires
every Tennessee court to cooperate with federal immigration authorities and
allows a judge who obstructs them to be referred to the board of judicial
conduct. It is not imported.

Two things came out of the version check.

First, **it is a vehicle bill.** The introduced draft is 481 characters and does
one thing: delete the words "less than ninety (90) days nor" from
§ 7-68-104(d). The enacted act is 2,178 characters about courts and immigration
enforcement. The subject was swapped in on the floor.

Second, and decisive, **no divided roll sits on the enacted text.** The House
adopted House Amendment 1 and passed it 71-24 on 2 April. The Senate then
adopted its own amendment and passed 27-3. The House refused to concur, the
Senate refused to recede, a conference committee was appointed, and the
conference report was adopted 72-17 in the House and 26-5 in the Senate on 23
April — neither divided. So the only divided rolls are on the House's own
amended version, and the conference report that became law is not among the
bill's `amendments[]`, which hold only the two House floor amendments. There is
no way to state from the record how the text the House voted differs from the
Act, so it is dropped rather than described from the wrong document.

This is the same class of drop as HB 1409 in batch-02 and SB 159's Senate roll.

## The other six version checks

Every remaining measure had its draft compared with its chaptered act by
extracted character count and by reading the operative section.

**HB 2018** needed the closest look. The House passed the draft 72-23 on 6
April; the Senate adopted an amendment and passed 27-5; the House concurred
76-18 on 16 April, which is not divided. So the imported House roll predates the
amendment. The draft already carried the operative rule in full — a law
enforcement agency must accept and honor any immigration detainer, meaning
custody for 48 hours beyond release excluding weekends and holidays. The Senate
amendment moved it from § 7-68-105(b)(4) into a new subsection (d) and confined
it to departments already operating under a 287(g) agreement. The rule the House
voted is the rule that became law, so one description serves, and the roll
carries `acknowledge_later_rolls: [1684476]`.

**HB 1709** and **HB 2502** are imported on their House concurrence rolls, which
are votes on the final text. **SB 1587**, **SB 1464**, **SB 750** and
**HB 1194** each have a single divided roll on the text that became law.

## What the seven acts do

- **HB 2018** — a sheriff's department under a federal 287(g) agreement must
  accept and honor immigration detainers, holding a person 48 hours past release
  excluding weekends and holidays.
- **HB 1709** — United States citizenship or qualified immigrant status becomes
  a condition of a range of state licenses, certificates and permits.
- **SB 1587** — it is an offense for an employer to knowingly allow a person
  unlawfully present to operate a commercial motor vehicle, and an offense for
  that person to do so.
- **SB 1464** — state entities must keep the names of officers participating in
  federal immigration enforcement confidential and out of public records.
- **HB 2502** — sales tax applies to transmitting money from Tennessee to a
  location outside the United States through a licensed money transmitter.
- **SB 750** — the centralized immigration enforcement division must report to
  the legislature by 1 July 2026 on criminal activity it associates with people
  in the country illegally, including criminal enterprises and threats.
- **HB 1194** — the Tennessee Lawful Employment Act's definition of a private
  employer drops from at least six employees to at least one, extending work
  authorization duties to nearly every private employer in the state.
