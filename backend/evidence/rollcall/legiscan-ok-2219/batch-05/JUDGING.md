# How batch 05 was judged

All six acts were read in full from the enrolled text with the strikethrough and underline
marks visible.

**HB 2764, income tax.** Two things happen and both belong in the description. The rate table
is rewritten from the 2026 tax year: six brackets become four, the top rate falls from 4.75
percent to 4.5 percent, and the first $3,750 of taxable income, or $7,500 for a couple filing
together, is taxed at nothing. Then a trigger is set. Each February the State Board of
Equalization compares collections with a base year, and where they have grown past a set
threshold every rate falls another quarter point, repeating until the rates reach zero. A
declared revenue failure postpones a cut rather than cancelling it.
`personal_income_tax_reduction`, yes = for. The no side is null: the objection is about what
the state can then afford, which is a different area.

**SB 460, natural gas.** The old law made natural gas the preferred fuel for new fossil fuel
power plants and expired at the start of 2020. The act revives it, removes the end date, and
widens the preference to every new plant and to added capacity at an existing one, not only
fossil fuel plants. That last change is the one a title would hide.
`environment_and_public_health`, yes = **against**, because the state is putting its thumb on
the scale for a fossil fuel against every other source. The reliability argument for gas runs
on a different axis, so the no side is null.

**SB 758, online days.** From the 2026-2027 school year a day when school is shut and lessons
go online no longer counts toward the required 180 days or 1,080 hours. Two such days may be
counted, but only where the state superintendent has approved the district's virtual plan, the
Governor has declared an emergency touching school operations, and the local board approves.
Full-time virtual schools are outside the rule, and the description says so, because without
that line a reader would think the act limits virtual schooling itself.
`public_education_quality`, yes = for.

**SB 212, TeachForwardOK.** A two-year trial. Outside experts review teacher training programs
at state colleges, approved programs get grants to take part, each program answers the review
within ninety days, and one is picked for $500,000 to carry out its plan if the money is
there. `public_education_quality`, yes = for. Everything in the act is conditional on funding,
and the description says so twice rather than promising money the act does not appropriate.

**SB 1618, pretrial release.** The act defines a public safety report, which is a risk
assessment run by a pretrial release program, and lists what it may contain. A court may use
one when setting release terms, and must use one where the charge is a violent crime. Two
limits keep the direction honest and are in the description: the report may not be the sole
basis for granting or refusing release, and both sides get the results on request.
`public_safety_and_crime_control`, yes = for. The objection that risk tools carry bias sits
inside the same area, so the no side is null.

**SB 743, disturbing a religious service.** The penalty is set at a fine of up to $500 and up
to a year in jail, with a second offense a felony carrying up to $1,000 and two years. The act
also rewrites what counts as a disturbance: knowingly blocking someone's way in or out, and
approaching within eight feet of a person without consent, on the public path within 100 feet
of an entrance, to hand over a leaflet, show a sign or argue a point.
`public_safety_and_crime_control`, yes = for.

That second change is the batch's judgement call. It restricts leafleting and protest on a
public sidewalk, which is a free speech question, and a single label cannot carry both a crime
control direction and a speech direction honestly. The measure is recorded on the area its
substance sits in, which is crime and punishment, and the buffer zone is described in plain
words in every record so a reader can weigh it without being told what to think.

## Things worth recording

- **Oklahoma measures can sit across a session break.** SB 743 passed the House on 6 May 2025
  and the Senate did not take the fourth reading until 4 February 2026. Both rolls are kept
  and both are described with their own tally.
- **A dropped emergency section keeps showing up in version diffs.** SB 758 and SB 212 both
  lost theirs between engrossment and enrollment. It is not a substantive change, and it is
  now the expected shape of an Oklahoma diff rather than a surprise.
- Five of six were signed; SB 212 became law without the Governor's signature.
