# How batch 03 was judged

All four acts were read in full from the enrolled text with the strikethrough and underline
marks visible.

**SB 176, contraceptive supply.** Two limits decide how this is described. The act does not
make any plan cover contraception; it only binds a plan that already does. And it says in its
own words that it does not require cover for medicine that could end a pregnancy. Within
those limits it is a clear access measure: a three-month supply the first time, a six-month
supply each time after, one six-month supply per six-month period, with the member and the
prescriber still free to choose less. `womens_reproductive_rights`, yes = for. The no side is
null — an objection here usually runs on insurer cost or on the six-month quantity itself,
not on the area.

**SB 1443, anesthesia payment.** The act binds health benefit plans, not doctors or patients:
when a plan decides whether anesthesia was needed and what to pay, it must weigh the
patient's graded physical condition and the complexity and urgency of the care, both as the
treating doctor judges them, and it must pay the standard severity add-ons of one, two or
three base units for the three sickest gradings. Plans covering Medicaid members under a
separate act are excluded, and the description says so.

Scored `corporate_accountability` rather than `healthcare_affordability`, on the Montana
HB 740 test. As an affordability measure it runs two ways, because higher payments to
providers are also higher costs to plans and premiums. As corporate accountability the
direction is clean: every provision constrains the insurer's discretion in favour of the
patient and the treating doctor.

**SB 631, sentence service.** The substantive change is small and easy to miss under a page
of wording tidy-ups. The act adds one crime to Oklahoma's list of offences where at least
eighty-five percent of the sentence must be served before parole eligibility — firing a gun
at or into a home or into a building used for public or business purposes — and it extends
the whole list to attempts, conspiracies and solicitations. Everything else is renaming child
pornography as child sexual abuse material and fixing cross-references, which the description
mentions without dressing it up as policy. `public_safety_and_crime_control`, yes = for.
The over-criminalisation objection sits inside the same area, so the no side is null.

**SB 1833, food aid.** The act cannot narrow SNAP by itself, because SNAP is federal, and the
description leads with that. It directs the Department of Human Services to seek a federal
waiver and to bar candy and soft drinks once one is granted, and lets the Director seek to
exclude other food judged to have no nutritional value. The waiver request has to argue
public health, the programme's statutory purpose and taxpayer savings, show that shop tills
can enforce it, plan outreach, and track spending and health outcomes.
`social_programs_and_welfare`, yes = **against**, following the Texas SB 379 and Alabama SB 57
line that narrowing what food aid may buy scores against that area. The no side is null.

## Things worth recording

- **Three of these four did not follow the ordinary route into law**: SB 176 became law
  without the Governor's signature, SB 631 became law over a veto, and only SB 1443 and
  SB 1833 were signed. Across three Oklahoma batches, checking the history line for how each
  measure actually became law has mattered every single time.
- **SB 1443 is the batch's judgement call.** Recording it under corporate accountability
  rather than healthcare affordability is what makes its direction honest; under affordability
  it would have been a filter-five drop.
- The builder refused to write once, on `programme` in SB 1833.
