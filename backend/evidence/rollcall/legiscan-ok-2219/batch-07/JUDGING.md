# How batch 07 was judged

All twelve acts, the six imported and the six dropped, were read from the enrolled text with
the strikethrough and underline marks visible. All six imported measures are recorded on
their Senate roll, because none of their House rolls was closely divided.

**HB 1168, abortion-inducing drugs.** The act creates the felony of trafficking in an
abortion-inducing drug: handing one over, or holding one to hand over, to a person the
supplier knows or has reason to know means to use it for an unlawful abortion. Up to ten years
and a $100,000 fine. The exceptions are in the description because they decide who is
exposed: the same drugs prescribed for other reasons or for an ectopic pregnancy or
miscarriage, pharmacists and distributors acting lawfully, and contraception used as directed.
`womens_reproductive_rights`, yes = **against**. The no side is null.

**HB 1087, school year and teacher pay.** Two strands, both in `public_education_quality`,
both for: one more school day (181) and six more hours (1,086), and pay scale steps for
years 26 to 35 of a teacher's career. The act also ends two rules on paying for raises, one
spent and one that had the State Board of Education fund the scale in districts outside the
state aid formula, and the description names both. Its version diff reads 0.922 because the
day and hour increases were written in at the final stage; the Senate roll is a fourth
reading, which is the chamber's vote on that final text.

**HB 2049, Medicaid parity.** The act makes the Oklahoma Health Care Authority enforce parity
between mental health or addiction care and physical care in the private plans that run
Medicaid: parity testing written into contracts, a complaints process, and publication of the
plans' reports within set times. `healthcare_affordability`, yes = for.

**HB 3467, adoption leave.** School staff's six weeks of paid maternity leave now covers the
adoption of a child under four, and sick leave may be used for an infant as well as a newborn.
`social_programs_and_welfare`, yes = for, on the same line as HB 1601 in batch 02.

**HB 3985, the Safe Neighborhoods Act.** In cities of more than 130,000 people, a property owner
may claim costs or lost value where the city or county follows a policy or practice of not
enforcing laws against public camping, obstruction, loitering, panhandling, public urination,
public drinking, drug use or shoplifting, or keeps up a public nuisance. Payments are capped
each year at the owner's property tax to that government, and in court the government carries
the burden of proof. `public_safety_and_crime_control`, yes = for. The objection that it
pushes cities to clear homeless camps runs on a different axis, so the no side is null.

**SB 1451, prior registration.** The registration form asks about any earlier registration,
signing counts as a request to end it, the other state is notified, and the Board publishes a
yearly count of notices and matched removals. `election_integrity`, yes = for, on the Arkansas
line: it tests the accuracy of the rolls and does not narrow who may register.

## The six that were read and dropped

- **HB 2105**, a technical follow-up to the 2024 felony reclassification. Most of it is
  renumbering. Dog fighting and eluding police each move up a class, beside a rule that juries
  be told the minimum time a sentence requires. No single direction.
- **HB 1187** lets a state employee opt out of the state health plan when covered by any
  separate plan, not only a group plan. One word, no direction.
- **SB 1067** fixes out-of-network ambulance payment rates at their May 2025 level until 2027.
  Patients are protected from extra bills either way; the dispute is insurer against provider.
- **SB 906** writes a four-to-one technician ratio into law for retail pharmacies. Whether that
  tightens or loosens practice depends on the Board of Pharmacy's current rule, which neither
  the act nor the official bill page states. The page carries no staff summary for this bill.
- **SB 574** divides opioid settlement money between the Attorney General, colleges and local
  governments.
- **SB 269** gives the Corporation Commission sole charge of carbon dioxide storage wells and
  lets a storage unit take in all the underground space in a reservoir once the operator holds
  63 percent of it. Storing carbon and pooling landowners' space pull opposite ways.

## Things worth recording

- **A Senate batch is small.** Six measures wrote 58 records, about nine or ten per roll,
  because only 24 of the 48 Senate seats are on the November ballot.
- Reading level was measured before importing. The first draft came in at a median grade of
  8.5 and was rewritten into shorter sentences, to 7.3.
