# How batch 02 was judged

Every description was written from the enrolled act, read with the strikethrough and
underline marks made visible. Nothing was judged from a title or from LegiScan's description.

**HB 1003, age of consent.** Two changes that have to be read together. The rape statute used
to reach a victim under sixteen; the act raises that to under eighteen. A second section then
holds the line for people close in age: it used to say that consensual sex with someone over
fourteen was not charged unless the older person was over eighteen, and it now says that
consensual sex with someone sixteen or older is not charged unless the older person is more
than four years older. Describing only the first change would have been badly misleading, so
the description states the combined effect: under sixteen is a crime whatever the ages, and
at sixteen or seventeen it is a crime where the other person is more than four years older.
The act also widens a separate rule on school staff, from students aged sixteen to nineteen
to any student under twenty. `public_safety_and_crime_control`, yes = for.

**HB 1393, parental consent in special education.** The act makes the State Board of
Education write a separate consent form before a student with a disability is assessed
against alternate standards instead of the standards their year group is taught to. The
description keeps the parts that matter to a parent: the form is in the parent's own
language, it must say what refusing means, and a district may only proceed without consent
where it documents real efforts to reach the parent and gets no answer, or where it wins a
due process hearing. `public_education_quality`, yes = for.

**HB 1601, the ARCHER Act.** The trap here is that the six weeks of paid maternity leave for
school staff **already existed** in Oklahoma law; the act does not create it. What the act
adds is the right to extend that leave with sick days the worker has already earned, for
recovery, bonding or care, without further approval from the board, capped at six more weeks
unless a doctor certifies more, to a combined twelve weeks under federal family leave law.
The description says so plainly. `social_programs_and_welfare`, yes = for, following the
Maryland paid-leave line.

**HB 3127, medical marijuana at work.** Read from the marks, this act runs one way: it
narrows a protection. Oklahoma law bars an employer from refusing to hire, disciplining or
sacking someone merely for holding a medical marijuana licence or merely for a positive test.
The exception used to be that the employer could act where the job involved safety-sensitive
duties. The act replaces that with a far wider one: the employer may act whenever it does so
under a written drug and alcohol testing policy meeting the state testing law. It also
imposes zero tolerance on safety-sensitive jobs whatever the employer's policy says. Scored
`civil_rights`, yes = **against**, because the act removes a protection against being sacked
for a lawful medical status. The employer-side reading — that a workplace should be able to
keep its own drug policy — runs on a different axis, so the no side is null.
Review on 2026-09-11 added `labor_rights`, yes = **against** (see batch-09). The act narrows
a job protection, which is the core of that area, and Iowa HF 767, which widened employer drug
testing, carries the same label. The civil rights label stays because Maryland SB 439, which
protected medical cannabis users at work, is recorded there. Both labels point the same way.

This is not the campaign's cannabis question. The standing rule that a cannabis measure
carries no honest direction is about legalising or regulating the drug. This act is about
whether a worker keeps a job protection, and that direction is readable from the text.

**HB 3151, school days.** A district may meet the school year rule by days or by hours. One
hours option let a district run as few as 166 days provided it reached 1,086 hours; from the
2027-2028 school year that floor rises to 173 days, with the hours figure unchanged. The act
also requires an in-person option where parent-teacher conferences are counted as instruction
time. Section 2 makes the whole act conditional: it takes effect on July 1, 2027 only if the
FY2028 appropriation to the State Department of Education is at least $175 million above
FY2026, and otherwise never becomes law. The first version of the description presented the
173-day floor as definite; a review caught the omission and both chambers' descriptions were
corrected and the records rewritten. `public_education_quality`, yes = for.

**HB 1576, Medicaid gene testing.** Rapid whole genome sequencing must be covered as a
separately payable service, subject to federal approval, for patients under twenty-one who
have a serious or sudden illness of unknown cause and who are in intensive care or another
high-dependency unit. All three conditions have to be met, and the description says so rather
than implying general cover. `healthcare_affordability`, yes = for. This is a veto override
and is described as one.

## Things worth recording

- **A same-day tally audit is one day too narrow in Oklahoma.** See PLAN.md. It cost nothing
  here because the correction was made before judging, but the same audit shape is used in
  every state.
- **Two acts became law without the Governor's signature** (HB 1003 in this batch, and three
  in batch one). Two more became law over a veto. Only two of the six were plainly signed.
- **The builder refused to write once**, on `licence` in HB 3127.
- Reading level was measured before importing: median grade 7.1, worst 9.1. HB 3127 sits
  highest because the terms it turns on — safety-sensitive, drug and alcohol testing policy —
  cannot be replaced without losing the rule.
