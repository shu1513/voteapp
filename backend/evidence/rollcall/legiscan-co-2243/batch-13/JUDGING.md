# Batch-13 judging notes

## Why the enrolled print is the right text here

A vetoed bill has no act. What the governor received — and what both chambers
had voted for by then — is the **enrolled** print. Every measure in this batch
carries one in the feed, and every one was read from it.

A curiosity worth recording: several of these vetoed bills also carry a
**Chaptered** text in the LegiScan feed, which a vetoed bill should never have.
The chaptered link was not used. The veto is confirmed from each bill's own
history, which carries an explicit "Governor Vetoed" action for all ten.

## HB 1005 removes an election, and that is the whole fight

Colorado's Labor Peace Act requires a second, separate vote before an employer
and a union may agree an all-union (union security) clause: a majority of every
employee eligible to vote, or three quarters of those who actually voted,
whichever is greater, by secret ballot under the labor director's supervision.
The bill would have struck that requirement from 8-3-108 (1)(c)(I) and repealed
the machinery in (1)(c)(II) and (IV) that ratifies and re-tests pre-1977
agreements. It would also have rewritten the article's declaration of purpose,
replacing "regular and adequate income" with "fair wages and benefits" and
"fair, friendly, and mutually satisfactory" relations with "good faith" ones.

This is the labor-area gap again. There is no labor research area, so the
measure is labeled **corporate accountability**, as the worker-protection
measures in batch-07 were.

## HB 1210's defense clause matters

The ban is not absolute. A person has not engaged in individualized price
setting if they can show the differing prices are justified by differences in
the cost of providing the good or service to different consumers — the act names
consumer selections and ride or delivery distance as examples. The description
carries that.

## HB 1255's health care carve-out

The 24-hour reporting duty does not apply where the operator has a good faith
belief that the content is a legally protected health-care activity as defined
in 12-30-121 (1)(d) — Colorado's shield for reproductive and gender-affirming
care. That exception is in the description because without it the duty reads
much broader than the bill was.

## HB 1286's weight threshold and seat rule

The bar applies to a commercial motor vehicle with a manufacturer's gross
vehicle weight or gross combination weight rating of **26,001 pounds or more**.
The driver must hold the appropriate commercial license with any required
endorsement, be physically present in the cab and prepared to intervene, monitor
performance, and intervene where needed. Only where hazardous materials are
carried must they be **seated in the driver's seat**.

## SB 184's rebuttal is narrow by design

Cancer in a covered individual is presumed occupational. The employer may rebut
only with clear and convincing medical evidence that the condition is a
pre-existing one the firefighter knowingly and intentionally falsified at hiring,
or that it was caused **solely** by a non-occupational circumstance such that it
is entirely unrelated to the service. The act says in terms that tobacco use,
genetic predisposition, family history or a missed pre-employment screening are
not alone enough. Seventeen cancers are listed by name, and volunteer and
seasonal wildland firefighters are covered.

## SB 134's exemption

Small issuers — those meeting the size description in the definitions — are
exempt, and a network may not deny their cards access to processing or impose
fee increases or penalties on the issuer or the branded financial institution
for the cost of the upgrades the act would have required.

## HB 1418's fee is on add-ons only

The five percent fee would have applied to an "add-on transaction" — buying an
item or ability giving a player an advantage, or a feature that alters the game.
It expressly excludes the transaction through which a player obtains **initial
access** to the game. Revenue would have split between a youth mental health
services access enterprise fund and a youth programming and protections
enterprise fund, with no more than three percent for administration.
