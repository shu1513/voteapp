# Batch-10 judging notes

## HB 1256 does not raise gate money

The legislative declaration spends four findings on the point that the $100
release allowance has not risen since 1972 and has lost 85% of its purchasing
power. The act does not change the amount. What it changes:

- the allowance "must not be subject to deductions for restitution, fees,
  transportation, clothing, or any other expense" incurred during incarceration
  or release;
- the department "may" furnish transport to anywhere in Colorado becomes
  "shall";
- an eligible offender without a state ID "may" participate in the offender
  identification program becomes "shall", with an affirmative opt-out; and
- an annual public report from 15 September 2027 on how many people were
  released, how many received the allowance, the total disbursed, and any
  administrative limits on eligibility.

A description that said the act raised gate money would be exactly the error
that hit HB 1004 in the 2025 session — reading the findings instead of the
operative text.

## SB 159 raises the cap for some and freezes it for others

Earned time goes from a maximum of 12 days a month to 14. But new subsection
(1.5)(a.5) holds inmates convicted — or previously convicted — of listed crimes
at 12 days: sexual assault (18-3-303, 18-3-305, 18-3-306), human trafficking of
a minor (18-6-701), the prostitution-of-a-child offenses (18-7-402 to 18-7-407),
possession of a dangerous weapon (18-12-102), possession of a defaced firearm
(18-12-109), **felony motor vehicle theft**, and crimes against a victim listed
in 24-4.1-302 (1). The achievement earned time of up to 60 days per program
milestone is unchanged. Both halves are in the description.

## SB 115's exclusion list is long

A petitioner must be 60 or older and have served at least 20 calendar years, and
must file within three years of the act taking effect. Ineligible: any sex
offense under 16-11.7-102 (3), human trafficking for involuntary or sexual
servitude, any sentence of life without parole, any offense whose victim was a
child under 12, and any offense against a peace officer, emergency medical
provider or firefighter on duty.

The Senate vote was **18-17**. Colorado's constitution requires 18 of 35 in the
Senate, so the measure passed by the minimum. LegiScan's `passed` flag agrees
here, but the tally is recorded because the same flag was wrong on HB 1187 this
session at 32-28 in a House that needs 33.

## SB 36 raises a trigger and widens a notice list

The prison bed vacancy trigger moves from **below three percent** to **below four
percent** for 30 consecutive days. When it trips, the department has 48 hours to
notify a list that grows from seven recipients to thirteen — adding the two
judiciary committees, every release and administrative hearing officer under
contract to the parole board, every community corrections board and program, the
state public defender's office, the alternate defense counsel, every chief
probation officer, the state court administrator and every county sheriff. Each
must acknowledge receipt in writing. A matching notice goes out when the rate
recovers.

Raising the trigger means the measures switch on sooner, not later, because the
test is a vacancy rate falling below the threshold.

## SB 13 narrows a crime

Bigamy under 18-6-201 covered a married person who "marries or cohabits" with
another. The act strikes cohabiting from both the marriage and civil union
forms of the offense and repeals 18-6-203, the definition of cohabitation as
living together "under the representation of being married". Marrying or
entering a civil union while already married or partnered remains an offense.

## SB 18's sealing is not absolute

From 1 July 2026 the court record of a name change petition filed by someone
under 18 is a suppressed record — reachable by judges, court staff, the parties
and their lawyers, authorized judicial staff, and anyone with a court order or
affidavit. The court may never publish either name online. Someone who filed
before that date may petition and the court "shall grant the request". A person
can reach a suppressed record by getting verbal consent from a party and filing
an affidavit under penalty of perjury.
