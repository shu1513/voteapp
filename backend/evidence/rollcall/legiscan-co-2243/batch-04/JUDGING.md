# Batch-04 judging notes

## HB 1013 permits something, it does not only restrict

Before the act, section 6-1-737 (4)(a) barred a landlord from charging a tenant
more for utilities than the utility charged for that tenant's own unit. The act
adds an exception: a ratio utility billing system is allowed if the total billed
to all tenants does not exceed the utility's bill for the whole premises, no
markup or administrative fee is added, common-area use is excluded, and the
allocation method is disclosed in the lease or an addendum.

It also adds a forward-looking requirement: premises built under permits applied
for on or after 1 July 2027 must be metered directly by the utility or by
submeter. The description states both halves, because saying only "protects
tenants" would be false and saying only "lets landlords bill by formula" would
be equally false.

## SB 2 has a second strand that cuts the other way

The headline is the percentage-of-income payment plan, and that is what the
label records. But sections 2 to 4 delay Colorado's nitrogen oxide limits on new
water heaters and furnaces: products fueled by propane and products designed
exclusively for manufactured homes are exempt until 1 January 2031, when a
40 nanogram per joule limit takes effect for manufactured-home units. The
description names this so a reader is not misled about what a yes vote carried.

The plan itself is a duty on investor-owned utilities only. Bands are 2% to 6%
of household income depending on heating fuel and utility size, the unaffordable
remainder appears on the bill as a fixed credit, no up-front payment can be
demanded as a condition of joining, and a late or partial payment cannot by
itself end participation — although the utility may still pursue the debt.

## HB 1196 requires description, not fixed rules

Subsection (1.7)(b) says in terms: "Nothing in this subsection (1.7) requires a
landlord to adopt or disclose fixed disqualifying criteria." What is required is
a general description of what data will be pulled, which factors are weighed,
and the name of any third-party screening service. The redaction duty covers
social security numbers, birth dates, driver's license and state ID numbers, and
bank, credit and debit card numbers. The act takes effect 1 January 2027.

## SB 109 changes the standard, not the quota

The accessibility point values are unchanged for Type A and Type B units. What
the act does is repoint the statute from "ICC/ANSI A117.1" to "ICC A117.1" 2017
edition as adopted by the enforcing code, rewrite the definitions of "dwelling
unit" and "accessible story", and rename the one-point tier from "Type B
visitable ground floor" to "Type C visitable dwelling unit". The exemption for
residential property with fewer than seven units is untouched.

## HB 1065's two halves

Section 2 creates the Transit Investment Area Act, which lets the state economic
development commission approve a project and pledge the growth in **state** sales
tax collected inside the area, for a financing term of up to 30 years. Section
10 creates a separate income tax credit for affordable housing in transit and
housing investment zones, capped at $8.3 million a year in allocations through
31 December 2033. A zone is the area within two miles of a transit or passenger
rail station as mapped by the office of economic development.

## HB 1120 in numbers

Collection by tax lien starts 1 July 2026. The owner must get at least 60
calendar days' notice before a lien is sold or struck off to the county. The
exclusive right to redeem runs one year; where the county holds the lien the
most recent owner may redeem after one year and up to three years; the act's
own title refers to an extended redemption period of up to nine years. Notices
must be in English and Spanish and must explain how to get county translation
or interpretation.
