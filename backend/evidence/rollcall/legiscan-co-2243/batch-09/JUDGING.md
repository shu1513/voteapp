# Batch-09 judging notes

## HB 1374 dropped — the House never voted on the text that became law

The version check flagged two later actions on the House roll of 11 April 2026:

    2026-04-14 S Senate Committee on Appropriations Refer Amended to Senate
                 Committee of the Whole
    2026-04-15 S Senate Second Reading Special Order - Passed with Amendments

Reading the whole history, the House passed the bill on 11 April, the Senate
amended it in committee and on second reading, the Senate passed it on 16 April,
and the next entries are the signatures on 22 May. **There is no House
concurrence action anywhere in the feed.** Whatever the explanation, the roll
the House is judged on is a vote on text the Senate then changed, so that
chamber is unusable. The Senate's own last vote was not divided, so nothing is
left to judge and the measure is dropped.

This is the first time this session that the version check has cost a measure
rather than merely being cleared.

## SB 181 dropped — it trims Colorado Works

Every change in the act narrows the program:

- 26-2-706.5 (4)(a) changes rules that "require" counties to offer extensions
  beyond the 60-month lifetime maximum for households showing good cause into
  rules that "permit" them to;
- no cost of living adjustment is applied to the basic cash assistance grant for
  the fiscal years starting 1 July 2026 and 1 July 2027;
- the escalator that was due to run from fiscal 2024-25 now starts in 2028-29;
- the reserve backstops in 26-2-709 (1)(b)(III)(B), (C) and (IV) are repealed,
  along with the encouragement in 26-2-709.5 (2) to seek more administration
  money.

The House passed it 46-18 during the budget. Like HB 1411 in batch-03, this
keeps a program alive by shrinking it and the no votes come from both
directions, so the measure is dropped rather than given a stance.

## HB 1313 and HB 1360 dropped — transfers, not policy

HB 1313 directs the treasurer to move the first $80 million of the state
affordable housing fund to the financing fund in 2026-27 and the first $40
million in 2027-28, then split the balance 40/60 between the support and
financing funds each July. HB 1360 caps combined administrative spending at two
percent and suspends that cap for one year while a diversion runs. Both are
plumbing.

## HB 1235 is about how a federal rule lands, not whether it does

Congress added community engagement — work and volunteering — requirements to
Medicaid. The act does not adopt or reject them. It tells the state board to
write implementing rules "in a manner that ensures all applicants and members
can obtain or maintain coverage with the least amount of administrative
burdens", repeals that authority automatically if the federal requirement is
repealed, and from March 2027 requires monthly published data on applications
approved and denied, renewals approved and denied, renewals done from existing
records, and the rate of re-enrollment within 90 days of a denial. The stated
purpose of the data is "to demonstrate the impacts" of the federal requirement.

The other half of the act is unrelated: non-emergency medical transportation
brokers must report rides requested, completed and canceled, total cost by
procedure code, call hold times, substantiated grievances by provider, and
provider terminations.

## HB 1414's cap has a page threshold

The $400 ceiling applies to a request by the patient's attorney where the record
**exceeds 664 pages**. At or below that count the pre-existing "reasonable fees"
rule governs. The ceiling is adjusted for inflation on 1 January 2028 and every
even-numbered year after.

## SB 155's fee cannot be passed on, and is capped in total

Half of one percent of the insurer's total multiperil homeowner's premium in the
preceding calendar year, first collected in 2027. The act says in terms that
each insurer "shall not surcharge the fee amount to policyholders", and lets the
enterprise lower or stop the fee so that total revenue does not exceed $100
million over its first five fiscal years.

## HB 1192 removes a body — the label reflects that

The act repeals the advisory committee that directed the homeless prevention
activities program and moves its standard-setting and enforcement duties to the
division. The requirement that money reach nongovernmental agencies for direct
client services is untouched. It is labeled government efficiency rather than
housing, because what the act changes is who decides.
