# Batch-02 judging notes

Every claim was read out of the enrolled act. These are the places where the
act is narrower than its title or its summary would suggest.

## HB 1132 does not require native planting

The title says "increasing pollinator habitats through the conservation of
native plant material". The act only **encourages** covered agencies to
prioritize native plants, and only where the material is available, the use is
financially viable, and it fits other goals. What it does *require* is
procedural: five-year and ten-year goals, joint purchasing, a staff training
program subject to funding, and pollinator zones along roadways that are
signed and left unmowed except for safety. The description says "where it is
workable" for that reason.

Four agencies are covered: the state forest service, natural resources (but not
the state land board), personnel, and transportation.

## HB 1226 closes no power plant

It applies to units that emitted 200 tons or more of nitrogen oxides or sulfur
dioxide in calendar year 2024. A unit that **ceases operations, converts to gas
or oil only, or already has both scrubber types** is exempt. The state proposes
the rule by July 2029 and compliance cannot be required before the end of 2034.
So the honest statement is a condition on units that keep running, not a
shutdown.

## HB 1268 approves nothing

It creates the *category* "renewable energy reinvestment area" and lets a local
permitting entity designate one on an eligible site: a brownfield, mining
affected land, a closed landfill, a decommissioned oil and gas site, or federal
cleanup land. The money mechanism is tax increment financing, and the notable
change is that the area need not be inside or next to the urban renewal area.

## SB 141's fee is optional, and that is the point

Colorado already collects an optional Keep Colorado Wild pass fee at
registration. This act adds a second optional $5 collision prevention fee, which
must be presented **after** the existing opt-out and clearly separated from it.
The act requires notice saying in plain terms that declining will not affect the
ability to register a vehicle. Money splits 75% to the collision prevention fund
and 25% to the wildlife cash fund.

## SB 142 creates no mandate

The act says so itself: it "does not create new state spending obligations or
new regulatory mandates". Its whole purpose is to remove legal ambiguity so that
towns and special districts stop paying legal fees and risk premiums to do
something the law never actually forbade.

## SB 21's truck program is capped and small

Only fleets of 50 or fewer heavy trucks, and only businesses under $100 million
in annual revenue, may apply. No more than 20% of the fund's yearly income may
go to truck replacement, trucks from 2006 or earlier are prioritized, and the
whole subsection repeals on 1 July 2032. The old truck must be surrendered to
the dealer and physically destroyed — a three-inch hole through the engine block
and the chassis rails cut in half.

## SB 3's duties fall on providers, not owners

A propulsion battery provider, not the car owner, must fund collection of
unwanted batteries and ensure responsible management. Landfilling is prohibited
outright. Labelling starts 1 July 2029; the in-vehicle state-of-health display
applies to model year 2028 and later.

## HB 1007's numbers

A portable-scale device is capped at 1,920 watts of output to the grid, one per
address, one per wall outlet, and must not energise the grid during an outage.
Devices of 391 watts or less are exempt from the solar installation and building
wiring rules. From 1 January 2027 an unlisted device above 391 watts may not be
sold. The HOA protection also starts 1 January 2027, and an owner can still be
required to secure the device and carry the liability.
