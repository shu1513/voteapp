# Batch-05 judging notes

## HB 1302 loosens a duty — the label says so

Almost everything else in this batch adds a requirement. HB 1302 removes one.
Colorado required the state bureau's firearm background check unit to be open at
least twelve hours a day every day except Thanksgiving and Christmas. The act
replaces that with hours "that best meet the business needs of the bureau",
determined by the director from continuously reviewed statistics.

The act does keep two guarantees: a request must be placed in the processing
queue immediately regardless of the waiting period, and submissions must be
accepted every day of the year except those two holidays.

Calling this a gun control measure in either direction would be a stretch. The
legislature's stated reason is that the 2023 three-day waiting period removed
the need for instant service, and the effect is staffing flexibility. It is
labeled **government efficiency**, and the description states plainly that the
fixed 12-hour rule is gone.

## HB 1288 dropped — a working group is not a policy

The act creates the jury selection working group in the judicial department,
names its 17 members and their appointing authorities, sets a 15 June 2026
appointment deadline, and requires a progress report by 1 December 2026. Its
duties are to solicit input, identify best practices on voir dire time, juror
questionnaires, rehabilitation of jurors, bench book scripts and training, and
to "consider making recommendations". Section 2 is the safety clause. There is
nothing else in the act.

A yes vote is a vote to study jury selection. That is not a stance a voter can
be told they agree or disagree with, so the measure is dropped.

## Two rolls with an unusual same-day twin

**HB 1312, House, 4 May 2026.** Three rolls that day: 1693126 concurrence 65-0,
1693127 repassage 46-18, and 1693615 repassage 47-18. Two repassage rolls on one
bill on one day. The history records a single "House Considered Senate
Amendments - Result was to Concur - Repass" action. Roll 1693615 records all 65
representatives (47 + 18); roll 1693127 records 64. The roll accounting for the

This is the same pattern as HB 1147 in the 2025 session's batch-11.

**SB 190, Senate, 11 May 2026.** Five rolls that day: three third-reading

records one third reading, "Passed with Amendments - Floor". The later roll is
judged as the chamber's final word and 1696211 is acknowledged as a peer. A
reader comparing the two will find one senator moved between them.

## HB 1064 changes purpose and process, not eligibility

Nothing in the act changes who may be sentenced to the youthful offender system.
What changes is the statement of purpose — now trauma-informed care, physical
safety, continuity of care and reentry — the vocabulary ("youthful offender"
becomes "juvenile or young adult", "offender" becomes "participant"), an
explicit statement that people with disabilities get equitable treatment in
sentencing, and a new intake process: an evaluation of criminogenic and health
needs by a trained evaluator, a written report, an individual plan aligned with
any IEP or Section 504 plan, and a case manager who meets the participant
monthly and records progress. Phase I plans are reviewed monthly with a
multidisciplinary team.

## HB 1123 is narrower than "prevent sexual abuse in jails" suggests

The operative rules are about strip searches. A search is permitted only at
intake from outside the facility where that is the facility's policy, where two
personnel reasonably believe the person is concealing a controlled substance, a
dangerous instrument or contraband, or under a written policy responding to a
documented prior incident of the same kind. Every search must be recorded on a
body-worn camera and tagged; overhead cameras may not be used; off-premises
access to the footage is barred; and viewing footage requires written approval
from the sheriff or municipal jail head. Facilities report annually to the jail
standards advisory committee and the attorney general. Retaliatory searches are
prohibited outright.

## HB 1309's threshold is a preponderance, and the effect is conditional

Where a court determines by a preponderance of the evidence that a party has
committed domestic violence, child abuse or neglect, or a sexual assault that
resulted in the child's conception, it may not allocate mutual decision-making
over the other party's or the child's representative's objection **unless** it
makes specific findings that mutual decision-making can happen without coercion,
intimidation, retaliation or risk of harm. It also may not appoint a parenting
coordinator solely to make mutual decision-making work. The description says
"cannot ... unless" for that reason.

## SB 190 releases footage at the end, not at once

The 21-day release rule for misconduct complaints stays, but now applies to
complaints that do **not** involve a death. Where a use of force kills someone,
the general public release comes after that 21-day period, and the recordings
depicting the death go to the immediate family only once three things are all
finished: the internal investigation including appeals, the multi-agency
investigation and its report, and any criminal case including appeals.
