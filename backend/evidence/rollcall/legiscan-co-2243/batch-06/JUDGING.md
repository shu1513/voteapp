# Batch-06 judging notes

## HB 1353 dropped — less testing pulls two ways

The act rewrites one clause of 22-7-1006.3. Before, the department administered
the social studies assessment to a representative sample of schools, once in
elementary and once in middle school, reaching every school at least once every
three years, with an opt-in for schools left out of the sample. After, it
administers the assessment in **seventh grade in public schools throughout the
state**. Section 2 cuts $302,835 from the statewide assessment appropriation.

So the act both narrows (two grade bands become one) and broadens (a sample
becomes every school). A yes vote is a vote for less social studies testing
overall and less spending on it — which reads as a gain to one voter and a loss
to another. There is no defensible single stance, so the measure is dropped.

## HB 1193 really is that small

The whole substance is the first sentence of 22-1-116: the words "the sight of
all children in the kindergarten" become "the vision of all children in
pre-kindergarten and the vision and hearing of all children in the kindergarten
and the first, second, third, fifth, seventh, and ninth grades". Hearing is not
added for pre-kindergarten. The religious and personal objection is untouched.
The description says only what the act does.

## SB 125 creates no new federal right

The legislative declaration is explicit that the purpose is to "codify federal
disability-based nondiscrimination requirements applicable to public elementary
and secondary education" so the rights survive "changes to federal enforcement
priorities". The new article 29.5 of title 22 covers identification, evaluation
and what local education providers owe a qualified student with a disability.
A reader should understand this as continuity, not expansion.

## HB 1141 sets three tests, and the third is the notable one

An educational institution denies equal enjoyment of a public accommodation
when it (a) excludes a student from a program on protected grounds, (b) without
a legitimate, non-pretextual basis treats a student differently from a similarly
situated student in a different subgroup of the same protected class, or (c) has
**actual notice** of a hostile environment and fails to take prompt and effective
steps to end it. Federal non-discrimination law is persuasive but not binding,
and state law governs any conflict.

## HB 1227's hearing is a check, not a veto

If the county moves to dismiss before adjudication and the child's guardian ad
litem or counsel objects and articulates a basis for abuse or neglect, the court
must hold a hearing. If the county shows a reasonable basis, the court dismisses.
Only if it does not does the case proceed. The act says in terms that it does
not let a child file a petition on their own behalf and does not limit anyone's
right to demand a jury trial.

## HB 1314's grandparent change is a definition change

Sections 2 amends the definitions of "grandparent" and "great-grandparent" in
14-10-124.4 to read "a person who is **or, if the child's father or mother is
deceased, was** the parent of a child's father or mother". Before, the present
tense could end the standing when the linking parent died. The exclusion for
someone whose parental rights were terminated is unchanged.

## HB 1259's preschool confidentiality clause is broad

Except under a court subpoena or order, a preschool provider and the department
must keep all identifying records and facts about children and their relatives
confidential, and — notwithstanding the open records act — must not release
child-level data that identifies a specific child or relative. The person in
interest may still get their own data.
