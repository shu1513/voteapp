# Batch-11 judging notes

## HB 1011 is a retail sales ban, not a transfer rule

The feed's description is "Concerning the transfer of certain pet animals in
Colorado", which sounds procedural. The act renames the Pet Store Consumer
Protection Act as the **"Pistol the Pomeranian Protection Act"** and replaces
its disclosure duties outright: on and after 1 January 2028 a pet store "shall
not sell, lease, offer to sell or lease, barter, auction, or otherwise transfer
ownership of a dog or cat", and brokers face the same bar.

The exceptions are specific: transfers to a government agency including law
enforcement animals, guide, signal and service dogs, transfers by a licensed
animal shelter or pet animal rescue, and hunting dogs sold to or by someone
holding a current Colorado hunting license. A pet store may still host adoption
displays if the animals are shown by a licensed shelter or rescue, the store
takes no fee, and each animal is sterilized. The declaration says in terms that
nothing in the act affects agricultural operations.

The Senate passed it **19-16**.

## HB 1342 changes a mental state, it does not create an offense

Section 33-6-131 already made luring bears unlawful. The act changes the test
from placing food "with the intent of luring a wild bear" to a person who
"knowingly places food or edible waste ... in circumstances where there is a
reasonable probability of luring a wild bear". A first violation still brings
only a warning. The fine ceiling for a third or subsequent offense rises from
$2,000 to $5,000. Acts related to agriculture, live animals and unharvested
crops stay exempt.

## HB 1017's carve-out matters

The act does not bar insurers from restitution generally. The new legislative
declaration says restitution to direct victims "must be prioritized, while
insurance companies should recover their losses through remedies other than
criminal restitution orders" — but the same paragraph says an insurer that is
itself a direct victim, including of a scheme to defraud it or where its own
property was damaged or stolen, "must be made whole through the criminal
restitution process". The description carries that second half.

## HB 1033's limit is one product, five variations

The "Tamale Act" adds a second track to Colorado's home kitchen exemption: a
producer may make and sell packaged foods requiring time and temperature control
for safety — tamales, burritos and tortas are named. The producer may sell **one
type** of such product with up to **five variations** of it, must specify the
individual products, and must give the list to the state or the local public
health agency. The existing non-hazardous list is also spelled out and gains
fruit empanadas and tortillas.

## HB 1004's numbers

The credit is 50% of a monetary contribution to a child care facility, and the
act moves its sunset from income tax years commencing before 1 January 2028 to
before 1 January 2038. The findings state that about 18,000 Coloradans claimed
it in 2022 and that it generated $60 million for child care providers.

## HB 1008 does not touch water rights

The act gives the division of parks and wildlife a statutory outdoor recreation
coordination, planning and management role tied to Colorado's Outdoors Strategy,
defines "accessible experiences" to include disproportionately impacted and
geographically underrepresented communities and people with disabilities, and
requires planning reports. It carries an express provision that nothing in it
may be construed to affect the administration of water rights, which is stated
in the description because Colorado land-and-water bills routinely raise that
question.
