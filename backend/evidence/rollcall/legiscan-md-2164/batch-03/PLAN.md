# Maryland 2025 batch-03 — selection

**Ten measures / seventeen final rolls**, drawn from LegiScan session 2164 (the
2025 Regular Session). Descriptions come from the official Department of
Legislative Services (DLS) Fiscal and Policy Note for the version that became
law; the official bill history supplies each chapter number.

## Gate

The same five checks every Maryland batch uses:

1. A divided floor vote: at least one nay, and the smaller side is at least a
   quarter of the larger side.
2. Enacted: the official bill history records a chapter.
3. A plain, statewide subject that fits one research area.
4. One final roll per measure per chamber; the later roll where a chamber voted
   again after amendments.
5. A defensible label direction. A no vote gets a stance only when it directly
   rejects the research area's mechanism.

## Pool this batch was drawn from

Session 2164 holds **320 divided-and-enacted rolls on 176 measures**. Batches
01 and 02 judged 20 of them, and 7 more are cross-filed twins of those, so the
unjudged pool was **119 distinct subjects**. This batch takes 10, leaving 109.

## Which text each roll voted on

The chaptered PDF file name is the version check, and each note's own version
stamp agrees with it.

- Chaptered on the `…t.pdf` text, so both chamber rolls are on the enacted
  text: HB 249, HB 881, HB 933, HB 1045, HB 1082, HB 1380, SB 154, SB 425.
- Chaptered on the `…e.pdf` text, so only the later post-amendment roll is
  used: HB 431 and HB 974. In both, the House passed its own text in March,
  the Senate amended, and the House agreed on April 7 — that April 7 roll is
  the one judged. Their earlier March rolls are on superseded text and are
  deliberately excluded.

Three measures contribute a single roll because the other chamber's vote was
not divided: HB 249 (Senate 47-0), HB 431 (Senate 42-0) and HB 974
(Senate 37-5).

## HB 881's reconsidered Senate vote

The Senate passed HB 881 46-0 on April 7, then adopted a motion to reconsider
that vote and passed it again 33-13 the same day. The 33-13 roll (1572861) is
the Senate's final action and the one judged; the 46-0 roll (1572860) is not
divided and so was never in the pool. Because the two share a date, the
superseded-stage gate fired, and the judgment carries
`acknowledge_later_rolls: [1572860]`. Roll ids ascend in time and the official
history states the order, so the direction is not in doubt.

A same-day, same-chamber peer query over all 35 rolls in this batch (both
sessions) found this as the only such case.

## Cross-filed twins

One twin per subject. HB 249 is used rather than SB 125, HB 881 rather than
SB 703, HB 933 rather than SB 679, HB 1045 rather than SB 940, HB 1380 rather
than SB 676, SB 154 rather than HB 103, and SB 425 rather than HB 902. HB 431's
cross-file SB 413 and HB 974 and HB 1082 have no enacted twin in the pool.

## Deliberately not selected

- **HB 49, building energy performance standards** — a departmental bill adding
  exemptions and analysis to the existing climate standards. A no vote here
  would likely be *more* opposed to the standards, not less, so no honest
  direction fits.
- **HB 599 / SB 373, airport fire and rescue bargaining, and SB 231, public
  employee relations** — the employer is the state, so `corporate_accountability`
  (companies) does not fit. Same reason HB 141 was deferred in 2026.
- **HB 1036 / SB 931 and HB 1035 / SB 937, energy omnibus** — still omnibus.
- **HB 1294, earned wage access** — it legalises and regulates at once, so the
  direction runs both ways.
- **HB 1502, Baltimore City syringe exchange** — local, not statewide.
- **HB 774, hunger-free campus** — extends only the designation to private
  colleges and explicitly bars them from grant money; too thin to carry a
  stance.
- Budget bills, speed-camera bills and other local bills, as in earlier batches.
