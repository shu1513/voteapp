## SB 767
Read from Chapter 132 of 2025 (`ls_text.py`). New section 2-318(h) of the Courts
and Judicial Proceedings Article gives full-time deputy sheriffs and correctional
deputies at the rank of major and below the right to organize and bargain with
the Sheriff and the County Administrator over wages and employee benefits
((h)(2)(iii)). A union is certified on a petition signed by at least 51 percent
of sworn deputy sheriffs at the rank of sergeant and below; if the Sheriff
challenges the petition within 20 days, an American Arbitration Association
neutral runs a secret ballot and the union needs 51 percent of sworn deputy
sheriffs and correctional deputies at the rank of major and below ((h)(4)).
Contracts run one to four years, need ratification by the unit, the Sheriff and
the Commissioners, and extra funding still needs the Commissioners' approval
((h)(3), (h)(6)(v)). Correctional deputies may not strike. The bill creates
bargaining rights, so a yes vote is `for`.

Review correction (2026-09-11): the first read used the chapter's marked-up
text, which still prints the Senate-struck introduced language. The struck
version had deputies bargaining with the County Commissioners over wages and
their share of health care premiums; the enacted version has them bargaining
with the Sheriff and the County Administrator over wages and employee benefits,
and the Commissioners keep only the ratification and funding-approval roles.
The first descriptions also flattened the two certification groups (petition:
deputy sheriffs at sergeant and below; ballot: deputy sheriffs and correctional
deputies at major and below) into "a majority of deputies". The descriptions
now state the enacted bargaining terms and drop the thresholds rather than
misstate them: a union is recognized by petition, or by secret ballot if the
Sheriff challenges the petition.
