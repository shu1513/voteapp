# Maryland 2026 batch-02 — selection

**Ten measures / nineteen final rolls**, drawn from LegiScan session 2240 (the
2026 Regular Session). Descriptions come from the official Department of
Legislative Services (DLS) Fiscal and Policy Note for the version that became
law, and the official bill history supplies each chapter number.

## Gate

The same five checks as every Maryland batch:

1. A divided floor vote: at least one nay, and the smaller side is at least a
   quarter of the larger side.
2. Enacted: the official bill history records a chapter.
3. A plain, statewide subject that fits one research area.
4. One final roll per measure per chamber; the later roll where a chamber
   voted again after amendments.
5. A defensible label direction. A no vote gets a stance only when it directly
   rejects the research area's mechanism.

## Pool this batch was drawn from

Session 2240 holds **335 divided-and-enacted rolls on 189 measures**.
Batch-01 judged 5. This batch judges 10 more, leaving **174 measures** for
later batches.

## The August 2026 sitting

The 2240 dataset carries a 32-roll August sitting under the same LegiScan
session id, separate from the regular session that ended April 13. Only six of
those rolls are kept floor votes and only five are divided.

**HB 862 is the veto override in that sitting.** The Governor vetoed it on
May 22, 2026 on policy grounds, and both chambers overrode on August 3, 2026,
which is what made it Chapter 879. Those two `Overridden` rolls are each
chamber's final action on the bill, so they are the ones judged here, and the
descriptions say plainly that the vote was on overriding a veto.

The other August rolls belong to HB 2100 (General Assembly and congressional
election districts) and HB 2101 (a duplicate-amendment withdrawal tied to the
arbitration bill). HB 2100 is **not selected**: mid-decade redistricting is a
question about the process itself, and `election_integrity` — secure, accurate,
auditable elections — carries no honest direction on it.

## Which text each roll voted on

- Chaptered on the `…t.pdf` text, so both chamber rolls are on the enacted
  text: HB 444, SB 417, SB 334, HB 862, HB 372.
- Chaptered on the `…e.pdf` text, so the later post-amendment roll is used in
  each chamber: SB 1, SB 810, HB 351, SB 255, HB 573.

**SB 334 is worth spelling out.** The Senate passed its own text on March 19,
the House amended and passed it on April 8, then on April 9 the House receded
— withdrew its amendments — and passed the Senate text 91-40. The chapter is
`CH_771_sb0334t.pdf`, the Senate's engrossed text, so both selected rolls are
on the enacted text after all.

**SB 255 contributes only one roll.** The House's final vote on April 13 was
91-19, which is not divided, so the earlier and now superseded April 11 House
roll is deliberately left out rather than attributed.

## Cross-filed twins

Exactly one twin per pair: HB 444 rather than SB 245, HB 351 rather than
SB 346, and HB 372 rather than SB 169. SB 810, SB 255, SB 334, SB 417, HB 573
and HB 862 have no enacted twin in the divided pool.

## Deliberately not selected

- **HB 2100, election districts** — see above.
- **HB 141, graduate assistant bargaining, and SB 6, nontenure faculty** — the
  employer is a state university, so `corporate_accountability` (companies)
  does not honestly fit, and no other area carries the direction.
- **SB 323, Youth Charging Reform Act, and SB 162, sentence reduction** —
  contested direction inside `public_safety_and_crime_control`.
- Budget and reconciliation bills, speed-camera bills, and local bills.
