# Maryland 2026 batch-03 — selection

**Eleven measures / eighteen final rolls**, drawn from LegiScan session 2240
(the 2026 Regular Session). Descriptions come from the official Department of
Legislative Services (DLS) Fiscal and Policy Note for the version that became
law; the official bill history supplies each chapter number.

## Gate

1. A divided floor vote: at least one nay, and the smaller side is at least a
   quarter of the larger side.
2. Enacted: the official bill history records a chapter.
3. A plain, statewide subject that fits one research area.
4. One final roll per measure per chamber; the later roll where a chamber voted
   again after amendments.
5. A defensible label direction. A no vote gets a stance only when it directly
   rejects the research area's mechanism.

## Pool this batch was drawn from

Session 2240 holds **335 divided-and-enacted rolls on 189 measures**. Batches
01 and 02 judged 15, and 5 more are cross-filed twins of those, leaving **131
distinct subjects**. This batch takes 11, leaving 120.

## Which text each roll voted on

Only **HB 711** is chaptered on the `…t.pdf` text — the Senate reported it
favorably without amendments, so both its rolls are on the enacted text. Every
other measure here is chaptered on the `…e.pdf` enrolled text, so this batch
uses the later post-amendment or post-concurrence roll in each chamber and
excludes the earlier rolls on superseded text.

Four measures contribute a single roll because the other chamber's votes were
not divided: HB 624 (Senate 42-0), HB 894 (Senate 42-4), HB 895 (Senate 41-1)
and SB 141 (Senate 44-0 in February, then 28-4 in April).

**SB 141 took the longest path.** The Senate passed it 44-0 in February; the
House amended and passed it 95-35 on April 10; the Senate refused to concur and
asked the House to recede; the House refused; and on April 13 both chambers
passed it again. The House's April 13 vote (85-38) is its final action and the
one judged.

**HB 711 became law without the Governor's signature**, under Article II,
Section 17(c) of the Maryland Constitution, as Chapter 874. Its descriptions say
so rather than claiming it was signed.

**HB 894's impact-fee provision is contingent** on two other bills failing, so
the descriptions do not state it as something the law did.

## Cross-filed twins

One twin per subject: HB 284 rather than SB 160, HB 315 rather than SB 335,
HB 624 rather than SB 411, HB 711 rather than SB 504, HB 894 rather than SB 389,
HB 895 rather than SB 387, HB 1017 rather than SB 984, SB 141 rather than
HB 145, and SB 475 rather than HB 687. HB 191 and SB 937 have no cross-file.

## Deliberately not selected

- **HB 1532, Utility RELIEF Act** — a genuine omnibus covering rates, data
  centers, the siting certificate process, competitive supply and solar
  permitting. Same reason HB 1036 was deferred in 2025.
- **HB 467 / SB 822, parole commission changes, and SB 323, youth charging** —
  contested direction, like the Second Look and geriatric parole bills batch-01
  deferred.
- **HB 604 / SB 28, state employee arbitration, and SB 6, nontenure faculty** —
  the employer is the state, so `corporate_accountability` does not fit.
- **HB 6, pregnant and parenting students** — mostly data collection.
- **HB 2100, election districts** — mid-decade redistricting carries no honest
  direction under `election_integrity`, as batch-02 recorded.
- Budget and reconciliation bills, speed-camera bills and local bills.
