**SB 2174 was imported in this batch and retracted after the #1244 review** — see the
retraction note at the end and `retracted/`.

## Selection

Same five filters as batch-01, drawn from the 67 rolls batch-01 left as candidates.
The pool had already been narrowed by the version rule, which in North Dakota does most
of the work: the state records no conference or concurrence vote, so a bill amended after
its floor vote has no later roll to use and drops out.

A proactive check over all ten picked rolls found no later or same-day peer in the same
chamber, so the superseded-stage gate never fired and no judgment needed
`acknowledge_later_rolls`.

## SB 2174, retracted

The description credited the act with barring county zoning and water-quality rules on feedlots
and with creating a petition process. The markup shows every one of those provisions was
already law; only their numbering changed from letters to numerals. What SB 2174 actually adds
is a two-way adjustment of setback distances through the state odor tool, and a cap on how
strict a county may be about odor. Those pull opposite ways, so the measure is a filter-5 drop.
Its 28 records were retired, the roll set back to pending, and the entry removed. The lesson
is the campaign's oldest one, in a new shape: **a renumbering is not a change**, and a stance
must rest on text the markup marks as new.

## SB 2159 is the reason this campaign renders pages

Its title — "Projects the state energy research center is permitted to pursue" — says
nothing. The flattened text reads `the state energy research center may not conduct
research`, which looks like a prohibition being kept. The markup shows `not` struck and a
new approval clause added, so the act **lifts** a flat ban on high-level radioactive waste
research. The rendered page confirmed it before the stance was written.

## Dropped under filter 5, after reading the act in full

- **HB 1154**, extending the moratorium on new nursing beds. A supply limit reads as cost
  control or as reduced access, and both readings sit inside healthcare_affordability.
- **HB 1279**, the coal conversion tax exemption. Its tax break and its carbon capture
  credit pull opposite ways inside the same area.
- **SB 2348**, health care sharing ministries as college coverage. Cheaper for a student,
  but a sharing ministry is not insurance and need not pay, and the area's own words
  include quality of care.
- **SB 2253**, whose substance is a noise rule on engine compression brakes. No area fits.
- **SB 2086**, pesticide control, almost entirely definitional modernizing.
- **HB 1444**, county roads, a local process change rather than infrastructure.
