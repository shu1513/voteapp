# North Dakota batch-03 — plan

6 measures, 6 roll calls, 165 records. **The not-enacted scope**: measures a chamber
passed that never became law.

## Why this scope matters in North Dakota

North Dakota has a legislative supermajority and a governor of the same party, so the
enacted pool is largely the majority's own agenda. What passed one chamber and died is
where the parties actually differ. This is the scope Pennsylvania opened and Arizona's
vetoed pool proved out, supplying areas the enacted pool produced none of.

That holds here. This batch is the first North Dakota coverage of
`womens_reproductive_rights`-adjacent health mandates, tribal consultation, and a landlord
duty — none of which the enacted scope reached.

## Wording rules for this scope

Bodies are conditional throughout, and the builder refuses to write a body that asserts the
law changed. North Dakota adjourned on 2025-05-02, so every tail states a completed fact
rather than a time-stamped hedge, and each tail names what actually ended the bill.

Each description was written from the print **that chamber voted**, chosen by date against
the bill's text stack, not from a later print or from an enrolled act that never existed.

## The measures

| measure | roll | area | direction | how it ended |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HB 1283 breast screening cost sharing | House 66-26 | healthcare_affordability | for | Senate rejected 22-24 |
| HB 1391 health status in anti-discrimination law | House 67-26 | civil_rights | for | Senate rejected 17-29 |
| HB 1455 tribal consultation on bills | House 63-28 | civil_rights | for | Senate rejected 7-40 |
| HB 1496 minimum heat in rented homes | House 70-19 | corporate_accountability | for | Senate rejected 11-35 |
| HB 1583 false political advertisements | House 55-34 | election_integrity | for | Senate rejected 11-36 |
| SB 2307 library sexual material | House 49-45 | civil_rights | against | vetoed, override failed 25-20 |

SB 2307 is the session's closest House vote in this batch and the only measure here that
passed both chambers. The Governor vetoed it and the Senate's override attempt fell short
of the two-thirds needed, which the tail states.

HB 1496 is `corporate_accountability` rather than `housing_affordability`: a minimum-heat
duty binds landlords as a defined class of business, and it changes neither housing supply
nor cost, which the Maryland HB 767 retraction put outside that area.
