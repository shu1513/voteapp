## One correction the font-weight check caught

SB 1575's plain text reads as though the Act **removes** the requirement for
an in-person site inspection of every licensed hospice — the old sentence
appears in brackets. It does not. The requirement is re-enacted a few sections
later in language the Act adds. Reading only the deletions would have produced
a description that said the opposite of what the law does.

## Why every nay here is null

All six take `nay: null`. In each case the strongest argument against sits
outside the research area the measure belongs to, so a no vote gets a record
of the vote and no stance tag.

- SB 1598 and SB 1568 are coverage mandates; the objection is the cost the
  mandate puts on insurers and employers, which is not a position about
  whether care should be affordable.
- HB 4075 puts public money behind a private loan; the objection is fiscal
  risk to the state. **The vote proves the point**: the nays crossed both
  caucuses in both chambers, so no single policy direction explains them.
- HB 4115's objection is patient safety from less frequent background checks,
  which belongs to `public_safety_and_crime_control`.
- SB 1575's objection is the pause on new licences reducing supply.
- HB 4142's objection is about marijuana policy rather than access to care.

**SB 1598 carries two labels** for two genuinely different mechanisms: an
insurance coverage rule, and a new power for the state Public Health Officer
to recommend immunizations and have them covered within 15 business days.
