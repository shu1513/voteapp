## HB 4116 is House-only, and this is the held roll

HB 4116 is the measure whose Senate roll the config holds back. LegiScan
reports that vote as 17-12 with McLane a yea; Oregon's journal reports 16-13
and names McLane among the thirteen nays, after he was granted unanimous
consent to change his vote. The importer stores that roll without a floor flag,
so it can never be queued, and **HB 4116 is recorded from its House roll
alone**.

Its earlier screening summary was also the **minority report's**, which
described a task force on short-term financial products. The enrolled Act is
nothing of the kind: it declares that Oregon does not want section 521 of the
1980 federal banking law to apply to consumer loans made here, and brings
lenders of $50,000 or less to Oregon residents under Oregon's own lending
chapter. The description is written from that Act.

## Why these stances

Three measures sit in `corporate_accountability`, which is defined as holding
companies to account for legal compliance, consumer protection and public
impact. HB 4116 caps what lenders may charge Oregonians; HB 4016 makes large
state contractors show they have paid their taxes; HB 4089 sharpens the
criminal law against employers who take work and do not pay for it. In each
the duty falls on firms, and the subject is compliance with the law.

**HB 4013 went to `social_programs_and_welfare` for want of anywhere better.**
No research area covers labour standards, a gap this campaign has now hit in
three states. The Act sets a floor under the limits on how many hours a child
may work, so the closest honest fit is the area for protecting vulnerable
people, following the 2025 SB 916 precedent.

All six take `nay: null`. The objections are cost of compliance, access to
credit, and state overreach, and none of those is a position within the
measure's own area.
