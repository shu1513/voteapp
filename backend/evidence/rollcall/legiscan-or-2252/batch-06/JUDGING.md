## HB 4153 runs the other way, and again the vote shows it

HB 4153 makes farm stores an allowed use on exclusive farm use land, and
allows paid visitor attractions alongside them — crop mazes, play structures,
farm-to-table meals, hay rides, seasonal events.

Oregon's exclusive farm use zoning exists to keep farmland in farming.
Widening what may be built and sold there loosens a conservation standard, so
the yea scores `against` and the nay `for`.

**The roll call corroborates the direction rather than contradicting it.** The
nays are Democrats in both chambers — Andersen, Chaichi, Fragala, Gamba,
Grayber, Hudson and Kropf in the House; Golden, Jama, Neron Misslin,
Patterson, Pham, Prozanski, Reynolds and Taylor in the Senate. This is a land
conservation split, not a party-line one, and the conservation side lost.

This is the second reversed measure in the session, after HB 4060.

## The three siting and funding measures

HB 4031 and HB 4076 both make it easier to build renewable generation, one by
waiving the state siting certificate where a local government has approved the
project and construction has begun in time for a federal tax credit, the other
by treating the land use standard as met where a project uses an existing
site's unused grid connection.

Both take `nay: null`, and the reason is worth naming: **the objection lives
inside the same area as the argument for.** Faster renewable build-out serves
the climate half of `environment_and_public_health`; skipping state siting
review cuts against the standards-and-review half. Where a measure trades one
part of an area against another, the yea gets a direction only if the Act's
centre of gravity is clear — here it is building clean generation — and the
nay gets no stance.

HB 4134 raises the state lodging tax from 1.5 to 2.75 percent and sends the
increase to wildlife recovery, the Conservation Corps and anti-poaching
enforcement. Its objection is the tax, which is not an environmental position.
Because it raises revenue it needed a three-fifths vote in each chamber.

## Two more measures dropped under filter 5

- **SB 1565** raises the two-year snowmobile registration fee from $10 to $30
  and does nothing else. No area fits a recreation user fee, and the nays
  crossed both caucuses. Its House roll is held in any case.
- **HB 4148** changes the split of local lodging tax revenue from 70/30 to
  50/50 between tourism and local services. That is local budget flexibility,
  and again the nays crossed both caucuses in both chambers.
