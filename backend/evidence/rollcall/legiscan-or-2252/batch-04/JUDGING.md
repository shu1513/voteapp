## HB 4060 runs the other way, and the vote shows it

This is the batch's reversed measure and the session's clearest case of a
**yes vote weakening an existing protection**.

Oregon is phasing out fluorescent lamps because they contain mercury. HB 4060
keeps the ban but adds two groups who may keep buying the lamps: school
districts, and any owner whose Oregon buildings total more than a million
square feet. It also repeals a section of the 2025 phase-out law.

Stance direction follows the **area description**, not the bill's own framing.
`environment_and_public_health` is defined as protecting air, water, climate
and community health through standards and enforcement. Narrowing a mercury
phase-out cuts against that, so the yea scores `against` and the nay `for`.

The roll call corroborates it rather than contradicting it. The eight Senate
nays are mostly Democrats — Campos, Golden, Neron Misslin, Patterson, Pham and
Prozanski — voting to keep the ban intact. This is one of the few measures in
the session where the authored nay stance is `for`.

## SB 1521 is dropped under filter 5

SB 1521 lets Portland-area cities require affordable units in new multiunit
housing **only if** the city pays the developer an offset worth at least the
expected loss in value.

The Act does two things at once: it authorizes the requirement and it
constrains it. Under `housing_affordability` the whole dispute — whether an
inclusionary requirement produces affordable homes or suppresses building —
sits inside that one area. There is no honest way to tell a reader what a yes
vote was for. This is the Arizona SB 1611 shape: where the trade is the dispute
itself, the measure is dropped rather than forced into a direction.

## Other stances

The four remaining measures take `nay: null`. SB 1523's objection is the
administrative burden on landlords; SB 1576's is building cost; HB 4144's is
the cost passed to consumers; SB 1590's is federalism and property rights.
None of those is a position within the measure's own area.

SB 1523 is described with **both** of its effects, including the one that runs
against renters: the Act also widens the electronic payment fees a landlord may
pass on.
