## Version check, per roll

Every description was written from the **enrolled Act**, not from the staff
measure summary, and each chamber's roll was checked against the text that
became law.

- **HB 4128** is the one measure here whose chosen roll is a concurrence. The
  House passed its own text 38-16 in February, the Senate amended it, and the
  House then agreed to the Senate's changes 38-18 on 2026-03-04. That later
  vote is the one on the enacted text, so it is the one recorded. The Senate's
  own vote was 26-2 and not divided, so HB 4128 is House-only.
- **HB 4114** is Senate-only for the same kind of reason: the House passed it
  36-4, which is not divided.
- The other four measures each have one floor roll per chamber, on the same
  engrossed text the Governor signed.

## Why these stances

**HB 4088 and HB 4127 carry an explicit nay stance.** Each Act's whole
mechanism is the research area's own subject, so voting no is a real position
within that area rather than an objection about something else. HB 4088 gets
two labels because it has two policy strands: reproductive care and
gender-affirming care, one label each.

**HB 4111 likewise.** The Act is about immigration from beginning to end.

**HB 4114, HB 4128 and HB 4036 take `nay: null`.** In each, the strongest
argument against sits inside the same research area as the argument for. A
broader right to sue officials can be argued to burden ordinary law
enforcement; limits on investor purchases can be argued to reduce the money
that builds housing; bond-funded preservation can be argued to be a costlier
way to house people than new supply. Where the counter-argument lives inside
the area, the nay voter gets a record of the vote and no stance tag.

That is why the tag count is 326 rather than 352: the 25 nay votes on those
three measures are recorded without a stance.
