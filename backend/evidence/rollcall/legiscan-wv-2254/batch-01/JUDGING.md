# Judging notes, West Virginia 2026 batch-01

Every description was written from the enrolled act. See the 2025 batch's
judging notes for how West Virginia marks changed language and why a plain text
dump of the page is not enough.

## HB 4005, Senate roll 368, 27-7, became law

Titled "Skills to work" in the feed and named the Workforce Development Act of
2026 in the act itself. What it does is delete West Virginia's own list of
seventeen occupations closed to workers under 18 and defer instead to whatever
federal law allows.

The deleted list, read from the committee substitute's strikethrough, covered:
motor vehicle driver and outside helper; explosives and highly flammable
substances; ore reduction, smelters and metal heat treatment; logging and
sawmilling; power-driven woodworking machines; radioactive substances and
ionizing radiation; power-driven hoisting apparatus; power-driven metal-forming,
punching and shearing machines; mining, including coal mining; slaughtering,
meat packing, processing and rendering; power-driven bakery machines;
power-driven paper-products machines; brick and tile manufacture; power-driven
circular saws, band saws and guillotine shears; wrecking, demolition and
ship-breaking; roofing above ground level; and excavation.

Only one of the seventeen survives in state code, and even that is made subject
to whatever federal law permits. The act also lets workers aged 16 and over do
hazardous farm work that federal law allows, and it deletes the rule that limited
a student apprentice to using such machinery "on an occasional and incidental
basis", defined as no more than five percent of training hours a day.

`social_programs_and_welfare`, a yes vote was against, `nay` null.

On the area: there is no labor research area, and this is not an employer
mandate, so the `corporate_accountability` workaround does not apply. The Oregon
precedent, where a floor under the hours a child may work went to
`social_programs_and_welfare`, is the closest fit and is followed here.

On the null nay: the West Virginia Senate has a large Republican majority, so
seven no votes cannot all be opposition from the minority party. A no vote here
cannot be read in one direction, so it carries no stance.

## SB 586, House roll 430, 68-27, became law

Bars the health secretary from requiring a public water system to have a
low-hazard backflow prevention assembly inspected more often than once every
three years. High-hazard assemblies keep annual inspection.

`environment_and_public_health`, a yes vote was against, `nay` null.

The description carries the act's own definitions rather than flattening them.
The act defines a low-hazard assembly as one that could cause only aesthetic
problems or a detrimental secondary effect on water quality, and a high-hazard
assembly as one conducive to introducing waterborne disease organisms or harmful
chemical, physical or radioactive substances. Saying only that the act "weakens
drinking water protection" would be the flattening error the Texas SB 2972 review
caught, so the qualification is stated.

## SB 641, House roll 431 (58-34) and Senate roll 600 (25-8), became law

Narrows which aboveground storage tanks West Virginia regulates under the tank
law it wrote after the 2014 chemical spill on the Elk River that left about
300,000 people without safe tap water.

The act adds a carve-out, paragraph (O) of the definition, for devices holding
10,000 gallons or less that sit inside a permit issued under the surface mining
article, are certified under §22-12-8 (the groundwater certification in the
Groundwater Protection Act), are outside a zone of critical concern, and hold
none of the substances listed in subdivision (13)(B). Paragraph (O) then says
such devices "shall meet the registration, notice, and signage requirements of
this article". It gives owners a defined notice period before an existing tank
is reclassified when a new zone of critical concern or zone of peripheral
concern is drawn. It allows remote non-destructive examination in place of a
person entering a confined space.

**Review fix, 2026-09-08.** The first description called these tanks "exempt"
and omitted two limits that paragraph (O) states: the groundwater certification,
and the proviso that the tanks must still be registered, give notice and post
signs. Read cold, "exempt" suggested the whole tank law fell away. Both
descriptions were rewritten to say what is carved out and what is kept, the
judgments were re-run, and all 83 records were rewritten in place
(`import-review-fixes-report.json`, 83 rewrite and 86 unchanged), followed by a
convergence run reporting all 169 unchanged. The rewritten text runs to five
short sentences rather than four; the extra sentence is the retained duties, and
the longest sentence is 41 words.

`environment_and_public_health`, a yes vote was against, `nay` null.

**Both chambers voted the same text, and the earlier Senate roll did not.** The
Senate first passed the bill on 4 March, roll 334. That version exempted brine
and produced-fluid tanks of "less than 50,000 gallons". The House committee
amendment cut the exemption to "10,000 gallons or less" and added the
substances-listed condition, so the House made the bill less deregulatory than
the Senate had. A word-by-word comparison of the engrossed and enrolled texts
confirmed this.

The House roll used here, 431 on 12 March, is the vote on the enrolled text. The
Senate roll used here, 600 on 13 March, is its agreement to the House version,
which is also the enrolled text. Roll 334 is not used, because it was cast on a
text that did not become law. This is the Illinois rule: import a divided roll
only when it is that chamber's vote on the text that became law.
