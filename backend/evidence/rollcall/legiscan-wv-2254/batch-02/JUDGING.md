# Judging notes, West Virginia 2026 regular session, batch 02

Every bill text here was read on the state's own site, wvlegislature.gov. West
Virginia dates every text version 0000-00-00, so the usual dated check on which
text a chamber voted cannot be run. The action history and the version names
carry the sequence instead.

## Which text each chamber voted

- **HB 4081** — the House voted the engrossed text. One floor amendment had
  broadened the food category by six words. Nothing else changed.
- **HB 4198** — the House voted the engrossed committee substitute. This matters,
  because the committee substitute deleted the whole complaint and investigation
  system the introduced bill built, including its ban on investigating complaints
  based on race or national origin, and replaced it with a penalty and licensing
  ladder. The description covers only what was voted.
- **HB 5319** — the House voted the engrossed committee substitute. The
  substitute softened the penalties at the front end, so the description gives
  the warning, then the fine, then the jail term, in that order.
- **SB 420** — the Senate voted the committee substitute. No engrossed version
  exists. The substitute changed a hard minimum coal output requirement into a
  target backed by rate treatment, so the description says "pushed", not
  "required".
- **SB 963** — the Senate voted the committee substitute. The substitute lowered
  the standard of proof from clear and convincing evidence to a preponderance of
  the evidence and added two routes that skip the hearing, so the description
  gives all three routes rather than the introduced bill's single hearing.

## What stage each measure reached

None became law, and each description says so plainly.

- HB 4081, HB 5319 and SB 420 passed one chamber and the other never voted.
- SB 963 passed the Senate. The only House roll call on it was a procedural
  motion to take a discharge motion off the table, which the House rejected 17 to
  77. That is not a vote on the bill, so the record says the House never voted on
  passage.
- HB 4198 passed both chambers in different forms. The Senate passed an amended
  version 31 to 3 and the House never agreed to those changes. The record says
  so.

## Why each imported measure carries the label it does

- **HB 4081, campus pantries** — `social_programs_and_welfare`, yes for. Direct
  state aid for basic necessities.
- **HB 4198, E-Verify mandate** — `immigration`, yes against. The bill requires
  every employer to run every new hire through the federal work authorization
  system, requires firing a worker whose check does not clear, and builds a
  ladder to permanent loss of a business license. Its practical effect is
  restriction, which is the direction against a research area whose stated goal
  is a welcoming lawful system. The name "Safe Harbor Act" describes one
  subsection, not the bill.
- **HB 5319, camping ban** — `social_programs_and_welfare`, yes against. This was
  the closest label call in the batch. The measure could also be read as a public
  safety measure. It was labeled the other way because the bill's own text says
  that having no shelter available is not a defense, which forecloses the
  safety-net reading on its face.
- **SB 420, First Energy Act** — `environment_and_public_health`, yes against.
  Minimum coal plant output, a state veto over retiring a coal or gas plant, and
  no cost recovery for new wind or solar. The bill's own title claims rate
  stability, but nothing in the text lowers a bill, so no cost-of-living label
  was used.
- **SB 963, firearm confiscation** — `gun_control`, yes against. It narrows when
  a domestic violence protective order may take a person's guns.

## The measures dropped for having no honest direction

- **HB 4481** is titled an accountability act and does give the utility regulator
  access to load forecast data. Its committee version also creates a new
  exemption from the open records law for large power customers, and says the
  regulator must protect that data without any hearing on whether withholding it
  is lawful. A yes vote is honestly arguable in both directions.
- **HB 4602** hires private contractors to do child welfare paperwork.
  Privatizing a public service has no settled direction, the same problem that
  keeps school choice financing out of this dataset.
- **HB 5260** allows medical cannabis edibles. It expands patient access and also
  puts a product into circulation that the bill itself treats as a child safety
  risk needing six paragraphs of restrictions.
- **HB 4462** bans the sale of cell-cultured food products, which reads as
  consumer protection or as protecting one industry from a competitor.
- **SB 888** decides whether courts must defer to a state agency reading of a
  statute. That is a rule about judicial method, not a policy a voter takes a
  side on.
