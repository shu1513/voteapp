# Indiana 2026 batch-03 — selection

Ten measures were read in full. Three were kept, giving five rolls and seven labels. Seven were
dropped, and two rolls of kept measures were dropped as version traps.

This batch is where the 2026 session turns into omnibus work, and the keep rate falls
accordingly. Of the ten, six carry a title beginning "Various" or naming a whole agency.

No AI provider was called at any point.

## Kept

| Measure | Area | Yes means | Rolls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HB 1001, housing matters | `housing_affordability` for, `environment_and_public_health` against | see below | House 72-21, Senate 35-13 |
| HB 1003, boards and commissions | `government_efficiency` | for | House 69-28 |
| SB 277, department of environmental management | `environment_and_public_health` | against | House 53-45, Senate 26-21 |

**HB 1001** carries two labels because it does two things. It makes every local unit publish
what it approves, refuses and how long it takes, plus prices, rents and affordability bands,
which is transparency aimed squarely at supply: `housing_affordability` `for`. It also strips
requirements. Arc-fault breakers may no longer be required in multi-family buildings, emergency
responder radio systems may no longer be required in large new buildings, the storm water
manual may no longer demand both a maintenance and a safety ledge, and floodplain fill needs no
more than three-to-one compensatory storage: `environment_and_public_health` `against`.

**HB 1003** is a 515-section rewrite, and it is kept on one label only. About 39 boards end,
several transfer wholesale to departments that already exist, and four identity commissions
merge into one. That is `government_efficiency` and the direction is unambiguous. Every other
area the act touches is either a side effect of consolidation or internally contradictory, most
sharply in building safety, where the act abolishes the rulemaking body but freezes its rules
against the department that inherits them. Two counter-strands are named in the description:
the natural resources commission loses both its party-balance cap and its requirement that two
members have environmental knowledge, and the college savings board loses its party cap too.

**SB 277** is single-subject and clearly directional despite its size. 84 repeals, more than two
dozen conversions of a duty into a choice, the end of Indiana's own environmental impact review,
the repeal of the state air operating permit chapter, a new bar on relying solely on federal
health risk values not adopted through federal rulemaking, a narrowed citizen petition right,
and the loss of the rules board's party cap and its members' for-cause removal protection. The
one substantial counter-strand is real and is stated in the description: the act gathers
scattered appeal rights into a single list and, for the first time, makes the agency's failure
to act reviewable.

## Every nay side is null

A no vote on HB 1001 may object to the reporting burden on small towns rather than to the
dropped building requirements. A no vote on HB 1003 may object to losing a particular board
rather than to consolidation. A no vote on SB 277 may object to the appeal-rights consolidation
or to the treatment of one industry rather than to deregulation as such.

## Dropped

**SB 179, department of transportation.** An omnibus with five unrelated subjects: county wheel
and vehicle excise taxes, the local road and bridge matching grant fund, the Indianapolis street
transfer, the department taking over federal environmental review, truck size and weight, and an
entirely new land surveying coordinate article. A yes vote is for public infrastructure, against
environment and public health, and for government efficiency, all at once. Its only usable roll
is also a version trap: the House passed a bill containing a design-build procurement package, a
county-councillor earmark ban and a township finance package, none of which became law.

**HB 1004, various education matters.** 27,000 words of change, most of it recodifying IC 20-20
into a new IC 20-20.5. A recodification with substantive riders has no single direction.

**HB 1003 is kept and HB 1033 is not**, which is worth saying plainly, because both are
consolidation-shaped. HB 1033, various judicial matters, rebuilds the governance of one court.
It adds a suitability screen a judge must pass before appearing on a retention ballot, and at
the same time deletes the requirement that a judge maintain a high standard of morality, deletes
the diversity factor from nominations, moves four attorney seats from bar associations to the
governor and chief justice, and makes committee deliberations confidential. "More vetting" and
"more political control" are both defensible from the same text. It also carries three sections
on retired judges carrying firearms, which are unrelated to everything else.

**HB 1302, various labor and safety matters.** Two unrelated halves. It repeals the public
database of employers of 14 to 17 year olds, and separately eliminates the state certificate for
coal mine belt examiners. One is a paperwork and transparency question, the other is mine
safety, and they point in different directions.

**HB 1052, various administrative law matters.** Horse racing commission housekeeping, a new
civil penalty for internet sweepstakes games, alcohol permit changes and tobacco sales
certificates. Several unrelated subjects.

**HB 1038, gaming matters** and **SB 23, alcoholic beverage permits for county fairs.** Casino
licensing and alcohol licensing map to no research area.

## Two rolls dropped on kept measures

HB 1003's own third reading of 2026-02-02 predates both the Senate's work and the conference
committee. SB 277's own Senate third reading of 2026-01-29 is a version trap in both directions:
that text repealed Indiana's nuclear facility permit chapter and turned four more agency duties
into choices, neither of which survived, and it did not contain the federal risk values
restriction that the House added.

## A process error worth recording

Two of the read requests in this batch carried the wrong roll dates, taken from a January third
reading rather than the February concurrence the worklist actually holds. The reader flagged the
mismatch rather than working from the wrong text. Both measures were dropped on their merits, so
nothing turned on it, but the check is the reason it cost nothing: **the roll date in a read
request should be copied from the worklist, not inferred from the bill history.**
