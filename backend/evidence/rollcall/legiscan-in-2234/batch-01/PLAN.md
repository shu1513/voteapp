# Indiana 2026 batch-01 — selection

Ten measures were read in full from their enrolled acts. Six were kept, giving eight rolls.
Four were dropped, and one roll of a kept measure was dropped as a version trap.

The reading used the annotated renderings in `../../legiscan-in-2143/tools/`, which mark
what a bill adds and what it deletes. Plain `pdftotext` flattens both, and that is what made
an earlier Indiana description materially wrong. No AI provider was called at any point.

## Kept

| Measure | Area | Yes means | Rolls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SB 284, law enforcement civilian oversight boards | `public_safety_and_crime_control` | against | House 70-25 |
| SB 112, candidacy documents | `election_integrity` | for | House 72-24 |
| HB 1274, insurance mandated gun free zone notices | `gun_control` | against | House 69-25 |
| SB 258, nuclear facility permits | `environment_and_public_health` | against | House 64-28, Senate 36-9 |
| SB 285, housing matters | `social_programs_and_welfare` | against | House 53-44, Senate 28-22 |
| HB 1193, civil rights commission | `civil_rights` | against | House 66-29 |

Five of the six score `against`. That is what the session's single-subject work looks like;
it is not a thumb on the scale. SB 112 is the one `for`, and it is also the thinnest measure
in the batch, which is said plainly in its own note below.

**SB 284** adds IC 36-8-29.1. Any civilian body a unit sets up to oversee a law enforcement
agency, whether it already existed on 1 July 2026 or is created later, "may operate only in
an advisory capacity" and "does not have binding authority". Merit boards under IC 36-8-3.5
are carved out. The area's own description names accountability, and this act removes the
binding form of it. There is no counter-strand in the text: all four sections run one way.

**SB 112** adds IC 3-5-7-6.3. When a candidate files a notice of withdrawal listing an
address different from the one on their voter registration, the signature counts as a request
to update the registration. This is a records-accuracy provision, and accuracy is named in
the area's own description, so it scores `for`. It is a narrow measure and the description
does not pretend otherwise.

**HB 1274** adds IC 27-2-30. A property and casualty insurer may not require an insured or
applicant to post a gun free zone notice. This is a judgment call worth stating: the area's
description lists background checks, licensing and safe storage as its mechanisms, and a
sign is none of those. It is scored under the area's goal clause instead, because the act
removes a lever that restricted where firearms may be present. Anyone re-reading this batch
should check that call first.

**SB 258** repeals Indiana's own nuclear permit chapter almost entirely: the permit
requirement itself (IC 13-15-9-1), the board's power to write rules on air and water
pollution from these plants (IC 13-15-9-2), the public hearing at which affected people could
be heard on environmental effects (IC 13-15-9-4), and the radiation-protection standards
including the maximum allowable discharge, monitoring and discharge reporting (IC 13-15-9-5).
The environmental feasibility report in IC 13-15-9-3 survives, redirected from the board to
the department. That survival is a preserved requirement, not a new protection, so it does
not offset the repeals. The two deletions of "except IC 13-15-9" elsewhere are conforming
cleanup, since the chapter they excepted no longer has a permit in it.

**SB 285** creates the crime of street camping in new IC 36-1-31.5, bars a political
subdivision from adopting or enforcing any policy that discourages enforcement of camping
bans, and widens "gravely disabled" in IC 12-7-2-96 so that lacking fixed shelter and
remaining outdoors in dangerous weather, after refusing an offered shelter, can support
involuntary commitment. The counter-strands are real and are stated in the description: the
officer must check for emergency detention grounds first, must warn and give shelter
information, the offence needs 48 hours and a 300-foot radius, diversion is available, and it
is a defence that no bed was open within five miles or that the person left a mental health
facility within six months. They constrain the mechanism; they do not reverse it.

**HB 1193** is the closest call in the batch and is kept with its counter-strand stated. It
widens what counts as a discriminatory practice, adding retaliation and violations of Titles
VI, VII and IX. It then withdraws the Civil Rights Commission from representing individuals
in five separate places, shifts the duty to file the fair housing suit onto the complainant
on a 30-day clock, strikes monetary damages from the commission's own pattern-or-practice
remedy, and makes referral to another agency with jurisdiction mandatory. The area is
"anti-discrimination enforcement", and enforcement is the half the act removes. The expansion
is definitional; the narrowing is operative and remedial. That asymmetry is why a direction
could be assigned here and not on SB 91.

## Every nay side is null

No measure in this batch met the test for authoring a nay stance, which asks that the act be
single-subject, that its whole operative content be the area's own mechanism, and that the
mainstream objection be to that mechanism rather than to cost, structure or a rider.

SB 258 came closest. Every operative section is environmental permitting, hearings and
radiation standards, which is the area's own mechanism, and both plausible no-vote readings
point the same way. It was still left null, because a legislator could also vote no on a view
about nuclear power itself rather than about the state's environmental role in it. On the
others the honest alternative readings are plain: a no vote on SB 284 or SB 285 may object to
the state overriding local government choice; a no vote on HB 1274 may object to the state
rewriting private insurance contracts; a no vote on HB 1193 may object to bringing federal
Title VI, VII and IX claims into state law.

## Dropped, and why

**SB 12, prohibition of ranked choice voting** (House 58-30). The only area that fits is
`election_integrity`, and the argument runs in both directions inside it. A ban can be
defended as removing a tabulation method that is harder to hand-audit, and opposed as
removing a method that better records what voters wanted. Neither direction is defensible
alone, which is the failure the 2025 batch-04 documented.

**SB 3, constitutional amendment ballot language** (House 63-22, Senate 36-9). This act
submits the bail amendment to the electors and prescribes the ballot wording. A vote to put a
question to the voters is not a vote on the question. Attributing a stance on pretrial
detention to it would be unfair to every legislator who voted only to let the electorate
decide.

**HB 1377, straight ticket voting** (House 71-20 and 69-27). Two strands run opposite inside
`election_integrity`. The act restores the straight party mark for multi-seat at-large
offices, which is a convenience expansion. It also rewrites IC 3-12-1-7(d) so that a voter
who casts a straight party ticket and then also marks one same-party candidate in a multi-seat
race loses the straight party vote for that office entirely. In a three-seat race that turns
three votes into one. A rule that discards votes a voter plainly intended cuts against the
area, and the two cannot be summed into one direction.

**SB 91, syringe exchange program** (House 70-22, Senate 33-13 and 38-10). The act narrows the
program hard: a 1,000-foot siting ban near schools, child care centers and places of
worship, strict one-for-one exchange replacing the old bar on recording identifying
information, a residency requirement, and photo identification to take part. It also moves the
chapter's expiry from 1 July 2026 to 1 July 2031. Without this act there would have been no
programme at all after July. Telling a voter their representative voted to restrict syringe
programmes would be misleading, and so would telling them the opposite. No single direction is
honest.

**HB 1193's own third reading of 2026-01-28** (House 71-20) is dropped as a version trap, not
under filter 5. The text the House passed that day also made any violation of the United
States or Indiana Constitution a discriminatory practice the commission could act on, and the
enrolled act drops that; the enrolled act also adds a mandatory referral duty that was not in
the House text. The 2026-02-26 concurrence is the vote on the enacted text and is the one used.

**SB 285's own Senate third reading of 2026-01-28** (Senate 29-19) is marked superseded. The
House rewrote the bill afterwards, and word-level comparison puts that text at 0.55 similarity
to the enrolled act. The Senate's concurrence of 2026-02-26 is its final action and is used
instead.
