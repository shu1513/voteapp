# Indiana 2026 batch-04 — a batch that kept nothing

Eleven measures were read in full from their enrolled acts. **None was kept.** Nothing was
judged and nothing was imported, so the database is unchanged by this batch. This directory
holds the selection reasoning only.

This is the second zero-keep batch in the campaign, after the 2025 batch-04, and it arrives for
the same reason: what remains of the session is omnibus work. Six of the eleven carry a title
beginning "Various" or naming a whole agency, and the other five turned out to be omnibus in
substance whatever their titles said.

No AI provider was called at any point.

## The one that hurt

**HB 1408, education matters,** was the closest thing to a keep in the session's tail, and it is
lost on a version trap rather than on its merits.

Its title is about education, and most of it is Ivy Tech Community College governance: campus
strategic plans with measurable goals, performance dashboards at every board meeting, an annual
labour market analysis, chancellor evaluation tools, and a duty to review building utilisation
before approving capital requests. Riding alongside that is an entire new article, IC 24-16,
regulating social media for under-sixteens. It requires verifiable parental consent to open an
account, turns off algorithmic content selection based on a child's use patterns, turns off the
named addictive features, restricts who may message or find a minor, requires continuing age
estimation, and bars a provider from using parental-consent data for anything else. That is
`data_privacy`'s own mechanism, and it would have carried a label.

**The social media article is not in the text the House voted on.** HB 1408's only divided roll
is the House third reading of 2026-02-02, taken on the House committee text of 2026-01-27. The
comparison against the enrolled act puts the two at 0.48 similarity across 52 differing runs,
and a direct search confirms IC 24-16 appears nowhere in the text the House passed. With no
other divided roll, the measure is lost. Had the check not been run, this batch would have
attributed a social media privacy law to a vote taken before it existed.

## The rest, and why each was dropped

**SB 88, education matters.** This one changed under reading. It publishes a directory of schools
where a non-enrolled student may sit the SAT, ACT, PSAT or armed services aptitude test, which is
a plain access improvement, and it adds the Classic Learning Test to the accepted list. Read in
full it also writes into good citizenship instruction a required lesson on "research based
actions that data indicates result in economic prosperity", the third of which is "waiting until
marriage to begin having children". A curriculum mandate of that kind is not something
`public_education_quality` can carry in one direction alongside two unrelated administrative
items.

**SB 200, public school matters.** Two unrelated halves. It voids indemnity, choice-of-law and
automatic-renewal clauses in school vendor contracts, which is a genuine `corporate_accountability`
strand. It also requires a school that lets colleges present to students to let military
recruiters present on the same terms, with a grievance route. The second half maps to no research
area, and the first is three contract clauses.

**HB 1423, Indianapolis public education corporation.** The largest single-subject measure among
the drops, and the hardest call. It creates a new municipal corporation for Indianapolis whose
nine members the mayor appoints, and moves the elected school board's taxing, levying, bonding,
referendum, capital, transportation, enrolment and school-rating powers into it. It then exempts
that appointed body from the three statutes that normally make an appointed taxing board clear
its budget and bonds with an elected fiscal body. The dominant, clearest textual effect is a
transfer of authority from a directly elected board to a mayoral appointment, and no research
area covers that. `government_efficiency` is the only area the text supports on its own words,
and there it would restate the act's stated purpose rather than a verified effect. The
counterweights are real but partial: the elected board keeps a closure veto and a bonding veto
that expires in June 2027, and three of the nine seats must go to sitting elected members.

**HB 1343, public safety matters.** Ninety-one sections, of which about thirty are a mechanical
cross-reference swap with no policy content. The substantive parts pull apart: a new National
Guard military police force with arrest, search and firearm powers in aid of civil law; a new
civilian cyber corps whose work product is exempt from the public records act; a new Class B
misdemeanor for an imitation firearm at school; a rewrite of the military family relief fund
that widens eligibility while adding an income ceiling, an asset test and a fault test; and a
doubling of the county jail medical copay from $15 to $30.

**HB 1406, tax and fiscal matters.** The House passed a one-section bill about property tax
statements, 92 to 0. It came out of conference with twenty-nine sections, including a new local
food and beverage tax for one city, a mandatory $35 million a year credit stream to development
authorities, a small town redevelopment credit with no repayment obligation, a bar on tax credits
to foreign adversary owned taxpayers, a retroactive change keeping utilities on a higher property
tax valuation floor, and tighter library levy review. It is a conference omnibus and its own
title says so.

**HB 1210, department of local government finance.** 297 sections across property tax
administration, local income tax, innkeeper and food and beverage taxes, tax increment financing,
local bonds, public notice, elections administration, gaming, pensions and a preemption on local
rental restrictions. It creates new local taxes in one place and tightens local income tax caps in
another. No single direction is honest.

**HB 1266, SB 199, SB 204 and SB 239** are the education omnibus group. Between them they carry
reporting timeline changes, board membership tweaks, two study-and-report duties that expire in
2027, an alternative charter teacher licensure path, a repeal of a financial aid report, charter
school conversion of existing public schools, innovation network funding mechanics and authorizer
conflict rules. Each is several unrelated items, and the licensure and charter conversion strands
are contested in both directions inside `public_education_quality`.

## What this leaves

After this batch the 2026 worklist has **no unbatched measures left**. Every one of the 47
divided-and-enacted measures has been read and dispositioned: seventeen kept across batches 01 to
03, thirty dropped or superseded.
