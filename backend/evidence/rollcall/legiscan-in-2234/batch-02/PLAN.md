# Indiana 2026 batch-02 — selection

Fourteen measures were read in full from their enrolled acts. Eight were kept, giving eleven
rolls and thirteen labels. Six were dropped. Five further rolls were dropped because their
member lists failed the check, and five more because they are version traps or superseded.

No AI provider was called at any point.

## Kept

| Measure | Area | Yes means | Rolls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SB 78, school wireless device policy | `public_education_quality` | for | Senate 36-12 |
| HB 1355, confined feeding operations | `environment_and_public_health` | against | House 73-22 |
| HB 1424, farm and homestead food sales | `environment_and_public_health` | against | Senate 38-10 |
| HB 1273, proxy advisors | `corporate_accountability` | against | House 72-20 |
| SB 76, immigration matters | `immigration` | against | House 61-28, Senate 37-11 |
| HB 1150, local regulation | `data_privacy` for, `environment_and_public_health` against | see below | House 72-19, Senate 37-10 |
| HB 1368, carbon | `environment_and_public_health` | for | House 57-40, Senate 29-19 |
| SB 270, township mergers | `government_efficiency` | for | Senate 34-15 |

**SB 78** moves the school phone ban from class time to the whole school day and forces each
school to choose a no-device or a stored-device policy. The counter-strand is real and stated:
the old blanket permission for emergency and health use is replaced by narrower named
exemptions, one of which needs the superintendent's say-so.

**HB 1355** is the closest call among the `against` measures. Three separate provisions begin
"the department may not require", which is the direction of the act. Two new duties run the
other way: solid-manure farms must control runoff, and anyone building an earth wall around
manure storage must stabilise it, keep it inspectable, and keep vegetation 16 feet clear.
Those constrain the mechanism without reversing it, and the description says so.

**HB 1424** removes licensing, inspection and time-and-temperature rules for producers up to
$1.5 million in sales, meat included, and bars health agencies from adding anything beyond
federal law. It keeps labelling with allergens, a mandatory notice that the seller is
unlicensed and uninspected, complaint investigation, refrigeration duties, and a food handler
certificate gate on shipping. Those are stated.

**HB 1273** is kept because its trigger is one-sided, and that asymmetry is what makes a
direction assignable. Every duty fires only when the advisor recommends against company
management. An advisor recommending with management, on no analysis at all, owes nothing.
Read as transparency alone the act would be neutral; read against its own trigger it raises
the cost of advising shareholders to vote against management.

**SB 76** mandates local compliance with federal immigration detainers, penalises
non-cooperative local governments up to $10,000 per knowing violation, lowers the threshold
for an injunction from knowing to any proved violation, and puts employer licences at risk.
The counter-strands are named in the description: a detained person may object on mistaken
identity or citizenship and the jail must then check, and an employer that checked work
eligibility with due care is not in violation.

**HB 1150** is the batch's one two-label measure, and the labels point opposite ways because
the act does two unrelated things. Barring a homeowners association from running a licence
plate reader, and allowing one only where police install it and the association cannot see the
data, is a limit on collection, which is `data_privacy`'s own mechanism: `for`. Barring
counties and cities from requiring building energy and water reporting, and extending the
fuel-source preemption to local rules, removes local environmental authority:
`environment_and_public_health` `against`. The fuel-source rule is written symmetrically, and
that is said in the note rather than hidden.

**HB 1368** is the hardest call in the batch and is kept `for`. The act is Indiana's
application to take over federal Class VI well permitting, and the bulk of its new language is
protective: a bar on the state writing off an aquifer as a drinking water source, mandatory
baseline groundwater chemistry, both the state environment agency and the federal agency
having to concur in writing before any depth waiver, an injection pressure cap at 90% of
fracture pressure, a 50-year post-injection monitoring floor, 25 mandatory findings including
that the project will not harm surface water or aquifers and is in the public interest, a
mandatory hearing with mailed notice to owners within half a mile, per-day fines and a new
criminal offence. Two changes cut the other way and are named in the description itself: the
old 90% purity floor on what may be injected is repealed, and coal is struck from the
resources whose rights outrank carbon storage. Measured by volume the protections run to
thousands of words and the counter-items to a few hundred, which is why a direction was
assigned rather than the measure dropped.

**SB 270** forces consolidation of townships scored by the state on missed reports, aid not
provided, rolled-over budgets and tiny budgets. Within `government_efficiency` the direction is
plain. The cost is equally plain and is stated in the description: elected township offices
end, the county executive rather than the voters picks the mergers, the state's scoring cannot
be appealed, and there is no referendum anywhere. That cost lands outside the 27 research
areas, so it could not be carried as a second label; it is carried in the description instead.

## Every nay side is null

No measure met the test for authoring a nay stance. The honest alternative readings are plain
in each case. A no vote on SB 76 or SB 270 may object to the state overriding local government.
A no vote on HB 1424 or HB 1355 may object to the state regulating farms at all. A no vote on
HB 1368 may object to carbon storage itself rather than to the permitting programme. A no vote
on SB 78 may object to taking a phone from a child who may need it.

## Dropped under filter 5

**HB 1042, cryptocurrency** (three rolls). A mandate that six public pension plans offer a
member-directed crypto option, plus a bar on every state agency except the financial regulator,
and on every local unit, restricting digital asset use, self-custody, mining or staking. No
research area maps this subject. The private-key court provision touches `data_privacy` but is
two sentences of a sixteen-section act.

**HB 1062, wake boarding and wake surfing.** A time and place restriction on a watersport.
Recreational boating regulation fits no area.

**HB 1155, traffic enforcement.** A pilot letting one very large subdivision set speed limits
and stop signs on its private roads, with police able to enforce them. It expires in 2028 and
is not policing policy in any area's sense.

**HB 1389, adoption and foster care matters.** The act bars government from penalising a
person who provides, or declines to provide, adoption or foster services consistent with a
sincerely held religious belief, with strict scrutiny, a private right of action and damages.
It is an antidiscrimination statute whose protected characteristic is religious belief, and
whose effect is to remove a lever the state could use to require equal service. Both readings
sit inside `civil_rights`, so no direction is honest.

**HB 1115, homeowners association governance.** On balance it gives owners more power, but its
subject is association governance procedure, not housing supply or cost, and
`housing_affordability` is the only near fit. Its own content is also genuinely mixed: it
writes the board's fining power into statute for the first time, carves out fees for optional
services, and deletes the provision letting governing documents demand 95% approval before
common areas are sold or the association dissolved.

**SB 176, shooting ranges.** Zoning preemption. It makes retail sporting goods stores and
indoor shooting ranges permitted uses by right and voids conflicting local ordinances. It does
not regulate firearm access, so `gun_control` does not reach it.

## Rolls dropped, on measures that were kept

Five rolls were dropped as version traps or superseded, each verified by word-level comparison
against the enrolled act. SB 78's own Senate reading predates the House rewrite. HB 1355's own
House reading predates the Senate rework. HB 1424's House reading predates the Senate rewrite.
HB 1273's House reading predates the two coverage exemptions the enrolled act adds. HB 1150's
House reading predates both the flag chapter and the licence plate reader chapter.

## The five flagged rolls all failed

All five rolls the worklist flagged were checked name by name against Indiana's own roll-call
PDF, and **all five disagree**, each with a member recorded on the wrong side. The 2025
batch-04 established that such a roll is unusable whichever direction the error runs, so none
was judged. SB 0001 and SB 0271 have no other divided roll, so those two measures are lost
outright. HB 1368 and SB 270 each keep another roll, which is what let them into this batch.
SB 176 also keeps another roll, but the measure failed filter 5 anyway.

In this session the flag has been a perfect predictor: every flagged roll failed the check, and
no unflagged roll has.
