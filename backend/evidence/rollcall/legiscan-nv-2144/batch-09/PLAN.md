# Nevada batch-09 — the last batch, and why Nevada's big election bills are not in it

**6 measures / 11 roll calls / 230 records / 41 candidates.** This batch finishes Nevada
session 2144. Every one of the 187 divided non-enacted rolls now carries a disposition.

## The six measures

| measure | area | rolls | what it would have done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AB 119 | public_safety_and_crime_control / for | house 27-15 | lets the Attorney General sue to stop armed private paramilitary groups |
| AB 306 | election_integrity / for | house 28-14, senate 12-8 | extra mail ballot drop-off locations between early voting and election day |
| AB 496 | election_integrity / for | house 26-15, senate 13-8 | daily public counts of mail ballots and signature cures |
| SB 155 | immigration / for | house 27-15, senate 13-7 | lets an experienced out-of-state officer without citizenship be hired as a peace officer |
| SB 156 | gun_control / for | house 25-14, senate 11-8 | a Special Counsel for the Prevention of Gun Violence |
| SB 414 | anti_corruption / for | house 27-15, senate 13-8 | wider financial disclosure by officials, and rules for inauguration committees |

Every label carries `"nay": null`.

AB 119 appears with the Assembly only: it passed 27-15 and a Senate committee let it die at a
deadline, which is the Pennsylvania class.

**AB 496** and **SB 414** each needed a chamber-specific description. On AB 496 the Assembly
voted a version that also required a voter to print their initials on the mail ballot
envelope, which the Senate removed. On SB 414 the Assembly voted a much larger version that
adds political appointees as filers, requires investment securities over $5,000 to be
listed, and deletes the rule letting officials leave out-of-state property off the form.

## Five measures dropped, and they are the biggest election bills of the session

This is the striking result of the batch. Nevada's 2025 election bills were negotiated
packages between a Democratic legislature and a Republican governor, and **the largest of
them contain real expansions and real restrictions side by side.** Filter 5 removes all of
them, and the reasons are worth stating rather than burying in the worklist.

**AB 499 — the voter identification bill.** The two chambers did not vote the same bill in
any meaningful sense. The Assembly's version contains no photo identification requirement at
all; it is a portal, data-matching and signature-cure package whose only voter-facing change
is expressly "A space for the voter to **voluntarily** write" an identifying number. The
Senate's version deletes that word, makes the number mandatory, and adds a photo
identification requirement to vote in person. It also drops the voter registration card from
the accepted list, deletes the option of bringing an adult to vouch for a challenged voter,
and stops counting a ballot marked with a cross or check. In the same bill it provides free
identification cards on an attestation of hardship with no proof required, a free digital
card, at least ten extra drop-off locations in Clark County, and $3.75 million. A single
section makes the identifying number mandatory and, in the next sentence, turns it into a
rescue path for a ballot whose signature was rejected. No honest label fits.

**AB 534 — the Secretary of State's 82-section omnibus.** It moves the in-person
registration deadline later while cutting four days off the window in which an online
registrant can still receive a mail ballot. It protects voters against mass challenges built
from database data, and adds a route to cancel a registration on a relative's affidavit of
death with no death certificate. It replaces guaranteed newspaper notice with whatever the
clerk "deems proper", mandates machine counting, and exempts exit pollsters from two
voter-protection rules. At least 13 distinct subjects.

**SB 74 — the elections omnibus.** Section 24 requires the clerk to send a mail ballot to a
voter who updates registration 14 or more days out, and in the very next subsection limits a
voter who updates later to voting in person. The two chambers voted **opposite** changes to
the electioneering zone: the Senate shrank it by measuring from the voting area, the Assembly
enlarged it by measuring from the building. At least 24 subjects.

**SB 422 — registration identification and DMV hours.** It newly accepts out-of-state,
territorial and tribal identification and creates a path for someone with no license at all,
which is a real expansion. It also adds an 18-day cutoff for automatic voter registration,
requires a utility bill or paycheck to be dated within 60 days, and converts what used to be
a completed registration into a provisional ballot that dies unless cured by 5 p.m. on the
Friday after the election.

**AB 79 — campaign finance.** It creates a $5,000 special-election contribution cap backed by
a felony, and in the same bill removes the Attorney General from campaign finance enforcement
entirely, removes court prosecution, drops court costs and attorney's fees from what a
violator owes, lets the Secretary of State reduce rather than only waive fines, and expressly
permits spending contributions on "any ordinary and necessary expense".

Recording these with `general` and no stance was considered and rejected. Unlike
redistricting or legislative pay, where the vote is a single recognizable question with no
defensible direction, these are packages whose halves point opposite ways; a no-stance record
would still have to say what the bill did, and any short summary of these bills favors one
half over the other.
