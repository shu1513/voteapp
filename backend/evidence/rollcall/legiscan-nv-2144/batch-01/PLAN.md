# Nevada batch-01 — selection

**Nothing is judged or imported yet.** This document records which votes were
picked, which were dropped and why, and everything the reading of the enacted
acts turned up, so the judging step does not have to read them again. Texas
batch-01 was committed the same way.

without a signature, and none is a joint resolution.

## How these were chosen

The five standard filters, in order.

1. **Divided.** The losing side is at least a quarter of the winning side.
2. **It became law.** 104 divided rolls on 73 measures clear filters 1 and 2.
3. **A nameable subject that maps to a research area.**
4. **One roll per measure per chamber**, and it must be the roll that stands.
5. **A defensible direction.** A measure whose own text pushes both ways in the
   same area is dropped, not filed under the nearest label.

## Two rolls dropped on filter 4, for version rather than subject

Nevada records no concurrence vote. When the second chamber amends a bill, the
first chamber concurs without a roll call, so its only recorded vote can sit on
text that did not become law. Twenty-five of the 104 pool rolls are in that
position. Where the difference is describable, both rolls are kept and each
description names its own version. Where the chamber's only divided vote
predates a change that would make the description wrong, the roll goes.

- **SB 347, Senate roll dropped.** The Senate approved taking any firearm "in
  the custody or control or immediate vicinity" of a person on a mental-health
  hold — language reaching guns at home. Assembly Amendment 579 narrowed it to
  "on the person or in the immediate vicinity". The Senate never voted again.
- **SB 246, Senate roll dropped.** The Senate's only vote was 20-0, so it fails
  the divided gate anyway, and it was cast on a bill that expressly said the
  **opposite** of the law on one of its two rights: the introduced text barred a
  woman from naming an obstetrician or gynaecologist as her primary care doctor.
  Assembly Amendment 564 struck that bar in four statutes and added the right.

## Two measures dropped on filter 5

**AB 396, accessory dwelling units and homeowner associations.** This is the
session's biggest housing measure and the drop should be expected to be
questioned. One half forces Clark and Washoe Counties and the five largest
cities to legalise accessory dwelling units, with a self-executing default
legalising them everywhere if a government misses July 1 2026, and the
Legislature's own findings in section 10.5 call such units "vital to increasing
the supply of affordable housing throughout this State". The other half runs the
opposite way **inside the same area**: it lets a homeowners association amend its
declaration to restrict leasing, lets its leasing rules override its own
governing documents, and deletes three existing protections outright — the bar
on requiring association approval before an owner rents, the bar on amending a
declaration to lower a rental cap, and the ban on tenant-registration fees and
demands for information not asked of owner-occupants. A counter-directional
strand inside the same area beats a de-minimis reading. Importing it with no
stance is not available either: the September 2026 label rule bars `general` on
roll-call records, because that tag is hidden from every legislative view.

**SB 443, prevailing wages on utility construction.** Two independent reasons.
It is a labour-standards measure and there is no labour or union-rights research
area — the same tooling gap the California campaign recorded, and worth
recording again. And its own exception undercuts it: an electric utility escapes
the mandate entirely whenever its main contractor already has a collective
bargaining, project labour or worksite agreement, so the largest covered
projects, new generation of 50 megawatts or more, can fall outside the very
requirement the act creates.

## What the acts do

Read from the enrolled act end to end, with the Legislative Counsel's Digest
used as an index to find what changed. Everything below is verified against the
operative sections.

### AB 121 (Chapter 227) — housing_affordability, yes = for

Landlords must offer at least one way to pay rent that costs the tenant no fee
and does not require bank account details; paying by personal cheque still
counts even though a cheque shows those details. If the landlord offers online
payment, the fee charged to the tenant may not exceed what the portal operator
charges the landlord, and it must be itemised separately in a written lease.
Rent must be stated, everywhere it is listed and in the lease itself, as a
single figure covering every mandatory fee on top of base rent, and the landlord
may not charge more than that figure. A tenant may sue over those last two rules
and, on winning, the court must award damages, any equitable relief, costs and
reasonable legal fees, plus $250 for each violation that involved deception. A
prospective tenant may ask for a copy of the lease. Application, credit-report
and background-check fees must be refunded if the landlord rents the unit to
someone else **and** did not actually do the work the fee paid for. No such fee
may be charged for a child in the household.

*Carry this limit:* electricity, natural gas and water for the tenant's own unit
may be billed as a separate monthly fee outside the single figure, at cost, but
only where the utility cannot bill the tenant directly, or for water where the
building is master-metered, and only with a marked, same-page disclosure.

*Version.* The Assembly voted the first reprint. The Senate added the utility
carve-out outright, changed "total amount" to "maximum total amount", and
dropped the word "fixed" so the rule now catches mandatory fees that vary. The
payment-method, portal-fee, lawsuit, lease-copy and application-fee rules were
in both versions unchanged.

### SB 166 (Chapter 177) — housing_affordability, yes = for

Deletes one word from Nevada's ban on dog-breed insurance discrimination. The
ban had applied only where breed was the **sole** reason an insurer refused,
cancelled, declined to renew or raised a rate; now it applies whenever the
decision is based on breed. The protection is extended to policies covering
multi-family residential buildings, alongside the homeowner, renter,
manufactured-home and umbrella policies already covered. Separately, anyone
receiving a state supportive-housing grant must agree in writing to let a tenant
keep at least one pet.

*Carry both limits:* an insurer may still act on a dog actually known to be, or
declared, dangerous or vicious, on sound underwriting and actuarial principles;
and the pet right is subject to public-health, animal-control and
animal-cruelty law.

*Version.* Clean. Both chambers voted the first reprint, which is the enrolled
act, and no amendment was adopted after either vote.

### AB 343 (Chapter 247) — corporate_accountability, yes = for

Hospitals must publish, and update every year, a list of the standard charge for
every item and service, and a list of at least 300 shoppable services, in a
machine-readable file that is free, needs no account and is searchable. A
hospital may skip the shoppable-services list if it runs a free, always-available
price-estimator tool. Each hospital must file an annual facility-fee report with
the state, signed under penalty of perjury, and the state must publish it within
45 days. **While a hospital is out of compliance with those publishing rules it
may not send medical debt to collections, sue on it, or report it to a credit
bureau.** A patient may complain to the Bureau of Consumer Protection, which
must investigate; collection freezes while it does; and if the Bureau finds a
violation the hospital must cancel the debt and refund anything paid. A knowing
violation is a deceptive trade practice. The state must publish the names of
hospitals found in violation, and must impose a penalty of at least $600, at
least $20 per bed, or at least $11,000, depending on hospital size, with each
day past a deadline counting separately.

*Version, and the two chambers voted materially different bills.* The Assembly's
2025-04-22 text let patients **sue hospitals directly**, with damages, legal
fees and punitive damages up to $10,000 per violation; capped penalties per
incident rather than setting minimums; required hospitals to file every list
with the state on a state template and keep old versions for three years; and
defined "hospital" to reach parents, subsidiaries and affiliates. Senate
Amendment 749 removed all of that, substituted the Bureau complaint process,
rewrote the penalties as bed-count minimums, and added the price-estimator
opt-out. The direction is the same on both versions, so each description names
its own version.

*Do not quote AB 343's internal cross-references.* Several are off by one in the
enrolled act itself.

### AB 483 (Chapter 482) — healthcare_affordability, yes = for

Health-care licensing boards, and the boards that licence ambulance attendants
and emergency medical technicians, must set up a process that moves an
application for a **first** licence to the front of the queue when the applicant
shows they will work mainly in a historically underserved community. Each board
must accept a letter from an employer in such a community stating the applicant
has accepted a job and when it starts, and may allow other proof.

*Say plainly what it does not do.* It reorders a queue. It sets no deadline,
waives no qualification, creates no licence and cuts no fee, and it does not
require the applicant to stay.

*One oddity:* the act defines "historically underserved community" twice and the
two differ. The health-board version also covers high-poverty public schools;
the emergency-services version does not.

*Version.* Both chambers voted the first reprint, which is the enrolled act.

### SB 246 (Chapter 288) — healthcare_affordability, yes = for

Across individual, group and small-employer plans, fraternal societies, hospital
and medical service corporations, health maintenance organisations, managed care
organisations, the state employees' benefit programme and Medicaid, a woman may
get covered gynaecological or obstetrical care without a referral from her
primary care doctor and without prior authorisation, and may name an
obstetrician or gynaecologist as her primary care doctor.

*Carry the conditions on the second right:* outside Medicaid the doctor must be
in the plan's network, meet the insurer's own criteria for primary care
providers, and accept the same terms the insurer applies to other primary care
doctors. The rules apply to plans delivered, issued or renewed on or after
January 1 2026.

*Area note.* `womens_reproductive_rights` was considered and not used. The act
changes nothing about contraception, abortion or fertility; its mechanism is
removing an insurance gatekeeping step, so the area whose claim rests on the
mechanism wins.

### AB 235 (Chapter 117) — data_privacy, yes = for

Adds three groups to the list of people who may ask that their personal
information be kept confidential in county recorder, county assessor, Secretary
of State and county and city clerk records, and who may ask the DMV to show an
alternate address on a licence or identification card: health care providers who
practise in reproductive health, employees and volunteers of health facilities
providing reproductive-health services, and providers of gender-affirming care.
Their spouse, domestic partner or minor child may ask too. Effective July 1 2025.

*Two cautions.* The act's own title and Digest describe a "court order", but the
operative sections say only that these people "may request" — the court-order
step lives in statutes this act does not touch, so do not write it as a
requirement of these sections. And most of the printed text is existing law
reprinted; judges, police officers and prosecutors were already covered.

*Version.* Clean. Never amended; both chambers voted the enrolled text.

### AB 123 (Chapter 517) — public_safety_and_crime_control, yes = for

Makes it a crime to address a threat or intimidation, directly or indirectly, to
a candidate for public office where the person **knows or has reason to know**
it communicates an intent to cause bodily injury to the candidate or a family
member, or damage to their property, and where it is done in a way that puts the
person receiving it in reasonable fear it will be carried out. A first offence
is a misdemeanour; a later one is a gross misdemeanour. A candidate may notify
the Secretary of State, who must investigate and, on reasonable suspicion, refer
the matter to the Attorney General, then give the candidate a written report.

*Write "knows or has reason to know", never "knowingly"* — the standard is
broader than the existing harassment statute's.

*The act carries an express free-speech clause* saying nothing in it bars
constitutionally protected speech, including political hyperbole. That clause
removes the free-speech objection as a counter-direction, which is why the
stance holds. `election_integrity` was rejected: the act does nothing about
secure, accurate or auditable elections.

*The act has no effective-date section.* October 1 2025 is Nevada's default,
recorded only in the chapter line.

*Version and roll choice.* Both chambers voted the second reprint, the enacted
text. The Senate passed the bill 13-8, reconsidered, then passed it 14-7; the
14-7 vote stands and carries the **lower** LegiScan id. See CODE-FINDINGS 2.

### SB 347 (Chapter 257) — gun_control, yes = for

Lets an officer authorised to make arrests take a firearm found on a person, or
in their immediate vicinity, when that person is placed on a mental-health
crisis hold. The officer must at once give a receipt describing the firearm and
a written notice explaining how to get it back. When the person is released, the
agency must make the firearm available for return, unless an officer has filed an
application for a protection order against high-risk behaviour, or the person is
already barred from having a firearm. Effective July 1 2025.

*Describe both halves.* The taking power is discretionary; the receipt, the
notice and the return duty are mandatory. A reader given only one half would be
misled. Before this act, Nevada's crisis-hold chapter had no confiscation or
return procedure at all.

### SB 260 (Chapter 499) — environment_and_public_health, yes = for

Orders the Administrator of the Division of Industrial Relations to write rules
saying what employers must do to monitor air quality and cut workers' exposure to
wildfire smoke at two levels of the air quality index, 150 and 200, and to set
the level at which workers may not do critical tasks outdoors. From January 1
2026 each covered employer must run a communications system that tells workers
when the index reaches 150 and what protection is available, and lets workers
report exposure and symptoms such as asthma attacks, difficulty breathing or
chest pain. The Administrator must also set training standards.

*Never write this as banning outdoor work at a stated index level.* The act sets
no exposure limit itself; it tells the regulator to pick one.

*Carry the exemptions:* mines, employers of commercial truck drivers, providers
of emergency services, and employers with ten or fewer workers are all outside
it, and the rules may not add workers' compensation liability.

*Version.* Both chambers voted the second reprint, the enacted text.

### AB 90 (Chapter 51) — civil_rights, yes = for

Adds regional facilities for the treatment and rehabilitation of children to the
definition of "detention facility" in Nevada's Juvenile Justice Bill of Rights,
so the rules that already apply to detention centres now apply there: tell the
child their rights, give the child and, where practicable, a parent or guardian
a copy, and post them where they can be seen. It also requires a juvenile court,
before sending a child to such a facility, to make the two findings it already
had to make for a state facility — that no suitable community alternative exists
or one was tried and failed, and that the child poses a public safety risk
judged on a risk assessment, any history of delinquency and the seriousness of
the offence.

*Area choice is deliberate.* `public_safety_and_crime_control` was rejected
because under that area a yes vote would read as **against**: the act makes
commitment harder. Choosing the area that flips the direction would mislead.

*Version.* Clean. Never amended; both chambers voted the enrolled text.

## Labels

Every stance takes `nay: null`. On each of these ten the realistic objection runs
on a different axis from the scored area — cost to local government, employer
burden, due process for people in crisis, or the reach of a new criminal law —
so recording a nay stance would attribute a position the vote does not evidence.
