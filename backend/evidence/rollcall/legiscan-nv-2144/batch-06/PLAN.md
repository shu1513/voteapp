# Nevada batch-06 — health care and the environment

**10 measures / 17 roll calls / 332 records / 41 candidates.** Same non-enacted scope as
batch-04, whose `PLAN.md` carries the scope argument.

Eight measures passed both chambers and were vetoed. Two did not become law for other
reasons, and both descriptions say which:

- **SB 173** passed both chambers, but the Assembly amended it and the Senate never voted
  on the changed bill.
- **SB 324** passed the Senate and the Assembly never voted on it — the Pennsylvania class.

Three measures have only one chamber in the batch because the other chamber's vote was not
divided under the campaign's gate: SB 352's Senate vote was 17-4, AB 244's Assembly vote was
36-6, and SB 324 never reached the Assembly.

## Three measures where the chambers voted different bills

**SB 128 is the most consequential split in the Nevada campaign so far, and it turns on one
word.** The version the Senate passed said an insurer "shall not utilize, employ or rely
upon an artificial intelligence system" to deny, cut back or end approved care — a flat ban.
The version the Assembly passed inserts "solely", so the software may still take part in a
denial as long as it is not the only basis. The same edit appears in all three places the
rule is stated. A second difference runs the same way: the Senate version made a clinician
"discuss with the patient the potential use of stem cell therapy", while the Assembly
version demotes that to a statement that it is "the public policy of this State to
encourage" such a discussion. Each chamber's record describes the bill that chamber voted.

**AB 411 also turns on one word.** The Assembly voted a text saying the pharmacy label
"must" carry the practice name once the prescriber asks; the Senate voted "may", leaving the
choice with the pharmacy.

**AB 282** splits more broadly. The Assembly's version made a doctor who broke the rule
guilty of unprofessional conduct and left the facility fine to be set by regulation with no
ceiling. The Senate's version reaches any billing company rather than only the treating
doctor and adds cancellation of unpaid balances, but replaces professional discipline with a
single fine of 10 percent of the overcharge capped at $5,000.

**SB 352** has a version note of a different kind. The Assembly voted the second reprint;
the Senate then refused to concur, a conference committee further amended the bill, and the
conference report was adopted without a divided roll. The final text adds an exemption for
policies that cover only a specified disease or a limited benefit, which the version the
Assembly voted did not contain. The Assembly record says so.

**SB 173** differs only slightly between the chambers: the Senate's definition of "cookware"
is open-ended while the Assembly's is a closed list of nine items, and the Assembly version
adds a report to the next Legislature. Neither changes what the descriptions say.

## Nothing was dropped from this batch

All ten measures read in one direction. Three carry counterweights that were considered and
did not change the judgment, because in each case the bill withholds or limits something
new rather than removing an existing right:

- **AB 259** writes the new violation out of the criminal half of Nevada's deceptive trade
  practice law. The conduct was not an offense before, so no existing exposure is removed.
- **AB 244** deliberately keeps its new offense out of the misdemeanor rule that covers the
  rest of the food safety chapter, making it civil-penalty-only. Same reasoning.
- **SB 173** says its food-contact-surface rules impose nothing new on health authorities,
  and its heat-island set-aside ranks behind existing bond obligations. Both limit the new
  duty; neither rolls anything back.
