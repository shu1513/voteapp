# Nevada batch-03 — the last of the enacted pool

## Scope

This finishes Nevada. Batch-02 left 27 measures carrying 35 rolls marked
`candidate:batch-03`. Of those, **7 measures and 7 rolls are imported here** and 20 measures
are dropped.

**Every one of the 104 divided rolls in the survey worklist now carries a disposition and a
reason.** Nothing in this session is left to judge.

## Eleven measures dropped on the title and digest

These were set aside without reading the act, because the subject itself has no research area
or the measure only creates a study, a report or a program: AB 43 (job order contract data),
AB 262 (unpaid volunteer election workers), AB 381 (animal cruelty), AB 451 (immunity for
returning a firearm), AB 461 (a powers-of-attorney program), AB 494 (federal-repeal reports),
AB 545 (emissions testing fees), SB 196 (a heavy equipment recovery fee), SB 293 (student
athlete name and likeness), SB 298 (peace officer discipline), and SB 41 (cannabis tax
permits). Each carries its own reason in the survey worklist.

SB 298 is the fourth measure this campaign has lost to the missing labor research area.

## The measure most likely to be questioned

**AB 540 is kept.** It is the session's largest housing act — $133 million appropriated, plus
up to $50 million in bonds — and it carries two elements that cut against housing
affordability: it widens "attainable housing" to reach households earning up to 150 percent of
area median income, and it deletes a rule that had aimed local fee relief at lower-income
projects. Both are named in the description rather than left out.

The judgment is that those are targeting changes, not repeals of protections, and are not of
comparable weight to the money. That is the line that separated AB 540 from **AB 396** in
batch-01, which was dropped because it deleted three tenant protections outright while
legalizing accessory dwelling units. A future reviewer who disagrees should look at those two
side by side.
