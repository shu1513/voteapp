# Nevada batch-05 — housing, renting and household money

**9 measures / 16 roll calls / 308 records / 41 candidates.** Same non-enacted scope as
batch-04, and the scope argument is in `../batch-04/PLAN.md`.

Seven measures passed both chambers and were vetoed. Two did not:

- **SB 193** passed the Senate 14-5 and the Assembly never voted on it. This is the
  Pennsylvania batch-02 class, the exception to filter 2 that the campaign already allows.
- **SB 391 lost on the floor with a majority in favor of it.** The Senate voted 13-8 for
  it, but the bill authorizes a fee, so Nevada required a two-thirds majority — 14 of 21.
  A failed vote is still a recorded position, and a bill that falls one vote short of a
  supermajority is the most decision-relevant kind.

## Dropped after reading the text

**SB 142 (property exempt from seizure) — dropped under filter 5.** It reads two ways.

Most of it helps people who owe a court judgment, and helps them a lot: the share of wages
a creditor may take falls from 18 or 25 percent to 10 or 15 percent, the first $850 a week
becomes fully protected, independent contractor pay counts as protected earnings, and the
flat protection for a bank account rises from $400 to $5,000.

But section 5 deletes the whole of the old subsection 1 — the $2,000 that was protected in
a bank account holding recently deposited federal benefits, with its list covering Social
Security, veterans' and black lung benefits — and the flat $5,000 that replaces it ends
with "unless the writ of execution or garnishment is for the recovery of money owed for
the support of any person." The old $2,000 protection had no such exception. So a person
living on federal benefits who faces a child support or spousal support order goes from
$2,000 protected under state law to nothing.

Section 1's inflation formula is also explicitly two-way — "increase or decrease in the
Consumer Price Index" — with no floor, so a fall in the index would shrink every exemption
in six statutes, including the $605,000 homestead.

A broad gain for most debtors, set against a complete loss of an existing protection for a
narrow and vulnerable group, is the same shape as AB 388 in batch-04 and as SB 277 and
AB 458 in earlier batches. Dropped, with the reason recorded in the worklist.

## AB 185: the two chambers voted materially different bills

This one needed two different descriptions, and it is the clearest version split in the
Nevada campaign so far.

**The Assembly voted the first reprint.** It stopped a homeowners association banning a
licensed child care home for 5 to 12 children, let renters run one unless the lease said
otherwise, and — in sections 7 and 8 — barred state and local licensing rules from
refusing a license to such a home for lacking outdoor play space if it sits within a mile
of a park. Senior housing was exempt.

**The Senate voted the second reprint,** which is a noticeably weaker bill. Sections 7 and
8 are gone entirely, so the licensing change vanishes. In exchange the protection now
covers a tenant as well as an owner. But the Senate version also exempts **any community
whose units share an interior wall, which is to say condominiums and townhouses**, and
lets an association cap the number at one facility per 200 units.

Both versions were kept and each chamber's record describes the version that chamber
voted. The Senate version was still judged one-directional: before the bill an association
could ban such a home outright, and after it could not in the communities the bill reaches.
The condominium exemption withholds the new protection rather than removing an existing
right, which is the same reasoning applied to SB 217's religious and small-employer
carve-outs in batch-04.

Two other measures had smaller version splits, both too small to change a description, and
both recorded in `JUDGING.md`: AB 283 (the Senate version gives the landlord 21 days rather
than 15 to prove service) and AB 112 (a savings clause drops four words).
