# Nevada batch-07 — civil rights, privacy and elections

**9 measures / 17 roll calls / 336 records / 41 candidates.** Same non-enacted scope as
batch-04, whose `PLAN.md` carries the scope argument.

Eight measures passed both chambers and were vetoed. SB 63 did not: the Senate passed one
bill, the Assembly passed a much smaller one, and the Senate never voted on the Assembly's
version.

## The nine measures

| measure | area | rolls | what it would have done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AB 217 | immigration / for | house 31-11, senate 14-6 | bars federal immigration officers from school grounds, and schools from sharing student information, without a court order |
| AB 140 | immigration / for | house 28-14, senate 13-8 | driver authorization cards last as long as a driver's license instead of expiring every four years |
| SB 141 | civil_rights / for | senate 13-8 | local jails must have a policy for transgender, non-binary and intersex prisoners |
| SB 85 | civil_rights / for | house 27-15, senate 11-8 | makes traffic stop data collection permanent by removing its 2025 end date |
| AB 589 | data_privacy / for | house 27-14, senate 13-7 | limits government collection of autism data; selling DNA without fresh consent becomes a felony |
| SB 63 | data_privacy / for | senate 13-8, house 26-15 | Senate: a Youth Online Safety Act. Assembly: only the rules for children under 13 |
| SB 102 | election_integrity / for | house 27-15, senate 13-8 | new felonies for false election documents and false presidential electors |
| AB 597 | election_integrity / for | house 27-15, senate 12-9 | lets a voter with no party registration vote in one major party's primary |
| SB 89 | gun_control / for | house 27-15, senate 12-8 | adds hate crime convictions to the list that bars owning a gun |

Every label carries `"nay": null`.

`immigration` was chosen for AB 217 and AB 140 over `civil_rights` because the campaign
already uses that area on 211 approved roll calls in both directions, including alongside
`public_education_quality`. `election_integrity` was chosen for SB 102 and AB 597 because
the campaign consistently files voting-mechanics measures there, in Illinois, Montana,
Pennsylvania, Maryland and Arizona.

## Two measures where the chambers voted different bills

**SB 63 is the largest version split in the Nevada campaign.** The Senate passed the Nevada
Youth Online Safety Act together with rules for the data of children under 13. The Assembly
deleted the entire Youth Online Safety Act — sections 1 through 22 all read "(Deleted by
amendment.)" — so nothing remained about recommendation algorithms, endless scrolling,
autoplay, livestreaming, like counters or notification curfews. The phrase "covered digital
platform" appears 22 times in the Senate's text and **zero** times in the Assembly's. The
Assembly also narrowed the profiling ban, added a list of thirteen exempt activities, cut
assessment retention from five years to three, and barred anyone but the Attorney General
from suing. It added one duty the Senate version lacked: rules for handling de-identified
data. Each chamber's record describes the bill that chamber voted.

**AB 217** splits on the penalty. The Assembly voted a version where a first offense was a
matter for school discipline and a repeat offense was a misdemeanor. The Senate removed the
crime entirely, leaving discipline as the only consequence, and dropped "providing a
penalty" from the bill's own title.

## Dropped after reading the text

**SB 160 (Nevada Equal Rights Commission) — dropped under filter 5.** It reads two ways.

The bill re-creates the Commission inside the Attorney General's office and adds real
duties: an attorney must review every discrimination complaint, the Commission must publish
how to appeal to the federal Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, and it may no longer
close an investigation without a final disposition except where a complainant has abused or
threatened a member.

But it also moves against complainants in two ways. Existing law gives the Commission one
year to reach a final disposition on a housing complaint; section 19 stretches that to
"within 18 months after the date the Commission receives the complaint" — six more months
of waiting. And section 11 lets the Attorney General "designate another agency to perform
the duties and functions of the Commission set forth in sections 13 and 18 to 22", which
are the investigation, complaint, hearing and order powers — the Commission's whole
substantive job. The strongest new remedies, including a $25,000 housing penalty and the
right to take a housing case to court, take effect only if the federal government makes a
determination that Nevada's remedies are substantially equivalent to federal law, which may
never happen.

Stronger duties set against a longer wait and a power to hand the work elsewhere is the same
shape as AB 388 in batch-04 and SB 142 in batch-05.
