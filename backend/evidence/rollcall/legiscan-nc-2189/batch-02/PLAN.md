# North Carolina batch-02: the divided votes on bills that became law

Batch-01 took the veto overrides. This batch takes what was left of the
divided-and-enacted pool: 125 rolls that had not been worked, on 45 measures.

## How the pool was narrowed

**Step 1, drop rolls that are not the chamber's last word.** 67 of the 125 are
an earlier stage of a bill the chamber voted on again later. A record citing
first passage, beside an unmentioned concurrence, reads as the member's final
position when it is not. 58 rolls on 39 measures survived.

**Step 2, drop every 2026 House roll.** LegiScan's member list for those rolls
is short by two seated members, and the tally it reports is wrong. See
`../CODE-FINDINGS.md`. 17 rolls were set aside here.

**Step 3, apply the fifth filter.** A measure stays only if a yes vote can
honestly be called a vote for or against something. 25 rolls failed.

the batch.

## Dropped on the fifth filter, with the reason

- **S 257, the state budget.** It locks in income tax cuts through 2032 and at
  the same time repeals the data centre electricity exemption and slows the
  automatic rate-cut trigger. It spends and it parks over three billion dollars
  in reserves. It carries roughly 900 statutory changes that are not spending at
  all. No single direction is honest.
- **H 926 and S 445, the Regulatory Reform Acts of 2025 and 2026.** Read section
  by section, the 2025 act has 24 loosening sections against 5 tightening across
  18 subject areas. The 2026 act has 29 against 11 across 27 areas. Same reason
  the batch-01 pool dropped the rules-cost bill.
- **H 834**, filed as a hunting and fishing licence bill and replaced 15 months
  later with an election package that loosens some rules for voters and tightens
  others.
- **H 376**, which loosens stormwater rules and wipes out stricter local
  ordinances, while tightening protections on the sale of a public water system.
- **S 474 and S 889**, the reappraisal pause. S 889 is a one-year delay bundled
  with an unrelated assessor exam fee. S 474 adds two counties to the pause and
  removes at least two others, so members from different counties were voting on
  opposite outcomes.
- **S 595**, a revenue omnibus with a new 40 percent credit that staff believed
  fit a single project, alongside a new tax on app-based car rentals.
- **H 517**, which adds an annual filing duty for every nonprofit and cuts a
  disclosure duty at the point of solicitation.
- **H 3 and H 1035**, local election acts. Moving municipal elections to
  even-numbered years raises turnout; the same change cancels one round and
  extends sitting terms a year. Those pull opposite ways.
- The rest are budget technical corrections, adjournment resolutions,
  appointment slates, single-city local acts, and one confirmation of a named
  individual.

## Judgment calls worth questioning

**H 58 is in, and it is a local act.** Its own long title says it eliminates the
mandatory school funding floor. It repeals the rule that Scotland County fund
its schools at least at the state average per student, and bars both the county
and the school board from any dispute process or lawsuit over the number. At
59-52 it is the closest vote in the pool, so it was not passed as a courtesy.
The election parts are housekeeping and the description says so.

**H 307 carries two labels.** It is a crime bill that tightens across the board,
which is the public safety strand. It also restarted executions and barred the
state from bringing back a task force on racial equity in criminal justice,
which is the civil rights strand. Neither strand covers the whole act, so no
stance is recorded for a no vote on either.

**S 249 names a partisan effect.** The description reports that the legislature's
own statistics packet shows the redrawn District 1 voting about 55 percent
Republican for president in 2024. That figure is published by the General
Assembly with the map. Without it the record would say only that two district
lines moved, which tells a voter nothing.
