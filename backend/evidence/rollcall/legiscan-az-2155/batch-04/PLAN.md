# Arizona batch-04 — the vetoed scope: water, welfare, government

32 measures, 50 rolls, 1,294 records across 54 candidates. The second half of the vetoed scope,
and the batch that **closes the Arizona 2025 session**: every divided roll call on a bill that
was signed or vetoed now carries a disposition.

Selection, wording and checks follow batch-03. Bodies are conditional throughout and the tail
states that the Governor vetoed the measure.

## The groundwater cluster, and the rule applied to it

Arizona's defining policy fight runs through this batch. Ten of the 32 measures are about
groundwater, and they needed one rule applied consistently rather than case by case.

**The rule: a measure scores `against` where it widens what may be pumped or narrows where the
limits apply, and `for` where it creates or extends a limit.** Arizona regulates groundwater
through Active Management Areas, and the eight `against` measures each loosen that structure —
letting the director remove land from an area (HB 2088), letting voters end one (HB 2089),
moving an old pumping right onto never-irrigated land (HB 2202), lengthening the historical-use
window (HB 2203), making those rights tradable (SB 1518), moving wine grapes and hydroponics
outside irrigation limits (HB 2573), and letting counties approve small subdivisions with no
water supply test (HB 2574). HB 2527 is the energy member of the same group: it would have
blocked retiring a power plant unless equal generation already existed.

The two `for` measures create limits where none existed: HB 2570 would have set up a way to
designate a temporary non-expansion area outside the existing zones, and SB 1300 would have put
an irrigation non-expansion area for part of the San Simon valley to Cochise County's voters,
with metering and annual reporting.

**Eight further groundwater measures were dropped** because they point both ways or are
procedural: HB 2572 extends adequacy rules while letting acres be added to a grandfathered
right; HB 2270 changes how models count stormwater recharge, which cannot be read as a
direction; HB 2091, HB 2116-style notice rules, HB 2551 and HB 2274 are bespoke or procedural.
**SB 1611, the groundwater savings credit measure, stays dropped from the enacted pool for the
same reason it was dropped there**: whether trading an irrigation right for credits conserves
water or frees credits for new building is exactly what is disputed.

## The welfare cluster

Seven measures tighten the safety net and all score `social_programs_and_welfare`/against,
following the Texas SB 379 and Alabama SB 57 line: work requirements and a waiver ban on food
assistance (HB 2121, HB 2122), a soda purchase ban (HB 2165), gambling-winnings data matching
against state health coverage and against food assistance (HB 2449, SB 1071), a jobless-rate
schedule that starts unemployment benefits at 12 weeks (HB 2450), and weekly job-search
reporting (SB 1296).

## What was dropped

Reasons are on the worklist rows. Beyond the groundwater drops above, the largest groups are:

- **28 vetoed budget bills** (HB 2947 to HB 2974, two whole packages) plus HB 2814, excluded by
  the standing appropriations rule without a read.
- **The cryptocurrency cluster** — HB 2324, HB 2749-style reserve funds, HB 2906, SB 1024,
  SB 1025, SB 1095, SB 1373. The Ohio HB 116 precedent, and Arizona cannot record a measure
  without a stance.
- **Two directions inside one area** — SB 1510 forces the elimination of long-vacant posts, which
  is both less waste and less capacity to deliver, and both sit inside `government_efficiency`.
  HB 2927 puts minutes online while relaxing executive-session notice.
- **Animal welfare** — SB 1658 and SB 1721. No research area covers it, which is an honest gap
  rather than a judgment.

## What remains in Arizona

Nothing in either pool. Every divided roll call on a signed or vetoed measure in the 2025
session is dispositioned. The open items are listed in `../README.md`: the four vaccine-adjacent
direction calls, the 48 divided votes on ballot referrals that the concurrent-resolution gap
puts out of reach, the 2026 session, and promotion to production.
