# Maine batch-04 — LD 613, the Death with Dignity waiting period

**One measure, 2 rolls, 125 records, labelled `general` with NO STANCE.**

This is the measure batches 02 and 03 deferred twice for a user direction call.
The call was delegated back; this file is the reasoning.

## What the Act does

Maine's Death with Dignity Act has been law since 2019. LD 613 does not
create, expand or repeal it. It changes one thing inside it: the waiting
period. A qualified patient must still make two spoken requests at least 15
days apart, sign a written request, and wait at least 48 hours between signing
and the writing of the prescription. The Act now lets the attending physician
shorten **part** of that wait when, in the physician's medical opinion, it is
in the patient's best interests given the patient's condition — with a written
note recording what was waived and why, and **a floor of 7 days that cannot be
waived at all**.

**As introduced the bill had no floor**: it would have let a physician waive
"any portion or all" of the waiting periods. Senate Amendment "A" (S-365) added
the 7-day minimum, and that is the version that became law. Any description
written from the introduced bill would have been wrong.

## Why `general` with no stance

The Illinois SB 1950 precedent (no research area carries an honest direction on
medical aid in dying) was written about a bill that would have **legalised** the
practice. LD 613 is a narrower question — access mechanics inside a framework
Maine already adopted — so the precedent does not transfer automatically, and
the two-directions-in-one-text test does not fail here either: the enacted text
moves one way.

It still gets no stance, because **the failure is in the mapping, not the
text**. The two candidate areas each break:

- `healthcare_affordability` ("improve access to affordable, quality care")
  would score this `for`, but only by assuming that assisted death is care
  whose access should improve. That assumption is the contested question
  itself, not a fact the statute settles.
- `civil_rights` is worse, not better: organised disability-rights groups
  oppose these laws on equal-protection grounds while autonomy advocates
  support them. An area with real constituencies claiming **both** directions
  cannot carry an honest one.

The standing instruction from the fluoride call applies with more force here,
not less: do not treat a contested direction as settled. That rules out a
stance — but not the record.

## Why import it rather than drop it

Filter 5 says a measure that would land on `general` gets dropped. That rule
was written to stop the Texas tail from padding batches with trivia, and this
is its opposite: **LD 613 drew 11 roll calls, more than any other measure left
in Maine's pool, and the Senate killed it 17-18 on June 9 before reversing
itself 19-16 on June 12.** A voter who cares about this subject is well served
by knowing how their legislator voted and badly served by silence.

The campaign already has the pattern: **Ohio H.B. 116** was imported under
`general` with no stance on exactly this reasoning — a divided vote worth
recording where no honest direction exists. LD 613 is that shape.

The result is 125 records tagged `general` with a **null stance**: the vote is
on the member's record, described neutrally, and no position is attributed to
anyone. The descriptions state the mechanics — what the law required before,
what a physician may now shorten, the documentation, and the 7-day floor — and
deliberately avoid both "improves access" and "weakens safeguards".
