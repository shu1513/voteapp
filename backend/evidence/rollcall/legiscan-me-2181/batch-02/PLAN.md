# Maine batch-02 — selection

**22 rolls / 11 measures / 1,392 records**, drawn from the 387 divided rolls
on measures that became law that batch-01 left. Every measure appears once per
chamber.

Same five filters as batch-01 (divided → became law → nameable subject → one
roll per measure per chamber → a defensible for/against direction), plus the
two gates the judge added on main after batch-01 shipped: every stance label
now states `nay` explicitly, and a roll cannot be approved while a later kept
floor vote on the same measure in the same chamber is unjudged.

Nine areas. Four are new to Maine: `healthcare_affordability`,
`immigration`, `civil_rights`, `public_safety_and_crime_control`,
`public_education_quality` — five, counting `corporate_accountability`, which
batch-01 reached only through LD 598.

**Every label is `for`.** That is the session, not a filter artifact: Maine had
a Democratic trifecta, and the divided-and-enacted set is overwhelmingly the
majority's agenda passing over minority objection. Batch-01's one `against`
(LD 556, energy-system preemption) came from a bill the minority carried.

## The two user direction calls, resolved

**LD 93 — labelled `healthcare_affordability`, deliberately NOT
`environment_and_public_health`.** The standing rule from FL SB 700 is that
this user does not accept a mainstream-consensus tiebreak on vaccine- and
fluoride-adjacent questions, so a direction that rests on "more adult
vaccination improves community health" is off the table. It is not needed. The
statute mandates nothing — it is a purchasing and financing mechanism: the
program buys vaccines in bulk and supplies them to providers, funded by
assessments on insurance carriers. Read as affordability and access, the
direction holds whatever one believes about vaccines, because the claim is
only that the law lowers the price and widens the supply of a product people
may choose. The efficacy premise is confined to the area we did not use.

**LD 427 — the House roll is the one that enacted the bill; the Senate roll is
a different version.** The sequence took four House votes: the report was
rejected 63-83, the House receded and concurred 72-70, enactment FAILED 71-74,
and the House then receded and concurred to enactment 82-65 after the Senate
enacted in non-concurrence. Roll 1591136 (82-65) is the decisive one. The
Senate's only roll (19-16) came before Senate Amendment "A" (S-348) removed the
bill's transit-stop parking provision, so its description names the version the
Senate actually voted and says the narrower bill became law.

## Dropped after a full read

- **LD 894** (paid family and medical leave) — the adopted vehicle is described
  as the committee's MINORITY report, the House then indefinitely postponed two
  further floor amendments, and the surviving text mixes worker-favorable and
  employer-favorable changes. Untangling which version carries which direction
  needs the journal, not the feed.
- **LD 1187** (mental health data in firearm fatality reports) — a reporting
  requirement. `gun_control` is about regulating firearm access; a change to
  what a state report must contain does not evidence a position on it.
- **LD 713** (data centers out of the business equipment tax exemption) — held
  from batch-01 for the same reason it is held here: which industries keep a
  tax exemption maps to no area with an honest direction.
- **LD 70** (funding free health clinics) — a pure appropriation, excluded on
  the standing rule that a spending vote carries no direction of its own.
