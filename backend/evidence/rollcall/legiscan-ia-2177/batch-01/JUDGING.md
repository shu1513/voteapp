# Iowa batch-01 — judging notes

Sources: the enrolled act on legis.iowa.gov (ground truth), the Legislative Services Agency's
explanation of the introduced bill (nonpartisan staff; used for triage only), and the bill
history. Sponsor material was not used. Every description cites that roll's own tally.

## SF 418 — sex and gender definitions (House 60-36, Senate 33-15) — civil_rights, yea against
The act defines sex by the body at birth in a new Code section 4.1A, strikes gender identity
from the Iowa Civil Rights Act (sections 216.2, 216.6, 216.6A, 216.7, 216.8 and the rest of
the chapter), requires birth certificates to record sex at birth, and reworded an existing
kindergarten-to-sixth-grade ban from "gender identity" to "gender theory". That last change
is a rewording, not a new ban, and the description says so. Both rolls were on the final
text; the Senate passed it first and the House passed it unamended the same day.

## HF 856 — DEI ban for public entities (House 59-32, Senate 34-16) — civil_rights, yea against
Division I bars any state entity, including local governments, from spending on diversity,
equity and inclusion offices, officers or programs, with carve-outs for teaching, research,
student groups, health services and compliance offices. Enforcement runs through the
attorney general on any person's report (section 19.3). Division II extends the 2024
regents-university limits to community colleges. The House passed the Senate's amended text
last, so both rolls are on the final text.

## SF 615 — Medicaid work requirements (House 56-30, Senate 33-13) — social_programs_and_welfare, yea against
Division I: 80 hours a month as a condition of Iowa Health and Wellness Plan eligibility,
with the listed exemptions, after federal approval. Division IV raises the resource
disregard for a couple in the Medicaid for Employed People with Disabilities program to
$21,000 (the $10,000 figure now applies to an individual). That is a small supportive strand
inside a bill whose whole weight is the work requirement, so it is described, not
disqualifying. The Senate passed the House amendment as amended on 2025-05-13 and the House
concurred and passed on 2025-05-14; the acknowledged roll 1572629 is the House concurrence
vote on the same day.

## HF 2788 — abortion drugs (House 58-29, Senate 30-11) — womens_reproductive_rights, yea against
In-person exam and coercion screening before any abortion (section 146A.1(1A)); signed
federal patient-agreement form and written confirmation of listed risks before abortion
drugs (146A.2); dispensing only in a health care setting directly to the woman, except in an
emergency (146F.2); private damages action against unlicensed dispensers, with chapter 148/155A licensees immune (146F.4);
license discipline (146A.2, 146F.5). It also carves miscarriage and ectopic-pregnancy care out
of the definition of abortion and repeals chapter 146C, the 2018 fetal-heartbeat chapter,
which chapter 146E (2023) replaced. The Senate roll is dated 2026-05-03 in both LegiScan and
the journal; it is not one of the skewed six.

## HF 924 — handgun age (Senate 33-14) — gun_control, yea against
Lowers the carry-permit and permit-to-acquire ages from 21 to 18 (sections 724.8, 724.15(2)),
while adding that a permit held by someone under 21 is not valid for a purchase from a
federally licensed dealer, which federal law forbids (724.15(1)). The description says both.
The House's own vote was on the earlier text (the Senate amended it), so only the Senate roll
is imported.

## HF 2296 — no local ID cards (House 69-22, Senate 31-14) — immigration, yea against
Two short sections bar counties and cities from issuing resident ID cards, except employee
badges and the state licenses some county treasurers issue. The direction rests on what the
act ends: local community ID programs whose users were mainly residents who could not get a
state ID. Earlier batches in other states labeled the same kind of ban the same way.

## SF 579 — local civil rights laws (House 60-26, Senate 29-16) — civil_rights, yea against
Local ordinances may not cover more categories than the state act (216.19(1)); the duty of
cities of 29,000 or more to keep a commission becomes optional (216.19(2), "shall" struck,
"may" added); complaints against political subdivisions go to the state, and any party may
transfer a stale complaint after twelve months (new subsections 9 and 10). The House amended
it (H-8157) and passed it 60-26; the Senate concurred and passed 29-16, so both rolls are on
the final text. Two House rolls read 60-26 that day; the rules-suspension motion is excluded
by the config and the passage vote is the one judged.

## HF 2527 — climate liability shield (House 66-24, Senate 33-13) — environment_and_public_health and corporate_accountability, both yea against
New chapter 673B: no liability for climate effects of greenhouse gas emissions unless the
plaintiff proves, by clear and convincing evidence, a direct injury from a violation of a
specific statutory limit or permit. Two strands, one label each.

## HF 706 — open meetings penalties (Senate 34-13) — anti_corruption, yea for
Fines rise to $500-$2,500 and $5,000-$12,500 for knowing violations; court-ordered removal
now follows any prior violation, not only one that drew a fine (the qualifier is struck);
a one-to-two-hour training within 90 days for new members. Section 3 adds two confidential
record classes (legislative security camera footage, state key-card entry logs). That is a
narrow exception inside a transparency bill and the description names it. Only the Senate roll
is on the final text.

## HF 299 — vaccine exemption notices (House 58-30, Senate 32-16) — environment_and_public_health, yea against
Requires exemption information in every immunization communication from the health
department, schools and child care providers, and on their websites and registration papers.
Earlier batches labeled information-about-exemptions mandates the same way.
