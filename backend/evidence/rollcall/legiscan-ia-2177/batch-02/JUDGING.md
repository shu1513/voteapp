# Iowa batch-02 — judging notes

Sources: the enrolled act on legis.iowa.gov, which is ground truth; the Legislative Services
Agency's explanation of the introduced bill, used only for triage because it describes the
bill as introduced and is often never rewritten; and the bill history. Sponsor material was
not used. Iowa's enrolled text draws deletions and insertions as lines at fixed coordinates,
so a plain text extraction keeps both the old and the new words; every description below was
written from the resolved markup.

## SF 473 — religious beliefs in foster care and adoption (House 58-34, Senate 28-16) — civil_rights, yea against
New section 237.10(9A) and an amended 600.7A bar the department from requiring a foster
parent, kinship caregiver or prospective adoptive parent to affirm a policy on sexual
orientation or gender identity against their beliefs, and from ruling the person out as a
placement on that ground. The direction is on anti-discrimination enforcement in placement,
which the act reduces. Religious liberty is a real interest on the other side, but it is a
question of the measure's worth, not of which way the act moves that research area.

## HF 2711 — affirmative action repeal (House 64-29, Senate 31-15) — civil_rights, yea against
Strikes the state affirmative action policy in chapter 19B and the plans and reports required
of state agencies, the regents, the judicial branch, school districts, area education agencies
and community colleges, plus parallel duties for the law enforcement academy and the board of
medicine. Section 13 shows the pattern: the equal opportunity policy stays, the affirmative
action sentence is struck.

## HF 928 — recounts and election contests (House 66-31, Senate 31-14) — election_integrity, yea for
Every strand points the same way on a secure and auditable count: a recount covers every
precinct where a vote was cast, recount boards must follow the secretary of state's guidance,
and the secretary may halt a county recount, take custody of the ballots, and fine a
commissioner up to twenty-five thousand dollars. The margin threshold for demanding a recount
is described because it narrows who may ask, but it is about who bears the cost, not accuracy.

## HF 767 — private sector drug testing (House 60-32, Senate 31-14) — corporate_accountability, yea against
Three changes, all favoring the employer: a safety-sensitive job becomes one the employer
designates rather than one that in fact fits the statutory test; notice may be delivered in
person or electronically with the worker's agreement; and liability narrows from "a person"
to "an employer". Iowa has no labor research area, so the mandate-against-employers workaround
is used in reverse here.

## HF 2329 — regulated loan interest (House 62-29, Senate 34-11) — cost_of_living_reduction, yea against
The old sliding cap fell to one percent a month above a seven hundred dollar balance; the act
replaces the whole scale with a flat three percent a month. The service charge also changes,
from the lesser of ten percent or thirty dollars to the lesser of three percent or one hundred
dollars, which falls for small loans and rises for large ones. Both figures are in the record.

## SF 304 — minors and vaccines for sexually transmitted infections (House 63-29, Senate 33-14) — environment_and_public_health, yea against
A single section carves vaccination out of the rule letting a minor consent alone to care for
a sexually transmitted disease. Testing and treatment are untouched, and the description says so.

## SF 383 — pharmacy benefit managers (Senate 36-14) — healthcare_affordability and corporate_accountability, both yea for
Two strands, one label each: patients keep their choice of pharmacy and independent pharmacies
get a reimbursement floor at the national average acquisition cost, no worse than the manager's
own affiliate. The House vote was on an earlier text, so only the Senate roll is imported.

## SF 2422 — food assistance and Medicaid eligibility (Senate 27-16) — social_programs_and_welfare, yea against
Federal immigration and citizenship checks, quarterly error-rate reports, a waiver request to
expunge unused food benefits after about three months, retroactive Medicaid limited to two
months with a permanent bar on seeking more, and an expenditure-neutrality test on every new
waiver. One strand runs the other way: coverage now starts the first day of the month of
application rather than the month after. It is small next to the rest and is described.

## HF 2501 — conduct of elections (House 63-29, Senate 31-16) — election_integrity, yea for
Investigation notices to the secretary of state, cancellation of registrations left incomplete
or unconfirmed for ninety days after contact fails, tamper-evident seals on all equipment, and
thresholds for ballot-question recounts. The cancellation provision touches voter access and is
stated plainly, but it reaches only registrations already incomplete and unreachable.

## HF 2254 — noncompete ban at the University of Iowa hospital (House 70-20) — healthcare_affordability, yea for
The introduced bill covered physicians only; the enrolled act covers nurse practitioners,
licensed practical nurses, pharmacists, physicians, physician assistants and registered nurses.
That is why the act, not the explanation, is the source. Labeled healthcare_affordability rather
than corporate_accountability because the employer here is a public hospital, which the
corporate_accountability workaround does not reach, and because the effect a patient feels is
that clinicians who leave can keep practicing nearby.
