# Delaware batch-02 — the rest of the pool

**17 measures / 18 rolls / 299 records across 29 candidates / 226 tags.** This batch
closes Delaware: after it, **every one of the 158 divided rolls in the session carries a
final disposition and nothing is left open.** Local `voteapp` only; production holds no
Delaware roll-call records.

Batch-01 took the marquee measures. This batch worked the whole remainder of the
divided-and-enacted pool — the 43 rolls on 38 measures that batch-01 left as candidates
— rather than skimming another batch off it.

## How the 38 measures were worked

Every one was screened from its own synopsis, then **the 20 that survived screening
were read in full from the enacted text**, top to bottom. That is the Pennsylvania
screen-then-verify split, and it earned its keep again: three measures that looked
clean at the screen died on the read.

**Three areas new to Delaware**: `corporate_accountability`, `healthcare_affordability`
and `immigration`. Delaware now covers 12 of the 27 research areas.

Every label carries `nay: null`. On each of these the realistic objection runs on a
different axis from the scored area — employer or landlord cost, local control, state
spending, or federal cooperation — so a no vote is not evidence of a position on the
area itself.

## Area calls worth recording

- **`corporate_accountability`, not `cost_of_living_reduction`, for the four utility and
  consumer measures.** That area is defined as price stability, competition, tariffs and
  trade; SB 60, SB 326, HB 67 and SB 201 all bind companies through consumer protection,
  which is what `corporate_accountability` names.
- **`social_programs_and_welfare` for HB 62 and HB 91**, following batch-01's HB 116 and
  HB 50: these support households below an income line rather than moving a market price.
- **`reduce_wealth_gap` for HB 423** — the area's own words are "expands asset building",
  which is what automatic retirement enrollment does.
- **`civil_rights` for SB 250.** The act's own new section bars treating a child
  differently for a parent's "marital status, age, gender, gender identity, or sexual
  orientation".

## Dropped after a full read

- **HB 79** (restraint and seclusion reporting) — expands reporting on mechanical
  restraint and seclusion, but the same act writes school resource officers out of the
  restraint limits and newly allows an untrained officer to fill in for an absent one.
  Both currents sit inside `public_education_quality`.
- **SB 10**, the Richard "Mouse" Smith Compassionate Release Act — opens three new routes
  to a reduced sentence and removes the Board of Parole gate, but also stretches the wait
  after a denial from one year to as much as three, adds an open-ended summary dismissal
  power, and adds a victim-and-witness safety finding. Batch-01 flagged this measure as
  the one to read first, and the read settled it.
- **SB 159** (substations for renewable projects) — clears a county obstacle to a large
  renewable project, and retroactively deems an already-denied application granted.
  Clean-power access and county land-use review pull opposite ways.

The 18 screened out are listed with reasons in `survey/divided-worklist.tsv`. The two
worth naming are **HB 130 and SB 24**, both union-rights measures: **no research area
covers labor or union rights**, the gap California recorded, and it costs Delaware two
divided-and-enacted measures here.

## Delaware is closed, with two standing reasons to come back

All 158 divided rolls are dispositioned: 59 out of gate, 41 dropped, 25 batch-01, 18
batch-02, 8 excluded, 7 superseded. Nothing is open.

1. **The session is still running and Delaware signs late.** 40 divided rolls sit on
   bills that had passed both chambers but were unsigned when the dataset was cut on
   2026-08-30. A later download turns some of those into new work.
2. **15 of 41 House districts are still unrostered.** Every House roll here reaches 19
   or 20 candidates; with a full roster it would reach about 40. Re-importing after the
   roster campaign finishes adds those members without re-judging anything.
