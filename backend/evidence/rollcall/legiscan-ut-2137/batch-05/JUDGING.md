# Utah 2025 batch-05 — how each measure was judged

Sources: the Office of Legislative Research and General Counsel's summary as an
index, and the enrolled text, read through `ut_text.py` with added and deleted
text marked, as ground truth.

All six rolls are Senate rolls. Each measure's House vote was lopsided enough
to fall outside the divided gate, so the Senate roll is the only one that
qualifies.

## HB 106 Income Tax Revisions

Cuts the corporate and individual income tax rates from 4.55 to 4.5 percent,
with retrospective operation to tax years beginning January 1, 2025. Widens the
$1,000 nonrefundable child tax credit from children aged one to four to every
child under six. Creates an employer child care credit, for employers that also
claim the federal credit under Internal Revenue Code section 45F: 20 percent of
construction costs, carried forward five years, and 10 percent of operating
costs, available only after a construction claim, with recapture on a sliding
scale from 100 to 25 percent if the facility stops operating within five years.
`personal_income_tax_reduction` / for.

A 2026 act, HB 190, later widened the employer credit to off-site facilities and
dropped the construction-first condition; a second, HB 290, raised the child tax
credit's income phaseout. Both build on this act, and neither changes what the
2025 vote did.

## HB 418 Data Sharing Amendments

Creates the Utah Digital Choice Act, effective July 1, 2026. A social media
service must return a requested copy of a user's personal data, including the
user's social graph (connections, the user's own posts, the user's responses,
and others' responses to the user's content, but not other users' private
content), in a portable format. Each social media company must run an
interoperability interface on an open protocol that lets a user share that data
with services the user designates, and may share or receive data through it
only with the user's consent. The act also adds a right to correct inaccurate
personal data to the Utah Consumer Privacy Act. The Division of Consumer
Protection enforces it, with fines up to $2,500 per violation.

A 2026 act, HB 408, refined the same chapter before it took effect (selective
transfer, real-time sharing, consent before other users' interactions travel
with a transfer, and a good-faith safe harbor). It builds on this act rather
than repealing it. `data_privacy` / for.

## HB 207 Sexual Offense Revisions

For a repeat sex offender, both the minimum and the maximum penalty rise by
five years for each prior conviction from a separate episode, where before only
the maximum did, and an enhanced sentence now carries mandatory imprisonment
under 76-3-406: no probation, no suspension, no reduction. The act deletes the
provision that made distributing child sexual abuse material a second degree
felony when it depicted a teen 14 or older, so it is a first degree felony in
every case, and it adds accessing, or maintaining access, with intent to view to
the possession offense. `public_safety_and_crime_control` / for.

## HB 274 Water Amendments

A retail drinking water supplier must, by July 1, 2027, build at least one
water conservation effort into the rate for at least the highest-use block of
its residential customers, and that conservation portion is conclusively
presumed reasonable. Contracts for secondary (untreated, outdoor) water entered
on or after July 1, 2025 must allow tiered billing, and by July 1, 2030 secondary
suppliers must bill by a tiered conservation rate, charging the lowest tier for
water used to grow food. Under 73-10-34(14)(f), a supplier exempt from metering
under (9), (10), or (11), or authorized to meter at strategic points under (13),
is not required to bill a tiered rate. A supplier out of compliance after April
1, 2030 loses state water money and faces a state engineer fine.
`environment_and_public_health` / for.

## HB 264 Tax Incentives Amendments

The official summary in Utah's bill record says systems placed in service before
January 1, 2035. The enrolled text, and the summary printed at the top of the
enrolled bill itself, say January 1, 2028. The earlier figure belongs to an
earlier version, and the enrolled text governs. The act limits the corporate and
individual energy system credits to systems placed in service before January 1,
2028, and repeals the individual credit for buying units of a qualifying solar
project and the corporate and individual alternative energy development credits,
with retrospective operation to tax years beginning January 1, 2025.
`environment_and_public_health` / against.

## HB 124 Education Industry Employee Privacy

A local education agency or the State Board of Education may not sell or
transfer an employee's work-related contact information to a third party as an
isolated or standalone transaction, and must publicly disclose any records
request, and fee, for that information. An agency may not require an employee to
use a work app on a personal device if its terms allow access to data outside
the app, must accommodate an employee who objects, and may not retaliate. The
state board investigates complaints. `data_privacy` / for.

The honest question on this one is whether it is really a labor measure aimed at
union outreach. The text protects the data and the device and names no
organization, so it is filed under the area it actually regulates.

## Why every no side is untagged

On each of these the no side mixes objections that are not the opposite of the
area scored: revenue cost on HB 106, compliance cost on HB 418 and HB 274,
sentencing philosophy on HB 207, fiscal policy on HB 264, and administrative
burden on HB 124.
