## HB 402, Foods Additives in Schools — environment_and_public_health, yea for

House 38-34 (the closest House vote in the batch), Senate 16-6, signed.

Bars a public school from selling, giving out or serving food on school grounds
during the school day if it contains any of nine additives, from the 2026-2027
school year. The scope words matter and are in the description: the ban is tied
to school grounds and the school day, not to the food generally.

The exceptions are stated rather than summarized away, because they are large:
a parent may still bring those foods to their own child, a teacher may hand
them out with written parental permission each semester, concession stands at
school events and vending machines are untouched, and the section does not
apply at a charter school or district with 5,500 students or fewer.

`nay` null: a no vote here reads as parental choice or administrative burden,
not as a position on public health.

## HB 403, SNAP Funds — social_programs_and_welfare, yea against

House 54-14, Senate 15-13, signed. Follows the Alabama SB 57 line, where a
waiver narrowing what food assistance may buy scored against the area.

The act orders a waiver request, not a ban, and the description says so twice:
it names the request, and it closes by saying nothing changes for shoppers
unless the federal government agrees. Describing a request as a restriction
would be the plan-described-as-a-program error.

The resubmission rule is stated in full — within three months if refused, then
once a year until granted — because a first draft compressed it to "every year"
and lost the three-month clock.

`nay` null: the objection could be paternalism, retailer cost, or benefit
adequacy, which are not one position on the safety net.

## HB 477, School Trespass — civil_rights, yea for

House 49-19, Senate 17-7, signed.

**The description was corrected before import and the correction is the point.**
A first draft said speaking at a school board meeting "cannot be treated as
criminal trespass". The enacted subsection is narrower: a complying comment
"does not satisfy the element of criminal trespass ... regarding an intent to
cause annoyance". It defeats one element, not the whole offense. The
description now says the comment cannot by itself show the intent to annoy that
the trespass law requires.

The second half narrows what counts as school property: the crime now reaches a
school itself and no longer reaches the offices or other property of a school
district or charter school. The statute's term is "LEA", which covers charter
schools as well as districts, so the description names both.

`nay` null: a no vote reads as school safety, which sits in a different area.

## SB 78, Homeless Individuals Protection — social_programs_and_welfare, yea for

House 43-28, Senate 18-8, signed.

Creates an ombudsman inside the Office of Homeless Services to investigate
complaints against homeless service providers. "Ombudsman" is a term of art, so
it is glossed in place as a state watchdog rather than left bare.

The duties described are the operative ones: run a website explaining how to
complain, require each provider to post the ombudsman's phone number and
website where clients can see them, choose which complaints to take up, and
report a provider's legal violation to the complainant, the office and a
prosecutor.

`nay` null: a no vote reads as opposition to a new position and its cost.

## HB 263, Election Record — election_integrity, yea for

House 47-23, Senate 19-6, signed. The area is the right one: this act is about
whether an election can be audited, not about who may vote, which the standing
rule sends to `civil_rights`.

Makes the video of ballot processing a public record, where before only a short
list of officials could watch it; requires chain-of-custody paperwork to be
kept as long as the ballots; requires a published ballot reconciliation; and
creates a way for county councils and other government bodies to examine
electronic copies of ballots and envelopes at the clerk's office without taking
copies away.

The reconciliation sentence was rewritten before import. A first draft listed
"by mail, in person, and at drop boxes", which reads as the whole list; the
statute also breaks out ballots sent and returned electronically. The
description now says the count is broken down by how each ballot was sent and
returned, without presenting a partial list as complete.

`nay` null: a plausible objection is voter privacy in the released material,
which sits inside `election_integrity` on the other side.

## HB 100, Food Security — social_programs_and_welfare, yea for

House 47-24, signed. House-only: the Senate's vote was not divided.

Free lunch for a student who already qualifies for a reduced-price meal, with
the State Board of Education paying the difference. The word "publicly" was
restored before import: the statute bars publicly identifying or stigmatizing a
student who cannot pay, and dropping "publicly" would have widened it.

The funding condition is carried rather than dropped, because it is the whole
qualification on the promise: if lawmakers do not appropriate enough, the board
splits what it has and a school may ask parents for the rest.

`nay` null: a no vote reads as fiscal.

## SB 284, Medicaid Doula Services — healthcare_affordability, yea for

House 55-16, signed. House-only.

The act directs a request for federal permission and makes coverage contingent
on approval, and the description keeps that conditional shape. "Doula" is
glossed in place as support, but not medical care, during pregnancy, birth and
the weeks after, which is the statute's own definition.

`nay` null: a no vote reads as fiscal or as doubt about scope of practice.

## Two measures dropped after a full read

**HB 69, Government Records and Information — filter 5.** The summary leads
with voter-history privacy, and that half is real: a new section bars a
government officer from disclosing how, when or where an identifiable voter
voted, and makes it a class B misdemeanor. The other half runs the other way
and is not small. Section 63G-2-802 now lets a requester recover attorney fees
only if the court also finds the defending party acted in bad faith; changes
"shall not ordinarily be awarded" to a flat bar where the requester's purpose is
commercial; and adds a new power to charge fees to a requester who acted in bad
faith. Section 63G-2-405 and 63G-2-801 each add a bad-faith finding before a
governmental entity can be sanctioned. A reader who cares about open government
could reasonably want to vote yes on one half and no on the other.

**SB 86, Workplace Protection — filter 3.** The official summary says the act
"reduces the number of employees a person may employ before being considered an
employer". The enrolled text strikes "15" and re-inserts "15". The threshold
does not move, so the bill a voter was told about is not the bill that passed.
What the act actually changes is the definition of sexual harassment, from
"conduct that is a violation of" Title VII and the state provision to
"harassment on the basis of sex, sexual orientation, or gender, as prohibited
in" the same two. That is a definitional refinement, not a recognisable
subject, so it is dropped rather than filed under a label it would not earn.
