## HB 37, Utah Housing — housing_affordability, yea for

House 39-30, Senate 22-6, signed. The closest House vote of either batch.

The act widens the menu of moderate income housing steps a city can take, and
the new entries are the substance: a density bonus for owner-occupied single
family homes, a matching bonus for apartments, and approval of a project funded
by the state's home investment program. Where a city grants one of the bonuses
it may attach conditions, and the description names them concretely rather than
as "certain requirements": most of the homes reserved for owner-occupiers for
five years, a quarter kept affordable, or a first look of up to 30 days for
local buyers.

The bill also lowers the population needed to form a new town from 100 to 75.
That is a small provision beside the housing strategies, but it is stated
because it is the change the enrolled text marks most plainly and a reader of
the act would meet it first.

`nay` null: a no vote here reads as local control over zoning, which is the
counter-argument that sits inside `housing_affordability` rather than a
position against housing supply.

## HB 251, Pollinator Program — environment_and_public_health, yea for

House 52-21, signed. House-only.

Three changes, all one direction: the three-year pilot becomes a permanent
program, the state's share of planting costs rises from up to 25% to up to 75%,
and the $60,000 yearly ceiling is struck. The repeal date being removed is
visible in the markup as a deleted subsection, which is why the description can
say the pilot was due to end in 2026 rather than guessing.

`nay` null: a no vote reads as spending.

## HB 49, Juror Eligibility — civil_rights, yea for

House 45-25, Senate 16-5, signed.

The old sentence barred anyone convicted of a felony that had not been expunged.
The new sentence keeps that bar but adds a second way back — a conviction
reduced to a misdemeanor — and confines the bar to convictions from a state or
federal court in the United States. Both changes widen who may serve, so the
direction is clean.

`nay` null: a no vote reads as a view about juries and serious convictions, not
as opposition to equal treatment generally.

## SB 181, Housing Affordability — housing_affordability, yea for

House 50-20, signed. House-only.

Parking minimums are the mechanism: a covered space may not be required to
exceed 10 feet by 20, an uncovered space nine by 20, a tandem space must count
as two, and a two-car garage must count as two. A garage may not be required at
all for owner-occupied affordable housing.

Two limits are carried into the description because leaving either out would
overstate the act. The statute counts a home as owner-occupied only where the
owner lives there as a primary residence for at least five years. And the caps
reach only a "specified municipality" — cities of the first through fourth
class, and fifth-class cities of 5,000 or more in a larger county — not every
city in Utah — and, in the county section, only a "specified county" (first
through third class). That definition was read from HB 37's enrolled text,
which amends and therefore reprints the section that carries it.

The garage ban is scoped differently and the description says so. Subsection
(5) reads "a municipality may not require a garage" for owner-occupied
affordable housing, with no "specified" qualifier, so it reaches every city and
county. Review caught the first draft applying the specified-municipality limit
to the whole act.

`nay` null: as with HB 37, the objection is local control, which sits inside
the same area.

## Two measures dropped after a full read

**HB 480, Landlord Communication — filter 5 and area fit.** The title points at
notices, and the act does allow a deposit disposition to be delivered
electronically and changes a tenant's notice window from five calendar days to
five business days. But most of the act is eviction procedure: orders of
restitution are restructured, the defendant is told they have three calendar
days to vacate, a sheriff or constable may delegate removal and storage to the
landlord, and a tenant cannot reach their property until removal and storage
costs are paid. It also adds a requirement that a court find the owner acted in
bad faith before the owner is liable for a tenant's court costs and fees, which
narrows an existing tenant remedy. Parts run each way, and the Maryland HB 767
retraction already settled that eviction procedure sits outside
`housing_affordability` because it changes neither supply nor cost.

**SB 91, Restaurant Tax — no area fits.** The act redefines "restaurant" and
then lets a county impose its 1% tax on customized prepared food sold at a
convenience store, gas station or grocery store, from January 2026. The subject
is real and a voter would recognize it, but `cost_of_living_reduction` is
defined as price stability, competition, tariffs and trade, and no other area
reaches a local tax. Filing it under the nearest slug would misdescribe why
members voted, so it is dropped rather than forced.

## A note on the identity of the HB 37 rolls

Both HB 37 rolls fall on 2025-03-07, the last day of the session, and the
Senate's is the suspension caption. Utah's own vote sheets name the questions
as `Concurrence / Concurred/Final Passage` for the House and `2nd Reading /
Passage on 2nd & 3rd Under suspension of the rules` for the Senate, and the
substitute in force at each timestamp is Substitute 5, which is the enrolled
text. Neither is a procedural motion wearing a passage caption.
