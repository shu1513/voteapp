# Utah 2025 batch-04 — how each measure was judged

Sources: the Office of Legislative Research and General Counsel's neutral
summary as an index, and the enrolled text, read through `ut_text.py` with its
added and deleted text marked, as ground truth. Sponsor floor remarks were not
used.

## HB 355 Mining and Critical Infrastructure Materials Amendments

Thirteen code sections covering sand, gravel and rock aggregate operations in
both the city and county land use codes. The act:

- drops the January 1, 2019 cutoff, so any existing legal use (a permit, or a
  nonconforming use not abandoned for 24 months) by an operator that has
  produced commercial quantities is vested;
- reverses the burden of proof: the person arguing a use is NOT vested must now
  prove it, where before the person claiming vested status had to;
- lets a vested operator expand onto contiguous land it controlled before May 7,
  2025, and onto contiguous "new land" it controls by January 1, 2026, and on
  that land raise production, change methods, stockpile, recycle, and batch
  concrete and asphalt;
- replaces local approval of expansion onto new land with a notice and a public
  meeting. The local body may act only on a finding, by a preponderance of the
  evidence, that the expansion endangers public health, safety and welfare, and
  even then may impose only mitigation no stricter than a state permit requires.
  It may not prohibit the expansion if the operator accepts that mitigation.

The honest counter-reading is `public_infrastructure` / for, since aggregate is
road and building material. But the act's mechanism is to remove local limits
on dust, noise and truck traffic near homes, which is the concern
`environment_and_public_health` exists to score. `environment_and_public_health`
/ against.

## SB 277 Government Records Management Amendments

Thirty code sections. The act repeals 63G-2-501 and 63G-2-502, abolishing the
State Records Committee, and creates a Government Records Office whose director
decides appeals under the Government Records Access and Management Act. The
committee's seven seats, per the section repealed, were a private-sector
records professional, an electronic records expert, the state archives
director, two citizen members, a local government representative and a news
media representative.

The director is appointed by the governor with Senate consent for a four-year
term, removable only for cause, must be "capable of adjudicating appeals ...
impartially", and is evaluated by survey every two years from 2027. District
court review of a director's decision remains.

The records appeal body therefore moves from a mixed panel with citizen and
press seats to one appointee of the head of the executive branch, whose own
agencies are the usual respondents. The safeguards are real and the
description names them. `anti_corruption` / against.

## HB 294 Infectious Disease Procedures Amendments

New section 26B-7-304.5 reads, in full, that the department and a local health
department "may not issue an order of constraint under any circumstance." An
order of constraint is one that applies to all or substantially all of a group
of people or of public places and imposes isolation, quarantine, a stay-at-home
order, physical control, a required action, or the closing of schools, theaters
or gatherings. The act also removes the words limiting that definition to
declared emergencies, so the ban is broader than emergency orders alone.

An order of restriction may now name only one individual. And a place of public
accommodation or a state-regulated enterprise may not require a face covering
of a person whose diagnosed medical or mental health condition or disability
makes wearing one distressing, though a health care facility may require one in
patient care areas without denying care.

`environment_and_public_health` / against: the area covers community health
through standards and prevention, and this act removes the prevention tool a
health department uses against an outbreak.

## HB 195 Firearm Retention Amendments

Two changes. A plea in abeyance agreement may not require the defendant to
forfeit a firearm unless the offense would make the defendant a restricted
person. And when a person is charged with a crime that would not make them a
restricted person, and a seized firearm is no longer needed for court, the
prosecutor must direct its return, after the agency confirms eligibility
through the Bureau of Criminal Identification. `gun_control` / against.

## Why every no side is untagged

On HB 355 a no vote can be a vote for local control or for stricter mining
limits; on SB 277 it can be a vote for the old committee's process rather than
for more transparency; on HB 294 and HB 195 the no side mixes public health or
safety views with objections to drafting. None supports a clean stance, so
each label's `nay` is null.
