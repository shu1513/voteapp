# Judging notes, SB 587

## What the law does

Two things, and they are quite different in size.

**It doubles the point at which competitive bidding is required.** Before this
law, a state agency, city, county or school board had to seek competing bids on
any construction project over $25,000. The floor is now $50,000. This one clause
reaches every local government in the state.

**It creates a second way to award the largest projects.** For a project
estimated at $20 million or more, an agency may now use "construction management
at risk", where one firm advises on the design and then builds the job for a
guaranteed price. That firm is chosen from a shortlist of at least three on an
overall best-value score rather than by lowest bid. Using the method is optional,
and the whole article expires on 1 July 2030 unless the Legislature renews it.

Smaller changes: a submitted bid is now binding for 90 days, and when every bid
comes in over budget the agency has 60 days rather than 30 to negotiate.

## Why the label is government_efficiency and not anti_corruption

The bill's whole design is faster purchasing: a higher small-purchase threshold,
a longer negotiation window, and a delivery method meant to hold schedule and
cost on large builds. Under `government_efficiency` the direction is clean, so a
yes vote is for it.

`anti_corruption` was considered and rejected, because on that topic the text
genuinely reads two ways and the standing rule is to drop rather than pick a
side.

Pointing toward less scrutiny: the no-bid threshold doubles; the new method
operates outside the ordinary bidding law with no public bid opening and no
lowest-bidder rule; none of the seven weighted scoring criteria is price;
proposal records stay sealed until the winner is announced; and the evaluation
committee is required to stay below a quorum of any public body, which keeps its
deliberations outside the open-meetings rules.

Pointing toward more: two separate 30-day formal public advertisements; a
minimum of three shortlisted firms with a written explanation if fewer exist;
evaluation records declared public; a required conflict-of-interest statement;
an annual public report that must say how many eligible projects did not use the
new method; and a five-year sunset.

Neither side of that is decorative. Claiming the bill is simply pro-competition
or simply anti-competition would overstate the text, so no direction was claimed
on it.

## Note on the title

The bill is titled "relating generally to government contracting" and most of it
is about the new at-risk contracting method. The change that touches the most
people — doubling the competitive bidding threshold — is one clause, and the
description leads with it for that reason.
