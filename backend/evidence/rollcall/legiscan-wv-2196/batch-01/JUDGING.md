# Judging notes, West Virginia 2025 batch-01

Every description was written from the enrolled act, read through the tool at
`~/legiscan-data/wv-work/wv.py`. That tool matters here: West Virginia marks new
language with an underline and deleted language with a strikethrough, and a
plain text dump of the page loses both. The page also carries a color legend
saying green is existing code and red is new code, but that legend is
decoration. Every class in these documents is black.

The enrolled version carries no change markup at all, because it is the clean
final law. To see what a bill changed, read the committee substitute or the
engrossed version, and use the enrolled text for what the law now says.

## HB 2513, Senate roll 303, 27-7, became law

Raises the annual training required of county school board members from seven
hours to twelve and adds fiscal management to the required topics. A member who
misses the first orientation must complete a make-up session within 30 days, and
failing to finish required training without good cause counts as neglect of duty.

`public_education_quality`, a yes vote was for, a no vote evidences nothing in
particular so `nay` is null.

The act also sets member pay at $260 per meeting attended unless the board votes
for less, and lets members claim mileage without producing receipts. That is a
second strand on a different axis. Following the Alabama HB 475 precedent it is
described in the record but carries no label of its own. The mileage change
could be read as slightly weaker expense accountability, but it is narrow beside
the training provisions, so under the standing rule it is described rather than
treated as disqualifying.

## HB 3444, Senate roll 447, 18-15, became law WITHOUT the Governor's signature

Repeals seven sections on inflammation of the eyes of newborns, including the
requirement that a newborn be given a preventive eye medication, the duty of
anyone assisting at a birth to report cases, the duties of local health officers
and the state board of health, and the penalties.

`environment_and_public_health`, a yes vote was against, `nay` null.

Two things were checked carefully. The description says the repeal removes the
legal duties and does not by itself change what doctors and hospitals choose to
do, because the act repeals a requirement rather than banning the treatment.
And the chapter history says the bill became law without the Governor's
signature, so the description says exactly that rather than "was signed into
law". That is the Maryland lesson.

18 is the constitutional majority in a 34-seat Senate, so this passed by the
narrowest possible margin.

## SB 526, House roll 507, 72-25, became law

⚠ **The title is wrong and an earlier version is misleading.** LegiScan titles
this "Creating Pharmacist Prescribing Authority Act", and the Senate committee
substitute did create a new article by that name, with prescribing authority for
any condition that is "minor and generally self-limiting" and with the pharmacist
permitted, not required, to notify the primary care doctor.

None of that is the law. The House amended the bill on the floor on 11 April,
before the roll call used here, and the enrolled act instead amends §30-5-10 and
is far narrower:

- prescribing is limited to drugs other than controlled substances, in line with
  federal labelling;
- it reaches only three conditions confirmed by a waived test, which are
  influenza, COVID-19 and RSV, plus the refill of an expired prescription for an
  epinephrine injector;
- the pharmacist **shall** notify the primary care doctor within 72 hours;
- a prescription is capped at a 30-day supply within six months.

The enrolled act creates no short title, so the description does not use the name
"Pharmacist Prescribing Authority Act". The House roll used here came after the
floor amendment, so it is a vote on the enacted text.

`healthcare_affordability`, a yes vote was for, `nay` null.
